# Meal Account and Meal Charge Procedure

Watertown School District 14-4 | Food Service
Administrative procedure under board policy EFB (Free and Reduced-Price Food Services). Revised August 2025; reviewed August 2026 with no changes.

## 1. Purpose

This procedure explains how student meal accounts work, what happens when an account runs low or negative, and how the district collects unpaid meal charges. It is shared with families at the start of each school year and when a student enrolls.

## 2. One account per family

- Each family has one meal account shared by all of its children in all district buildings. Students use a personal lunch number at the register (elementary students use a card with a barcode).
- Families may add money online through the Food Service page (a small convenience fee is charged by the payment processor, not the district), or by cash or check made out to "Watertown School District" at any school office or the District Office.
- Money stays in the account from year to year. When the last child in a family graduates or leaves the district, the family may request a refund of any balance over $5.00, or donate it to the district's meal account assistance fund.

## 3. Prices

Prices are set by the school board each spring. See "Meal prices and free and reduced application info 2026-27" for the current year. Students approved for free meals are never charged for a reimbursable breakfast or lunch. A la carte items and extra milk are not covered by free or reduced-price benefits and require a positive balance.

## 4. Low and negative balances

- **Low balance (under $10.00):** an automatic email and ParentSquare message goes to the family each Monday.
- **Negative balance (below -$10.00):** the building office sends a letter home and Food Service calls the family. We include a free and reduced-price meal application with every negative-balance letter.
- **Below -$50.00:** the building principal or counselor contacts the family to talk about options, including a payment plan.
- Students in grades 9-12 with a negative balance may not buy a la carte items until the balance is positive.

## 5. No child is refused a meal

A student who comes through the line without money will receive the regular reimbursable meal of the day. Students are never given a different meal, have a meal thrown away, or are identified in front of other students because of an account balance. Staff talk with parents, not students, about money owed (a quiet reminder to a high school student is allowed).

## 6. Collection of unpaid meal charges

- Unpaid charges are a family debt, not the student's. The district does not use a collection agency for meal debt and does not withhold report cards, transcripts or participation in activities because of meal charges.
- Balances unpaid at the end of the school year carry forward. Balances the district determines are uncollectible are written off in June and paid from the general fund, as federal rules require; they are not paid from the food service fund.
- At the end of 2025-26 the district wrote off $3,418.62 in uncollectible meal charges (reported to the board in July 2026).

## 7. Families who need help

Any family can apply for free or reduced-price meals at any time during the year. If a family's circumstances change (a job loss, a new baby, a divorce), they should apply right away. Food Service staff will help with the application by phone or in person.

The district's meal account assistance fund, supported by donated account balances and an annual contribution from a local service club, may pay down a negative balance for a family in hardship. Requests go through the building counselor or principal.

## 8. Staff and adults

Staff and adult visitors must pay at the time of purchase or have a positive balance. Adult accounts may not go negative.
