## Superintendent's third listening session set for Tuesday, November 10, at the High School

WATERTOWN - Superintendent Lauren Hegdahl will hold the last of her three fall listening sessions on **Tuesday, November 10, from 6:30 to 8:00 p.m. in the Watertown High School library**. The session is open to parents, students, staff, taxpayers and anyone else who cares about Watertown's schools. No sign-up is needed.

"The first two sessions told me a lot about what this community values," Hegdahl said. "I heard from parents who want phones out of classrooms, from grandparents who have paid school taxes for forty years, and from employers who want more of our students in the trades. I want to hear from people who haven't been able to come yet, and an evening at the High School should work for more working families."

Hegdahl started as superintendent on September 21. The listening sessions are part of her 90-day entry plan, which also includes visits to every school building and a review of the district's budget and facilities. She will report what she has learned to the school board in December.

### What we heard so far

The first session, on Tuesday, October 6, at the Intermediate School, drew 38 people. The most common topics were class sizes, student cell phone use during the school day, and the new lunch menu.

The second session, on Thursday, October 15, at noon at the Watertown Regional Library, drew 22 people. Topics included property taxes and how the school budget works, career and technical education and the dual-credit partnership with Lake Area Technical College, cell phones, school lunches and staff pay.

Some early responses:
- **Phones.** The district is forming a Personal Device Review Committee of parents, teachers, students and principals to review the current policy on student phones and other devices. The committee will meet in November and survey families. A recommendation is expected to go to the school board in January.
- **Lunch.** Food Service has added a second serving line at the High School on its two heaviest days and will offer larger entree portions for high school students twice a week starting in November.
- **Taxes and the budget.** The district's finance office has posted a plain-language explainer about the school portion of the property tax statement on the district website. The district has no bond or opt-out election planned.
