# Gifted Education Services, 2026-27: Overview for Families and Staff

Watertown School District 14-4 | Teaching and Learning | Megan Aamot, Ed.D., Director
Revised September 2026. Posted on the Teaching and Learning page; principals and counselors use this for family questions.

## Who we serve

The district identifies students in grades 2-8 who need learning beyond the regular classroom in one or more areas, and offers advanced coursework at the High School. This year about 210 students in grades 2-8 are identified (about 8% of those grades). Identification is not a label for life; we review each student's services every year.

## How students are identified

1. **Referral.** Any teacher, parent or student may refer a student at any time. Referral forms are on the Teaching and Learning page or at any school office.
2. **Universal review in grade 2.** In January, every second grader takes a short reasoning assessment in class. Students who score in the top range are reviewed further. Families of second graders receive a letter in December explaining the review.
3. **Multiple measures.** A building team looks at classroom work, teacher observations, the reasoning assessment, and state and district assessment results. No single score decides.
4. **Family notice.** Families receive a letter with the team's decision within 30 school days of a referral. Families may ask for a meeting to discuss the decision.

## Services by level

**Grades 2-4 (Jefferson, Lincoln, Fieldcrest Elementary).** A gifted education teacher meets identified students in small groups twice a week for enrichment in math reasoning and project-based learning. Classroom teachers adjust work using pre-assessments, so students who already know the material move ahead.

**Grades 5-6 (Intermediate School).** Advanced math classes for students working above grade level; a weekly enrichment block for identified students; academic competitions (math league, quiz bowl, a spring invention fair).

**Grades 7-8 (Middle School).** Accelerated math (Algebra I in grade 8 for qualifying students, with high school credit); advanced English and science sections; clubs such as robotics, MathCounts and Science Olympiad.

**Grades 9-12 (High School).** Honors and Advanced Placement courses, dual credit through Lake Area Technical College and university partners, independent study, and academic competitions. Counselors help students plan an advanced course sequence.

## Staffing

- 2.0 FTE gifted education teachers (one serving Jefferson and Fieldcrest Elementary, one serving Lincoln and the Intermediate School).
- Middle School and High School advanced courses are taught by content teachers with training in advanced coursework.
- A gifted education coordinator role (stipend) is held by one of the gifted education teachers, who also coordinates the grade 2 universal review.

## Communication with families

- Identified students' families receive a service plan each fall (by October 30) and a progress note each semester.
- Parent information night: Thursday, January 28, 2027, 6:30 p.m., Intermediate School library (topic this year: "Raising curious kids without burning them out").
- Questions about a specific student go first to the classroom teacher or the gifted education teacher, then the building principal.

## What changed this year

- Universal review moved from grade 3 to grade 2, so students can be served a year earlier.
- The Intermediate added a second advanced math section (demand: 52 students).
- The Middle School started a Science Olympiad team (14 students; first competition in February).

## Budget

Gifted education is funded from the General Fund: two teaching positions, $9,500 for materials and competition fees, and stipends for activity advisers. Families pay no fees for gifted services; competition travel is paid by the district.
