Dear Mr. Gruber,

Thank you for your letter of September 28 and for including a copy of your tax statement. You asked two questions: why the school line is the largest part of your property tax bill, and whether the district plans to ask voters for more money.

On the first question, it is true that in South Dakota the school district's share is usually the largest piece of a homeowner's property tax statement. That is because, under the state funding formula, local property taxes pay for a large share of the cost of educating students, with state aid making up the rest. The school line on your statement combines several levies: the general fund levy, which the state sets and which helps pay for teachers and day-to-day operations; the capital outlay levy, which pays for buildings, buses, equipment and repairs; and the special education levy. The school board adopts the district's tax request each year within the limits state law allows, and the county auditor turns that request into the rate you see on your statement based on the taxable value of property in the district.

When property values rise across the district, the rate per thousand dollars of value usually goes down, but an individual bill can still go up if that home's assessed value rose faster than average. Your assessment is set by the county Director of Equalization, not by the school district, and questions about it go to that office.

On the second question: the district has no bond election or opt-out scheduled, and the school board has not discussed one. In the spring of 2027 the board plans to begin a long-range facilities review, and any recommendation that came out of that work would be discussed in public meetings before the board voted on anything. A bond issue would require an election, and an opt-out would require a public board resolution that voters may refer to an election. You would hear about either long before a vote.

Our business manager, Sheri Voegeli, has prepared a short explainer on how the school portion of your tax bill is built. I have enclosed a copy, and she would be glad to walk through your statement with you; she can be reached at (605) 882-6312 ext. 1102.

I appreciate a taxpayer who reads the statement closely and asks. I hope you will come to one of my listening sessions this fall; the next one is Thursday, October 15, at noon at the Regional Library.
