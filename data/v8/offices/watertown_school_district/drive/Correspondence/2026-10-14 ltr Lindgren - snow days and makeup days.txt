Dear Ms. Lindgren,

Thank you for your note and for the good questions about winter. Since I am new to Watertown, you are kind to warn me that "the first blizzard will be your real job interview." I suspect you are right.

You asked how we decide to close or delay the start of school, and why last year some families heard the news later than others.

How we decide. On a morning when the weather is a concern, a road check starts at 4:30 a.m. Our transportation coordinator, our buildings and grounds staff and the transportation contractor's dispatcher drive the routes, including gravel roads, and check lots and sidewalks. I also look at the National Weather Service forecast, especially wind chills, and talk with neighboring superintendents and the city street department. I make the decision by 5:30 a.m. The options are a two-hour late start, an early dismissal, a full closing, or, on some mornings, running routes on hard-surface roads only.

How families hear. Notices go out by 6:00 a.m. by ParentSquare, which calls, texts, emails and sends an app notification to every parent and guardian listed for a student in our student information system. We also post on the district website and Facebook page and give the notice to local radio and TV. No sign-up is needed. Our technology director believes that most of last winter's delays came from out-of-date phone numbers and from parents who had never replied "Y" to the ParentSquare opt-in text, which is needed for text messages. Kayla Brende, our communications coordinator, will send a reminder to all families in early November asking everyone to check their contact information with their school office.

Makeup days. Three snow days are built into this year's calendar. If we use more than three, the first two makeup days are Monday, February 15, and Friday, April 2. Any others would be set by the school board.

Our full winter weather page, including what a two-hour delay means for preschool and breakfast, is at watertown.k12.sd.us/weather.

One last thing I want to say clearly: the decision to close is about whether it is safe for students and staff to travel across the whole district. If a family lives somewhere the roads are worse than in town and decides it isn't safe to send a child, that is the family's call, and the absence will be excused.

Thank you for writing, and for the welcome.

Sincerely,
Lauren Hegdahl
Superintendent, Watertown School District 14-4
