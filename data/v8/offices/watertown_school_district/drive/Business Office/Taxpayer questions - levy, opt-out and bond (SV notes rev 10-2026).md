# Taxpayer Questions: The School Line on Your Property Tax Statement

Business Office notes for the superintendent, front desk and website | Sheri Voegeli | rev. October 2, 2026 (rev. 1 was August 2025; updated for Dr. Hegdahl and the Gruber letter)
A shorter version is posted on the website under Budget and Finance.

## The short answers

- **Is the district planning an opt-out or a bond?** No. The district has no opt-out or bond election scheduled, and the school board has not discussed one. The board plans to begin a long-range facilities review in spring 2027. Any proposal that came out of it would be discussed in public meetings first.
- **Why is the school line the biggest part of my bill?** Because South Dakota funds schools through a mix of state aid and local property taxes, and the local share is collected through the school levies on your statement. The city and county lines pay for city and county services.
- **Who sets my home's value?** The county Director of Equalization assesses property. Questions or appeals about your assessment go to the county, not the school district.

## How the school line is built

The school line on a county tax statement combines several separate levies:

1. **General fund levy.** State law sets the rate for each class of property (agricultural, owner-occupied, and other). The district does not choose a higher rate. This levy, together with state aid, pays for teachers, classroom costs and day-to-day operations.
2. **Capital outlay levy.** The board sets this within a limit in state law. It pays for buildings and repairs, equipment, technology and textbooks.
3. **Special education levy.** The board sets this within a limit in state law. It pays for services for students with disabilities.
4. **Pension levy.** A small levy within a state limit, used for early retirement obligations.
5. **Opt-out or bond levy (if any).** The district has none at this time.

Each summer the board adopts a **tax request** in dollars for each levy. The county auditor divides each request by the taxable value of all property of that class in the district to get the rate (dollars per $1,000 of taxable value) printed on your statement. The amount you owe for the school line is your property's taxable value multiplied by those rates.

## Why my bill can rise when the rate falls

When property values across the district rise, the rate per $1,000 usually drops, because the same request is spread over more value. But a bill can still go up if one home's taxable value rose faster than the average, or if the request itself went up (for example, because state law allowed growth for new construction).

**Illustration only (round numbers, not actual rates):**
- A home with a taxable value of $250,000.
- Suppose the combined school rate is $9.00 per $1,000. School line: 250 x $9.00 = $2,250.
- Next year, district-wide values rise 8% and the rate falls to $8.40. If this home's value also rose 8% (to $270,000), the school line is 270 x $8.40 = $2,268, nearly the same.
- If this home's value rose 15% (to $287,500), the school line is 287.5 x $8.40 = $2,415, an increase, even though the rate went down.

## Opt-outs and bonds: how they work

- **Opt-out.** A district may "opt out" of the state's property tax limits for the general fund, for a set number of years and dollar amount, by a resolution of the school board adopted in a public meeting. Voters may refer the resolution to an election by petition. Watertown has no opt-out in effect.
- **Bond.** A bond issue borrows money for a major building project and is repaid through a separate debt service levy. A school bond requires approval by voters at an election. The district has no bond levy outstanding.

Either one would be discussed in public board meetings and work sessions well before any vote.

## Timing

- Tax statements for taxes payable in 2026 were mailed by the county in the winter; first-half payments were due April 30 and second-half payments are due October 31. (Check the county treasurer's site for exact dates.)
- The district receives the fall settlement from the county in early Nov. The business manager reports it to the board at the Nov. 23 work session.
- The board adopts next year's tax request at its July annual meeting.
