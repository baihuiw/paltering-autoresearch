Dear Mr. and Mrs. Ferraro:

Thank you for your email of September 24 telling us that our meter reader left your back pasture gate open when he read your meter on September 22, and that your two goats were out on the road for part of the afternoon before a neighbor helped you round them up. I am very sorry. That should not have happened, and I am grateful no one, animal or driver, was hurt.

I asked our Customer Service Supervisor, Brandi Coles, who supervises our meter readers, to look into it. She spoke with the reader the same day she received your email. He told her he had gone through the gate to reach the meter box, which sits just inside your fence line, and believed he had latched it behind him. Because your account shows a meter read at 1:48 p.m. and you found the gate open shortly after 3:00, we accept that he did not latch it properly. He has been reminded of our rules, and the matter has been noted in his file.

What our meter readers are expected to do

Our four meter readers read about 25,800 meters every month on 22 routes, and nearly all of those visits go unnoticed, which is how it should be. The rules they follow are simple:

- Close and latch every gate they open, and check it before walking away.
- Never enter a yard or field with a loose dog or other animal they are unsure about. If they cannot reach the meter safely, they skip it, leave a "We couldn't read your meter" card, and that month's bill is estimated from your past usage.
- Never cut through yards to save time.
- Wear the Authority uniform and photo ID badge at all times.
- Park legally and off the road where they can.
- Report any damaged meter box lid so a crew can replace it.

What we have done for your account

We have added a gate note to your account. The handheld device our readers carry now shows, before they reach your property, that the meter is inside a livestock gate that must be latched. The note stays on the account until you ask us to remove it. If you would rather the reader not go through the gate at all, Brandi can talk with you about other options.

If anything like this happens again, or if you have any concern about how a reader behaves on your property, please call Brandi Coles directly at (540) 586-7210, ext. 12, or email bcoles@bedfordwater.com. She keeps track of these reports, and a second concern about the same reader is handled more formally.

Your next reading on this route will be around October 22. Thank you for taking the time to write to us, and for being so understanding in your email. A neighbor who helps catch goats is worth a lot; I hope you will pass along our thanks to yours.
