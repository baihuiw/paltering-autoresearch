Why the water turned brown

The main under Mountain Avenue is a 6-inch cast iron pipe, among the oldest in the Town. Over many decades, iron and manganese build up as a thin layer of sediment on the inside of pipes like this one. On September 21 the main cracked around its full circumference, and our crew had to turn off the valves on either side to make the repair. About 26 homes, including yours, were without water for up to four hours.

When a main is refilled after a repair, the water rushes back in faster than normal and in a different direction. That stirs up the sediment, and it can travel into homes nearby before it settles or flushes out. The result is water that looks yellow, orange or brown. It is unpleasant, but it is not a health risk. Our crew flushed the nearest hydrant after the repair, but your home is near the end of the line on that block, and it is clear some sediment reached you after they left. I have asked our crews to flush longer at the end of a block after repairs on older mains.

How to clear it

If the water at your home is not fully clear, or if it happens again:
- Run the cold water tap in your bathtub (usually the lowest faucet with the highest flow) for 5 to 10 minutes.
- If it is still discolored, wait 30 minutes and try again. Sediment often settles out on its own.
- Avoid running hot water until the cold runs clear, so sediment does not get drawn into your water heater.
- Do not do laundry until the water is clear.
- Remove and rinse your faucet aerators if flow seems weak.

If your water is still discolored after a few tries, call Customer Service at (540) 586-7210, ext. 1, and we will send a crew to flush the hydrant again.

Your laundry

Please do not use bleach on the stained items; it can set rust stains permanently. We keep a rust-stain remover packet at our front counter for exactly this purpose, free to customers. It is added to a wash with the stained items and works on most cotton and polyester. I have enclosed two packets with this letter. If they do not do the job, please let us know.

Your bill

You asked whether we would credit your bill. The Authority does not give bill credits for discolored water itself, because the water is safe and the charges cover the cost of delivering it. However, we do allow a one-time flushing-water allowance when a customer has to run water to clear their lines after our work. We have credited your account for 1,000 gallons of water and sewer at this year's rates ($6.85 + $8.95 = $15.80). The credit will appear on your next statement. I hope it at least covers the water you ran to clear the pipes.

A heads-up

Our crews will flush hydrants on Mountain Avenue on Wednesday, October 21, as part of the fall flushing program for Bedford Central. Your water may look discolored that day for a few hours. Please hold off on laundry until your cold tap runs clear, and keep the second packet handy just in case.

Longer term, the cast iron mains in that part of town are on our list for replacement. The recent project on Oakwood and Edmund Streets is the kind of work that ends these problems for good, and I will keep Mountain Avenue on the list as we plan future years.
