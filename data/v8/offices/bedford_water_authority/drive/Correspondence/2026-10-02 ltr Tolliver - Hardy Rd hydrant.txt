Dear Mr. Tolliver:

At the Board meeting on September 15 you asked when the fire hydrant on Hardy Road near the church in Stewartsville would be painted and flow-tested. I started as Executive Director a few days later, and your question was one of the first follow-ups on my list. I am glad to report that the work is done.

What we did

On Wednesday, September 30, our hydrant crew (Crew 1) cleaned the hydrant, scraped off the loose paint, primed it and gave it a fresh coat. The same morning they exercised the hydrant valve and the gate valve that serves it, checked that the hydrant drains properly after it is closed (important before freezing weather), and replaced a worn cap gasket on the pumper nozzle. They then flow-tested the hydrant using the next hydrant down the line on Hardy Road as the pressure gauge.

The results:
- Static pressure (no water flowing): 72 pounds per square inch
- Flow measured at the test hydrant: 1,050 gallons per minute
- Residual pressure while flowing: 48 pounds per square inch
- Calculated available flow at 20 pounds per square inch: about 1,590 gallons per minute

In plain terms, this is a strong hydrant. The 8-inch main on Hardy Road was repaired in August after a small corrosion leak, and the test shows it is carrying water well. We have sent the test results to the fire company that covers Stewartsville so they have them on file.

How hydrants are color-coded

You may notice that the top of the hydrant is now a different color than before. The Authority paints hydrant bodies yellow so they are easy to see, and paints the top (the bonnet) and the caps to show how much water the hydrant can deliver, following the national fire protection standard that fire departments use:

- Blue: 1,500 gallons per minute or more
- Green: 1,000 to 1,499 gallons per minute
- Orange: 500 to 999 gallons per minute
- Red: less than 500 gallons per minute

The Hardy Road hydrant tested above 1,500 gallons per minute, so its bonnet and caps are now blue. Before this test the bonnet was green, based on a 2014 test. A firefighter arriving at night can tell at a glance how much water to expect.

A hydrant painted white, or one with a black bag over it, is out of service; please call us if you ever see one, or a hydrant that is leaking or has been hit by a vehicle.

What comes next

We flow-test and paint hydrants on a rotating schedule, and we are replacing older hydrants across the system. This year's program will replace 40 hydrants; 14 are done so far. Stewartsville hydrants will be flushed in the spring, and we will post the dates on our website and Facebook page.

Thank you for raising this at the meeting, and for keeping an eye on the hydrants in your community. Questions like yours help us set priorities. If you ever have a concern about a hydrant, you can call Rusty Tharpe, our Distribution and Collection Superintendent, at (540) 586-7210, ext. 51, or use the "Leak or main break" choice on the contact form at bedfordwater.com.
