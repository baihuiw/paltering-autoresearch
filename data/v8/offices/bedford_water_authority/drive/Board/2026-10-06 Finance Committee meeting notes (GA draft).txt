1. AUGUST 2026 FINANCIAL REPORT
Mr. Ashworth reviewed the August report. Operating revenue was $2.02 million, compared with $1.94 million in July. Water sales were 4.8 percent above August 2025, which he attributed to the rate adjustment and a dry second half of the month. Sewer revenue was flat with last year. Operating expenses for July and August together were 15.6 percent of the FY2027 operating budget of $27.4 million, slightly under the straight-line 16.7 percent. Electricity is slightly above budget from high pumping in late August.
Days cash on hand at August 31: 211 (214 at July 31). The drop reflects the semiannual debt service payment on the 2025 revenue bonds made August 1.
Mr. Price asked for a one-page trend chart of days cash going back three years for the November packet. Mr. Ashworth will add it.
Ms. Okafor asked about the investment balances. Mr. Ashworth said the operating reserve is held in the state local government investment pool and two bank CDs, with a weighted yield of about 4.3 percent.
September figures will close the week of October 12 and go into the October 20 packet with August.

3. INVESTMENT POLICY REVIEW
The investment policy (Finance Policy 2.20) is due for its two-year review. Mr. Ashworth proposed three changes: (a) update the list of permitted investments to match current state code; (b) raise the maximum maturity for operating reserve investments from two years to three; (c) require quarterly instead of semiannual investment reports to the committee. Ms. Okafor supported (a) and (c) and asked for more information on (b), including how much of the reserve would be affected and what yield difference to expect. Mr. Price agreed. Staff will return with a redline and a short analysis in November. No action taken.

4. REQUEST FOR A SENIOR / FIXED-INCOME DISCOUNT
At the September 15 Board meeting, Brenda Siler of Forest asked the Board to consider a discount for seniors, and the Chair referred the request to this committee.
Mr. Ashworth explained that there is no discount program today. The Authority is funded only by customer bills and fees, so any discount would be paid for by other customers through rates. He summarized the drivers of the July 1 increase (typical bill from $119.60 to $124.20, up $4.60 a month or 3.8 percent): debt service on the 2025 revenue bonds, chemicals up about 14 percent from FY2025, purchased water from Lynchburg up 4 percent, electricity up 6 percent, and the 3 percent COLA. Customers in hardship can call Customer Service and be pointed to local assistance funds.
Discussion: Ms. Okafor said several Town residents had raised the same concern with her and that she would like to see what other Virginia utilities do, including income-based programs and voluntary round-up funds. Mr. Price said any program should be simple to run and should not require Customer Service to verify income. Mr. Kessinger said his previous utility ran a round-up program through a local nonprofit and he would share how it worked.
Consensus: staff to bring options, with estimated price tags and the effect on the typical bill, to the January 2027 committee meeting so the committee can decide whether anything goes into the FY2028 budget. Mr. Kessinger will send Ms. Siler a short letter saying the committee is studying it.
