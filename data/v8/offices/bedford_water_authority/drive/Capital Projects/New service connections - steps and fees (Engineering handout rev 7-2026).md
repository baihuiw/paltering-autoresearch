# Getting Water and Sewer to a New Home
### Steps and connection fees for a new single-family service
_Engineering Department. Handout rev. 7/2026 (fees effective July 1, 2026). Replaces rev. 1/2025._

Building a new house? This sheet explains who does what, what you pay and how long it takes. Questions go to Engineering at (540) 586-7210, ext. 3, or engineering@bedfordwater.com. On the website contact form, choose "New service / connection fees."

---

## Step by step

**1. Ask for an availability letter (free).**
Email or call Engineering with the lot address or tax map number. We check our maps and send a water/sewer availability letter, usually within **5 business days**. The letter tells you whether a water main runs in front of the lot, whether public sewer is available, and the meter size we expect to set. If there is no main in front of the lot, you have two choices: a private well and septic system permitted through the county health department, or paying to extend a main to the lot under the Authority's developer policy.

**2. Apply for the connection.**
Send the application with the lot address, tax map number and a recorded plat or site drawing showing the house and the meter location you want.

**3. Pay the connection fees.**
Fees must be paid **before the county building permit is released** for the water and sewer work. Pay at the Customer Service counter (check, or card with the $3 convenience fee) or by mail.

**4. The Authority installs the tap and meter box.**
Our crew taps the main, runs the pipe to the property line and sets the meter box, usually within **15 business days** of payment. Allow more time in winter or when a road bore is needed. We call before the crew comes so you can stake the meter location.

**5. Your contractor installs the owner's line.**
The owner's contractor runs the water line from the meter box to the house, and the sewer lateral from the cleanout to the house. These lines belong to the owner. They are checked by the county building official as part of the house permit, not by the Authority.

**6. Open your account before you move in.**
Visit the Customer Service counter or call ext. 1. A new account needs the $25 application fee and the residential deposits ($125 water, $125 sewer). The meter is turned on once the account is open.

**What the fees pay for.** Capital recovery is a new customer's share of the treatment facilities, pumps and mains already built. The tap fee covers our crew, materials and the meter box; the meter fee covers the meter and radio unit.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can my builder pay the fees?**
Yes, and many do. Builders often pay the connection fees on the owner's behalf and include them in the contract price. The account itself is opened in the owner's or occupant's name.

**I already have a well. Do I have to connect?**
Not if your lot is not required to connect under county rules. If you do connect, a well may be kept for outdoor use only, and there must be no physical connection between the well piping and the public water piping. Water Quality (ext. 7) handles cross-connection questions.

**Can I get a separate irrigation meter?**
Yes. An irrigation meter is a second water connection, so it carries the water capital recovery, tap and meter fees for its size, but no sewer fees, and sewer is not billed on the water it records. An approved backflow preventer is required and must be tested each year.

**Are fees going up next July?**
We don't know yet. Connection fees are reviewed with each year's budget. The FY2028 budget goes to the Finance Committee in March 2027, with a public hearing in April 2027. **The fees in effect on the day you pay are the fees you pay.** The tap must be installed within 12 months of payment or the application is renewed.

**Who do I call about a leak after move-in?**
At or before the meter: call us, press 9 after hours. Between the meter box and the house: that is the owner's line (see "Who fixes what" on bedfordwater.com).
