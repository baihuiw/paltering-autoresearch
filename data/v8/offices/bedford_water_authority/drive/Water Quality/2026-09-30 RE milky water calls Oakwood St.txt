Yes, that's us. The contractor tied the last section of the new 8-inch on Oakwood into service about 7:30 this morning, and we moved the last houses on the block over from the old cast iron.

A new main always traps some air at the high points. Under pressure it dissolves into the water and comes back out at the faucet as tiny bubbles. That's the milky look. "Fizzes" is a good description.

We flushed both ends before tie-in, but the hump near the 700 block holds air. I'll send Crew 1 back this afternoon to open the hydrant at the 700 block for 15-20 minutes. That should move most of it.

Agree with Rusty, this is air. Easy way to confirm, and it's what we tell customers: fill a clear glass and set it on the counter. If it's air, the glass clears **from the bottom up** within a minute or two as the bubbles rise to the top.

Air in the water is not a health concern. It's safe to drink and use.

Brandi, here are talking points for the desk. Feel free to trim:

1. "Crews put a new water main in service on Oakwood Street this morning. When a new pipe is filled, small amounts of air get trapped in it. The milky look is tiny air bubbles, like a freshly poured soda."
2. "You can check it yourself: fill a clear glass and let it sit for a minute. If it clears from the bottom up, it's air."
3. "Air in the water is harmless. The water is safe to drink, cook with and use."
4. "Running your cold tap for a few minutes helps clear it. It usually goes away within a day or two."
5. "If the water doesn't clear in a glass, or if you see color or particles, please call us back at ext. 1 and we'll send someone."

For Mrs. Hensley: yes, it is safe for the grandbaby. If she'd feel better, she can let a glass clear and use that. I can call her myself if she wants.

Please log these as "Cloudy/milky" in the WQ call log, not "Discolored," so they don't get mixed in with the Mountain Ave calls.
