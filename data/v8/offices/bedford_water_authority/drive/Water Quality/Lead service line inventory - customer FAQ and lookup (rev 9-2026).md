# Lead Service Line Inventory: Questions and Answers
_Bedford Regional Water Authority, Water Quality. Rev. 9/2026 (EC). For the website, the Customer Service counter and the Forest community association talk on Oct 22._

---

Every water system must keep an inventory of the pipes that bring water from the main to each home and business. This sheet explains how to look up your address and what we have found so far.

## What we have found so far
Our records did not show the pipe material for about **3,400** addresses. As of the end of September, our staff have field-checked **2,180** of them. **We have not found a single lead pipe.** We have found **41 galvanized steel lines** that must be replaced under federal rules, and each of those is scheduled for replacement.

## How do I look up my address?
1. Go to **bedfordwater.com**.
2. Choose **Water Quality > Lead Service Line Inventory**.
3. Type your street address in the search box and select it from the list.
4. The map zooms to your property and shows two results: the **Authority side** (from the main to the meter box) and the **customer side** (from the meter box to your house).

If you do not have internet access, call Water Quality at (540) 586-7210, ext. 7, and we will look it up for you.

## What do the categories mean?
Each side of the pipe is listed in one of four categories:

| Category | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Non-lead** | Verified copper, plastic or other material that is not lead. |
| **Galvanized Requiring Replacement** | Galvanized steel that is or may have been downstream of lead. Federal rules treat these as needing replacement. |
| **Lead** | Lead pipe. None found in our system to date. |
| **Lead Status Unknown** | We do not yet have a record or a field check. It does **not** mean the pipe is lead. |

## My address says "Lead Status Unknown." What should I do?
You can ask for a **free verification visit**. Call Water Quality at ext. 7, or use the contact form at bedfordwater.com and choose **"Water quality"** from the menu. A staff member will look at the pipe where it enters your house, usually at the basement wall or crawl space near the main valve. The visit takes about 15 minutes and is usually scheduled **within 3 weeks**. We will update the map within a few days of the visit.

You can also check yourself. Find where the water pipe comes through the wall or floor, before the main valve. Gently scratch the pipe with a coin:
- **Copper** looks like a penny where scratched.
- **Plastic** is white, black or blue and sounds hollow when tapped.
- **Galvanized steel** is dull and dark, and a magnet sticks to it.
- **Lead** is dull and dark, scratches to a bright silver color, and a magnet does **not** stick.

You can send a photo through the contact form, but only a staff visit can change the map to Non-lead.

## I live in an older home near Oakwood Street or Edmund Street.
During the 2026 Oakwood/Edmund water main replacement (project WS-26-12), the Authority replaced the pipe from the main to the meter box with new copper at every home on the project. Those Authority-side lines are now listed as Non-lead. The owner's pipe from the meter to the house was not part of the project; if yours shows "Unknown," ask for a free visit.

## What if my pipe is galvanized?
We will contact you directly by letter. The Authority replaces its side at no charge. We will explain the options for the owner's side and coordinate the work so both sections can be done at the same time when possible.

## Can I get my water tested for lead?
Households with **a child under 6 or a pregnant resident** can ask for a **free lead test kit**. Call ext. 7. We mail or drop off a sample bottle with instructions; you take the sample first thing in the morning before using any water and return it to our office. The Authority's contract lab analyzes it, and results come back in about **2 to 3 weeks**. We will call you with the result and send a letter.

Other customers who want a test can call us for the names of certified labs.

## What can I do in the meantime?
These are good habits in any home, whatever your pipes are made of:
- **Run your cold water** for 30 seconds to 2 minutes if it has been sitting in the pipes for several hours, such as first thing in the morning or when you get home from work.
- **Use cold water for drinking, cooking and preparing baby formula.** Hot water can pick up more metal from household plumbing. Boiling water does not remove lead.
- **Clean your faucet aerators** every few months. Unscrew the screen at the end of the faucet and rinse out any grit.

## Is lead in the water coming from the treatment plants?
No. The water leaving our plants does not contain lead. Lead, when it occurs, comes from old pipes and plumbing fixtures inside buildings and in the line to the house. Our treatment includes corrosion control that helps keep metals from dissolving into the water.
