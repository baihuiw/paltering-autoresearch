# Marla's standard talk: "What I expect in the 2027 session"

BH notes, rev. Oct 15, 2026. For civic clubs, chambers, ag groups and church groups that ask for 15-20 minutes plus questions. Marla speaks from these notes, not a script. Update after each talk.

## Opening (2 min)

- Thank the host by name. Something local: their town, a business she knows, a 4-H family.
- Who she is: fourth-generation rancher near Cottonwood Falls; 4-H leader 14 years; extension council; first public office. "I'm a rancher, not a politician."
- How she got here (one sentence, no drama): chosen by the district convention in September and appointed by the governor after the previous representative resigned. Don't say more about the previous representative.

## How the Legislature works right now (3 min)

- The session starts Monday, January 11. Kansas has a part-time citizen Legislature: 125 House members, 40 senators.
- Right now it's the interim: committees study issues and make recommendations; members do homework and listen.
- What she's done so far: town halls in every county; district office open in Eureka; first bill draft request (county bridges); research request on school transportation.

## Five things she expects to work on (8-10 min; pick 3 if short on time)

1. **Property taxes.** Most common topic in her mail. Supports a limit on how fast a homeowner's valuation can rise each year; a firm cap needs a constitutional amendment, so she'll vote to put it before voters. Wants clearer valuation notices and a recorded vote when local governments raise more than the revenue neutral rate.
2. **Rural schools.** Kids riding over an hour each way; driver shortages; the transportation part of the funding formula. Meeting with all five superintendents in November.
3. **Roads and bridges.** County bridges posted below the weight of today's trucks; local match is hard for small counties; her bill draft request asks for more state help.
4. **Rural health and EMS.** Ambulance crews of five covering a county; long transfers; hospital staffing. "Out here, the ambulance is the hospital for the first hour."
5. **Water.** From the Neosho to the Verdigris; rural water districts extending lines to farms on failing wells; state water plan.

Tailor: for a chamber, add workforce and housing. For ag groups, add ag land valuation and deer damage. For Wilson County, add the Fredonia hospital. For Emporia, add southwest Emporia stormwater (city matter; she listens).

## Questions (5-10 min)

Common questions and her short answers:
- *"Will you cut my property taxes?"* "I'll work to limit how fast valuations climb and to make sure local increases come with a vote. The bill itself is set locally, and I want you to know who's raising it."
- *"Are you running?"* At an office event: "That's a question for another day; today I'm here as your representative." No election talk at office events.
- *"What about [national issue]?"* "That's for Congress. I'll stick to what Kansas can do."
- *"Can you help my cousin with unemployment / a license / a refund?"* "Yes. Have them call Diane at the district office."
- Anything she doesn't know: "I don't know yet. Give Kelsey your name and I'll get back to you." Then do it.

## Logistics she likes

- Arrive 15 minutes early; stay 15 minutes after.
- Kelsey sends a short bio if the host wants to introduce her.

## Notes from past talks (what worked, what didn't)

- Livestock Assn: the bridges story (grain truck detour) landed; people nodded. Shorter on the Legislature-process part next time.
- Farm Bureau: lots of questions on ag land valuation; she should have a one-line explanation of use-value appraisal ready ("Farm ground is valued on what it can produce, not on what it would sell for").
- Chamber: three minutes is very short; open with the district office and close with one priority.
