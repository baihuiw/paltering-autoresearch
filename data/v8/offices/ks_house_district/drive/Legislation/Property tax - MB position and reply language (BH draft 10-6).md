# Property tax: Marla's position and reply language

BH draft, Oct 6, 2026. Reviewed with MB Oct 8 (her edits in). Use for replies, town hall prep and the Chamber coffee. This is the most common topic in our mail by a wide margin.

## What people are telling us

- Valuations jumped again this year. Retirees in particular say the house hasn't changed, only the number. Typical letters cite 15-25% increases.
- Farm ground: questions about ag use value and why the bill went up when the land didn't.
- Several people think the state sets their valuation. It doesn't; the county appraiser does, under state law and Property Valuation Division guidelines.

## Marla's position (her words, from the 10/8 review)

"I support a limit on how fast a homeowner's valuation can climb in a single year, and I'll vote for one. People who've lived in their homes for decades shouldn't be taxed out of them by a number on a notice. I also want local budget increases to come with clear notice and a public vote, so folks know who raised their taxes and why."

- She supports a cap on annual increases in residential valuation. Note for replies: a hard cap on valuation increases has been described by the Revisor as requiring a constitutional amendment, which means a two-thirds vote in each chamber and then a statewide vote. Say "I'll support putting a limit before voters" rather than promising a law that takes effect next year.
- She supports keeping and strengthening the notice and hearing process when a local government wants to raise more than the revenue neutral rate.
- She has not taken a position on any specific bill number from prior sessions. Don't cite one.
- She does not support shifting the cost to sales tax or income tax without knowing the numbers. If pressed: "I want to see the numbers before I vote to move the burden onto someone else."

## Reply language (adapt; keep it short)

> Thank you for writing about your valuation. You're far from alone; it's the number one thing people in our district write to me about. I support a limit on how much a home's valuation can increase in one year, and I'll vote to put one before Kansas voters. I also want any local budget increase above the revenue neutral rate to come with clear notice and a recorded vote.
>
> In the meantime, if you think your valuation is wrong, you can appeal it. The deadline is 30 days from the date your county mailed the valuation notice, so the deadline for this year's notices has passed for most people, but you can also protest when you pay your tax bill. Your county appraiser's office can explain both options.

Adjust the appeal paragraph to the season: valuation notices go out in the spring; payment-under-protest comes with the December tax bill.

## Who to refer to

- County appraiser (each county): valuation questions and appeals.
- County treasurer: tax bills, payment, payment under protest.
- KDOR Property Valuation Division: statewide guidance; not individual valuations.
- Homestead refund and SAFESR (seniors' refund programs): KDOR. Diane can help people check eligibility.
