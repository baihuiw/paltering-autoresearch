# Electric and gas rates: who regulates what

Reference for replies and phone calls. KR, Oct 2026; checked by BH.

We get a few calls a week about utility bills, usually after a rate change or a cold snap. The first question to sort out is **who the caller's provider is**, because that decides who regulates the rates.

## Who sets rates

| Provider type | Who decides rates | Where to send the caller |
|---|---|---|
| Investor-owned electric or gas utility | Kansas Corporation Commission (KCC). Rate changes go through a case at the KCC, with public comment and often a public hearing. | KCC Office of Public Affairs and Consumer Protection, (800) 662-0027 or (785) 271-3140. They take informal complaints by phone, email or online. |
| Rural electric cooperative | Most co-ops in Kansas have chosen to be deregulated from KCC rate oversight and are governed by their member-elected board. | The co-op's member services office and its annual meeting. Members elect the board. |
| City-owned utility | City council | City hall |
| Propane | Not rate-regulated | Supplier; for help paying, LIEAP (DCF) |

The **Citizens' Utility Ratepayer Board (CURB)** represents residential and small commercial ratepayers in KCC cases. Callers who want to follow or comment on a rate case can look for it on the KCC website and submit public comments there.

## Help paying

- **LIEAP** (Low Income Energy Assistance Program), through DCF. Applications open in the winter; check DCF for this season's dates rather than guessing. Callers can apply online or on paper.
- Most utilities and co-ops have payment plans and some have local assistance funds; tell callers to ask before a shutoff notice.
- Kansas has a cold weather rule that limits shutoffs for KCC-regulated utilities in the winter months if customers set up a payment plan. Callers should contact their utility and the KCC for the details that apply to them.

When rate cases come before the KCC, I want the commission to hold utilities to the lowest reasonable cost, and I'll support making it easier for ordinary customers to understand and comment on rate increases before they're approved. Here at home, my office can help you reach the right people about your bill."

- She has not taken a position on any specific pending KCC case. Don't say she has.
- Don't comment on co-op board decisions; those belong to the members.

## Reply language

> Who sets your rate depends on your provider. If your electricity comes from an investor-owned utility, rates are set by the Kansas Corporation Commission after a public case; you can reach the KCC's consumer protection office at (800) 662-0027. If you're served by a rural electric cooperative, rates are set by the co-op's member-elected board. If you need help paying this winter, the LIEAP program through DCF may help, and my office can walk you through it at (620) 583-4130.

## Which provider? Quick way to tell

Ask the caller what name is on the bill. If it's a co-op, the bill usually says "cooperative" and lists a member number. City utilities print the city's name. If they're not sure, have them read the logo or the "questions about your bill" phone number. Don't guess; the answer changes who they should call.
