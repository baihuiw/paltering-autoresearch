# Rural school transportation funding: background for replies

BH, Oct 14, 2026. Draft until the KLRD memo arrives (requested 10/13, promised by 10/23; AC-26-033). After it comes, check every number below against it before using one in a letter.

## Why we're getting these letters

Parents and two superintendents in the district have raised it since the office opened: bus routes in our counties are long, some kids ride more than an hour each way, and districts say what the state formula provides for transportation doesn't cover what it costs them to run buses over big, thinly populated areas. Fuel, drivers and buses have all gone up. Driver shortages mean some routes were combined this fall, which makes the rides longer.

## How it works (plain version, to be checked)

- Kansas funds schools through a base amount per student plus "weightings" for particular costs. One of these is a **transportation weighting**, which is meant to help districts pay to bus students who live 2.5 miles or more from school.
- The weighting is calculated with a formula that takes density into account (how many students per square mile a district serves). That's intended to give sparse rural districts more per rider.
- Districts and some legislators argue the formula lags behind current costs and doesn't fully reflect very long routes, gravel roads and the number of small routes a rural district has to run.
- Don't state dollar figures or percentages until we have the KLRD memo.

## Marla's position (agreed 10/14)

"Our schools are the heart of our towns. A child in Madison or Yates Center shouldn't spend two hours a day on a bus because the formula was built for somewhere else. I want the transportation formula looked at with real numbers from rural districts, and I'll push for changes that reflect what it actually costs to get kids to school out here."

- She wants to sit down with the superintendents in the district before supporting any particular formula change. Kelsey is setting those meetings for November (after 11/3).
- She has not endorsed any specific bill. Don't promise one.
- Fine to say she has asked the Legislative Research Department for the history and numbers on the formula.

## Reply language

> Thank you for writing, and for telling me about your kids' bus rides. I've heard the same thing from parents and school leaders across the district. I've asked the Legislature's research staff for the history and the numbers on how the state's transportation funding works for rural districts, and I'm meeting with our superintendents this fall. I want the formula to reflect what it really costs to get kids to school in a district our size, and I'll be pushing for that when the session starts in January.

## Superintendent meetings (November)

Kelsey is scheduling one-hour meetings with each superintendent in the district for the weeks of Nov 9 and Nov 16. Questions Marla wants to ask each one:

1. How many routes do you run, how long is the longest ride, and how has that changed in five years?
2. What did transportation cost you last year, and how much of it did the state formula cover?
3. How many drivers are you short? What do you pay, and what would it take to fill the seats?
4. Have you combined routes or cut stops because of drivers or buses?
5. If the formula changed one thing for districts like yours, what should it be?
