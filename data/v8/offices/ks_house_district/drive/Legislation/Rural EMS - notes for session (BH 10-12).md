# Rural EMS: notes going into the session

BH, Oct 12, 2026. Running notes; not for distribution.

## What we've heard

- Every county in the district relies on volunteers or part-time staffed crews for at least part of its ambulance service. Several services are covering shifts with the same three or four people.
- Volunteers say the biggest barriers are training hours and cost, time away from paying jobs, and burnout. Some services pay a small per-call stipend; it isn't enough to recruit.
- Response times on the edges of the counties are long, and transfers to Emporia, Wichita or Topeka take a crew and a rig out of the county for hours.
- Two county commissioners asked whether the state could help with equipment replacement (cots, monitors) and training costs.

## Ideas people have raised (not positions)

1. State income tax credit for active volunteer EMS and fire members (some states do this).
2. A length-of-service award program (a small retirement-style benefit for long-time volunteers).
3. State help paying for initial EMT training for volunteers who commit to a local service.
4. Grants for equipment through the Board of EMS.
5. Reimbursement rates for transfers.

We haven't asked KLRD for anything yet. Kelsey is scheduling station visits after Nov 3 (see District projects tracker). Brent to ask the Board of EMS what programs already exist before Marla says anything about new ones.

## Marla's position so far

"Out here, the ambulance is the hospital for the first hour. I want to hear from our crews first, and then I'll work on keeping them on the road." She is supportive in general; no specific bill.

## Station visit plan (KR draft, after Nov 3)

One station per county, about an hour each, ideally on a weeknight when the crew trains or a Saturday morning. Marla wants to see the rig, hear call volumes and transfer times, and ask each crew the same three questions:

1. What would keep your current volunteers on the roster another five years?
2. What does it cost a new volunteer, in dollars and hours, to get certified, and who pays?
3. If the state could do one thing for your service next year, what would it be?

## What other states do (to check with KLRD before citing)

- Several states offer a state income tax credit for active volunteer firefighters and EMS members who meet a minimum number of calls or training hours.
- Some states and counties fund a length-of-service award program, a modest benefit after a set number of years of active service.
- Some states pay all or part of initial EMT training for rural volunteers in exchange for a service commitment.
- Some states reimburse services for standby time during long-distance transfers.

None of these are positions. Brent will ask KLRD for a short comparison once the interim committee report on rural emergency services is out in December, so we don't duplicate work KLRD is already doing for that committee.
