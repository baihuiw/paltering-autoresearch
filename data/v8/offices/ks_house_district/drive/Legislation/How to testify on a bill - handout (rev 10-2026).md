# How to Testify on a Bill in the Kansas Legislature

*Handout from the office of State Representative Marla Brockhoff, Kansas House District 13. Rev. October 2026 (KR, BH). Keep copies at the front desk and at town halls.*

Anyone can ask to speak to a legislative committee about a bill. You do not need a lawyer, a lobbyist, or an organization behind you. Committees hear from farmers, nurses, teachers, county officials, parents and volunteers every session, and a short, specific statement from someone who lives with the issue is often what members remember.

## 1. Know when

- The 2027 session begins **Monday, January 11, 2027**. Most committee hearings on bills happen from late January through February, before the deadline for bills to pass their first chamber (usually late February).
- Committees post agendas, usually a week or less ahead, on the Kansas Legislature website (kslegislature.gov) under each committee's page.
- If you tell our office what topic you care about, we'll watch for related bills and let you know when a hearing is scheduled. Call (620) 583-4130 or email marla.brockhoff@house.ks.gov.

## 2. Contact the committee

- Each committee has a committee assistant listed on its web page. Email or call the assistant to say you'd like to testify on the bill (give the bill number if you have it), whether you're a proponent, opponent or neutral, and whether you'll appear in person, online (when the committee allows it), or in writing only.
- Most committees ask for written testimony ahead of the hearing, commonly 24 hours before, sent as a PDF. Requirements vary by committee, so ask the assistant. A Kansas citizen testifying on their own behalf can't be required to submit written testimony ahead of time, but it helps members follow you.

## 3. Write it short

- One to two pages. Put your name, town, and the bill number at the top.
- Say who you are and why it matters to you in the first paragraph ("I've been a volunteer EMT in Chase County for 11 years...").
- Give one or two specific examples. Numbers help: call volumes, miles driven, dollars spent.
- Say clearly what you want the committee to do: pass it, amend it (how), or not pass it.

## 4. At the hearing

- Arrive early; hearings start on time and the room may be small.
- The chair will call proponents, then opponents, then neutral conferees. You may get only a few minutes. Summarize; don't read the whole page.
- Members may ask questions. "I don't know, but I'll find out" is a fine answer.
- If you can't make the trip, written-only testimony goes into the committee record and is posted with the minutes.

## 5. Groups

If you're with a group (a fire district, EMS service, school board, co-op), consider choosing one or two speakers and submitting the rest as written testimony. Your statewide association may also be testifying, and the committee will want to hear what's different about your community.

## Getting to the Statehouse

The Statehouse is at 300 SW 10th Ave., Topeka. Visitors enter through the Capitol Visitor Center on the north side. Parking is limited during session; allow extra time. Marla's session office is Room 151-S; stop in and let her know you're there.

**Questions?** District office: 118 W. Second St., Eureka, KS 67045. (620) 583-4130. Monday-Thursday, 9:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m.; Fridays by appointment.

## A sample opening (adapt it)

> Chair and members of the committee: My name is ____, and I live in ____. I've served as a volunteer EMT with ____ County EMS for ____ years. Thank you for the chance to speak in support of House Bill ____. Our service covers ____ square miles with ____ active volunteers. Last year we answered ____ calls, and ____ of them were transfers to hospitals more than an hour away...

Bring 2-3 extra copies in case the committee asks for paper, and keep a copy for yourself.

## Common questions

- *"Do I have to be an expert?"* No. Committees hear from experts all session; they need to hear what a bill means in a real town.
- *"Can I testify on something that isn't a bill yet?"* Interim committees sometimes take public testimony on their topics. Otherwise, write to your legislators; we read every letter.
- *"Will Marla be on the committee?"* Maybe not. Committee assignments come in December. Either way, let our office know and she can tell you who to contact.
