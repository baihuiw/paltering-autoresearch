## What this means for us

- **School groups and family visits**: January through early April is when the House is in the chamber most days. Mornings Tuesday through Thursday are the best bet to see the House in session and catch Marla between committees. Late February is busy on the floor; good for watching, bad for a sit-down.
- **Testifying**: hearings cluster in late January through February. Bills get scheduled on short notice (committee agendas usually post the week before). People who want to testify should tell us early what topic they care about so we can watch for the bill.
- **Pat Engstrom** returns as session legislative assistant in January and keeps Marla's Topeka calendar. The district office keeps the district calendar. Kelsey and Pat should share one calendar starting Jan 4.
- **District office in session**: Mon-Thu hours stay the same. Marla will be in Topeka Monday afternoon through Thursday or Friday most weeks; she plans Friday or Saturday district stops.

## Interim (now)

The Legislature is not in session from adjournment until January. Interim committees meet in Topeka on set days and make recommendations for the session. Members not on an interim committee may attend or watch the livestream on the Legislature's website.

## Questions people ask about the calendar

- *"Can I watch online?"* Yes. House and Senate floor sessions and most committee meetings are streamed on the Legislature's website and archived.
- *"Is the session 90 days?"* Kansas sessions in odd-numbered years have no fixed length in the constitution; even-numbered-year sessions are limited to 90 calendar days unless extended. In practice both usually end in spring. Don't promise a date for sine die.
- *"When will we know if a bill passed?"* Bill status is on the Legislature's website by bill number. We can look it up for anyone who calls.
