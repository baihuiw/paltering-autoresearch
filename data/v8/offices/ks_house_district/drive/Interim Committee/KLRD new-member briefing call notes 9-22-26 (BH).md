## How the interim works

- The Legislature isn't in session from adjournment until January. Between sessions, the Legislative Coordinating Council (LCC) authorizes interim committees: some are standing joint committees that meet year-round, some are special committees created for one interim with a specific charge.
- Each interim committee meets on approved days in Topeka (some meetings are partly virtual), takes testimony from agencies and the public, and adopts a report with recommendations. Reports go to the full Legislature in January and are posted with the committee's materials.
- KLRD staffs the committees: agendas, background memos, minutes and the final report. Committee materials are posted on the Legislature's website under the committee's page, and meetings are livestreamed.
- Members receive per diem plus mileage and subsistence for approved interim meeting days. Claims are filed after each meeting; Brent will set up the paperwork.
- Members can attend any interim meeting as a guest and may be recognized to ask questions at the chair's discretion. They get no per diem for meetings of committees they don't sit on.

## Bill drafting

- Any member can request a bill draft from the Revisor of Statutes. Requests are confidential to the member until the bill is introduced.
- The Revisor assigns a draft number ("rs 27-xxxx"). Prefiled bills are introduced the first day of session.
- Requests should explain the problem and what you want to happen; the Revisor writes the legal language. Bring examples (other states' laws, an agency letter, a constituent's situation without personal details).
- Turnaround depends on volume; anything requested after Thanksgiving may not be ready for prefiling.

## Research requests

- Members can ask KLRD for background memos on any topic. KLRD is nonpartisan and confidential to the member. Typical turnaround one to three weeks.
- Good uses for us: history of a funding formula, what other states do, fiscal background. Brent will keep a list (see agency contact log entries with KLRD).

## Other useful things

- Fiscal notes: the Division of the Budget prepares a fiscal note on each bill once introduced.
- Committee testimony rules vary by committee; committee assistants are the contact. Citizens testifying for themselves can't be required to submit written testimony ahead.
- Session office: Room 151-S. Pat Engstrom returns as session assistant in January.
- Legislator email is the house.ks.gov account. Keep official business there.

## Marla's questions on the call (and answers)

- *Can a member introduce a bill on behalf of a constituent?* Yes; members can request drafts on any subject. Constituents can't request drafts from the Revisor directly.
- *How many bills does a typical member introduce?* Varies widely; many new members carry a few and co-sponsor others. Committee bills are common.
- *Can staff talk to KLRD directly?* Yes, for the member's requests; requests are confidential to the member.
- *What's the best way to learn the budget?* Sit in on budget committee meetings in January and read the KLRD budget analysis.
