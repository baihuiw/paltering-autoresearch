# Special Committee on Property Valuation - livestream notes

Tue Oct 6, 2026, 9:00 a.m.-3:30 p.m. (watched from the district office). BH. Marla not a member; notes for her and for reply language.

## Morning: background

- KLRD walked through how residential property is valued (market value, sales comparison) and how agricultural land is valued (use value based on productivity, not market sales). Reminder that the county appraiser sets values; the state's Property Valuation Division (PVD) oversees appraisal standards.
- KLRD summary of the last several years: residential values statewide up sharply since 2021; ag use values more stable; commercial mixed. Several committee members noted that rising values don't automatically raise taxes if local mill levies come down, and that the revenue neutral rate process requires a hearing and a vote if a local government wants to collect more.
- A committee member asked whether a cap on annual valuation increases could be done by statute. Revisor's answer: the constitution requires uniform and equal assessment, so a hard cap on valuation for one class would very likely require a constitutional amendment. A statutory approach could change notice and appeal procedures or adjust exemptions.

## Midday: county testimony

- A county appraiser association representative: appraisers follow sales; the market went up; capping valuations shifts the tax burden to property whose values didn't rise as fast, often newer homes and commercial property.
- A county commissioners' association representative: counties have lowered mill levies in many places; the public sees only the valuation number and blames the county; asked for simpler notices.
- Two county treasurers (written only): payment-under-protest process is underused because people don't know about it.

## Afternoon: public testimony

- Retirees from three counties described 15-30% valuation increases with fixed incomes. One asked for a freeze for homeowners over 65 (there are existing senior refund programs; she wasn't aware of them).
- A farm organization supported keeping use-value appraisal for ag land and opposed any change that would shift costs to ag land.
- A homebuilders' representative raised concerns about caps shifting costs to new construction.

## Committee discussion

- Interest in (1) clearer valuation notices that show the estimated tax at last year's levy, (2) a longer appeal period, (3) expanding awareness of the existing senior refund programs, (4) a possible constitutional amendment to limit annual increases for owner-occupied homes. No votes taken.
- Next meeting late October; KLRD to bring options.
