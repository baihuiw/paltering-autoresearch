# KPERS and retirement questions: notes

DV, Oct 2026. We've had several KPERS cases since opening (C26-0007 retirement estimate, C26-0065 survivor benefit) and a lot of walk-in questions from family members helping a parent. Notes for whoever is at the front desk.

## What KPERS is

The Kansas Public Employees Retirement System covers state employees, school employees (teachers, bus drivers, cooks, paras), and employees of many cities, counties and other local employers. Police and firefighters and judges have their own plans within KPERS.

## Common questions

**"I want an estimate of my retirement benefit."**
- Members can get an estimate online through their member account or by contacting KPERS member services. If they want us to ask for them, they sign a request with their information; KPERS mails the estimate to the member, not to us. (C26-0007: estimate mailed in about two weeks.)

**"My parent died. What do we do?"**
- Report the death to KPERS as soon as possible. KPERS will tell the family what forms are needed for any survivor benefit, life insurance benefit (for some members), or return of contributions. They'll need a certified death certificate (see the vital records notes).
- Survivors and beneficiaries need to sign their own forms. We can help request the forms and sit with families to fill them out (C26-0065).

**"My dad's paperwork" (adult children helping a parent)**
- KPERS will only discuss an account with the member, or with someone who has legal authority (power of attorney, guardianship, executor) and has filed proof of it with KPERS. Have the family bring a copy of the power of attorney.
- If the member can sign, the simplest way is for the member to sign a request and authorization with us at the office.
- Office hours for this kind of visit: Monday-Thursday 9-4, or a Friday appointment (9-noon). The paperwork can be dropped off on a Friday and Diane makes the KPERS contact the next Monday.

**"Can I work after I retire?"**
- There are working-after-retirement rules and they depend on the employer and plan. Send them to KPERS; don't guess.

**"Is my KPERS taxed?"**
- Tax questions: a tax preparer or KDOR. We don't give tax advice.

**"Is KPERS going broke?"**
- Policy question for Marla. Brent has the general answer: the Legislature has made extra payments in recent years and Marla supports keeping the promises made to public employees. Don't quote funding numbers.

## Things to bring

- Member's name, date of birth, last 4 of SSN, employer.
- For survivors: certified death certificate, beneficiary's ID.
- For someone acting for the member: power of attorney or court papers.

## Logging

Open a case (C26-xxxx); log the KPERS contact (AC-26-xxx) with what was requested and when to follow up.

## Example: adult child helping a parent (how a typical visit goes)

1. Daughter calls Monday; her father is a retired school custodian and got a KPERS letter he doesn't understand. She works out of town Monday through Thursday.
2. Kelsey books a Friday appointment (9-noon) and asks her to bring the letter, her father if he can come, and any power of attorney.
3. Friday: whoever is in reads the letter with her, makes a copy, and has her father sign the request form if he's there. If he's not and there's no power of attorney, we give her the form to take home for his signature.
4. Monday: Diane contacts KPERS member services with the signed request and logs it. KPERS answers the member directly; Diane calls the daughter to make sure the answer arrived.

Most "KPERS paperwork" questions are one of three things: a beneficiary change, a tax form (1099-R), or a letter about a change in the monthly amount. All three are answered by KPERS member services; our job is to get the right signature on the right form.
