DEER DAMAGE ON CROPS - KDWP NOTES
DV, Sept 23, 2026 (from C26-0005 Stenzel); rev. Oct 8 after three more calls.

We get these every fall when wheat comes up and deer move onto it, and again
when alfalfa is short. Callers usually say they "called the state and got
transferred around." Here is who actually handles it.

WHO: Kansas Department of Wildlife and Parks (KDWP)
  - Deer damage line: (888) 497-8661
  - Or the nearest KDWP office; they route to the district wildlife biologist
    or natural resources officer (game warden) for the county.
  - KDWP says staff will contact the landowner within five working days of
    being told about the damage.

WHAT HAPPENS
  1. The landowner (or tenant farming the ground, with the owner's okay) calls
     and describes the damage: crop, acres, where, how long.
  2. A district wildlife biologist or natural resources officer comes out and
     looks at the field with the landowner.
  3. If the damage is significant, KDWP can issue a deer damage control
     permit. These are free. They let the landowner take a set number of deer
     on that property outside the regular seasons, with conditions (who can
     shoot, which deer, what happens to the carcass).
  4. KDWP may also suggest other steps first or alongside: hunting during the
     regular seasons, extra antlerless tags, letting hunters on the ground,
     repellents or fencing for high-value crops.

TIPS FOR CALLERS
  - Have the legal description or the road address of the field and the
    acres affected ready. Photos help.
  - Write down the name of whoever you talk to and when.
  - If no one has called back in five working days, call our office and we'll
    reach the district office for you (log it, AC-26-xxx).

THINGS WE DON'T DO
  - We can't issue or promise a permit. KDWP staff decide after the site visit.
  - Season dates and tag numbers change every year; send people to KDWP's
    site rather than reciting them.

OTHER WILDLIFE CALLS
  - Beaver dams flooding a field or road ditch, coyotes after calves, feral
    hogs: KDWP district wildlife biologist or natural resources officer for
    the county; feral hogs also go to the Kansas Department of Agriculture's
    animal health program. Give the KDWP number; log it if we call.
  - Injured deer on the road: local law enforcement.
