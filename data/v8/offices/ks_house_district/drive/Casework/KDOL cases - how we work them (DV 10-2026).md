# KDOL unemployment cases: how we work them

DV, Oct 2026. KDOL cases are our most common casework after property tax questions. This is what has worked since we opened.

## First call with the constituent

Ask:
1. When did you file? Is it an initial claim or weekly claims that stopped paying?
2. What does the claim status say online? ("Pending", "under adjudication", "fraud hold/ID verification", "overpayment", "denied")
3. Have you received any letters from KDOL? Ask them to read or send us the letter. Most answers are in the letter.
4. Employer name and last day worked.

Have them sign the KDOL authorization form (gray box, KDOL folder). They can sign at the office or we email the PDF; a photo of the signed form is fine.

Tell them honestly: we can find out where a claim stands and make sure it hasn't fallen through the cracks; we can't make KDOL decide in their favor or skip the line. Most of the time a status check gets things moving.

## What the statuses usually mean

- **Pending / adjudication**: an adjudicator has to decide an eligibility question (why the job ended, availability, severance). Waits of several weeks are common when the employer contests or doesn't respond. Ask the liaison whether an adjudicator is assigned and if anything is needed from the claimant.
- **ID verification / fraud hold**: KDOL needs identity documents. The constituent can upload them; if that keeps failing, we send them through the liaison (AC-26-029 is an example).
- **Overpayment**: KDOL says the claimant received more than they were due. The constituent can appeal or request a waiver; the letter has the deadline. Waiver forms: KDOL site, or we print one (C26-0006).
- **1099-G**: tax form corrections go through KDOL's 1099-G request process.
- **Work search waiver**: some claimants (e.g., temporary layoffs with a return date) may qualify; ask the liaison.

## Sending to the liaison

Email from your office account, subject "Constituent case - [last name] - [claim date]". Include the authorization form as an attachment, a two-line summary, and the specific question ("Is an adjudicator assigned? Is anything needed from the claimant?"). One case per email. Log it (AC-26-xxx).

Typical turnaround: 2-3 business days for a status answer. If no answer in 5 business days, follow up once by email, then Brent calls.

## Hardship

If the constituent is facing loss of housing or utilities (mortgage, eviction notice, shutoff notice), say so in the email to the liaison; KDOL can sometimes prioritize a review. Ask the constituent before sharing details. Also give them DCF and local resources (LIEAP, food pantry list at the front desk).

## Layoffs

When a plant or employer lays off a group, KDOL's workforce centers sometimes hold on-site sessions. Watch for that and post it on Facebook. If several people from the same employer call us, mention it to the liaison in one email so KDOL sees the pattern.

## Phone hang-ups

People tell us they get disconnected on the KDOL line during busy times. Suggest calling right when the line opens, using the online portal for anything it handles, and letting us check status through the liaison rather than waiting on hold.

## Appeals

If KDOL denies a claim, the determination letter explains how to appeal and the deadline. Appeals go to a referee (hearing officer), not to the adjudicator who made the first decision. We can help people read the letter and file on time, and we can tell them how the hearing works, but we don't represent anyone at a hearing. For legal help, refer to Kansas Legal Services.

## Employers who call

Employers sometimes call about a claim against their account. Treat them like any constituent: we can route a question to the liaison with their authorization, but we don't take sides in a contested claim.
