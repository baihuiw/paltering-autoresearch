# Licensing boards: out-of-state license transfers

DV, Oct 2026. We get these regularly now: people moving here from Oklahoma, Missouri, Nebraska and Colorado who need a Kansas license before they can work. Every board has its own process and its own word for it ("endorsement", "reciprocity", "licensure by credentials"). Below is what we've learned from cases so far. Always send the constituent to the board's current application and checklist; rules and fees change.

## General steps (most boards)

1. Find the board's application for out-of-state license holders. It's usually under "forms and applications" on the board's site.
2. Read the checklist that comes with it. Most ask for: the application, the fee, a copy of a government photo ID, proof of the current out-of-state license (often a verification sent directly from the other state's board), and sometimes proof of work experience, training hours or exam scores.
3. Submit everything at once. Missing items are the most common reason a file sits.
4. Ask the board for the expected processing time and write it down for the constituent. If it's past that time, we can ask licensing staff for a status check (with the constituent's okay).

## Board notes

**Board of Cosmetology** (cosmetology, nail technology, esthetics; also tanning and tattoo/body art)
- 2420 NW Button Rd, Topeka, KS 66618; kboc@ks.gov.
- Out-of-state license holder application. The checklist asks for a legible copy of a current government photo ID and a copy of the Social Security card; names must match on the application and the documents (include name-change papers if they don't).
- Work history matters: the board's out-of-state application asks for recent experience in the licensed profession. If the constituent hasn't worked long enough, there may be extra steps (exam or training hours); have them call the board before paying anything.
- Allow about 7-10 business days for documents to be processed once received; the whole transfer usually takes at least three weeks. Tell people this up front so the salon can plan.
- The board's site is not easy to navigate; Diane can sit with someone at the office and go through it (C26-0067 example: checklist emailed to constituent 10/15).

**Board of Nursing**
- Kansas is in the nurse licensure compact; nurses with a multistate license from another compact state can usually work here without a new license until they change primary residence. After the move, they apply for a Kansas license. Background check (fingerprints) adds time. C26-0004 was a background-check delay; resolved in a week after a status check.

**Board of Pharmacy** - pharmacists, technicians. Technician registration is usually quick once the application is complete (C26-0057).

**Behavioral Sciences Regulatory Board** - counselors, social workers, psychologists. Several license types; slow when supervision hours need verifying.

**Board of Healing Arts** - physicians, PAs, physical therapists and others. Long applications; recruiters and hospitals often handle them.

**Teachers** - not a board; KSDE licensure. Out-of-state teachers usually qualify for a Kansas license with a valid license; KSDE's licensure office answers questions.

**Commercial driver's license** - not a board; KDOR / driver's license office. Transfer at a driver's license office in person.

## What we can and can't do

- We can explain the steps, help fill out forms, check status with licensing staff, and make sure a complete file isn't stuck.
- We can't ask a board to waive a requirement or approve someone. If a requirement seems unfair, that's a question for the Legislature; note it on the bill ideas list (occupational licensing - faster transfers).

## Follow-up rule

If someone tells us they wrote or called and never heard back, apologize, look for their message (contact form inbox, phone pad, casework sheet), and get them a real answer within two business days.

## Script for the first call

"Which profession, and which state are you licensed in now? Have you started the Kansas application yet? ... Okay. Each profession has its own state board, and each has an application for people already licensed in another state. Let me send you the link and the checklist. The main thing is to send everything at once, including the verification from your current state's board, because the file doesn't move until it's complete. If you've already applied and it's been longer than the board said it would take, we can ask them for a status check with your okay."
