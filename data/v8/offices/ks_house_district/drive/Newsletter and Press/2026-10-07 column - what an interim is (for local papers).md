# Statehouse Notes - Column #2

**So what does a legislator do in October?**

A neighbor stopped me at the elevator last week and asked, with a twinkle, whether I'd "found the Statehouse yet." Fair question. The Kansas Legislature isn't in session from spring until January, so what does a state representative do in the fall?

It turns out the off-season, which Topeka calls the "interim," is busier than I expected.

First, there are interim committees. Between sessions, legislative leaders set up committees to study particular problems and make recommendations for the next session. These committees meet in Topeka on set days, hear from state agencies and the public, and write a report. Anyone can watch them online. I was appointed to one, and I've been watching others on topics I hear about from you, including property valuations and rural emergency services.

Second, there's homework. Any legislator can ask the Legislature's research staff, which is nonpartisan, for background on how a program works or what other states do. I've asked for a history of how the state helps rural school districts pay for busing, because I keep hearing from parents whose kids ride more than an hour each way. Legislators can also ask the Revisor of Statutes to draft a bill ahead of the session. My first request is for state help replacing county bridges that are posted for lighter loads than the trucks crossing them.

Third, and most important to me, there's listening. I've held town halls in Strong City and Madison, and I'll be in Yates Center, Fredonia and southwest Emporia this month. I've been to the homecoming parade in Eureka and a livestock association meeting in Cottonwood Falls. Every one of those conversations goes into a notebook I keep in my truck.

And finally, there's the work of helping people with state agencies, which my district office in Eureka does every day. In our first three weeks we've helped people sort out stuck unemployment claims, missing tax refunds, professional licenses, birth certificate corrections and retirement paperwork. We can't change an agency's decision, but we can make sure your question reaches someone who can answer it. If you're stuck, call (620) 583-4130 or stop by 118 W. Second St. in Eureka, Monday through Thursday, 9 to 4. Fridays are by appointment.

The session begins January 11. Between now and then, I intend to keep my boots muddy and my notebook full. Please keep the questions coming.

*Marla Brockhoff is the state representative for Kansas House District 13. Reach her at marla.brockhoff@house.ks.gov or (620) 583-4130.*
