My kindergartner gets on the bus at 6:50 and gets to school at 8:05. The
district combined two routes because they can't find drivers. I know it's not
your job to run the buses but is anyone in Topeka paying attention to this?

----- REPLY -----
Dear Jenna,

Thank you for writing. An hour and fifteen minutes is a long ride for anyone,
let alone a five-year-old, and you're right that people in Topeka need to hear
about it.

Route decisions belong to your local school board, and your superintendent is
the best person to ask about the combined routes and whether anything can
change this year. But the money behind those routes is very much a state matter.
Kansas helps districts pay to bus students who live farther from school, and
school leaders in our district have told me that funding hasn't kept up with
what it costs to run long routes over big, thinly populated areas, especially
with fuel and driver shortages.

I've asked the Legislature's research staff for the history and numbers on how
that funding works for rural districts, and I'm meeting with superintendents in
our district this fall. I want the formula to reflect what it really costs to
get kids to school in a district our size, and I'll push for that when the
session starts in January.

Thank you for telling me about your family. Please keep me posted.

Sincerely,
Marla Brockhoff
State Representative, Kansas House District 13
