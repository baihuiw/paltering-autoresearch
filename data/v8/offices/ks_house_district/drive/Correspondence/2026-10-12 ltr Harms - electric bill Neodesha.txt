----- ORIGINAL MESSAGE -----
Letter, received Thu Oct 8 (mail)
From: Eldon Harms, 610 N. 6th St, Neodesha KS 66757

Representative Brockhoff: My electric bill for September was $212 for a
two-bedroom house and I keep the air at 78. Two years ago it was $140. Who do I
complain to? Is anybody watching these companies? I'm 81 and on Social Security.
Eldon Harms

----- REPLY -----
Dear Mr. Harms,

Thank you for your letter. A jump like that is hard on anyone, and harder on a
fixed income. You deserve a straight answer about who decides what you pay.

Who sets your rate depends on your provider. If your electricity comes from an
investor-owned utility, rates are set by the Kansas Corporation Commission after
a public rate case. You can bring a question or complaint about your bill to the
KCC's Office of Public Affairs and Consumer Protection at (800) 662-0027; they
will look into it with the utility. If your electricity comes from a rural
electric cooperative or a city utility, rates are set by the co-op's
member-elected board or by the city council, and those are the people to tell.

You may also qualify for help with winter bills through the Low Income Energy
Assistance Program (LIEAP) at the Kansas Department for Children and Families.
Applications open in the winter. Diane Volkmann in my office would be glad to
help you apply when it opens; call her at (620) 583-4130.

For my part, I believe families on fixed incomes can't keep absorbing bigger
bills. When rate increases come before the KCC, I want the commission to hold
utilities to the lowest reasonable cost, and I'll support making it easier for
ordinary customers to understand and comment on increases before they're
approved.

Sincerely,
Marla Brockhoff
State Representative, Kansas House District 13
