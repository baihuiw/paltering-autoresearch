Message:
I'm one of five volunteers left on our ambulance. Five. We cover a lot of
miles. Last month two of us did a transfer to Wichita and the county had nobody
for four hours. What is the state going to do for rural EMS before there isn't
any?

----- REPLY -----
Dear Dana,

Thank you for writing, and thank you for what you do. Five people covering that
much ground is not sustainable, and the four hours you describe are exactly what
keeps county commissioners up at night.

I've heard the same thing from crews and commissioners across the district.
What I'm hearing most is that volunteers need help with training costs and time,
some recognition for long service, and help replacing equipment, and that long
transfers leave counties uncovered. An interim committee of the Legislature has
been taking testimony on rural emergency services this fall, and I'm following
that work closely.

I don't want to promise a particular bill before I've listened to the people
doing the work. After the first week of November, my office is setting up visits
to EMS stations in each county in the district. I'd like yours to be one of
them. Kelsey Rudd in my office will be in touch to find a date that works for
your crew.

When the session starts in January, if a bill comes up on EMS, I'll make sure
you know about it so you can testify if you'd like. Anyone can testify, and
committees listen closely to people like you.

Sincerely,
Marla Brockhoff
State Representative, Kansas House District 13
