# Woodson County rural water line extension: project notes

BH, Oct 14, 2026. For the support letter (draft due 10/23) and for constituent questions.

## The project

- **Sponsor:** a Woodson County rural water district serving farms and homes north and west of Yates Center.
- **What:** about 11 miles of new distribution line and a booster pump to serve roughly 30 households and several livestock operations now on private wells.
- **Cost:** about $640,000. The district is applying to KDHE's public water supply loan fund (state revolving loan fund) for the bulk of it; members who hook on pay a benefit-unit fee; the district has some reserves.
- **Why:** private wells in the area are shallow; several tested high for nitrates last year, and two went dry in the late summer. Some families are hauling water from town for drinking and cooking.
- **Timeline (district's estimate):** loan decision in the winter; bid in spring 2027; construction summer-fall 2027.

## Where it stands (10/14)

- Application under review at KDHE. The district asked our office on 10/12 for a support letter.
- The board president attended the Yates Center town hall on 10/15 and thanked Marla.
- Their engineer's preliminary report (copy in the paper file) lists 32 potential hookups; 24 have signed letters of intent.

## Our role

- Support letter for the application. Same approach as the fire district letter: describe the community need; no request for a particular outcome; KDHE decides under its rules.
- Answer constituent questions about how the loan program works. Questions about where the line will go, hookup fees and timing go to the rural water district office.

## Draft points for the letter (Marla's notes 10/14)

1. People are hauling water. Mention it.
2. Nitrate results: refer generally ("several wells tested above safe levels"); don't quote individual test results.
3. Ranches: water for livestock is a real cost in dry years; reliable rural water keeps young families on the land.
4. Keep it to a page.

## Questions people may ask

- **"Can the state just pay for it?"** The main state program for this is a low-interest loan, not a grant, and the water district pays it back through members' rates. There are some grant programs for small or disadvantaged systems; the district's engineer is checking eligibility.
- **"Will my rates go up?"** That's for the rural water district board, which sets its own rates.
- **"Can I get on the line?"** Contact the rural water district office.
