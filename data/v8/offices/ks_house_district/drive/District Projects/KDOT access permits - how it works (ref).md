# KDOT access (entrance) permits: how it works

Office reference, compiled 9/22/2026 (BH) from KDOT's public permit pages and a call with the area office. For answering constituent questions about driveways and field entrances on state highways.

## Who decides

- Any new or changed entrance onto a state or U.S. highway (US-50, US-54, K-99, K-177, etc.) needs an access permit from the Kansas Department of Transportation.
- Permits are issued by KDOT's area offices under the state's access management standards. The area engineer signs off.
- County roads and city streets are not KDOT's; those go to the county road and bridge department or the city.
- Legislators do not issue, approve or deny permits. Our role is to help constituents understand the process and pass along questions.

## What KDOT looks at

- **Sight distance.** Whether a driver pulling out can see far enough in both directions at the posted speed. On a 65 mph highway with truck traffic, this is the biggest factor.
- **Spacing.** Distance from other entrances, intersections and bridges.
- **Drainage.** Culvert size and placement so the ditch keeps working. KDOT sets the minimum culvert size.
- **Design.** Width, surfacing, turning radius for the type of vehicles that will use it (field entrance vs. commercial entrance).
- **Change of use.** Turning a field entrance into a commercial entrance (store, farm market, shop) is a new permit, even if the opening is already there.

## Process and timing

1. The owner submits an application to the area office with a site sketch.
2. A permit technician reviews it and usually visits the site.
3. If it meets the standards, the area engineer issues the permit, typically in two to four weeks. If not, KDOT tells the owner what to change.
4. The owner (or their contractor) builds the entrance to the permit conditions. KDOT may inspect.
5. Work in the highway right-of-way without a permit can be ordered removed.

## Common questions we get

- "Can the Representative speed up my permit?" No. We can ask the area office for a status update with the owner's permission.
- "Can a neighbor block my permit?" Neighbors can raise safety concerns with KDOT; KDOT decides based on the standards.
- "Who do I call?" The KDOT area office for the county. Numbers are on KDOT's website and on our agency phone list.
