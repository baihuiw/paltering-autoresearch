# Kittitas County Firewise Neighborhood Grants, 2026-27

**Guidelines and application instructions. Community Development Services, Fire Marshal's Office. Issued September 2026.**

## Purpose

The county offers matching grants to neighborhoods that are working together to reduce wildfire risk around their homes and want to earn or keep **Firewise USA** recognition. The grants pay for the work that is hard for one household to do alone: thinning and limbing along shared roads, hauling or chipping slash, defensible-space work for elderly or disabled neighbors, and the risk assessment and action plan Firewise USA requires.

## How much

| | |
|---|---|
| Total available this round | **$40,000** |
| Maximum per neighborhood | **$5,000** |
| Minimum request | $500 |
| Match required | **1:1** (one dollar of match for each grant dollar) |

**Match** can be cash, or in-kind: volunteer labor counted at $32.00 per hour, donated equipment time at the county's equipment rental rates, or donated materials at fair value. Most neighborhoods meet the match with volunteer hours on work days. Example: a $5,000 grant needs $5,000 of match, which is about 157 volunteer hours.

## Who can apply

- A neighborhood, homeowners association, road association or informal group of at least **8 homes** in unincorporated Kittitas County. Cabins and seasonal homes count.
- The neighborhood must be a recognized Firewise USA site, or have started the process (contacted the Fire Marshal's Office and scheduled or completed a risk assessment).
- One application per neighborhood. A neighborhood that received a grant in 2025-26 may apply again, but first-time applicants get priority.
- Individual homeowners cannot apply on their own; contact the Fire Marshal's Office about a free home assessment instead.

## What the grant can pay for

- Contractor thinning, limbing and brush removal on common areas and along shared roads and driveways
- Chipper rental, chipping contractors, or dumpster and hauling costs for slash
- Defensible-space work for residents who cannot do it themselves
- Risk assessment, action plan preparation and Firewise Day event costs
- Signs and reflective address markers for the neighborhood

It cannot pay for: work on a single private lot that isn't part of a neighborhood project (except for residents who can't do it themselves), equipment purchases over $500, food, or work done before the grant agreement is signed.

| Date | Step |
|---|---|

Grants are paid as **reimbursement** after the work is done and the match is documented. Neighborhoods that need an advance for a contractor can ask for up to 25 percent up front.

## How projects are scored

- Wildfire risk of the area (from the risk assessment) - 30 points
- Number of homes that benefit - 20 points
- Neighborhood commitment (meetings held, volunteers signed up, match pledged) - 20 points
- Clear work plan and budget - 15 points
- Help for residents who cannot do the work themselves - 10 points
- First-time applicant - 5 points

## How to apply

1. Call or email the Fire Marshal's Office to talk through your project (optional, but most successful applicants do).
2. Fill out the two-page application (on the county website under Community Development Services, Fire Marshal, or at the CDS counters in Ellensburg and the Cle Elum annex).
3. Attach a map of the neighborhood, the participating addresses, a simple budget, and a signed match pledge.
4. Submit by email or in person by December 1.

## Quick answers

- **Can two small neighborhoods apply together?** Yes, if together they have at least 8 homes and one person signs as the lead.
- **Can the match come from a fire district or the conservation district?** Yes, if that work is part of the same project and is documented.
- **Can we pay ourselves for the work?** No. Neighbors' work counts as volunteer match; the grant pays contractors, rentals and hauling.
