# Bowers Field (ELN) - Noise Abatement and Engine Run-Up Procedures

*Kittitas County Public Works, Airport Division. Adopted on the Airport Advisory Committee's recommendation, 2014; revised March 2022. Posted in the terminal, on the airport web page and in the pilot briefing binder.*

Bowers Field is a public-use airport owned by Kittitas County. Most nearby land is farm and rural residential, and homes sit close to the airport on the north and east sides. These procedures are **voluntary** for aircraft in flight: the Federal Aviation Administration, not the county, controls aircraft in the air, and the county cannot restrict lawful flight operations at a federally funded airport. Ground operations on airport property, including engine run-ups, are under county rules and the airport rules and regulations.

## 1. Engine run-ups

**Where.** Pre-flight run-ups are done on the designated run-up pads at the approach ends of Runway 11 and Runway 29, or in the hold short area for Runway 7/25, with the aircraft pointed into the wind and away from buildings, fuel trucks and people. Run-ups are not done on the main apron or in front of the terminal.

**When.**
- Normal pre-flight run-ups may be done at any time immediately before departure.
- **Maintenance run-ups** (any run-up above idle that is not immediately before departure, including after engine work) are allowed only between **7:00 a.m. and 9:00 p.m. Monday-Friday and 9:00 a.m. and 9:00 p.m. on weekends and holidays.** Maintenance run-ups longer than 5 minutes at high power must be done at the Runway 29 pad, the farthest from homes, and the airport office must be told in advance.
- Between 10:00 p.m. and 7:00 a.m., operators are asked to keep run-ups to the minimum the aircraft manual requires.

**Who.** Maintenance shops on the field are responsible for their customers' and employees' run-ups.

## 2. Flight procedures (voluntary)

- **Calm wind runway** is Runway 29 when traffic allows.
- Use the published traffic pattern altitude. Avoid low, slow flight over the homes north and east of the field.
- Climb at best rate (Vy) to pattern altitude after departure unless safety requires otherwise. Reduce power and propeller RPM as soon as it is safe.
- Avoid repeated touch-and-go practice between 9:00 p.m. and 7:00 a.m.
- Flight schools are asked to spread pattern work between runways when wind allows and to brief students on these procedures.
- Helicopters use the published arrival and departure routes over the airport and avoid hovering over the north fence line.

## 3. Wildfire and emergency operations

Firefighting, medical, law enforcement and military aircraft are exempt. During fire season, air tanker and helicopter operations may run early and late; the airport posts notice on the web page when a fire base is active.

## 4. Questions and noise comments

Neighbors can report noise concerns to the airport office:

- **Airport office:** (509) 962-7536, Monday-Friday, 8:00 a.m.-4:30 p.m. (voicemail after hours)
- **Airport Manager:** Rick Tolliver
- **Online:** the county contact form ("Bowers Field")

Please give the date, time, location and a description (e.g., "engine running at high power for 10 minutes, east side"). Staff log every comment, try to identify the aircraft or operator, and contact them when the comment involves a ground run-up or a repeat pattern. Comments are summarized at each Airport Advisory Committee meeting (second Thursday of most months, 5:30 p.m., terminal conference room). The public may speak at those meetings.

## 5. Planning and new development at the airport

New buildings, aprons and taxiways are sited through the airport layout plan. The layout plan update now under way includes a noise review, and the Airport Advisory Committee holds public meetings on it. New maintenance or run-up locations will be reviewed with neighbors' comments in mind.

## 5a. Common questions from neighbors

**Why can't the county ban early flights?** Airports that have accepted federal grants must stay open to all lawful aircraft on reasonable terms. The county can set rules for ground activity on its property, like run-up hours and locations, and it can ask pilots to follow voluntary procedures, but it cannot set curfews on flights.

**What is a run-up?** Before takeoff, pilots briefly run a piston engine at higher power to check magnetos and controls, usually 30 seconds to a minute. A maintenance run-up after engine work can last several minutes at high power and is louder and longer, which is why it has set hours and a set location.

**Will more aircraft mean more noise?** Some. Planning projects at the airport go through the layout plan, which includes a noise review and public meetings. Neighbors' comments on where new aprons, buildings and run-up pads go are part of that review.

**Who do I call at night?** Leave a voicemail at the airport office with the time and what you heard. If an aircraft appears to be in distress or flying dangerously low, call 911.
