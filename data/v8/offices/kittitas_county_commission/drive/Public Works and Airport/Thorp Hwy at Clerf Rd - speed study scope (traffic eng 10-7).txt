KITTITAS COUNTY PUBLIC WORKS - TRAFFIC ENGINEERING
SPEED AND SAFETY STUDY SCOPE
Thorp Highway at Clerf Road (school bus stop area)
Prepared October 7, 2026 for the Board's Oct 20 agenda (item 5b, study authorization)

1. BACKGROUND
Residents raised vehicle speeds on Thorp Highway near the Clerf Road school bus stop at the board's September 15 and October 6 meetings and in several letters and calls (see Board office letter OL-26-0396 and dispatch ticket RD-26-1412). The posted speed limit on this segment is 50 mph. The school district's morning bus stops at Clerf Road at about 7:10 a.m. and the afternoon drop-off is about 3:45 p.m. Sunrise in early November is about 6:50 a.m., so the morning stop is in low light through the winter.

2. WHAT THE STUDY WILL MEASURE
- Speeds: pneumatic tube counters at two locations (about 1,000 feet east and west of Clerf Road), seven continuous days, the week of November 2. Results by hour, including the 85th percentile speed, average speed, and the share of vehicles over the limit.
- Volumes and vehicle types (passenger vehicles, single-unit trucks, combination trucks). Gravel and hay truck traffic is heavy in this corridor in the fall.
- Sight distance at the bus stop and at the Clerf Road intersection, in both directions.
- Crash history, 2021-2026 (state crash database): preliminary review shows 6 reported crashes within 0.5 mile, 2 involving injury, none involving a school bus or pedestrian.
- Field review during the morning and afternoon bus times (lighting, shoulder width, where children wait).

3. OPTIONS TO BE EVALUATED
- Speed limit change (requires an engineering study under state law; the county can't lower the limit on a county road without one).
- "School Bus Stop Ahead" warning signs with flashing beacons (solar, timed to bus schedule). Estimated $14,000-$18,000 a pair, installed.
- Speed feedback signs (permanent, solar). About $8,000 each.
- Shoulder widening or a paved standing area at the stop (coordinate with the school district on stop location).
- Enforcement: requests go to the Sheriff's Office, which decides patrol priorities.

4. INTERIM MEASURES (before the study)
- Portable radar speed trailer to be placed eastbound near Clerf Road October 12-23, then westbound October 26-30. (Trailer also collects speed data, which will be included in the report.)
- "School Bus Stop Ahead" static signs (existing) inspected 10/6; one replaced (faded).
- The school district's transportation office has been contacted about whether the stop can move to the wider shoulder about 300 feet west. Their answer is expected by the end of October.
Flashing beacons can't be installed before the study because the county's sign policy requires the engineering review first. If the study supports them, they can be ordered in December and installed in early 2027, weather permitting.

5. SCHEDULE
- Oct 20: Board authorizes the study (the budget is in the 2027 draft TIP, project 2, $85,000, which covers the study and the likely improvements).
- Week of Nov 2: counts.
- Late November: analysis.
- December regular meeting: results and recommendation to the Board.

5a. PRIOR COUNT (for reference)
A 48-hour count in May 2024 about 1,500 feet east of Clerf Rd recorded:
- Average daily traffic: 2,840 vehicles (both directions)
- 85th percentile speed: 58 mph eastbound, 56 mph westbound
- Share over 50 mph: 61 percent
- Trucks (single-unit and combination): 11 percent
Traffic has grown since then with new homes south of Thorp Hwy and fall hay hauling, so the November count will be compared with these numbers.
