# 2027 Property Tax Levies - Draft Worksheet

*Auditor's Office, finance division. DRAFT 10/2/2026 for Board review at the October 28 budget wrap-up; levy resolutions on the November 16 agenda. Assessed values are preliminary until the Assessor certifies them in late November; the resolutions state dollar amounts and the Assessor sets the rates.*

## Background

The county levies two regular property taxes:

- **Current expense levy** (general fund), up to $1.80 per $1,000 of assessed value.
- **County road levy** (road fund, unincorporated area only), up to $2.25 per $1,000.

Under state law (RCW 84.55) each levy can grow by no more than **1 percent a year** over the highest previous levy, plus the value of **new construction**, annexations and certain state-assessed property, unless voters approve more. The Board must hold a public hearing on revenue sources and adopt levy resolutions by November 30 (RCW 84.55.120). The hearing on revenue sources is combined with the November 16 regular meeting; the budget hearing itself is December 7.

The county can also "bank" unused levy capacity from earlier years. Kittitas County has **no banked capacity** in either levy; the Board has taken the full 1 percent each year since 2019.

## What it means for a typical home

A home in the unincorporated county assessed at $450,000 in both years:

| | 2026 | 2027 (draft) |
|---|---|---|
| Current expense | $540 | $523 |
| County road | $585 | $563 |
| **County total** | **$1,125** | **$1,086** |

Because values rose faster than the levies, county rates go down. Individual bills still change with each property's new value, and the county levies are only part of the bill (schools, fire districts, the state school levy, hospital, library and EMS levies are set by other bodies).

## Options for the Board

1. **Take the 1 percent and new construction** in both levies (as shown). Adds about $253,100 to the general fund and $171,400 to the road fund over 2026.
2. **Take new construction only** (no 1 percent). General fund about $132,400 less; road fund about $94,100 less; the forgone 1 percent can be banked for later use.
3. **Shift road levy to the general fund** (diversion under RCW 36.33.220). Not recommended this year; road fund needs its full levy for the 2027 chip seal program and snow and ice.

## Questions the Board office usually gets

**"My value went up a lot. Will my county taxes go up the same amount?"** No. Under the 1 percent limit, the county's total levy grows by about 1 percent plus new construction no matter how much values rise. When values rise faster, the rate per $1,000 goes down. A property whose value rose more than the county average will pay a larger share; one that rose less will pay a smaller share.

**"Why is my bill going up if the county rate is going down?"** Most of a property tax bill is set by other districts (schools, the state school levy, fire, hospital, library, EMS), including voter-approved levies. The Treasurer's office can show how a bill breaks down by district.

**"Can I appeal my value?"** Yes, to the county Board of Equalization. The deadline is on the value notice from the Assessor. The commissioners do not hear value appeals.

**"Does the road levy pay for city streets?"** No. The county road levy is collected only in the unincorporated area and spent on county roads.
