## Commissioners: wildfire readiness doesn't end with fire season

ELLENSBURG - With smoke from the late-season fire near Liberty fresh in everyone's mind, Chair Renee Gustafsen and the Board of County Commissioners are reminding residents that fall is the time to prepare for next year.

"Wildfire readiness is the top public safety priority I hear about from upper county residents, and it's mine too," said Gustafsen, who was sworn in on September 16. "The county's job is to make sure new development is built to be defensible, and to help neighborhoods that want to do the work."

What the county is doing:

- **Free chipping days.** County crews will chip brush that residents have cleared from around their homes on October 17 (Easton and Ronald), October 24 (Cle Elum and Roslyn area) and October 31 (Thorp and Manastash). Piles must be at the roadside by 7 a.m.
- **Firewise grants.** The county has $40,000 in matching grants for neighborhoods working toward Firewise USA recognition. Applications are open until December 1.
- **Standards for new development.** Every new subdivision in the wildland-urban interface must meet ignition-resistant building standards and provide secondary emergency access, and the county Fire Marshal inspects that work before accepting it.
- **Burn restrictions.** Outdoor burning restrictions remain in effect in the unincorporated county until further notice.

What residents can do:

- Clear a five-foot noncombustible zone around your home and keep gutters clean.
- Know two ways out of your neighborhood when you can.
- Sign up for emergency alerts through the Kittitas County Sheriff's Office, which runs emergency management and evacuation notices.

More information is on the county website under Community Development Services and Sheriff - Emergency Management.
