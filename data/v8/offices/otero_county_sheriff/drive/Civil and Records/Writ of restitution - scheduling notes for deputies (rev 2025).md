# Writs of Restitution - Scheduling and Execution Notes

Civil and Records / Patrol. Revised April 2025; reviewed September 29, 2026 with the County Attorney (no changes needed). LP

## Before the date

1. Writ received from the court with the fee ($75). Check that it is signed by the court, has the correct address, and is dated.
2. Lorraine (or whoever has civil that week) calls the landlord or agent and sets a date and time. We give the occupants written notice by posting the writ on the door; removal is not scheduled sooner than the time the law allows after the writ issues.
3. Enter the date on the civil calendar and the patrol schedule. Two deputies for any occupied residence; one is fine for a vacant unit confirmed by the landlord.
4. Check for protection orders, prior calls at the address, and anyone with known medical needs or small children. If there is a reason for concern, a sergeant reviews the plan.

## On the day

- Deputies arrive first and make contact. Occupants get a reasonable chance to collect medications, documents and immediate necessities.
- Deputies keep the peace. The landlord supplies the labor and changes the locks.
- Deputies do not move property, decide who owns what, or store anything. Disputes over property go to the court.
- Animals: if animals are left, call the town (inside town limits) or the county animal officer through dispatch.
- Note the time of entry and the time possession was turned over to the landlord on the return.

## After

- Return of service completed and filed with the court within 2 business days.
- Copy to the landlord.

## Numbers so far in 2026 (through September)

- Writs received: 23 (La Junta 14, Rocky Ford 5, unincorporated county 4)
- Executed: 16; cancelled by landlord (paid, moved out or agreement): 7
- Average time from the day we get the writ to execution: 9 business days

## Holiday period

The courts and many landlords avoid scheduling evictions in late December. If a writ comes in after December 10, check with the landlord whether they want to proceed before the holidays and note the answer in the file.

## Common questions from landlords

- **"Can you do it tomorrow?"** Not usually. We need the writ, the fee, and time to post and give the notice the law requires. Most are scheduled 7 to 12 business days after we get the writ.
- **"Will you bring a locksmith?"** No. Bring your own locksmith and your own crew.
- **"The tenant left already."** Tell us. We still post the writ and turn over possession on the scheduled date, but one deputy is enough if you confirm the unit is empty.
- **"Can you remove a mobile home?"** Removing a mobile home from a lot is a different process; ask your attorney. We can execute a writ for possession of the home itself.

## Common questions from tenants

We refer tenants to the court's self-help center and to legal aid. Deputies can explain the time on the writ and what will happen that day. They should be polite, patient and specific about the time.

## Checklist for the deputy on the day

- Copy of the writ, the civil file number, landlord's name and phone.
- Body camera on from arrival to departure.
- Knock and announce; give occupants the time the writ allows to gather necessities.
- Note anyone present, any animals, and anything unusual.
- Times: arrival, entry, possession turned over, departure.
- Return to Civil and Records before end of shift.
