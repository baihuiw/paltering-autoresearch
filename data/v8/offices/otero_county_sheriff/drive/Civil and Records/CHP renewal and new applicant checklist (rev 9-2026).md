# Concealed Handgun Permits - Applicant Checklist

Otero County Sheriff's Office, Civil and Records Section
222 E. 2nd St., La Junta, CO 81050 | (719) 384-5941
Revised September 2026 (LP). Based on C.R.S. 18-12-201 to 18-12-216.

## Appointments

Permit appointments are **Tuesdays and Thursdays, 8-11:30 a.m. and 1-4 p.m.**, at the front counter. Call the main line to book. Each appointment takes about 20 minutes for a renewal and 40 minutes for a new application (fingerprints and photo).

**Walk-ins:** we take a walk-in only if an appointment slot is open that day. Calling ahead is the best way to avoid a wasted trip. From October 13 we have a second clerk at the counter on Tuesday and Thursday afternoons.

You must apply in the county where you live. Otero County residents only.

## Renewals

You may renew up to **120 days before** your permit expires. If your permit has expired, you may still renew within **180 days after** expiration with a $15 late fee. After 180 days you must apply as a new applicant. **An expired permit is not valid for carrying**, even during the 180-day renewal window.

Bring:
- Your current Colorado concealed handgun permit
- A valid Colorado driver's license or state ID showing your Otero County address
- The completed renewal application (the state form; copies at the counter or on our website). **Do not sign it before you come in**; it is signed in front of the clerk.
- Payment

You do **not** need to retake a training class or be fingerprinted again for a renewal, unless the state background check asks for it. We take a new photo at the appointment.

If your address has changed, Change the address on your driver's license first.

## New applications

Bring:
- Valid Colorado driver's license or state ID
- The completed application (unsigned)
- Proof of handgun training (certificate from a qualifying course, DD-214 or other proof listed on the form)
- Payment

Fingerprints and photo are taken at the appointment.

## After your appointment

The state background check usually takes 2-4 weeks for a renewal and up to 90 days for a new permit. We call you when the permit is ready for pickup. Permits are valid five years.

## Things to know

- Report an address change within 30 days.
- Carry your permit and ID together.
- Our office can answer process questions; we cannot give legal advice about where you may carry.

Questions: Lorraine Pacheco, Civil and Records Supervisor, (719) 384-5941.

## Common questions at the counter

**My permit expires in two months. Is it too early?**
No. You can renew any time in the 120 days before the expiration date on your permit. Renewing early does not shorten your new permit; the new five-year term runs from the date the new permit is issued.

**Do I need an appointment, or can I walk in?**
Please make an appointment. We take a walk-in only when a slot is open that day, and Tuesday and Thursday mornings usually fill a week ahead. Afternoons from 1 to 4 have more openings since mid-October.

**I moved here from another Colorado county. Can I renew here?**
Yes, if you now live in Otero County and your driver's license or ID shows your Otero County address. Bring your current permit from the other county.

**I moved here from another state.**
Colorado permits are issued only to Colorado residents, through the sheriff of the county where you live. You apply as a new applicant.

**Can I mail in a renewal?**
Not at this time. The application is signed in front of the clerk, and we take a new photo.

**How will I know it's ready?**
We call the phone number on your application. Bring photo ID to pick up the permit. If someone else picks it up for you, call us first.

**Can you tell me whether my neighbor has a permit?**
No. Permit records are confidential under state law.

**I'm a military member stationed out of state.**
Call Lorraine; there are special rules for renewals by mail for active duty members.
