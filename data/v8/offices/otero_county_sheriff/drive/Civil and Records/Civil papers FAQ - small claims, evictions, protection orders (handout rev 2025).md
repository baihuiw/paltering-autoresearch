# Civil Papers: Questions We Hear Most

Otero County Sheriff's Office, Civil and Records Section
222 E. 2nd St., La Junta, CO 81050 | (719) 384-5941 | Counter Monday-Friday, 8 a.m.-5 p.m.
Handout, revised November 2025; fee line checked September 2026 (LP). We can explain our process. We cannot give legal advice.

## General

**Does the court send my papers to the Sheriff?**
No. After you file at the Otero County Combined Courts, the clerk gives you court-stamped copies. You bring (or mail) them to us with the service instruction sheet and the fee. In small claims, you can also ask the clerk about service by certified mail, or use a private process server or any adult who is not a party to the case.

**How much does it cost?**
$35 for each person served. $10 for each additional paper served on the same person at the same time. Protection orders: no fee. The fee is paid in advance, by cash, check or money order to "Otero County Sheriff." See the civil fee guide for writs and levies.

**What if you can't find the person?**
We make up to three attempts at different times of day. If we can't serve, we file an affidavit of non-service with the court and mail you a copy. The fee covers the attempts, so it is not returned. If you get a new address, bring us a new or alias summons from the court.

**Can you tell me when they were served?**
Yes. Call Civil and Records with your case number. We also mail you a copy of the return of service the day we file it.

**Can you serve someone outside Otero County?**
No. Papers for another county go to that county's sheriff. Pueblo, Bent and Crowley counties each have their own civil sections.

**Can a deputy serve them at work?**
Yes, if you give us the work address and hours. Some employers don't allow us past the front desk; we may have to wait for a break or the end of a shift.

## Small claims

**How long before trial does the person have to be served?**
At least 15 days before the trial date. Bring us your papers as soon as you have them, and ideally three weeks before trial. If the trial is close, ask the court whether you need a new date.

**I'm the defendant. What do I do with these papers?**
Read them carefully, and note your court date. You can contact the court clerk or the self-help center. We can't advise you on your case.

## Evictions (landlords and tenants)

**What does the Sheriff's Office do in an eviction?**
Three things, each only when the paper comes to us:
1. Serve a demand for rent or possession, if the landlord asks us to.
2. Serve the summons and complaint in the eviction case.
3. After the court enters judgment and issues a writ of restitution, carry out the writ: we post it, schedule a date with the landlord, and keep the peace while the landlord takes possession.

**Will a deputy move my things out?**
No. The landlord provides the labor. Deputies keep the peace. Deputies do not decide who owns property or store anything.

**How much notice does a tenant get before the writ is carried out?**
The writ is posted on the door and the removal is not scheduled sooner than the law allows. Tenants with questions about their rights should contact the court's self-help center or a legal aid office.

**Do you do evictions on holidays or in bad weather?**
We don't schedule writs on county holidays. In extreme cold or storms we may reschedule with the landlord.

## Protection orders

**I have a temporary protection order. How fast will it be served?**
Protection orders are attempted the same day or the next morning, including weekends. There is no fee. Give us as much information as you can about where the person will be. Until the order is served, it may be hard to enforce, so let us know right away if you see the person.

**The person was served. What now?**
Keep a copy of the order with you. If the person violates the order, call 911.

## Other

**Can I pick up a copy of my return of service?**
Yes, at the counter, no fee for parties to the case.

**Do you take cards?**
Not at the civil counter at this time. Cash, check or money order.
