# Records Requests: Procedure and Fees

Otero County Sheriff's Office, Civil and Records Section
Revised June 2025; fee table revised September 2026 (LP)

Requests for law enforcement records fall under the Colorado Criminal Justice Records Act (C.R.S. 24-72-301 et seq.). Requests for other office records (budgets, policies, contracts) fall under the Colorado Open Records Act (CORA, C.R.S. 24-72-201 et seq.). The front counter takes both.

## How to request a report

- **At the counter:** Monday-Friday, 8 a.m.-5 p.m. Fill out the one-page records request form.
- **By email:** sheriff@oterogov.org with "Records request" in the subject, or through the contact form on the website (choose "Records request").
- **By mail:** Civil and Records, 222 E. 2nd St., La Junta, CO 81050.

Give us the date, the location, your name and role (driver, owner, victim, party) and the case number if the deputy gave you one. The deputy's card at the scene has the case number on it.

## When reports are ready

A report is released after the deputy finishes it and a sergeant approves it, usually 5 to 7 business days after the incident. We will let you know if it is not ready.

## Crash reports

- Crashes worked by our deputies in the unincorporated county: request from us.
- Crashes inside La Junta or Rocky Ford city limits are usually taken by those police departments; ask them. If a deputy took the report, request it from us.
- Crashes on US-50, CO-10, CO-71 and the other state routes are usually worked by the Colorado State Patrol.
- Insurance companies may request a copy directly with the claim and case number.
- Private property crashes (parking lots) often get a short "information exchange" report. These are available the same way.

## Fees

| Item | Fee |
|---|---|
| Crash report or incident/offense report (per report) | $5.00 |
| Additional pages beyond 10 | $0.25 per page |
| Certified copy | add $5.00 |
| Photographs on disk or by link | $10.00 per case |
| Body-worn camera or dash camera video | research time after first hour, plus $10.00 media |
| Clearance letter / local records check (per name) | $10.00 |
| Research and retrieval beyond the first hour (CORA) | hourly rate set by state law |

Victims of a crime may receive one copy of the report free.

Payment: cash, check or money order to "Otero County Sheriff". Emailed copies are sent after payment is received; we can take payment by mail.

## What we release and what we don't

We release crash reports and most incident reports to the people involved, with some information removed (Social Security and driver's license numbers, dates of birth of others, medical information, juvenile names, victim contact information in some cases). Records of open investigations may be withheld under the Act. If we deny any part of a request, we tell you in writing and the reason.

## Response time

Under CORA we respond within three working days, which can be extended by seven working days when the request is large. Most report requests are filled within the three days once the report is approved.

## Common questions

**A deputy took a report at a store parking lot. Is that a crash report?**
Usually a parking lot crash gets a short information exchange report, unless someone was hurt or it was a hit and run. The fee and the way to request it are the same: $5 per report.

**My insurance company says they need the report. Should I get it or should they?**
Either. If you get it, you can email it to your adjuster. If they ask us directly, they need the case number and the date. Most adjusters in the valley ask us directly.

**How do I know the case number?**
It's on the card the deputy gave you. If you don't have it, give us the date, the place and your name, and we will find it.

**The report has a mistake in it.**
Ask for the deputy's supervisor through the main line. A deputy can add a supplemental report to correct a fact, such as a plate number or insurance policy number. We can't change a deputy's description of what happened, but your statement can be added.

**Can I get a copy of the 911 call?**
911 recordings are kept by the dispatch center. Ask at the counter and we will forward the request.

**How long do you keep reports?**
Routine incident and crash reports are kept at least ten years under the state retention schedule for sheriffs' offices; some longer.
