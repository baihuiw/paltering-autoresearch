# Patrol Bulletin 26-14: Hunting Season - Trespass Calls and Parks and Wildlife

Lt. Cody Brandvold. Issued October 9, 2026. Read at every briefing through October 16. Supersedes Bulletin 25-12.

## Season dates (Otero County area, approximate - check the CPW brochure)

- Pronghorn rifle: early October
- Deer rifle (plains units): late October through mid-November
- Waterfowl and pheasant: November through the winter
- Archery and muzzleloader seasons overlap in September and October.

Expect more trespass calls, shots-fired calls and pickups parked on county road shoulders at dawn and dusk.

## Who handles what

**We (Sheriff's Office) take:**
- Criminal trespass on private land (C.R.S. 18-4-503 and 18-4-504), including hunters on posted or fenced land without permission. Under Colorado law, hunting on private land requires the owner's permission whether or not it is posted.
- Shots fired near homes, livestock or people; reckless endangerment; damage to fences, gates, livestock or property.
- Traffic: vehicles blocking roads, parked in the travel lane, driving across fields.
- Any call where someone is threatened or there is a dispute between a landowner and a hunter.

**Colorado Parks and Wildlife (district wildlife officer) takes:**
- Hunting license and tag violations, illegal take, shooting from a vehicle or from or across a public road, spotlighting, wanton waste.
- CPW officers can also cite for trespass under the wildlife statutes.

**Both:** most calls involve both. **We respond; we do not tell the caller to call CPW instead.** Dispatch notifies the on-duty CPW officer (through the CPW dispatch line) at the same time for any call involving hunting, so the closest officer goes first. Our deputy secures the scene and identifies people and vehicles; the wildlife officer handles the wildlife side when they arrive.

## For callers (what dispatch and front counter should say)

- If it is happening now (trespassers on the land, shots being fired near the house or livestock), **call 911** or the dispatch line, (719) 384-5941. Don't wait or try to confront anyone.
- Give the location, number of people, vehicle description and plate if it can be seen safely, and direction of travel.
- Dispatch will send a deputy and notify Parks and Wildlife.
- For a past wildlife violation with no one on scene, residents may also call **Operation Game Thief, 1-877-265-6648**, which can pay rewards and allows anonymous reports.
- Landowners can post their land with signs or purple paint and keep a list of people they've given permission. A written permission list helps us sort out who belongs there.

## Response

- Response time depends on where deputies are. South end nights are the longest (see the Sept activity report). We will tell the caller the honest estimate.
- If we can't get there quickly, the caller's description, photos and plate are the best evidence for a later summons.

## Last year

Fall 2025: 61 hunting-related calls (trespass 33, shots fired 14, road/parking 14). 9 trespass summonses; 6 CPW citations from joint calls. Most calls: Road 18/Road 20 area north of Fowler, and CO-10 east.

## Checklist for the responding deputy

1. Make contact safely. Hunters are armed by definition; keep it calm and professional.
2. Identify everyone: names, DOB, hunting license and tag numbers, vehicle plates.
3. Ask the landowner whether permission was given, and to whom. Check for posting.
4. Photograph vehicles, gates, damage, livestock out, any animals taken.
5. Tell CPW what you have when the wildlife officer calls or arrives.
6. Decide on trespass: warning, summons, or arrest (rare). Document the landowner's wishes.
7. Write the report before end of shift; tag it "HUNTING" in the records system so we can count them this fall.
