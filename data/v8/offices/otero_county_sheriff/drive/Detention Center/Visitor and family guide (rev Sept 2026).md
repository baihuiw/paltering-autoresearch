# Otero County Detention Center - Visitor and Family Guide

222 E. 2nd St., La Junta, CO 81050 | Main line (719) 384-5941 | Lobby open 24 hours for bonds and deposits
Revised September 2026

Having someone you love in jail is hard. This guide answers the questions families ask most. If you don't find your answer here, call the main line and ask for the shift sergeant.

## Finding someone

Use the inmate lookup on the county website or call the main line. We can tell you whether a person is in custody, the booking number, the charges as booked, the bond and the next court date. We cannot discuss the case.

## Visits

**Video visits** are available 7 days a week, 8 a.m. to 9 p.m.
- From home: schedule online through Corvane up to 7 days ahead. Remote visits are $4.00 per 20 minutes.
- At the lobby: free. Schedule at the lobby kiosk.

**In-person visits** are Saturdays and Sundays, 1 to 4 p.m., by appointment.
- 30 minutes, up to two adults plus children with a parent or guardian.
- Every adult needs a current photo ID.
- Dress code: no sleeveless shirts, shorts above the knee, or clothing with gang, drug or alcohol images.
- Phones, bags and keys go in the free lockers.

New bookings can have their first visit after 72 hours.

## Putting money on an account

See the lobby kiosk (cash is free), Corvane online or by phone, or mail a money order. Details are on the kiosk and in the inmate handbook.

## Health care

The Detention Center contracts with a correctional health company for medical care.

- Every person is screened by medical staff at booking, including questions about medications, chronic conditions, pregnancy and withdrawal.
- Medical staff are available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.
- A physician is on call at all times, and a nurse practitioner holds clinic every week.
- Detention officers are trained in CPR, first aid and the use of naloxone (Narcan), and an automated external defibrillator is in every housing area.
- Emergencies go to Arkansas Valley Regional Medical Center by ambulance.

**Medications.** Families may not bring medications, inhalers, insulin or medical supplies to the jail. Tell the booking officer, or call and give the shift sergeant the name of the person's pharmacy and doctor. Medical staff will verify prescriptions and order needed medications through the jail pharmacy. People with diabetes receive blood sugar checks and insulin on a schedule set by the medical provider.

**Privacy.** Because of medical privacy law, we cannot discuss a person's medical care with family without that person's written permission. Your loved one can sign a release with medical staff.

**If you are worried** about someone's health, call the main line and ask for the shift sergeant. Your concern will be passed to medical staff right away.

## Mail

Letters and postcards only. Full name and booking number on the envelope. No packages. Books must come directly from a publisher or retailer.

## Phone calls

Inmates call out on the tablet system. You cannot call in to an inmate. To leave an urgent family message (a death or serious illness), call the main line and ask for the chaplain or shift sergeant.

## Property and release

Property can be picked up weekdays 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. with a written release. At release, any money left on an account is returned on a release card.

## Concerns

Inmates can file a grievance on the tablet. Families can write to the Detention Commander, Captain Gil Arguello, at the address above.
