# La Junta Rotary - Sheriff Strohm remarks

Tuesday, September 22, 2026, noon. About 10 minutes plus questions. Notes as delivered, typed up by Bonnie Sauer from the Sheriff's outline and her notes of the Q&A.

## Remarks

Thank you for having me. I've been sheriff for exactly one week, so you'll forgive me if I don't have all the answers yet. I'll tell you what I do know.

I grew up in Manzanola. I spent twenty-six years with the State Patrol, most of them working these same roads. I was a volunteer firefighter for eleven years. I'm not running in November. The commissioners asked me to keep the office steady until the voters choose, and that's what I intend to do. In January I'll hand the keys to whoever wins and go back to fixing fence.

What does the Sheriff's Office do? More than most people think. We run the county jail, which holds about 86 people on an average day, some of them for Bent and Crowley counties. We patrol about 1,200 square miles of the county outside the towns. We serve civil papers for the courts: small claims, evictions, protection orders. We issue concealed handgun permits. We provide security at the courthouse. And the sheriff is the county fire warden, and our Office of Emergency Management handles disasters.

We do it with about 70 people and a budget of $6.7 million from the county general fund. That budget is set by the commissioners, and I'll be making our case for 2027 like every other department.

Three things I want to get right in four months:

**Patrol.** At night we often have only two deputies for the whole county. We have two openings and I want them filled. Until then, I've asked our lieutenant to keep one car south of the river at night, because people in Cheraw and Timpas are a long way from La Junta.

**The jail.** It's a new building and a good one. A jail is the place where small things become big things if nobody's watching, so I'm going to be in there every week.

**Answering people.** When you call us, you should get a call back. When you write to me, I'll answer. If we can't do something, we'll tell you why.

## Questions

**Q: Is the office going to do anything about speeders on county roads?**
A: Yes. I've heard about CR 31 by Cheraw, and Road 18 up by Fowler. We're working on a fall traffic plan. With two openings I can't promise a car on every road, but I can promise we'll put the time where kids are.

**Q: How do I get my concealed carry permit renewed? I heard it's hard to get through.**
A: It has been, and that's on us. We were short a clerk. Appointments are Tuesdays and Thursdays. You can renew up to 120 days early. We're adding help at the counter in October.

**Q: Do you serve papers for small claims, and what does it cost?**
A: We do, $35 per person served. You bring the stamped copies from the court to our front counter.

**Q: Will you put a school resource officer back in the schools?**
A: Not with two vacancies. I'd rather have deputies on the road at night. But we'll be in the schools for talks and walk-throughs, and I'll revisit it when we're staffed.

**Q: How can Rotary help?**
A: Two ways. Tell people we're hiring, deputies and detention officers both. And when you hear something about the office that bothers you, tell me directly. My door's open and my number is the main line, 384-5941.

**Q: What's it like to go from the State Patrol to running a jail?**
A: Different. On the highway you see people for ten minutes. In the jail you see them every day, and so do our detention officers, who have one of the hardest jobs in the county. They're the ones I'd like you to thank when you get the chance.

**Q: What should people do if they want to complain about a deputy?**
A: Call or write the Undersheriff or me. We take every one seriously and we'll tell you what we found out, as far as the law allows.
