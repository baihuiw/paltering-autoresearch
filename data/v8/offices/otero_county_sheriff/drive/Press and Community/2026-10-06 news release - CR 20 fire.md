# Sheriff thanks firefighters, posse after County Road 20 grass fire

LA JUNTA - Sheriff Lonnie Strohm on Tuesday thanked the Manzanola, Rocky Ford and Fowler fire departments, the Otero County Sheriff's Posse and county road crews for their work on Friday's grass fire south of Manzanola, which burned about 1,150 acres before it was contained late Friday night.

The fire was reported at 3:31 p.m. Friday, October 2, in grassland west of County Road 20. Winds from the southwest gusting to 35 mph pushed it toward homes between County Roads 20 and 22. As county fire warden, the Sheriff ordered an evacuation of the area at 4:05 p.m.

An evacuation alert went out at 4:12 p.m., seven minutes after the order. Deputies and posse members knocked on all 64 doors in the evacuation area within 75 minutes, and residents and their animals were directed to the Otero County Fairgrounds, where the American Red Cross set up a shelter.

Crews held the fire at the canal and along County Road 21, and a county grader cut a line on the east side. The fire was contained at 9:40 p.m., and residents were allowed home at 10:15 p.m. Two outbuildings and about 300 bales of hay were lost. No one was hurt and no homes were damaged.

"Friday was this county at its best," Strohm said. "Volunteer firefighters left their jobs to fight this, posse members hauled trailers for people's horses, and our deputies made sure nobody was left behind. I'm proud of every one of them."

The cause is under investigation by the Manzanola fire district. Officials believe it was accidental.

## Sign up for emergency alerts

Strohm urged every resident to register for Otero County Emergency Alerts so they get evacuation orders and other warnings by phone call, text or email.

"The alert is only as good as the number we have for you," Strohm said. "Take five minutes and sign up, and help your parents and neighbors do it too."

To register, visit the Sheriff's Office page on the county website and choose "Emergency Alerts," or call the Office of Emergency Management at (719) 384-5941 ext. 2240 during business hours, and staff will register you over the phone. Residents can register a cell phone, a home phone and an email address, and choose the addresses they want alerts for, such as a home, a farm or a parent's house.

## Fire danger remains high

Dry grass and wind will keep fire danger high through the fall. Residents are asked to avoid parking on dry grass, check trailer chains so they don't drag, and call 911 right away if they see smoke.
