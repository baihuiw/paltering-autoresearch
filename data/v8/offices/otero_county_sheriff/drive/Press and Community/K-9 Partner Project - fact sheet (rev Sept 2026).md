# K-9 Partner Project - fact sheet

Otero County Sheriff's Office | revised September 2026 (MP)
For the front counter, the posse table at events, and the website donations page.

## What it is

The K-9 Partner Project is the Sheriff's Office's effort to put a working police dog back on the road in Otero County. The office has not had a K-9 team since 2021. Neighboring agencies' dogs have been called in for searches and drug sniffs, which can mean a wait of an hour or more.

A dual-purpose dog can:
- track a lost child, a wandering elder or a person who ran from a crash;
- search buildings and fields so deputies don't have to go in blind;
- detect narcotics during lawful vehicle and building searches;
- visit schools, 4-H clubs and community events.

**Community goal: $16,300** for the dog and the handler course. The handler position and ongoing care are part of the Sheriff's Office budget request to the Board of County Commissioners.

## Where donations go

- Donations are deposited with the County Treasurer into a restricted account (1100-2190, Sheriff donations - K-9).
- Every dollar donated is held in a restricted account and can only be spent on the K-9 program.
- Donations are not used for salaries, overtime or general operations.
- Donors who give $50 or more receive a thank-you letter that can be used for tax records. Otero County is a government entity; please check with your tax preparer.
- A donor may ask for a refund of an unspent donation in writing to the Administrative Services Manager.

## Vendor

Sandhill K-9, Pueblo West, CO (owner Dale Whitacre). Sandhill selects, trains and certifies the dog and runs the handler course. Dogs are usually Belgian Malinois or German shepherds, 18 months to 3 years old.

## Questions people ask

**Will the dog live with the deputy?** Yes. K-9 dogs live with their handler and ride in a patrol vehicle with a climate-controlled insert.

**Can kids meet the dog?** Yes, once the team is certified. School and 4-H visits will be scheduled through the Sheriff's Office.

**What happens when the dog retires?** Retired dogs usually stay with their handler.
