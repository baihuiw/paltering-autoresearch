# Fire Restrictions in Otero County: Stages, Criteria and Burn Permits

Otero County Office of Emergency Management (a division of the Sheriff's Office)

## Who decides

- **Unincorporated county:** the Board of County Commissioners adopts and lifts fire restrictions by resolution (C.R.S. 30-15-401(1)(n.5)). The Board acts on the recommendation of the Sheriff, who is the county fire warden (C.R.S. 30-10-513), with OEM and the fire protection district chiefs.
- **Inside towns:** each town (La Junta, Rocky Ford, Fowler, Manzanola, Swink, Cheraw) sets its own rules.
- **State and federal land:** the Comanche National Grassland and state wildlife areas follow federal or state orders, which can differ from the county's.

Restrictions stay in effect until the Board lifts them. The Board can adopt or lift restrictions at any regular public hearing (2nd and 4th Mondays) or at a special meeting called on short notice when conditions change quickly.

## Stages

**Stage 1 (most common)** prohibits:
- Open burning, including trash, ditches, fields and slash, except with a written exemption from the fire district
- Fireworks (sale and use) in the unincorporated county
- Campfires except in permanent constructed pits in developed campgrounds
- Smoking outdoors except in a vehicle or building, or a cleared area 3 feet across
- Welding or cutting without a spotter, extinguisher and cleared area

**Stage 2** adds a ban on all campfires and charcoal, and limits on chainsaws and other equipment during the hot part of the day. Otero County last used Stage 2 in 2022.

## Criteria OEM uses before recommending restrictions

OEM watches fire danger daily from the Remote Automated Weather Station readings for the county's fire danger rating area and the National Weather Service forecasts. We recommend Stage 1 when:

1. The **energy release component (ERC)** is at or above the **80th percentile** for at least five days in a row, **and**
2. At least two of the following:
   - Red flag warnings forecast or issued
   - Fire districts report several human-caused starts in a week, or their crews and equipment are stretched
   - Long-range outlook is for above-normal temperatures and below-normal rain
   - Neighboring counties have adopted restrictions

We recommend lifting when the ERC has been below the 80th percentile for about a week after significant rain, and the fire chiefs agree.

A single large fire is not by itself a criterion. Most grass fires in the county are started by equipment, dragging chains, vehicles or debris burns that get away, and restrictions don't change the risk from equipment and traffic. In the fall, cooler nights and shorter days can bring the ERC down even when grass is dry and wind is high.

## Burn permits - without restrictions in place

Even with no county restrictions, **open burning requires a permit from, or notification to, the local fire protection district**, and no one may burn when a red flag warning is in effect or on windy days. Burning household trash is discouraged everywhere and banned inside most towns. The fire district can refuse or cancel a permit on any day.

| Area | Who issues burn permits |
|---|---|
| La Junta and surrounding rural area | La Junta Rural Fire Protection District |
| Rocky Ford and surrounding rural area | Rocky Ford Rural Fire Protection District |
| Fowler area | Fowler Rural Fire Protection District |
| Manzanola area | Manzanola Rural Fire Protection District |
| Swink area | Swink Rural Fire Protection District |
| Cheraw area | Cheraw Fire Protection District |

Call the non-emergency line, (719) 384-5941, and dispatch will connect the caller to the right district or give the chief's number. Dispatch also keeps the day's list of permitted burns so deputies don't respond to a permitted burn as a wildfire.

## What residents can do

- Report illegal or unattended burning to 911 or the dispatch line.
- Keep grass mowed around buildings; clear 30 feet around the house.
- Check trailer chains and don't park on dry grass.

## Questions residents ask

**"There was just a big fire. Why aren't restrictions back on?"**
Restrictions are tied to measured fire danger over several days, not to a single fire. Most of our grass fires start from equipment, vehicles or burns that get away, and Stage 1 doesn't stop haying, driving or welding with precautions. When the readings meet the criteria, OEM and the Sheriff recommend restrictions and the Board can act quickly.

**"My neighbor is burning trash on a windy day."**
Call 911 or dispatch. Burning on a windy day or during a red flag warning is not allowed with or without restrictions, and the fire district or a deputy can put it out.

**"Can I still have a campfire or use my grill?"**
Without restrictions, yes, with care: in a pit or ring, attended, with water nearby, and not on windy days. Check with your fire district.

**"Where do I check whether restrictions are in effect?"**
The county website, the Sheriff's Office Facebook page, or call (719) 384-5941.
