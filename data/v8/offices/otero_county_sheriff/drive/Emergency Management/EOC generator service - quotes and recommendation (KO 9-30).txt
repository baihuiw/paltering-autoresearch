Re: Emergency Operations Center generator - service agreement for 2027-2029

Background

The EOC in the lower level of the administration building runs on its own 60 kW diesel standby generator, separate from the detention center's two large units. The building contractor's one-year warranty service on the EOC generator ended in April. Since then we've had no maintenance agreement. The county facilities department checks the fuel level monthly but can't do load bank testing or the annual service.

The detention center generators are covered under the building's facilities contract and are not part of this.

What we need

- Two preventive maintenance visits a year (oil, filters, coolant, belts, battery test)
- Annual load bank test (2 hours at 75% load)
- Monthly automatic exercise check reported by the remote monitor
- Emergency service response within 4 hours, 24/7
- Fuel polishing every two years

Quotes (three, per purchasing policy, received Sept 14-25)

| | Vendor 1 (Pueblo) | Vendor 2 (Colorado Springs) | Vendor 3 (La Junta) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Annual price, 3-year term | $1,980 | $2,350 | $1,720 |
| Load bank test included | Yes | Yes | No (+$650/yr) |
| Emergency response | 4 hrs | 6 hrs | Next business day |
| Remote monitoring | Included | Included | Not offered |
| Parts discount | 15% | 10% | none |
| Effective cost per year | $1,980 | $2,350 | $2,370 + |

Recommendation

Vendor 1 at $1,980 per year for three years ($5,940 total). It's the lowest real cost once the load bank test is counted, and it's the only one that meets the 4-hour emergency response. The local shop is good and I use them for small engines, but they don't have a load bank or after-hours service.

Budget

OEM operating (1130) has $12,250 remaining for 2026. The first year would start January 1, 2027, so this is in the 2027 request at $1,980 (already in the worksheet under OEM operating). Nothing needed from the 2026 budget.

Board

Service agreements over $1,500 a year go to the Board. I asked Bonnie to put it on the October 26 agenda. The County Attorney has the vendor's standard agreement; she's asked for two changes (Colorado governing law, and the insurance certificate naming the county).
