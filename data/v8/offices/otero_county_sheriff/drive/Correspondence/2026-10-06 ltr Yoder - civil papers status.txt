I paid $35 three weeks ago to have papers served for my small claims case and I still haven't heard anything. The court date is October 22. If he isn't served, what happens? Can I get my money back?
-----

Dear Marlene,

Thank you for writing, and I understand the worry with your court date coming up.

I asked our Civil and Records supervisor, Lorraine Pacheco, to look at your file. Here is where it stands.

Your papers came in on September 14. Our civil officer tried to serve them three times, on September 16 (afternoon), September 21 (evening) and September 29 (early morning), at the address on your instruction sheet. No one answered on any of the three, and a neighbor told our officer on the last attempt that the person may have moved to Pueblo County. Under our procedure, after three attempts we file an affidavit of non-service with the court and mail a copy to you. That went out on October 1, so you should have it in the mail now. I'm sorry no one called you first.

What happens next:

- In small claims, the defendant has to be served at least 15 days before the trial. With an October 22 trial, there isn't time to serve him at a new address before then. You can ask the court to reset the trial date and issue a new summons (an "alias summons"). The clerk at the Combined Courts can help with that form.
- If he now lives in Pueblo County, the papers need to go to the Pueblo County Sheriff, or you can use a private process server. If you have a new address in Otero County, bring us the new summons and we'll try again.
- The $35 fee covers our attempts, even when we can't make service, so we can't give it back. I know that's not what you hoped to hear. If you bring us a new summons for an Otero County address, Lorraine will apply it as an additional attempt at no charge, since you didn't get a call from us.

A few things that may help with the next try:

- If you know where he works, put the employer's name and address and his usual hours on the service instruction sheet. Many papers are served at work.
- A recent photo or a description of his vehicle and plate helps our officer recognize him.
- If he has family in the county who might tell you where he is staying, that address is worth listing too.
- Ask the court clerk whether service by certified mail is available for your case. It is cheaper, but it only works if he signs for the letter.

If you want to talk it through, call (719) 384-5941 and ask for Lorraine.

Sincerely,
Lonnie Strohm
Sheriff, Otero County
