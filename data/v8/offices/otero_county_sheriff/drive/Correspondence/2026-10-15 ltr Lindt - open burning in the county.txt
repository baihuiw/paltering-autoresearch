Dear Harold,

Thank you for writing. You have good reason to be careful this fall, and I appreciate you speaking up.

Here's where things stand on fire restrictions, and what you can do about burning next door.

**Why there are no county restrictions right now.** Stage 1 fire restrictions were in place in the unincorporated county from June 22 until the Board of County Commissioners lifted them on September 14 (Resolution 2026-57). That followed about 1.8 inches of rain in early September, and the fire chiefs and our Emergency Manager agreed the conditions that called for restrictions had passed. Restrictions are set by the Board by resolution, on the recommendation of the Sheriff (I'm also the county fire warden) and the Emergency Manager.

Fire danger has climbed again since then, and our Emergency Manager is watching the daily readings. Restrictions are recommended when the measure of how dry the fuels are (the "energy release component") stays above a set level for several days in a row along with red flag days or other signs. In recent weeks we came close but didn't meet that. The rain on October 7 helped. If conditions change, I can ask the Board to put restrictions back, and the Board can act at any meeting.

**Burning still needs a permit.** Even with no restrictions, anyone burning a ditch or field needs a permit from, or has to notify, the local fire district, and nobody may burn on a windy day or during a red flag warning. In your area that's the Swink Rural Fire Protection District.

**What you can do.** If you see burning on a windy day or a fire that isn't being watched, call 911 or our dispatch line, (719) 384-5941. Dispatch keeps a list of permitted burns for the day, and can send the fire district or a deputy right away. The fire district or a deputy can put out a burn that endangers people or property, whether or not it has a permit.

**What happens on a call like this.** When dispatch gets a report of a burn, the dispatcher checks the day's permit list. If the burn is permitted and the wind is low, the dispatcher may simply note the call. If it's windy, if a red flag warning is in effect, or if the burn isn't on the list, the fire district is sent, and a deputy if one is nearby. The firefighters decide whether the burn has to be put out. Repeat problems can lead to a summons under the district's rules or state law.

I've also asked our Emergency Manager, Ken Ostdiek, to remind the fire districts to post their permit rules for fall ditch burning. You can reach him at ext. 2240 if you'd like to talk with him directly.

Thank you for caring about your neighbors' safety.

Sincerely,
