----- Incoming (letter, received Sept 30, 2026) -----
Gene Frederick, Road GG, east of Manzanola
Sheriff, there has been a pickup with no plates sitting in the bar ditch on Road GG by my driveway for five weeks. The windows are busted out now and kids have been in it. I called in August and somebody put a sticker on it, but it's still there. When I pull out with the tractor I can't see around it. Who is supposed to haul it off?
-----

Dear Gene,

Thank you for your letter, and for your patience. Five weeks is too long, and I'm sorry it took a letter to get it moving.

You're right that this is ours. A vehicle left on a county road right-of-way in the unincorporated county is handled by the Sheriff's Office under the state's abandoned vehicle law. Inside the towns, the town police handle it.

Here is what happened and what happens now:

- I checked our call log. A deputy tagged the pickup on August 27 with the orange notice that starts the clock. Under Colorado law, a vehicle left on a public right-of-way can be towed after the notice period if no one claims it. That period has long passed.
- The tow should have been called after the notice expired, and it wasn't. The tag went into a pile of paperwork when we were short a sergeant on days. That's on us.
- The pickup will be towed this week by the rotation tow company. The deputy will run the VIN and try once more to reach the last registered owner, as the law requires, and the tow company will hold the vehicle. If no one claims it, it is disposed of under the state process.
- Road and Bridge will check the ditch and the sight line at your driveway once it's gone.

What helps us:
- Call dispatch, (719) 384-5941, any time a vehicle has been left on a county road for more than a couple of days, especially if it blocks a view or is in the travel lane. Give the location and a description.
- If people are going into the vehicle, call while it's happening. Broken glass and kids don't mix.

If the pickup is not gone by Friday, please call Lt. Cody Brandvold, who supervises patrol, at the main number.

Sincerely,
Lonnie Strohm
Sheriff, Otero County
