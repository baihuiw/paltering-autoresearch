Dear Ray,

Thank you for writing before the season instead of after. I'm sorry about last year. Being bounced between agencies while your cattle are on the road is not how this should work.

Here is how it will work this fall.

**Call us first, and call while it's happening.** Call 911 if anyone is in danger or there's shooting near your house or stock, or the dispatch line, (719) 384-5941, for a trespasser who is there now. You only need to make one call. Our dispatcher sends a deputy and, at the same time, notifies the Colorado Parks and Wildlife officer on duty. Whoever is closest goes first. You should not be told to call the other agency, and I've made sure our dispatchers and deputies know that (we issued a written bulletin on October 9).

**Who does what.** Trespass on private land, damage to gates and fences, livestock let out, and shots near homes or stock are ours. License and tag violations and shooting from a road are Parks and Wildlife's, though their officers can write trespass too. Most calls involve both, and we work them together.

**What helps.** From a safe distance: the vehicle description and plate, how many people, where they went in, and photos if you can. Don't confront anyone. If your gate was left open, tell dispatch that livestock are out so the deputy knows it's urgent.

**Posting.** Posting with signs or purple paint is a good idea. Under Colorado law, hunters need permission to hunt private land whether it's posted or not, but posting removes any argument. Keep a short written list of the people you've given permission, and give a copy to our office if you like; it helps the deputy sort things out quickly.

**Response time.** I won't promise you a deputy in ten minutes on Road 18 on a Saturday morning in November. We have two deputy positions open and we are interviewing this month. But the deputy will come, and if we can't get there in time, your plate number and photos are often enough for a summons later.

**Gates.** If you'd like, a deputy can stop by before the deer season opens to look at where people have been getting in and suggest where signs will do the most good. Call the main line and ask for the patrol sergeant on days.

For a wildlife violation after the fact, you can also call Operation Game Thief at 1-877-265-6648.
