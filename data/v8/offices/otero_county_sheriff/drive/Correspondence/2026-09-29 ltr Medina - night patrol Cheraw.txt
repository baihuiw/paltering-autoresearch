Dear Teresa,

Thank you for your email after the Cheraw town meeting last Thursday, and for staying afterward to talk. I'm sorry about the break-in at your shop, and I'm glad no one was there.

You asked two things: how long it should take a deputy to get to Cheraw at night, and what we are doing about it.

Here is the honest picture. At night we schedule two deputies for the whole unincorporated county, about 1,200 square miles, plus whatever the towns ask of us. When both are on calls, or one is transporting someone to the jail, the wait for the next call can be long. In August our average response at night to the Cheraw area was 19 minutes, and some calls waited much longer.

What we are doing:
- We have two deputy positions open and are interviewing in October. Filling them is the single biggest thing that will help.
- I have asked our patrol lieutenant to build the schedule so one car stays south and east of the river at night whenever we have two on.
- Deputies on night shift will make extra passes through Cheraw and along CR 31 this fall.
- The Colorado State Patrol and the Bent County Sheriff's Office back us up when we are stretched, and I talk with both regularly.

If you see something suspicious, please call 911 right away rather than waiting until morning. For non-emergencies, the dispatch line is (719) 384-5941.

I'll be at the next Cheraw town meeting, and I'd welcome your report on how things look then.

Sincerely,
Lonnie Strohm
Sheriff, Otero County
