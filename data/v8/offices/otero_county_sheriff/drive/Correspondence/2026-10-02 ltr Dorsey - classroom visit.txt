Dear Ms. Dorsey,

Thank you, and please thank your third graders for their letters. I'd be glad to come myself.

Bonnie Sauer, who keeps my calendar, has me down for **Wednesday, October 28, at 10:30 a.m.**, for 40 minutes in your classroom. If that doesn't work for your class, call Bonnie at (719) 384-5941 and she will find another morning. I'll bring a deputy with me, and if the weather is good the class can look at a patrol car in the parking lot afterward. We'll keep the siren off.

So you know what to expect, here's what I usually do with this age:

- Read a few of their letters out loud and answer their questions (they always want to know about the handcuffs and whether I've ever been in a car chase).
- Talk about what a sheriff and deputies do: help people in an emergency, keep the roads safe, help when someone is lost, and work with firefighters.
- Practice when and how to call 911, and what to say. We give them a card with their address to fill out at home with their parents.
- Remind them that if they're ever lost or scared, a deputy, a police officer or a firefighter is someone safe to go to.

Each student gets a junior deputy sticker. If there's anything you'd like me to cover or leave out, just let Bonnie know.

We get more school requests in the fall than we can always fill on the date asked, so thank you for giving us a range of mornings. It makes it much easier to send someone. Wednesday mornings in November are blocked on my calendar for school visits, so other teachers at your school are welcome to ask for those.

A few practical things for the day:

- We'll check in at the school office about 10 minutes early.
- If you'd like the students to see the patrol car, we'll need a spot in the parking lot or along the curb near the playground door.
- If a priority call comes in while we're there, the deputy may have to leave. I'll stay and finish.
- If any of your students might be upset by a uniform or a patrol car, please let Bonnie know ahead of time. We can keep it low-key and skip the car.

Thank you for inviting us. I'm looking forward to it.

Sincerely,
Lonnie Strohm
Sheriff, Otero County
