Dear Mr. Pietrzak,

Thank you for your letter of September 8 about your water bill. I'm sorry you've had trouble getting an answer. I'm writing for the Supervisor's office while the township fills the Supervisor's position.

Here is the short version, because it confuses almost everyone. The township still owns the water and sewer system that serves your home, but since January 1, 2024 the Bay County Department of Water and Sewer (BCDWS) has operated it for us under an agreement approved by the Township Board. BCDWS reads the meters, sends the bills, takes payments and handles billing questions, leaks, main breaks and sewer backups. The township office no longer has access to customer accounts and can't adjust a bill.

The phone number printed on the old township water bills now goes to a line that isn't staffed, which is why nobody called you back. Please use the customer service number printed on your most recent BCDWS bill. I've enclosed a copy of our "who to call" sheet with their information.

On the bill itself: you mentioned your usage nearly doubled from the last quarter. When you call BCDWS, ask them to re-read the meter and to check the reading history for a leak. In our experience the most common cause of a sudden jump is a toilet that runs or leaks silently into the bowl. A simple test is to put a few drops of food coloring in the tank and wait 15 minutes without flushing; if color shows up in the bowl, the flapper is leaking. A dripping outdoor faucet or a water softener stuck in its regeneration cycle can also do it.

If BCDWS finds that the meter itself was wrong, they will correct the bill. If you aren't satisfied with their answer, please let me know and I will ask the township's contact at BCDWS to look at it.

Sincerely,

Carla Rheaume
Deputy Supervisor, Hampton Charter Township
