Dear Ms. Kaczmarek,

Thank you for your letter about the rental house next to yours on 4th Street and the furniture, tires and garbage bags that have been in the yard since August. I understand how frustrating it is to look at that every day.

Our Building and Zoning Administrator, Matt Krawczak, handles property maintenance under the township's blight ordinance. I asked him to look at it, and he visited the property on September 29. Here is what happens next, so you know what to expect:

1. **Letter to the owner.** Because it is a rental, the notice goes to the owner of record (from the rental registration and the tax roll), with a copy to the tenant. It lists what has to be removed and gives 10 days to do it.
2. **Second visit.** Matt goes back after the deadline. Most cases end here; about nine out of ten of our blight cases this year were cleaned up after the first letter.
3. **Ticket.** If it's still there, the owner gets a municipal civil infraction ticket, which is handled in district court, with fines that increase for repeat violations.
4. **Township cleanup.** If the owner still doesn't act, the township can have the property cleaned up by a contractor and bill the owner. An unpaid bill is added to the property's tax bill.

The rental registration program also matters here. Owners of rental homes in the township must register them and pass a rental inspector's visit every three years. An owner who ignores blight letters can have the rental certificate suspended until the property is brought back into compliance. Matt has told me he will use that if he has to.

I know 10 days plus a second visit can feel slow when you are living next to it. If you see the situation get worse (for example, garbage attracting animals), please call Matt directly at ext. 230 and he can move faster under the health and safety provisions.

Thank you for letting us know. Please call me if it isn't resolved by the end of October.

Sincerely,
