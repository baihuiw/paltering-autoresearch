# Blight and property maintenance: enforcement procedure

Building and Zoning, Charter Township of Hampton. Staff procedure and resident explainer. Revised February 2024 (MK); numbers section updated January 2026. Ordinance references are to the township's blight ordinance and rental registration ordinance as amended.

## What counts as blight

- Junk or rubbish stored outside: furniture, appliances, mattresses, bagged garbage outside of collection day, scrap, tires.
- Inoperable or unlicensed vehicles stored outside for more than 14 days (except in a fully enclosed building).
- Grass or weeds over 8 inches tall on developed lots.
- Buildings that are open to entry, have broken windows or doors, or are in danger of collapse.
- Outdoor storage of building materials not tied to an active building permit.
- Accumulations that attract rodents or create a health hazard.

## How a case moves

| Step | What happens | Normal timing |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Report | Resident calls, emails, uses the website form, or staff sees it. Reports can be anonymous. Front counter takes the address and a short description. | Day 0 |
| 2. Site visit | Administrator views the property from the road or a neighbor's lot (with permission) and photographs it. | Within 5 business days |
| 3. First notice | Letter to the owner of record (and occupant, if a rental) listing what must be corrected. | Mailed within 2 business days of the visit; **10 days** to correct (grass: 5 days) |
| 4. Follow-up visit | Administrator checks after the deadline. | Within 3 business days after the deadline |
| 5. Second notice or ticket | Partial progress: second letter with 7 more days. No progress: municipal civil infraction ticket. | |
| 6. Court / abatement | Contested tickets go to the district court. If the owner still doesn't correct, the township may hire a contractor to clean up and bill the owner; unpaid costs are placed on the winter tax roll. | Board approval needed for abatement over $2,500 |

Fines (civil infraction): first offense $100, second $250, third and later $500, plus court costs.

**Immediate hazards** (open building, rodents, garbage that's rotting) can skip to a 48-hour notice.

## Rentals

- Every rental dwelling (including single-family homes and duplexes) must be registered with Building and Zoning. Registration is $60 per unit, for three years, with an interior and exterior rental inspector visit before a certificate is issued.
- For blight at a rental, the owner is responsible, not just the tenant. Notices go to the owner at the address on the registration and on the tax roll, with a copy to the tenant.
- If an owner doesn't correct blight after a ticket, the administrator can suspend the rental certificate. Renting without a valid certificate is a separate civil infraction for each month.
- Owners outside Bay County must list a local agent on the registration.

## What we tell the person who reported it

- Thank them, give the case a number, and tell them the timeline above so they know a 10-day letter is the normal first step.
- We can tell them the case is open and when the follow-up visit is, but not share letters to the owner (those are available by FOIA once the case is closed).
- Ask them to call back if it gets worse.

## Questions residents ask

**"I reported it a week ago and nothing has changed."** The first step is a written notice to the owner with 10 days to correct. Nothing usually changes until that deadline passes. Tell them the date the notice went out and the date of the follow-up visit.

**"Can't you just make them clean it up today?"** Only for an immediate health or safety hazard. Ordinary blight follows the notice process so the township's tickets hold up in court.

**"Who is the owner?"** Ownership is public on the tax roll. We don't give out the tenant's name or phone number.

**"Will the owner know I complained?"** We don't tell owners who reported a property.
