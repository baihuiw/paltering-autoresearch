# Special land use and site plan review: staff guide

Building and Zoning, Charter Township of Hampton. Internal reference for the counter and new Planning Commission members. Revised January 2025.

## What a special land use is

Some uses are allowed in a zoning district only with Planning Commission approval, because they can affect neighbors more than the uses permitted by right. The zoning ordinance lists them district by district. The commission decides whether the specific proposal meets the ordinance's general standards (compatibility, traffic, utilities, nuisance) and may attach reasonable conditions.

A special land use approval runs with the land, not the owner. It lapses if the use does not begin within the time set in the approval.

## Steps

1. **Pre-application meeting** with the Zoning Administrator. Bring a sketch.
2. **Application** with fee, site plan and a written description of the operation.
3. **Staff review**: zoning, the township engineer (stormwater), the fire department (access, water supply), and the Road Commission if there is a new drive.
4. **Notice.** Michigan's Zoning Enabling Act requires notice of a special land use request to be published in a newspaper of general circulation at least 15 days before the hearing, and mailed or delivered to the owners of property within 300 feet and to the occupants of structures within 300 feet. We also post the agenda at the hall and on the website.
   - The 300-foot list comes from the assessing parcel layer. Run it from the parcel the application covers.
   - Keep a copy of the list as mailed and the date it went to the post office in the file.
5. **Public hearing** at a Planning Commission meeting (second Thursday, 7 p.m.).
6. **Decision** with any conditions, by motion and roll call.
7. **Approval letter** to the applicant listing the conditions; copy to the file and the Supervisor.
8. **Inspection** before operation begins, and periodic compliance checks.

## Timing

Applications received by the 15th of a month are usually heard the following month. Allow at least 45 days from a complete application to a decision.

## Appeals and complaints

- Questions about conditions or complaints about an approved use (noise, dust, hours) go to the Zoning Administrator, who inspects and enforces.
- The township attorney handles anything involving a challenge to a decision.

## Site plan review

Uses permitted by right still need site plan approval if they involve a new building, an addition over 1,000 sq. ft., new parking, or a change of use. Small site plans may be approved administratively; the rest go to the Planning Commission. No public hearing is required for a site plan alone.
