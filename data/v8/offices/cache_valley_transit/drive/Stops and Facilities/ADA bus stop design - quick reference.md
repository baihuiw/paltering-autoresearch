# ADA bus stops: quick reference for staff

Planning and capital projects reference. Kept with the stop inventory. Not legal advice; when in doubt, check the full 2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design (section 810) and the FTA ADA circular.

## Boarding and alighting area (810.2)

- **Surface:** firm, stable and slip resistant; connected to streets, sidewalks or pedestrian paths by an accessible route.
- **Dimensions (810.2.2):** a clear length of 96 inches minimum, measured perpendicular to the curb or vehicle roadway edge, and a clear width of 60 inches minimum, measured parallel to the vehicle roadway.
- **Slope (810.2.4):** parallel to the roadway, the same as the roadway to the maximum extent practicable; perpendicular to the roadway, not steeper than 1:48.

The 96-inch depth is what gives a wheelchair user room to come off a deployed ramp and turn.

## Shelters (810.3)

If we build a shelter, it must be connected to the boarding area by an accessible route and have a clear floor space (30 by 48 inches) entirely inside the shelter.

## Signs (810.4)

Route identification signs on new stops follow the visual character requirements; braille is not required for bus stop signs.

## When the rules apply

Requirements apply to new stops and to stops we alter. Where we only replace a sign or bench, the boarding area does not have to be brought up to standard, but we prioritize stops on the Phase 1 and Phase 2 lists and stops near senior housing, clinics and schools.

## Our own practice

- Every new pad is checked at closeout: measure depth from back of curb, cross slope, and a ramp deployment test with an operator.
- Stops that can't meet the standard (no right-of-way) are noted in the stop inventory, and Call-A-Ride eligibility letters can list them as a condition.
