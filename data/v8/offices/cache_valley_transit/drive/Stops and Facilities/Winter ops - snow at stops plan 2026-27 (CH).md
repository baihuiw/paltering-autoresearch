# Snow and ice at bus stops - winter plan 2026-27

*Cody Hales, Capital Projects and Facilities Coordinator. Rev. September 30, 2026. Goes to the board as part of "winter operations readiness" (10/28 management report). Supersedes the 2025-26 plan.*

## Who does what

| Where | Who clears it | Standard |
|---|---|---|
| The 64 priority stops (every shelter plus the busiest boarding stops; list in "Snow priority stops 2026-27") | Contractor: Kestrel Grounds Services LLC, contract CVTD-26-019 | Within **24 hours after a snowfall of 2 inches or more ends**, measured at the admin building |
| Transit Center platforms and admin building | Connect facilities crew (2 techs) | Before the first bus, 5:30 a.m. |
| All other stops (about 410 signed stops) | Connect facilities crew, on a rotating list, after priority stops are done | Within 72 hours, as crew time allows |
| Streets and the snow windrow left by plows | The city or UDOT, whichever owns the road | City and UDOT priorities |
| Sidewalks between stops | Adjacent property owners and the cities, under local rules | Local rules |

## Contractor scope (CVTD-26-019)

The contract was renewed in September for its second of three option years at the same unit prices as last season ($38.50 per stop visit; $14.00 per stop for ice melt only; seasonal cap $46,000). The contractor must:

- Clear the boarding area at the curb (at least 5 feet along the curb and back to the sidewalk), the path from the sidewalk to the shelter, and the shelter floor.
- **Cut an opening through the plow windrow** at the boarding area so riders can reach the bus door. This is the single most common reason for rider calls.
- Apply ice melt to the cleared area.
- Return within 24 hours if a city plow buries a cleared stop again on a later pass (paid as a second visit).
- Send a completion log with time stamps and photos after each event.

Snowfalls under 2 inches: no contractor call-out unless Cody or the operations supervisor sees icing at a priority stop. Then ice melt only.

## Call-out

The facilities on-call person checks snowfall at 4:00 a.m. and 2:00 p.m. on snow days. If the 2-inch trigger is met, they text the contractor's dispatcher and log the time. The 24-hour clock runs from when the snow stops, not when it starts. For long storms the contractor may make a first pass before the snow stops.

## A typical storm day

- **4:00 a.m.** On-call checks snowfall at the admin building. 2 inches or more, still snowing: text the contractor to stand by. Facilities crew starts the Transit Center platforms.
- **5:30 a.m.** Transit Center clear before the first bus. Communications posts any delays on the website, Facebook and the Transit app.
- **Snow stops.** Contractor called out; 24-hour clock starts. Contractor starts with shelters and the hub stops, then works the priority list by area.
- **10:00 a.m. and 3:00 p.m.** Dispatch sends Cody the list of stops operators reported as buried or icy. Priority stops go to the contractor; others go on the crew's list.
- **Next day.** Cody checks the contractor's photo log against the priority list and sends back any stop that is missing or not cut through to the curb. Riders who reported a stop get a reply within one business day.

## Rider reports

Riders report buried or icy stops with the website contact form ("Bus stops") or by phone at (435) 752-2877. The front desk sends them to Cody by email the same day, with the stop location and the stop ID if the rider knows it (the number on the stop sign). Operators report stops they can't serve safely by radio; dispatch keeps a list each storm.

- A priority stop not cleared within 24 hours: Cody calls the contractor.
- A non-priority stop with heavy plow buildup: facilities crew adds it to the next run. Stops that get buried repeatedly are candidates to add to the priority list mid-season (allowed under the contract up to 75 stops).

## Working with the cities

The windrow problem comes from street plows pushing snow onto corners and curbs where stops are. Cody sent each city's street superintendent the stop map in October and asked that plow operators avoid pushing snow onto stops where they can. Logan City asked for a list of stops at corners with a history of buildup; it went over on 10/2 with 18 stops. We don't control city plowing and riders should not be told the cities will change their routes.

## Last winter (2025-26) in brief

- 11 call-outs, 618 contractor stop visits, $27,940 spent (of $46,000 cap).
- 83 rider reports; 61 were plow windrows at stops, mostly after the second plow pass on arterials.
- Average contractor clearing time after a call-out: 13.5 hours; 7 visits went past 24 hours (all in the Jan. 14-15 storm).
- New for this year: the contractor must send photos within 2 hours of each visit, and the priority list grew from 58 to 64 stops.

## Equipment and supplies

Facilities crew: one pickup with plow and spreader, two walk-behind snow blowers, 12 tons of ice melt in the salt shed (reordered 9/28; delivery 10/20). Snow shovels and ice melt buckets at the Transit Center.
