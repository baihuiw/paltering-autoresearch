# Route study, round 1: summary of findings (excerpt)

*Excerpt from the consultant's round 1 summary memo to the district, dated August 2026, sections 2-4. Circulated to managers by Janelle Ostler 9/16/26 ahead of round 2 planning. The full memo, with appendices and the open-ended comment coding, is on the planning share. Board one-on-ones with the consultant continue through October.*

## 2. Who we heard from

- **Survey:** 1,912 responses (1,604 online, 248 paper, 60 by phone). 61% currently ride Connect at least once a week; 22% ride occasionally; 17% don't ride now.
- **Language:** 1,788 English, 124 Spanish. Spanish responses were concentrated in Logan's west side, Hyrum and Smithfield.
- **Where respondents live:** Logan 58%; North Logan and Hyde Park 13%; Smithfield and Richmond 9%; Providence, Millville, Nibley and Hyrum 14%; elsewhere 6%.
- **Who:** USU students 31%; working adults not in school 41%; retired 16%; high school students 4%; other 8%. One in nine respondents reported a disability that affects how they travel.
- **Stakeholder interviews (9):** USU Parking & Transportation; Cache County School District; two large employers on the west side of Logan; Logan Regional Hospital; Bear River Health Department; a senior center; a refugee and immigrant services organization; and the Cache Valley chamber.
- **Operator focus groups (2):** 23 operators across all shifts.

## 3. What we heard

**3.1 Zero fare is the system's identity.** Asked what they value most, 72% of riders named zero fare first. Several employers said zero fare is part of how they recruit. Nobody we interviewed suggested charging fares.

**3.2 The biggest gap is time of day, not place.** The most common request, from riders and non-riders alike, was service later in the evening. Many routes end around 8:30 p.m., and the last trips on several routes leave the Transit Center before 8:00 p.m. Respondents who work retail, health care, restaurant and warehouse shifts told us they can get to work by bus but can't get home.

**3.3 Frequency matters more than coverage to people who ride now.** Current riders preferred "a bus every 15 minutes on fewer streets" over "a bus every 60 minutes on more streets" by 58% to 42%. Non-riders leaned the other way (46% to 54%), mostly because they want service closer to home. Round 2 should test this trade-off with real examples.

**3.4 Job centers are growing faster than the network.** The industrial and distribution area along 1000 West and the North Logan business park were named in more open-ended comments than any other destinations not served today. Employers said shift start times (5:30 a.m., 2:00 p.m., 10:00 p.m.) don't match bus hours.

**3.5 Directness and transfers.** Riders from the south end of the valley (Providence, Millville, Hyrum) said trips into north Logan and USU take too long with a transfer at the Transit Center. Riders on the Loop asked for a direct connection to the hospital.

**3.6 Reliability.** About one in five comments about a specific route mentioned late buses, most often on the routes that run through school zones in the morning and on Main Street in the late afternoon. Operators in the focus groups said running times on several routes have not kept up with traffic growth.

**3.7 Bus stops.** Shelters, benches and lighting were the most common stop requests. Snow at stops came up in 140 comments, nearly all describing plow ridges blocking the curb at corners.

**3.8 Bikes.** Riders who bring bikes said racks are often full on the Loop and the routes serving campus in the morning. Several asked whether bikes could ride inside when the bus is empty.

**3.9 Information.** Riders like the Transit app. Non-riders said they didn't know where routes go or that the bus is free. Spanish-speaking respondents asked for printed information at stops in Spanish.

**3.10 What operators told us.** Operators in both focus groups said the network works best where routes are simple and run straight on major streets, and worst where routes loop through neighborhoods or share timepoints with school traffic. They asked that any new network leave enough layover for restroom breaks and recovery time, and that schedules be tested in winter conditions. Several said riders frequently ask them for evening service and for a direct trip from the south end of the valley to USU.

## 4. Implications for round 2

1. Test the frequency vs. coverage trade-off with two sketch networks, each costed within current resources.
2. Ask specifically about evening service: which routes, how late, and which days.
3. Map the job centers: ask employers for shift times and ask riders to drop pins on places they want to reach.
4. Ask about the Transit Center transfer: would riders accept a different hub structure for faster trips?
5. Include bike and stop questions so the district can plan capital spending alongside the network.
6. Reach non-riders, especially on the west side and in the south end, through pop-ups at places people already go.

*The consultant will present draft concepts after round 2. No new network is recommended at this stage.*
