Outgoing letter OL-26-027
Sent by email 9/22/2026 from gm@rideconnectutah.gov
To: Arlo Sorensen, Smithfield
Re: Contact form message of 9/17 (Opinion)

Dear Mr. Sorensen,

Thank you for your note, and for riding Route 15 to your volunteer shifts at the hospital for the past six years. You asked me, as the new general manager, to promise that Connect will stay free.

I can tell you where things stand. Connect has been zero fare for decades, and the district's funding is built around it: our buses are paid for by a quarter-cent local sales tax and federal and state transit grants, not by fares. The district has no plans to charge fares, and no fare proposal is before the Board of Trustees. Any fare would require a board vote after public notice and a public hearing, so riders would hear about it well ahead of time and have a chance to speak.

You mentioned reading that some cities are bringing fares back. A number of systems that dropped fares during the pandemic have done that. Our situation is different: we were fare-free long before 2020, and in a system our size, collecting fares would cost a good share of what it brought in and would slow down every trip.

In my first week I rode every route, and the thing riders told me most often was what you told me: that free buses are one of the best things about living in Cache Valley. I agree, and I've told the board so.

If you'd like to share your view with the trustees directly, the board welcomes public comment at its meetings, held the fourth Wednesday of most months at 5:30 p.m. at our administration building in North Logan.

P.S. Thank you also for your years of volunteering at the hospital. Our Route 15 operators tell me they know the volunteers by name.

Sincerely,

Dana Whitcomb
General Manager, Cache Valley Transit District
