Dear Ms. Jeppson,

Thank you for writing ahead of winter, and I'm sorry about the stop at 600 West and 200 North last season. You described climbing over a ridge of plowed snow to reach the bus, and that should not happen.

Here is how snow at bus stops works. Connect contracts with a snow removal company to clear our priority stops, which include every shelter and our busiest stops, within 24 hours after a snowfall of 2 inches or more ends. The contractor clears the area where you board, cuts an opening through the ridge left by the street plows, and puts down ice melt. If a plow buries the stop again on a later pass, the contractor comes back. Our own facilities crew clears other stops as time allows, usually within a few days.

The streets themselves are plowed by the cities and UDOT, and the ridges at corners come from their plows. We've shared a map of our stops with each city's streets division and asked their crews to avoid pushing snow onto stops where they can, but we don't control their routes.

Because of your note and others like it, I'm glad to tell you that your stop has been added to our priority list for this winter, so it will be cleared under the contract after every qualifying snowfall.

If a stop is buried this winter, please tell us right away through the contact form at rideconnectutah.gov (choose "Bus stops") or by calling (435) 752-2877. Your report goes to our facilities coordinator, Cody Hales, the same day.

One more thing that helps: if you see a city plow bury the stop after it has been cleared, let us know that too. Our contract requires the crew to come back, and your report tells us where to send them first.

Sincerely,

Dana Whitcomb
General Manager, Cache Valley Transit District
