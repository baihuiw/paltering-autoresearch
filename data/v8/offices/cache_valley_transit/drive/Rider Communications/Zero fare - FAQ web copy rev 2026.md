# Why is Connect free? (zero-fare FAQ)

*Web copy for rideconnectutah.gov/zero-fare. Rev. September 2026 (KO); new GM signature line added 9/18. Spanish version in progress.*

Connect has been a zero-fare system for decades. You never need a ticket, pass or app to ride our fixed-route buses or Call-A-Ride. Just get on.

**How can the buses be free?**
Connect is funded mainly by a local sales tax of one quarter of one percent (0.25%) collected in Cache County, plus federal transit grants from the Federal Transit Administration and grants from the State of Utah. Everyone who shops in Cache Valley, including visitors and students, helps pay for the buses, and everyone can ride them.

**Isn't charging a fare a better deal for taxpayers?**
For a system our size, fares would bring in less than you might think. Fare boxes, cards, cash counting, armored car pickup and fare enforcement all cost money, and fares slow down boarding, which makes every trip longer. Many small systems collect only a small share of their operating cost in fares. Zero-fare service also brings in more riders, which helps us qualify for federal formula funding.

**Are you going to start charging fares?**
No. The district has no plans to charge fares. Any fare would require a vote of the Board of Trustees after public notice and a public hearing, and no such proposal is before the board. Some transit systems around the country that went fare-free during the pandemic have since gone back to fares. Connect was fare-free long before that, and our funding was built around it.

**Is Call-A-Ride free too?**
Yes. Call-A-Ride, our paratransit service for riders certified as unable to use the fixed-route buses, has no fare. Personal care attendants ride free with a certified rider.

**Who can ride?**
Anyone. You don't need to live in Cache County, be a student or show ID.

**Do I have to tap anything when I board?**
No. Our buses count riders automatically with sensors at the doors. That count is how we report ridership to the federal government and plan service.

**What about the Route 16 to Preston?**
Route 16, our commuter route to Franklin County, Idaho, is also free.

**How many people ride?**
Connect carried about 197,000 fixed-route rides in September 2026 alone, plus about 3,400 Call-A-Ride trips. Ridership is highest during the USU semesters, but riders of every age use Connect to get to work, school, medical appointments and shopping.

**Does zero fare mean crowded buses?**
Sometimes, on the Loop and the routes serving campus at the start and end of the school day. We add trips where we can and track crowding through our automatic passenger counters. Our route study is looking at where more frequent service is needed most.

**Do riders without a fare behave differently?**
Our operators tell us zero fare makes their job easier: no fare disputes, no counting change, faster boarding. Rider conduct rules (no smoking, no alcohol, keep aisles clear, be respectful) apply on every bus, and operators can call a supervisor when they need help.

**Where does the sales tax money go?**
About two-thirds of our operating budget pays the operators, dispatchers, mechanics and Call-A-Ride drivers who run the service. The rest pays for fuel, parts, insurance, our facility, bus stops and administration. The board adopts the budget each December after a public hearing, and our monthly financial reports are part of each board packet at rideconnectutah.gov/board.

**How do I tell you what I think about zero fare?**
We love hearing from riders. Use the contact form at rideconnectutah.gov (choose "Opinion"), or come to a board meeting and speak during public comment. Board meetings are the fourth Wednesday of most months at 5:30 p.m. at 3021 N. 300 W., North Logan.

*"Riders here tell me zero fare is the best thing about living in Cache Valley. It's also one of the smartest things this district does." - Dana Whitcomb, General Manager*
