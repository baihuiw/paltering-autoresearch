# Bikes on Connect buses

*Web copy for rideconnectutah.gov/bikes. Rev. September 2026 (KO). Replaces the 2023 page. Printed version goes in the "How to Ride" rack card.*

Every Connect fixed-route bus has a bike rack on the front. Bikes ride free, like you do. Here is how it works.

## The basics

- Each rack holds **two bikes**. Racks are first come, first served.
- **Full-size bikes are not allowed inside the bus**, even when the rack is full or the bus is nearly empty. Operators can't make exceptions: a bike in the aisle blocks the path for riders using wheelchairs, walkers and strollers, and it becomes a hazard if the bus stops suddenly.
- **Folding bikes** are welcome on board if they are fully folded before you board and kept out of the aisle and the priority seating area.
- Bikes must be standard two-wheel, single-rider bikes with wheels at least 16 inches and no wider than the rack's wheel slots. Tandems, cargo bikes, bikes with trailers or child seats that can't be removed, and gas-powered bikes can't use the rack.
- **E-bikes** may use the rack if they weigh 55 pounds or less with the battery in. If you can't lift it into the rack yourself, it is too heavy. Operators can't help lift bikes.
- Remove anything loose before loading: water bottles, pumps, bags, helmets.

## Loading your bike

1. Wait on the curb until the bus has fully stopped. Make eye contact with the operator or give a wave so they know you're loading.
2. Squeeze the handle on top of the rack to pull it down.
3. Lift your bike into the open slot closest to the bus first, with the front wheel facing the curb. If that slot is taken, use the outer slot with the front wheel facing the street.
4. Pull the support arm up and over the top of your front tire, as close to the frame as it will go.
5. Board through the front door and let the operator know where you're getting off.

## Unloading

Tell the operator you have a bike on the rack before your stop, then exit through the front door. Lift the support arm off the tire, lift your bike out and walk it to the curb. If the rack is empty, fold it back up. Never walk into the street in front of the bus or behind it.

## When the rack is full

The next bus on your route will have its own rack. You can ask the operator to radio dispatch for the next bus's arrival time, or check the Transit app. During the USU fall and spring semesters, racks on routes serving campus (the Loop and Routes 01, 02 and 15) fill quickly between 7:30 and 9:30 a.m. and from 3:30 to 5:30 p.m. Riders who can be flexible by 15 minutes usually find an open spot.

## Bikes and winter

Racks stay in service all winter. Knock snow and slush off your bike before loading it, and expect the rack to be wet or icy. In heavy snow, operators may ask riders not to use the rack if road spray is building up on it or if it blocks the headlights; that's rare and usually lasts only until the storm passes.

## Bike parking

Bike racks are available at the Transit Center, the USU stops on 700 North, and several shelters. Connect is not responsible for bikes locked at stops. Our route study is asking whether riders would use secure bike lockers at the Transit Center.

## Lost bikes

Bikes left on a rack are removed at the end of the trip, tagged and taken to our administration building at 3021 N. 300 W., North Logan. We hold them for 30 days, like any lost item. Call (435) 752-2877 to claim yours.

## Coming soon

This fall we're testing **three-bike racks** on two Loop buses. We are watching how they handle snow and road spray and whether they affect the operator's view. If the test goes well, we plan to add them to more buses in 2027. Tell us what you think: the route study survey has questions about bikes and buses.
