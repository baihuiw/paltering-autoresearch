## A Message from Mayor Paul Haverkamp

Dear neighbors,

It is an honor to write to you for the first time as your mayor. I was sworn in on September 15, and I've spent my first days meeting with department heads and listening to residents.

People want two things from their city: honest answers and the basics done well. Clean water, safe streets, parks we're proud of, and a city that's easy to get around. ## Fall Paving Begins in October

The 2026 street resurfacing program runs from early to late October, weather permitting, and covers about 3.1 miles: Holgate Avenue (Fifth to Pontiac), Jefferson Avenue (Third to Sixth), Hopkins Street (Clinton to Wayne), Washington Avenue (Perry to Clinton), Elliott Road (Domersville to the corporation limit) and Summit Street (Jefferson to Holgate).

Each street is milled, then repaved about a week later. "No Parking" signs go up at least 48 hours before work starts on your block, and residents on affected streets will receive a door hanger with dates and a phone number for the street department. The program is paid for by the voter-approved street levy. Six curb ramps on Hopkins Street will be rebuilt to current accessibility standards as part of this year's work.

## Third Ward Sidewalks and Curb Ramps: Design Under Way

Residents of the East Second Street and Wayne Avenue neighborhood have told the city for years that their sidewalks are in rough shape: heaved and broken slabs, corners with no curb ramp at all, and ramps too steep for a wheelchair or walker.

The city has hired Hoeffel & Strayer Engineering of Defiance to design the **Third Ward Sidewalk and Curb Ramp Improvements**. Survey crews finished their field work in August, and the engineers are now preparing plans. The project area includes East Second Street from Clinton Street to Wayne Avenue, Wayne Avenue from East Second Street to East Fifth Street, and parts of East Third and East Fourth Streets between Wayne and Jefferson Avenues.

The plans call for replacing about a mile of damaged sidewalk and rebuilding 32 curb ramps to meet current accessibility standards, including ramps at five corners that have none today. The project will be funded through a combination of state infrastructure grant dollars and the city's Capital Improvement Fund. Because it is a city project, property owners in the project area will not be assessed for the work.

Construction is expected next season. Residents along the project route will receive a letter before work begins in front of their homes. Questions can go to the Engineering Department at (419) 784-2101, ext. 231.

## Residential Sidewalk Repair Program

Outside of city projects like the one above, property owners are responsible for the sidewalk in front of their property. The city's **50/50 cost-share program** pays half the cost of replacing qualifying sidewalk through the city's contractor. Applications for the 2027 program are accepted through **December 31, 2026**, and your share can be paid up front or over five years on your property tax bill. Forms are at the City Building and on the city website under Engineering.

## Fall Leaf Collection

Curbside leaf collection runs **Monday, October 26 through Friday, December 4**. Crews will make at least three passes through each ward. A ward-by-ward schedule will be posted on the city website and the City Building bulletin board by October 19.

- Rake leaves into the tree lawn, not the street. Leaves in the street clog storm drains.
- Don't park on top of leaf piles. Hot exhaust can start a fire.
- Bagged leaves can be dropped off at the compost site on Island Park Avenue, Saturdays 8 a.m. to noon through November 21.
