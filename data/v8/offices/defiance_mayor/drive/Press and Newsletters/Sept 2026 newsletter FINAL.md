## A Message from Mayor Paul Haverkamp

Dear neighbors,

It is an honor to write to you for the first time as your mayor. I was sworn in on September 15, and I've spent my first days meeting with department heads, walking job sites with our engineers and listening to residents who stopped me at the grocery store to tell me what they think. Please keep doing that.

I owned an insurance agency on Clinton Street for twenty years, and I served on the Planning Commission. In both jobs I learned that people want two things from their city: honest answers and the basics done well. Clean water, safe streets, parks we're proud of, and a city that's easy to get around.

That last one matters a great deal to me. You'll read about some of that work in this issue, along with fall paving, leaf collection and Trick-or-Treat night. My door at the City Building is open, and my email is mayor@cityofdefiance.com.

## Fall Paving Begins in October

The 2026 street resurfacing program starts in early October and runs through late October, weather permitting. This year's program covers about 3.1 miles of city streets, including:

- Holgate Avenue (Fifth Street to Pontiac Street)
- Jefferson Avenue (Third Street to Sixth Street)
- Hopkins Street (Clinton Street to Wayne Avenue)
- Washington Avenue (Perry Street to Clinton Street)
- Elliott Road (Domersville Road to the corporation limit)
- Summit Street (Jefferson Avenue to Holgate Avenue)

**What to expect:** Each street is milled (the top layer ground off) and then repaved about a week later. Between milling and paving the street is open to traffic but rough, and manhole covers and valve boxes may stick up slightly. Drive slowly.

**Parking:** "No Parking" signs will go up at least 48 hours before work starts on your block. Please move vehicles off the street during posted hours so crews can work. Vehicles left in the work zone may be towed.

**Driveways:** You may be unable to use your driveway for a few hours on paving day. Residents on affected streets will receive a door hanger with dates and a phone number for the street department.

The program is paid for by the city's voter-approved street levy. Six curb ramps on Hopkins Street will be rebuilt to current accessibility standards as part of this year's work.

## Third Ward Sidewalks and Curb Ramps: Design Under Way

Residents of the East Second Street and Wayne Avenue neighborhood have told the city for years that their sidewalks are in rough shape: heaved and broken slabs, corners with no curb ramp at all, and ramps too steep for a wheelchair or walker. Help is on the way.

The city has hired Hoeffel & Strayer Engineering of Defiance to design the **Third Ward Sidewalk and Curb Ramp Improvements**. Survey crews finished their field work in August, and the engineers are now preparing plans. The project area includes:

- East Second Street from Clinton Street to Wayne Avenue
- Wayne Avenue from East Second Street to East Fifth Street
- Parts of East Third Street and East Fourth Street between Wayne Avenue and Jefferson Avenue

The plans call for replacing about a mile of damaged sidewalk and rebuilding 32 curb ramps to meet current accessibility standards, including ramps at five corners that have none today. The project will be funded through a combination of state infrastructure grant dollars and the city's Capital Improvement Fund. Because it is a city project, property owners in the project area will not be assessed for the work.

Construction is expected next season. Residents along the project route will receive a letter before work begins in front of their homes.

"Every resident deserves to be able to get to the corner store, the bus stop or a neighbor's house safely," Mayor Haverkamp said. "Fixing these sidewalks and ramps is exactly the kind of basic work I want this administration to be known for."

Questions about the project can go to the Engineering Department at (419) 784-2101, ext. 231.

## Residential Sidewalk Repair Program

Outside of city projects like the one above, Defiance's Codified Ordinances make property owners responsible for keeping the sidewalk in front of their property in good repair. To help, the city offers a **50/50 cost-share program**: if your sidewalk is on the city's annual repair list, or you apply and your sidewalk qualifies, the city pays half the cost of replacement through the city's contractor.

- Applications for the 2027 program are accepted through **December 31, 2026**.
- Engineering staff will inspect each application and let you know whether it qualifies.
- Your share can be paid up front or spread over five years on your property tax bill.

Application forms are available at the City Building and on the city website under Engineering.

## Fall Leaf Collection

Curbside leaf collection runs **Monday, October 26 through Friday, December 4**. Crews will make at least three passes through each ward. A ward-by-ward schedule will be posted on the city website and the City Building bulletin board by October 19.

Tips for a smooth pickup:

- Rake leaves into the tree lawn, not the street. Leaves in the street clog storm drains and can cause flooding.
- Keep piles free of sticks, rocks, grass clippings and trash. The vacuum equipment can't handle them.
- Don't park on top of leaf piles. Hot exhaust can start a fire.
- Bagged leaves can be dropped off at the compost site on Island Park Avenue, Saturdays 8 a.m. to noon through November 21.

## Do I Need a Permit?

As fall projects get started, a reminder about what needs a permit from the city's Building and Zoning Department:

| Project | Permit needed? |
|---|---|
| Fence (any height) | Yes, zoning permit |
| Shed over 200 square feet | Yes, zoning permit |
| Deck attached to the house or over 30 inches off the ground | Yes, building permit |
| Replacing shingles with no structural work | No |
| New driveway or widening a driveway | Yes, right-of-way permit for the apron |
| Work on the public sidewalk | Yes, right-of-way permit (no fee) |
| Swimming pool over 24 inches deep | Yes, zoning permit |

Most residential zoning permits are issued within five business days. Call Building and Zoning at (419) 784-2101, ext. 230, before you start. It's much easier than fixing a problem afterward.
