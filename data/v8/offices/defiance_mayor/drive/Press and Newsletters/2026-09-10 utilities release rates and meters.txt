NEW WATER AND SEWER RATES TAKE EFFECT WITH OCTOBER BILLS; INCREASE WILL FUND TREATMENT PLANT UPGRADES

Meter replacement program continues this month in the Second, Third and Fourth Wards

DEFIANCE, Ohio - Water and sewer customers will see a 6 percent rate increase on bills issued on or after October 1, 2026, following City Council's approval of Ordinance 2026-38 on September 8. It is the first adjustment to water and sewer rates since October 2023.

For a typical household using 9,000 gallons per quarter, the combined quarterly water and sewer bill will rise from $100.00 to $106.00, an increase of about $2.00 a month.

The additional revenue will help pay for major work at both of the City's treatment plants. At the Water Treatment Plant, the Filter Rehabilitation and Clearwell Improvements project is replacing filter underdrains and media that in some cases date to 1974 and repairing the clearwell where treated water is stored before it enters the distribution system. The project is financed through a low-interest loan from the Ohio Water Development Authority and is scheduled for completion in spring 2027. At the Water Pollution Control Plant, the City is planning replacement of aeration blowers and improvements to sludge handling equipment to keep the plant in compliance with its discharge permit.

"These are not glamorous projects, but they are the reason water comes out of the tap clean and safe every day," said Safety-Service Director Tom Wiechers. "The filters at our water plant have served this community for more than 50 years. This investment means they will be ready for the next 50."

The rate increase also reflects higher costs for treatment chemicals, electricity and materials. The City's rate consultant recommended the 6 percent adjustment as the smallest increase that would cover operating costs, fund the plant improvements and meet the City's loan obligations.

METER REPLACEMENT CONTINUES

The second phase of the City's residential meter replacement program began September 1. Crews from Brandt Metering Services, the City's contractor, are replacing about 1,190 residential meters this month in neighborhoods in the Second, Third and Fourth Wards. Affected customers have received a letter and a door hanger with instructions for scheduling an appointment if the meter is inside the home.

The new meters are read by radio, so meter readers no longer need to enter yards or homes, and estimated bills will become a thing of the past. The new meters are also more accurate than the older meters they replace, many of which are more than 25 years old and slow down as they wear.

"Older meters tend to under-record as they age, which means some customers have been paying for less water than they actually use, and others make up the difference," said Utilities Superintendent Kevin Shaffer. "The new meters make billing fairer for everyone. They will also let us spot a likely leak on a customer's side of the meter much sooner, which can save a family a lot of money."

The first phase of the program, completed in July, replaced about 3,410 meters. A third and final phase, covering the remaining residential meters, is planned for spring 2027.

QUESTIONS ABOUT YOUR BILL

Customers with questions about the new rates or about their bill can contact the Utility Billing Office, Monday through Friday from 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., or by email at utilitybilling@cityofdefiance.com. Billing staff can review an account's meter readings and usage history on request and can help customers check for leaks.

Information about the new rates will also be printed on the October 1 bill and posted at cityofdefiance.com.

The Utility Billing Office reminds customers that third-quarter bills, covering July through September, often run higher than spring bills because of lawn watering, gardens and filling pools.
