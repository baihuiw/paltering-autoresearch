# Mayor Haverkamp Announces Senior Services Initiative

**Kickoff event October 14 in Council Chambers; new resource line, free smoke alarms and File of Life packets for older residents**

DEFIANCE, Ohio - Mayor Paul Haverkamp today announced a Senior Services Initiative to make it easier for older residents and their families to get help from the city, and to bring together the safety programs the city already runs for seniors.

"About one in five Defiance residents is 65 or older, and many of them have lived here their whole lives," Mayor Haverkamp said. "They should be able to pick up the phone and get a straight answer from their city, whether the question is about trash pickup, snow removal or a smoke alarm. This initiative is about making the city easier to deal with for the people who built it."

The initiative has four parts:

**1. A single point of contact.** Constituent services coordinator Luis Ortega will serve as the city's senior services contact. Residents and family members can call the Mayor's Office or email mayor@cityofdefiance.com and ask for senior services. Mr. Ortega will take the question and follow it through the right department, rather than passing callers from office to office.

**2. Free smoke alarms and File of Life packets.** The Defiance Fire Department will install ten-year sealed smoke alarms at no cost in the homes of residents 65 and older, and will provide File of Life packets, the magnetic red folders that hold a resident's medications, conditions and emergency contacts on the refrigerator where paramedics can find them. The department installed 265 smoke alarms through August this year and expects to pass 400 by year end.

**3. Fire safety in senior housing.** The Fire Department's Fire Prevention Bureau inspects every senior apartment building and licensed care facility in the city once a year. The department also offers free fire safety and fall prevention talks in senior building community rooms; building managers can call the Fire Prevention Bureau to schedule one. In September the city began using new inspection software, which puts inspection records and follow-up dates on inspectors' tablets in the field.

"Every senior housing building in Defiance is inspected by our Fire Prevention Bureau every year, and our inspectors know these buildings and the people who manage them," said Fire Chief Dale Rummel. "The new software means follow-ups are tracked automatically instead of on a paper calendar. Add a working smoke alarm in every apartment and home, and we can keep making real progress."

**4. A senior resource guide.** The Mayor's Office will publish a printed and online guide this winter listing city services and the county and nonprofit services most often asked about, including transportation, meals, utility assistance and home repair programs.

## Kickoff event

Residents, family members and caregivers are invited to the Senior Services Initiative kickoff on **Wednesday, October 14, at 11:30 a.m. in Council Chambers**. Mayor Haverkamp will speak, followed by short presentations from Luis Ortega and the Fire Department. The Fire Department will have smoke alarm sign-up sheets and File of Life packets available. A light lunch will be served. The event is free; to reserve a seat, call the Mayor's Office.

## About the Mayor

Paul Haverkamp was appointed mayor in September 2026. He owned a local insurance agency for 20 years and served on the Defiance Planning Commission.
