# Defiance City Update

**City of Defiance, Ohio | October 2026**
Published October 23, 2026 | cityofdefiance.com | City Building, 631 Perry Street | (419) 784-2101

## A Message from Mayor Paul Haverkamp

Dear neighbors,

It has been a little over a month since I took the oath of office, and I want to thank everyone who has called, written, or stopped me at the hardware store with an idea or a question. I've heard a lot of good ideas. Please keep them coming.

In my first weeks I sat down with every city department, chaired my first Board of Control meetings and attended my first City Council meetings. I also got out of the building: to the Senior Center, the Habitat for Humanity build in the Fourth Ward, our schools, the library, and Harvest on the Riverfront, where I think I saw half the town.

This fall is about getting the basics done before winter. Our paving crews resurfaced about three miles of streets in October. Our hydrant crews flushed every hydrant in the city. Leaf collection starts Monday, October 26, and the Street Department has its salt and brine supplies lined up for the first snow.

Council is also working on the 2027 budget. Budget meetings are open to the public, and I'd encourage you to come and listen.

My door at the City Building is open, and my email is mayor@cityofdefiance.com.

Sincerely,
Paul Haverkamp
Mayor

## Leaf Collection Begins October 26

The Street Department's leaf vacuum trucks start their rounds on **Monday, October 26** and continue through **Friday, December 4**, weather permitting. Crews make three passes through the city. Each pass goes ward by ward in the same order: First, Second, Third, then Fourth Ward.

| Pass | Dates |
|---|---|
| Pass 1 | October 26 - November 6 |
| Pass 2 | November 9 - November 20 |
| Pass 3 | November 23 - December 4 |

There is no collection on Veterans Day (Wednesday, November 11) or on Thanksgiving Day and the day after.

Crews work street by street, so we can't give a specific day for each street. Not sure which ward you live in? The ward map is on the city website.

**Please:**
- Rake leaves to the tree lawn, not into the street.
- Leaves only. No bags, brush, grass clippings or pumpkins. Crews will skip mixed piles.
- Don't park on or next to leaf piles.

**Bagged leaves and brush** can be dropped off at the compost site on Island Park Avenue on Saturdays from 8 a.m. to noon through November 21. Residents only; bring a utility bill or driver's license.

The 2026 resurfacing program is nearly finished. As of this writing:

- **Holgate Avenue** (Fifth to Pontiac): paved October 12-13.

- **Hopkins Street** (Clinton to Wayne): paved October 15.

- **Elliott Road** (Domersville to the corporation limit): patching, milling and paving the week of October 19.

## Fire Prevention Week and Free Smoke Alarms

Thank you to everyone who visited the Station 1 open house on October 10. Fire Prevention Bureau staff talked with families about escape plans, and children tried out the county's smoke house trailer.

The Fire Division's **free home smoke alarm program** installs alarms in single-family homes for homeowners and renters. Firefighters test the alarms and help each family make an escape plan. More than 300 homes have been served so far this year.

**Residents 60 and older** can sign up for a free alarm, a File of Life packet and other services through the city's Senior Services Initiative. Call Luis Ortega in the Mayor's Office at (419) 784-2101, ext. 207. Twenty-three residents signed up for alarms at the initiative's kickoff on October 14 alone.

**Reminder:** When you set your clocks back on Sunday, November 1, test your smoke alarms.

## Getting Ready for Winter

The Street Department has its supplies lined up for the 2026-2027 snow season:

- **Road salt:** The city will buy 1,400 tons through the state purchasing contract at $71.84 per ton, down from $78.20 last season. The salt dome on Carter Road is already about half full.
- **Brine:** Crews pre-treat main streets with salt brine before storms. The city buys liquid calcium chloride through the state co-op at $1.06 per gallon, also down from last year.

**Snow emergency routes** are marked with red and white signs. When a snow emergency is declared, vehicles must be moved off these streets so plows can clear them curb to curb.

## Your Water

- **Hydrant flushing is complete.** Water Division crews flushed all 1,412 city hydrants between September 14 and October 2. If you still notice discolored water, run your cold tap for several minutes and call the Water Division at ext. 260 if it doesn't clear.
- **Water Treatment Plant improvements:** Filters 3 and 4 went back into service on September 29 after rehabilitation. Work on the north clearwell continues through December.
