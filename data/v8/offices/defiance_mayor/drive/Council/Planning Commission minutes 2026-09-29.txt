3. ZONING MAP AMENDMENT Z-26-04: 1536 AND 1542 NORTH CLINTON STREET, R-2 TO O-1
Applicant/owner: Hartwell Dental Group, Inc.

Mr. Kessling presented the staff report. The applicant owns its dental office at 1530 North Clinton Street, zoned O-1, and has acquired the two single-family lots immediately to the north, 1536 and 1542 North Clinton, each about 0.21 acre and zoned R-2. The applicant proposes to remove the two houses, build an addition of about 2,400 square feet to the existing office, and add 22 parking spaces. The O-1 district permits medical and dental offices. Staff found the request consistent with the 2019 Comprehensive Plan, which shows the west side of North Clinton Street from Third Street north to the bridge approach as "Mixed Office/Residential Transition." Staff recommended approval, with the site plan (item 4) to address buffering.

Dr. Brian Hartwell, 2215 Riverview Drive, spoke for the applicant. He said the practice has seven dentists and hygienists and 24 staff, and has outgrown the building it moved into in 2008. Patients currently park on the street. He said the practice preferred to stay on North Clinton rather than build on the edge of town, and had bought the two houses from willing sellers over the past two years.

Chair Kretzer opened the floor to the public.

Marilyn Voigt, 1548 North Clinton Street, said she lives immediately north of 1542. She said the dental office had always been a good neighbor but she was worried about car headlights and parking lot lights shining into her kitchen and back yard. She asked for a solid fence, not a chain-link fence with slats.

Robert Gaffney, 615 Third Street, asked whether rezoning the lots would allow "any business at all" to go there if the dental office ever left. Mr. Kessling said the O-1 district permits offices of all kinds, medical and dental clinics, banks without drive-throughs, and a few similar uses, but not retail, restaurants or auto-oriented uses.

No one else wished to speak.

Commissioner Rupright said a professional office was an appropriate transition between Clinton Street and the residential blocks behind it. Commissioner Beining asked whether staff had considered a conditional rezoning. Mr. Cline said Ohio law does not generally allow conditions to be attached to a rezoning itself, but conditions can and should be attached to the site plan, which is how the Commission protects neighbors.

Motion by Rupright, second by Beining, to recommend to Council approval of Z-26-04, rezoning 1536 and 1542 North Clinton Street from R-2 to O-1. Ayes: Kretzer, Rupright, Beining, Brinkman. Nays: none. Motion carried, 4-0.

4. SITE PLAN SP-26-11: HARTWELL DENTAL GROUP OFFICE ADDITION AND PARKING, 1530-1542 NORTH CLINTON STREET (contingent on Z-26-04)

Mr. Kessling and Ms. Dunlap reviewed the site plan. The addition is single-story on the north side of the existing building. Access remains from the existing North Clinton Street drive; the two residential driveways would be removed and the curb restored. Stormwater would be detained in an underground chamber and released to the North Clinton Street storm sewer at the pre-development rate. Engineering had two comments: the detention calculations should use the city's current rainfall data, and the sidewalk across the two closed driveways must be replaced to city standards.

Commissioner Beining asked about lighting. The applicant's engineer said the plan uses LED fixtures on 16-foot poles with full-cutoff shields. Commissioner Brinkman asked for a lighting limit at the property line.

Motion by Brinkman, second by Rupright, to approve SP-26-11, contingent on Council's approval of Z-26-04, with the following conditions:
(a) A six-foot solid wood or vinyl privacy fence along the north and east property lines of 1542 North Clinton and the east line of 1536 North Clinton;
(b) Landscaping in accordance with Chapter 1171, including a row of evergreen shrubs inside the fence;
(c) Full-cutoff parking lot fixtures not exceeding 16 feet in height, with light levels not exceeding 0.5 foot-candle at the residential property lines, and lights on a timer to turn off by 9:00 p.m. except for security lighting at the building;
(d) Revised stormwater calculations to the City Engineer's satisfaction;
(e) Sidewalk replacement across the two closed driveways to city standards.
Ayes: Kretzer, Rupright, Beining, Brinkman. Nays: none. Motion carried, 4-0.

5. SITE PLAN SP-26-09: NORTHTOWNE OUTLOT B, RETAIL BUILDING (9,200 SQUARE FEET)
Applicant: Northtowne Outlot Partners LLC; engineer: Ridgeway Civil Engineering, Napoleon

Mr. Kessling reported that the property is zoned B-3 General Business and the proposed use, a multi-tenant retail building with three tenant spaces, is permitted. The outlot is part of the Northtowne commercial development and shares access drives with the main center.

Ms. Dunlap said Engineering completed its review on September 25. Comments had been addressed except for: (a) a cross-access easement to the adjacent outlot to the east, which the applicant has agreed to record; (b) a sidewalk along the frontage on the public street, which the applicant had initially proposed to omit; and (c) a final stormwater maintenance agreement. She said the outlot drains to the existing Northtowne regional detention basin, which has capacity for this site.

Greg Puhl of Ridgeway Civil Engineering, for the applicant, said the owner had agreed to build the sidewalk. He said tenants have not been announced, but one space is expected to be a restaurant without a drive-through. Commissioner Rupright asked about truck deliveries. Mr. Puhl said deliveries would use the rear drive and that the dumpster enclosure is masonry to match the building.

Commissioner Beining asked how many parking spaces were provided. Mr. Kessling said 46, which exceeds the code requirement of 37 for retail of this size; if a restaurant occupies one unit, the shared parking agreement with the main center covers any shortfall.

Motion by Beining, second by Brinkman, to approve SP-26-09 with the conditions that (a) a cross-access easement be recorded before a certificate of occupancy is issued; (b) a five-foot concrete sidewalk be built along the full frontage; and (c) a stormwater maintenance agreement be executed and recorded. Ayes: Kretzer, Rupright, Beining, Brinkman. Nays: none. Motion carried, 4-0.

7. DISCUSSION: GROUND-MOUNTED SOLAR IN RESIDENTIAL DISTRICTS

Mr. Kessling said Building and Zoning had received five inquiries this year about ground-mounted solar arrays in back yards. The Planning and Zoning Code addresses roof-mounted panels as part of the building, but does not address ground-mounted arrays, so staff has been treating them as accessory structures. He distributed examples from four other Ohio cities. Common provisions include: rear yard only; maximum height of 12 to 15 feet; setbacks equal to those for accessory buildings; a cap on the area as a percentage of the rear yard; and screening when visible from the street.

Commissioners discussed glare, setbacks on corner lots, and whether the area of an array should count toward lot coverage. Commissioner Brinkman suggested looking at battery storage at the same time. Chair Kretzer asked staff to prepare draft language for the November meeting.

No action.
