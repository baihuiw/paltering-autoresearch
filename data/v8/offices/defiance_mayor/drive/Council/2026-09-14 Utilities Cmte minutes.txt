4. BIOSOLIDS FIELD LEASE, DOMERSVILLE ROAD (CROP YEARS 2027-2029)

Mr. Shaffer reviewed the lease of the city's 41-acre biosolids application field on Domersville Road. The city applies Class B biosolids from the Water Pollution Control Plant to the field under its Ohio EPA permit, typically in late summer after wheat harvest and in early spring before planting. The field is farmed by a tenant who keeps the crop. The current three-year lease expires December 31, 2026.

The city requested sealed proposals in August, with a minimum of $120 per acre per year. Two proposals were received on August 28:

- Dale Wittenmyer: $142 per acre per year ($5,822 per year)
- Second proposer: $131 per acre per year ($5,371 per year)

Mr. Shaffer recommended the high proposal. Mr. Wittenmyer has farmed the field since 2018 and has followed the application schedule without problems. Hagans said fall application on the field would start after soybean harvest, likely in mid-October, and that Mr. Wittenmyer has been "easy to work with on timing."

Councilwoman Okuley asked whether the rent goes to the Sewer Fund. Ms. Marquardt said yes.

Mr. Wittenmyer said he appreciated the opportunity to continue and had no questions.

The lease draft prepared by the Law Director includes: a term of January 1, 2027 to December 31, 2029; rent payable in two installments (April 1 and November 1); a requirement to coordinate planting and harvest with the city's application schedule; a prohibition on grazing and on food crops for direct human consumption, consistent with biosolids rules; the city's right to terminate on 90 days' notice if the field is needed for plant purposes; and liability insurance of at least $1 million.

Motion by Okuley, second by Gaskins, to recommend that Council adopt an ordinance authorizing the lease to Dale Wittenmyer at $142 per acre per year for crop years 2027 through 2029. Vote: 3-0. The Clerk will assign an ordinance number for first reading September 22. [Clerk's note: meeting since moved to September 23.]

5. ANNUAL TREATMENT CHEMICALS, NOVEMBER 1, 2026 - OCTOBER 31, 2027

Mr. Shaffer reviewed the bid specifications for the annual supply of liquid sodium hypochlorite (12.5 percent) for disinfection at the Water Treatment Plant and the WPCF, estimated at 46,000 gallons, and ferric chloride for phosphorus removal at the WPCF, estimated at 210 dry tons. Bids will be opened September 30 and awarded by the Board of Control in October.

Prices in the current contracts are $1.79 per gallon for sodium hypochlorite and $596 per dry ton for ferric chloride. Mr. Shaffer said suppliers have told him to expect modest increases. He estimated next year's chemical budget at about $215,000 for the two products combined, compared with about $206,000 this year. He noted that the current Water Treatment Plant chemicals line will be short about $9,500 before the end of October because of higher usage while two filters were out of service for the rehabilitation project. Ms. Marquardt said she would include a transfer in the next appropriation ordinance.

Councilwoman Gaskins asked how many suppliers had been sent the bid package. Mr. Shaffer said five, including the three that bid last year.

No action required.

6. WATER TREATMENT PLANT FILTER REHABILITATION AND CLEARWELL IMPROVEMENTS: STATUS

Ms. Dunlap reported that the project is a little over half complete by value. Underdrain and media replacement in Filters 3 and 4 is nearly done, and the new air scour blower has been delivered. She expects Filters 3 and 4 to return to service by the end of September, after which the contractor will move into the north clearwell, which will be out of service through December. Filters 1 and 2 will be rehabilitated from January through April 2027. Substantial completion remains May 14, 2027.

Change orders to date are $31,480, about 1.1 percent of the $2,846,000 contract. The largest was for additional concrete repair in the Filter 4 gullet once the old underdrain was removed.

Mr. Shaffer said that while two filters are out, the plant is producing about 2.4 million gallons per day on average, well within the capacity of the two remaining filters, and that he does not expect any service interruption.

7. WPCF: CLARIFIER NO. 2 DRIVE; NPDES PERMIT RENEWAL

Hagans reported that at the September 9 oil change on the Secondary Clarifier No. 2 drive, the drained oil showed bronze particles from gear wear, and staff found a crack at one mounting ear on the gearbox housing. The torque alarm setpoint has been lowered and the drive is running at reduced torque, with the sludge blanket checked twice per shift. Clarifier No. 1 carries average flow without trouble; wet-weather peaks are being handled with step-feed. The manufacturer's representative has quoted a replacement gearbox at $41,780 with about a nine-week lead time. Mr. Shaffer said he will take it to the Board of Control at the end of the month. Installation would be done by plant staff with the manufacturer's representative, with the clarifier drained for about three days.

Councilwoman Gaskins asked what happens if the gearbox fails before the new one arrives. Mr. Shaffer said the plant can meet its limits on one clarifier at normal flows, and that he has asked operators to call him for any forecast of more than 1.5 inches of rain.

Mr. Shaffer said the plant's NPDES permit renewal application is being assembled, and he expects a draft to go to the Safety-Service Director in October. He did not expect significant new limits.
