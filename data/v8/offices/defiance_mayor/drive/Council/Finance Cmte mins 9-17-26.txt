DEFIANCE CITY COUNCIL
FINANCE COMMITTEE
Minutes
Thursday, September 17, 2026, 5:00 p.m.
Conference Room B, City Building

Present: Nancy Ruhl, At-Large (Chair); Kyle Brinkman, At-Large; Rita Okuley, Second Ward
Staff: Angela Marquardt, Finance Director; Tina Speiser, Accounts Payable (item 5); Steven Cline, Law Director (items 4-5); Sandra Moll, Clerk of Council (recording)
Also present: Mayor Paul Haverkamp (items 1-3)
Public: none

Note: Councilman Brinkman arrived at 5:20 p.m. following the OML regional meeting in Findlay. Items 1-2 were taken with two members present for discussion only; no votes were taken until he arrived.

1. Welcome

Chair Ruhl welcomed Mayor Haverkamp, who said he had asked to sit in on his first Finance Committee meeting to "hear how the numbers are presented to Council." He said he expected the 2027 budget to be tight and that he would ask department heads to hold operating requests flat where possible.

2. August 2026 financial report

Ms. Marquardt reviewed the August report.

General Fund, through August 31 (66.7 percent of the year):
- Revenue: $9,792,366, 66.7 percent of the annual estimate of $14,680,000.
- Expenditures: $9,560,215, 63.8 percent of appropriations of $14,980,000.
- Cash balance at August 31: $5,516,269, up $232,151 from January 1. Unencumbered: $4,903,788.

Municipal income tax (all funds, net of refunds): $9,612,480 through August, up 2.9 percent from 2025 and about 1.4 percent above the year-to-date estimate. Withholding, about three-quarters of collections, is up 3.4 percent; net profit payments are flat. Income tax is distributed 80 percent to the General Fund and 20 percent to the Capital Improvement Fund.

Other revenue: investment income is at 73.5 percent of the estimate, although Ms. Marquardt cautioned that yields are coming down and she will budget 2027 investment income lower. Licenses, permits and fees are at 74.4 percent. Property tax is at 51.6 percent because the second-half settlement had not yet been received at August 31.

Councilwoman Okuley asked about overtime. Ms. Marquardt said Police and Fire overtime are both running above last year, Fire because of two long-term injury leaves, but are covered within each department's total appropriation for now. If that changes she will bring a transfer in November. Street Department overtime is at 52 percent, which is normal before leaf and snow season.

Special revenue and enterprise funds were reviewed briefly. The Water and Sewer Funds are on budget. The Street Construction, Maintenance and Repair Fund shows materials and supplies at 91 percent of appropriation (see item 4).

3. 2027 budget calendar

Ms. Marquardt presented the proposed calendar:

- September 24: budget memo and workbooks issued to departments
- October 2: operating requests due
- Tuesday, October 6, 6:00 p.m.: Committee of the Whole, session 1 (2026 projections; 2027 revenue outlook; General Government, Police, Fire/EMS)
- October 9: capital requests due to Finance
- Wednesday, October 21, 6:00 p.m.: Committee of the Whole, session 2 (remaining departments; capital plan)
- October 30: Mayor's recommended budget final
- November 10: first reading, 2027 annual appropriation ordinance
- November 24, 6:30 p.m.: public hearing on the budget; second reading
- December 8: third reading and adoption

She noted that Ohio law allows a temporary appropriation through March 31 if Council wants more time, but that the city has adopted a permanent budget in December for each of the last six years. Councilwoman Okuley asked whether the second session could be moved to a Tuesday, since Wednesday evenings conflict with church and school activities. Ms. Marquardt said she would look at Tuesday, October 20 and confirm with the Mayor. Otherwise the committee agreed with the calendar by consensus. [Clerk's note: second session subsequently set for Tuesday, October 20.]

Mayor Haverkamp asked that the Capital Improvement Plan 2027-2031 working draft be shared with Council before October 20. Ms. Marquardt said Engineering is updating it and it would be in the October 20 packet.

4. Proposed Ordinance No. 2026-42, appropriation amendments

Ms. Marquardt reviewed four items for an appropriation ordinance for the September 23 meeting:

a. General Fund, Council - legal advertising: +$4,000 from contingency. Publication costs for ordinance summaries and the special meeting notices this month have exceeded the $9,000 budget. Year to date: $8,740.
b. EMS Capital Fund: BWC safety intervention grant of $34,206 (receipts) and an additional appropriation of $68,412 for two powered stretcher and load systems for Medic 2 and Medic 3. The grant is a 50/50 match; the city's half comes from the EMS Capital Fund balance. The purchase itself goes to the Board of Control on September 28.
c. SCMR Fund: transfer $12,500 from contractual services to materials and supplies (hot mix, cold patch). Contractual services has a balance because the crack sealing contract came in under estimate.
d. Water Fund: transfer $9,500 from WTP contractual services to WTP chemicals, per the Utilities Superintendent (reported to the Utilities Committee on September 14).

Councilman Brinkman (arrived) asked why the stretchers needed an emergency clause. Ms. Marquardt said the grant agreement requires the purchase order to be issued by October 15. Mr. Cline confirmed.

Motion by Okuley, second by Brinkman, to recommend Ordinance No. 2026-42 to Council with an emergency clause. Vote: 3-0.

5. Proposed Resolution No. 2026-15, certification of weed and rubbish abatement charges

Ms. Speiser presented the list of 38 parcels with unpaid charges for weed cutting and rubbish removal performed May through August 2026. Total: $11,846.00, consisting of $9,946.00 in contractor charges and $1,900.00 in the existing $50 per-parcel administrative charge. Every owner was invoiced within 30 days of the work and sent a second notice in August. Seven parcels paid after the second notice and have been removed from the list.

By ward: First 9; Second 11; Third 8; Fourth 10.

Mr. Cline said that the new Chapter 1355 (Ordinance No. 2026-35), if passed, would raise the administrative fee to $150 for future abatements but would not apply to these charges.

Motion by Brinkman, second by Okuley, to recommend Resolution No. 2026-15 to Council with an emergency clause, so that it can be certified to the County Auditor before October 1. Vote: 3-0.

6. State audit

Ms. Marquardt reported that the Auditor of State's regular audit of the city's 2025 financial statements is in its final stage. Fieldwork ended August 28. She expects the report to be released in November, and is not aware of any findings. The exit conference will be scheduled with the mayor, the Finance Committee chair and the President of Council.

7. Other

Councilwoman Okuley asked whether the city's cybersecurity insurance renewal would be affected by the requirements discussed at the OML meeting. Councilman Brinkman said he had brought back the materials and would share them with Ms. Marquardt and Ben Rohrs in IT. Ms. Marquardt said the renewal is in January and that multifactor authentication on all city email accounts was completed in the spring.

8. Adjournment

The meeting adjourned at 6:08 p.m. The next meeting will be scheduled after the budget sessions, likely in November.
