CITY OF DEFIANCE, OHIO
MINUTES OF THE REGULAR MEETING OF CITY COUNCIL
Tuesday, October 13, 2026
Council Chambers, City Building, 631 Perry Street

CALL TO ORDER

President of Council Gary Lehman called the regular meeting to order at 7:01 p.m. He led the Pledge of Allegiance, followed by a moment of silence.

ROLL CALL

Present: Dan Pfahler (First Ward), Rita Okuley (Second Ward), Jo Ellen Sprow (Third Ward), Marcus Bell (Fourth Ward), Nancy Ruhl (At-Large), Kyle Brinkman (At-Large), Theresa Gaskins (At-Large). Seven members present. President Lehman presiding.

Also present: Mayor Paul Haverkamp; Megan Ostrander, Chief of Staff; Steven Cline, Law Director; Angela Marquardt, Finance Director; Tom Wiechers, Safety-Service Director; Rachel Dunlap, City Engineer; Dale Rummel, Fire Chief; Craig Fenstermaker, Parks and Recreation Director; Ron Diehl, Street Superintendent; Sandra Moll, Clerk of Council. Approximately 26 members of the public and one member of the press.

APPROVAL OF MINUTES

The Clerk noted one correction to the draft minutes of September 23 as distributed: in the Fire Chief's report, the number of calls was a partial September figure through September 22 (301), and the text has been revised to say so. Chief Rummel confirmed the correction.

Motion by Ruhl, second by Gaskins, to approve the minutes of the regular meeting of September 23, 2026 as corrected.
Yeas: Pfahler, Okuley, Sprow, Bell, Ruhl, Brinkman, Gaskins. Nays: none. Motion carried, 7-0.

RECOGNITION

Mayor Haverkamp recognized Gene Wolfrum of the Street Department, who retires October 30 after 31 years with the city. Street Superintendent Diehl said Mr. Wolfrum had plowed the same downtown route for most of his career and had trained "about half the people in the department." The mayor presented Mr. Wolfrum with a certificate and a framed photograph of the Street Department crew. Mr. Wolfrum thanked his family and coworkers and said he would "still be watching the plow routes from my front window."

COMMUNICATIONS

a. The Clerk noted a notice from ODOT District 1 regarding railroad crossing work on Hopkins Street, October 12-14, with a detour by way of Second Street and Holgate Avenue. The notice is on file.

b. The Clerk read a letter from the Downtown merchants' group offering $2,500 toward downtown holiday lighting (see Resolution No. 2026-16).

PUBLIC COMMENT

Brian Kellermeyer, 1611 Hopkins Street, said the Hopkins Street railroad crossing closure had been hard on residents on the east end of the street, because the detour by way of Second Street and Holgate Avenue added "ten minutes each way." He said he understood the work was done by the railroad, not the city, but asked whether the city could get more notice next time. He said the signs went up Saturday afternoon for a Monday closure. Safety-Service Director Wiechers said the city received the railroad's final dates late in the prior week and posted notice the same day it was confirmed. He said the railroad expects to reopen the crossing Wednesday evening, and the city would ask for a longer notice period in the future. Mayor Haverkamp apologized for the short notice.

Marilyn Voigt, 1548 North Clinton Street, spoke about Ordinance No. 2026-44. She lives next door to one of the two lots proposed for rezoning. She said she did not oppose the dental office, which "has been a good neighbor," but was concerned about headlights and parking lot lights shining into her back yard. She asked Council to require a solid fence and shielded lights. Councilman Brinkman said the Planning Commission had recommended both a six-foot solid fence along the residential lot lines and full-cutoff lighting as conditions of site plan approval. Law Director Cline said those conditions would be enforced through the site plan, and that the public hearing on November 10 would be another opportunity to comment.

Carl Hoenie, 902 Riverview Drive, asked when leaf collection would start and whether the city would come to each street more than once. Mr. Wiechers said curbside collection begins Monday, October 26 and that crews make three passes through the city, each pass going First, Second, Third and then Fourth Ward. The schedule would be posted on the city website and Facebook by October 19. He asked residents to rake leaves to the curb but not into the street, and not to mix in brush.

Teresa Vance, 520 Arabella Street, a vendor at Harvest on the Riverfront, thanked the city and the Parks Department for the event. She said it was the best-attended year she could remember. She asked whether vendors could have access to electric hookups next year. Parks Director Fenstermaker said he would look at the cost of temporary power.

No one else wished to speak. Public comment closed at 7:24 p.m.

REPORT OF THE MAYOR

Mayor Haverkamp reported:

- Harvest on the Riverfront was held Saturday, October 10 in Kingsbury Park in good weather. Parks staff estimated attendance at about 6,000 over the day. He thanked Parks, the Street Department, the Fire Department and the volunteers. The Pumpkin Run 5K at Pontiac Park that morning had 214 registered runners.
- Fire Prevention Week concluded with the Station 1 open house on October 10. He thanked Lt. Mayes and the firefighters' association.
- The Senior Services Initiative kickoff is tomorrow, Wednesday, October 14, at 11:30 a.m. here in Council Chambers. Members of Council are welcome. About 58 people have registered and lunch will be provided.
- Ladder 1 has been out of service since September 24 because of a failed main lift cylinder. The Board of Control approved the repair on October 5 at a cost of $18,400. The truck left for the repair shop in Toledo this morning and is expected back in early November. In the meantime, neighboring departments provide aerial coverage under existing mutual aid agreements. Ordinance No. 2026-45 appropriates the repair cost.
- Fall paving is underway. Holgate Avenue was milled October 5-6 and paved yesterday and today. Jefferson Avenue was milled October 7 and Hopkins Street on October 8. Washington Avenue was milled October 9.
- The Hammond Street Park restroom is expected to be complete by October 23. A reopening is tentatively planned for Monday, October 26 at 1:30 p.m. The portable toilets will stay until then.
- He has signed a proclamation setting trick-or-treat for Saturday, October 31, from 5:30 to 7:30 p.m. He asked drivers to slow down that evening and asked residents who are handing out treats to turn on their porch lights.
- The riverfront tree-planting volunteer day is Saturday, October 17, from 9 a.m. to noon, starting at the Riverside Park boat ramp lot.
- He has appointed Linda Hohenberger to the Planning Commission seat he vacated when he became mayor and asked Council to confirm the appointment (Resolution No. 2026-17).
- He reminded members of the fire and EMS levy renewal work session on October 27 at 5:30 p.m., before the regular meeting. He said the administration would present the levy as a renewal, with no increase in the rate.
- City offices will be closed Wednesday, November 11 for Veterans Day.

REPORTS OF THE ADMINISTRATION

a. Finance Director. Ms. Marquardt reported that municipal income tax collections through September totaled $10,760,696, 2.8 percent above the same period in 2025. The September financial report will go to the Finance Committee with the November packet. She said 31 capital requests were received by the October 9 deadline and two more on October 12. As usual, the total requested is well over what the city can fund, and the administration will bring a recommended list to the Committee of the Whole on October 20. She summarized Ordinance No. 2026-45 (see Legislation).

b. Safety-Service Director. Mr. Wiechers reported:
- Fall hydrant flushing was completed October 2. Crews flushed 1,412 hydrants and identified 9 hydrants needing repair, 6 of which have been repaired.
- The Board of Control on October 12 approved the purchase of liquid calcium chloride for this winter at $1.06 per gallon under the ODOT cooperative contract, approved repairs to leaf vacuum No. 2, and authorized up to four seasonal laborers for leaf season.
- Remaining paving: Jefferson Avenue is to be paved October 14, Hopkins Street October 15 and Washington Avenue October 16. Elliott Road patching, milling and paving and the Summit Street overlay are scheduled for the week of October 19.
- The Ralston Avenue punch list was completed September 29 and the final pay request will come to the Board of Control. The final contract amount is $357,512.40, compared with the $364,800 contract.

c. City Engineer. Ms. Dunlap reported:
- Filters 3 and 4 at the Water Treatment Plant returned to service September 29. The filter and clearwell project is about 58 percent complete and remains on schedule for substantial completion in May 2027. The Board of Control approved Pay Application No. 5 on October 12.
- The Washington Avenue waterline project had a Change Order No. 2 for $8,420, approved by the Board of Control on October 12, to remove an abandoned 4-inch cast iron line found near Third Street.
- The Corps of Engineers permit application for the Third Street storm outfall was submitted October 8.
- The Harding Street culvert replacement will be advertised November 5, with bids opened November 24.
- Engineering's review of the Northtowne outlot retail site plan is complete; the Planning Commission approved the site plan September 29 with conditions.

d. Fire Chief. Chief Rummel reported that the department answered 392 runs in September, 284 of them EMS, bringing the year-to-date total to 3,447. The 90th-percentile response time for September was 8 minutes 40 seconds. The Fire Prevention Bureau completed 44 inspections in September, 361 for the year. The home smoke alarm program installed 47 alarms in September, 312 so far this year. He thanked Council for its support of the Ladder 1 repair.

e. Law Director. Mr. Cline reported that Ordinance No. 2026-35 (nuisance abatement) took effect October 24 [sic; see note] and that code enforcement had begun using the new notice forms. [Clerk's note for draft: Mr. Cline's report should read that the ordinance "will take effect October 24," 30 days after the mayor's approval on September 24. To be confirmed with the Law Director before approval.]

REPORTS OF COMMITTEES

a. Committee of the Whole. President Lehman reported that the Committee of the Whole met October 6 for the first 2027 budget session. The Finance Director presented the preliminary General Fund revenue estimate and department operating requests. The second session is October 20 at 6:00 p.m.

b. Safety Committee. Chair Gaskins presented the minutes of the October 7 meeting. The committee recommended passage of Ordinance No. 2026-43, reviewed the Ladder 1 repair and the mutual aid coverage plan, and reviewed the outline for the October 27 levy work session.

The committee recommended acceptance of the holiday lighting donation and reviewed the Hammond Street Park restroom schedule and the Riverside Park shelter roof replacement, which the Board of Control awarded October 12.

f. Streets and Sidewalks Committee. Chair Sprow said the committee meets Thursday, October 15 at 5:30 p.m. in Conference Room B. The agenda includes sidewalk snow clearing on school walking routes, as Councilman Bell requested, the 2027 sidewalk cost-share program, and the 2027 resurfacing candidate list.

g. Planning Commission. Councilman Brinkman reported that the Commission met September 29. It recommended approval of the rezoning in Ordinance No. 2026-44 by a 4-0 vote, approved the Northtowne outlot site plan with conditions, approved a lot split on Elliott Road, and began discussion of regulations for ground-mounted solar panels in residential districts. Mayor Haverkamp attended as an observer.

LEGISLATION

THIRD READING

Ordinance No. 2026-39. An ordinance vacating a portion of the 16-foot alley between Jackson Avenue and Agnes Street, from Seventh Street to Eighth Street, upon petition of all abutting owners, and reserving a utility easement.

The Clerk read the ordinance by title. Councilwoman Sprow asked whether the abutting owners would now be responsible for the alley. Law Director Cline said title to the vacated alley passes to the abutting owners, each to the centerline, and that they take it subject to the utility easement. The owners will be responsible for maintenance. City Engineer Dunlap said the County Auditor and Recorder would be sent a certified copy and the plat.

Motion by Sprow, second by Bell, to adopt Ordinance No. 2026-39.
Yeas: Pfahler, Okuley, Sprow, Bell, Ruhl, Brinkman, Gaskins. Nays: none. Ordinance No. 2026-39 passed, 7-0.

SECOND READING

Ordinance No. 2026-40. An ordinance authorizing the Mayor to enter into a lease of approximately 41 acres of city-owned land on Domersville Road for agricultural use for crop years 2027 through 2029.

The Clerk read the ordinance by title. Law Director Cline noted that the Board of Control approved the lease renewal on September 28, and that leases of three years or less in the city's standard form are within the Board's authority without Council action. He said the ordinance had been introduced on the Utilities Committee's recommendation before the Board acted, that it is no longer needed, and he advised that it be withdrawn. Councilman Pfahler, as sponsoring committee chair, asked whether the lease terms the committee reviewed were the ones signed. Mr. Cline confirmed they were. Councilman Pfahler withdrew the ordinance on behalf of the Utilities Committee. There was no objection. Ordinance No. 2026-40 was withdrawn.

Ordinance No. 2026-43. An ordinance authorizing the Board of Control to enter into a three-year contract for emergency medical services billing and collection services.

The Clerk read the ordinance by title. Safety Committee Chair Gaskins said the committee had reviewed the proposals with the Fire Chief and Lt. Sanderson on October 7 and recommended passage. Councilman Bell asked whether the new vendor, at 6.75 percent of net collections, would provide the department with monthly reports. Chief Rummel said yes, including collection rates by payer type, which the current vendor does not provide in a usable form. The ordinance will be placed on third reading October 27.

FIRST READING

Ordinance No. 2026-44. An ordinance amending the Zoning District Map of the City of Defiance to rezone 1536 and 1542 North Clinton Street from R-2 Single-Family Residential to O-1 Office District.

The Clerk read the ordinance by title. Councilman Brinkman summarized the Planning Commission's recommendation. The applicant, Hartwell Dental Group, owns both parcels and its existing office at 1530 North Clinton Street, which is already zoned O-1. The two houses would be removed for an addition of about 2,400 square feet and 22 parking spaces. The Commission recommended approval 4-0. Law Director Cline said a public hearing is required before Council acts on a zoning map amendment, with notice published at least 30 days in advance.

The ordinance will be held for the public hearing (see New Business).

Ordinance No. 2026-45. An ordinance amending the 2026 annual appropriation ordinance to provide for additional appropriations and transfers within the Fire and EMS Levy Fund, the Street Construction, Maintenance and Repair Fund and the Capital Improvement Fund, and declaring an emergency.

The Clerk read the ordinance by title. Finance Director Marquardt summarized it:

1. Fire/EMS Levy Fund: an additional appropriation of $18,400 to apparatus maintenance for the Ladder 1 lift cylinder repair, from the fund's unappropriated balance. The repair shop requires a purchase order before scheduling the work.
2. Street Construction, Maintenance and Repair Fund: an additional appropriation of $9,860 for the leaf vacuum No. 2 repair and $14,600 for seasonal leaf laborers, plus the employer share on those wages, from the fund's unappropriated balance.
3. Capital Improvement Fund: receipt of insurance proceeds of $18,800 for the August storm damage to the Riverside Park shelter house, and an appropriation of $23,800 for the roof replacement. The city's $5,000 deductible is covered by a transfer within the Parks capital lines.

Councilman Pfahler asked whether the fire levy fund could absorb the Ladder 1 repair without affecting the Engine 2 replacement planned for 2028. Ms. Marquardt said yes; the repair is about 2 percent of the fund's projected year-end balance. Councilwoman Gaskins noted that Ladder 1 is 19 years old and asked that the levy work session include the apparatus replacement schedule. Chief Rummel said it would.

Motion by Ruhl, second by Gaskins, to suspend the rule requiring an ordinance to be read on three separate days.
Yeas: Pfahler, Okuley, Sprow, Bell, Ruhl, Brinkman, Gaskins. Nays: none. Motion carried, 7-0.

Motion by Ruhl, second by Pfahler, to adopt Ordinance No. 2026-45 as an emergency measure.
Yeas: Pfahler, Okuley, Sprow, Bell, Ruhl, Brinkman, Gaskins. Nays: none. Ordinance No. 2026-45 passed as an emergency measure, 7-0.
