3. ORDINANCE NO. 2026-43 - EMS BILLING AND COLLECTION SERVICES, 2027-2029

Chief Rummel and Lt. Sanderson reviewed the request for proposals and the review team's scoring.

RFP issued: July 13, 2026. Proposals due: August 14, 2026. Four proposals received.

Proposer (city)                              Fee (% of net collections)   Score (of 100)
Ameridale Revenue Services, LLC (Findlay)     6.75%                        88
Current vendor (Columbus)                     8.50%                        79
Proposer C (Toledo)                           7.25%                        74
Proposer D (Indianapolis)                     6.40% + $4.50 per claim      66

Review team: Chief Rummel, Finance Director Marquardt, Lt. Sanderson.

Lt. Sanderson said Proposer D's percentage looked lowest but the per-claim fee would bring its effective cost to about 8.9 percent at the city's volume of roughly 3,300 billable transports per year. Ameridale scored highest on reporting, on its interface with the department's electronic patient care reporting software, and on references from three Ohio fire departments of similar size.

Ms. Marquardt said 2025 net EMS collections were $598,400, deposited in the Fire/EMS Levy Fund. At 6.75 percent the fee would be about $40,390, compared with $50,860 at the current 8.50 percent, a savings of about $10,470 per year. The 2027 levy fund request carries $54,000 for the billing service, which was prepared before the proposals were scored and can come down.

Councilman Bell asked how a resident would be billed. Chief Rummel said the city bills the patient's insurance (Medicare, Medicaid or private) and, for city residents, accepts the insurance payment as payment in full under the city's existing "soft billing" policy. Non-residents are billed for any balance. That policy is set by Council and does not change with the vendor.

Councilman Pfahler asked about the transition. Lt. Sanderson said the current vendor will continue to work claims for runs through December 31, 2026 for 120 days, and Ameridale will begin with runs on January 1, 2027. Ameridale has proposed a half-day training session for crews in December on documentation.

Motion by Pfahler, second by Bell, to recommend that Council pass Ordinance No. 2026-43 on second and third readings in the regular course. Vote 3-0.

4. LADDER 1 - OUT OF SERVICE; REPAIR AND COVERAGE

Chief Rummel reported that Ladder 1 was taken out of service on September 24 when the main lift cylinder began leaking during the weekly aerial check. No one was hurt and the aerial was not in use on an emergency at the time. The manufacturer's authorized service center in Toledo inspected the truck and recommended replacement of the cylinder seals and rod and inspection of the holding valves. The quote is $18,400. The Board of Control approved the repair on October 5.

The truck is scheduled to go to Toledo on Tuesday, October 13. The shop estimates three weeks, so it should return in early November, followed by the annual third-party aerial test before it goes back in service.

Coverage: under the county mutual aid agreement, an aerial from a neighboring department is dispatched automatically on any reported structure fire in the city. Chief Rummel said there have been two structure fire responses since September 24 and an aerial arrived on both within acceptable time. Engine companies carry ground ladders sufficient for most residential buildings.

Councilman Bell asked how old Ladder 1 is. Chief Rummel said 19 years. Typical service life for an aerial is 20 to 25 years with a mid-life refurbishment, which this truck had in 2017. He said he would include the aerial in the apparatus replacement schedule shown at the levy work session, but that Engine 2 (planned for 2028 at about $890,000) remains the first priority.

Ms. Marquardt said the shop needs a purchase order before it will schedule the work, so she will ask Council on October 13 to appropriate the repair from the Fire/EMS Levy Fund apparatus maintenance line, with suspension of the rules (Ordinance No. 2026-45).

No action.

5. FIRE AND EMS LEVY RENEWAL - WORK SESSION OUTLINE

Chief Rummel and Ms. Marquardt reviewed the outline for Council's work session on Tuesday, October 27 at 5:30 p.m.:

a. What the levy pays for: share of fire and EMS operating costs, apparatus and equipment
b. Call volume trends: 2026 is on pace for about 4,600 runs, compared with about 4,350 in 2025; EMS is about 72 percent of runs
c. Average cost per run, about $1,040
d. Apparatus and equipment replacement schedule, 2027-2031 (Engine 2, SCBA, medic remount, Ladder 1 outlook)
e. Revenue the levy produces at the current rate; County Auditor certification process
f. Timeline options for placing the renewal on the ballot
g. Public information: what the city may and may not do

Ms. Marquardt said the administration's recommendation will be a renewal, with no increase in the rate. Councilwoman Gaskins asked that the presentation use plain language and that the city's informational materials stick to facts. Mr. Wiechers said the Law Director would review all public materials, and that the presentation would be posted with the agenda. The deck is due in final on October 21.

Councilman Pfahler asked for the per-run cost broken out between fire and EMS. Ms. Marquardt said she would try but that many costs are shared.
