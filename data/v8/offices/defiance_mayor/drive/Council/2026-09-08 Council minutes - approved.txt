President Lehman reminded speakers of the three-minute limit and asked them to state their names and addresses for the record.

Walter Brodbeck, 1022 Jefferson Avenue, spoke on Ordinance No. 2026-38. He said he was a retired machinist on a fixed income and that every increase "adds up," noting that his property taxes and his electric bill had also gone up this year. He said he understood the plant needed work but asked why the city could not use some of its income tax money instead. He asked Council to consider a discount for seniors.

Sharon Kemmer, 418 Fifth Street, said she had attended both Utilities Committee meetings on the rate study and thought the committee had done its homework. Mike Doriot, 1210 Holgate Avenue, raised traffic speeds on Holgate Avenue between Fifth Street and Pontiac Street. He said cars regularly pass his house at 40 miles per hour or more in the evenings and asked whether the speed trailer could be placed on Holgate again. He also asked when Holgate would be repaved. Safety-Service Director Wiechers responded that Holgate Avenue from Fifth to Pontiac is the first street in the fall resurfacing contract, with milling planned for early October. Denise Wentz, 715 Seneca Street, thanked the Parks Department and the pool staff for the season. She said her grandchildren were at the pool almost every day and that the lifeguards were "professional and kind." She asked whether the pool could stay open past Labor Day in future years if the weather was warm. Parks Director Fenstermaker said most of the pool staff are high school and college students who are back in school by late August and that staffing is the limiting factor, but that the department would look at a weekend-only extension for 2027.

Ted Nofziger, 2210 Elliott Road, asked when the Elliott Road patching would happen. He said the pavement near the corporation limit "is coming apart in chunks." City Engineer Dunlap said full-depth patching on Elliott Road from Domersville Road to the corporation limit is part of the 2026 resurfacing contract and is expected in mid-October, followed by milling and paving.

No one else wished to speak. Public comment closed at 7:19 p.m.

Ms. Marquardt reported that municipal income tax collections through August totaled $9,612,480, which is 2.9 percent above the same period in 2025 and about 1.4 percent above the budget estimate. Withholding is up 3.4 percent and net profit collections are about flat. The full August financial report will be distributed Friday, September 11. She said the draft 2027 budget calendar would go to the Finance Committee on September 17. She expects Committee of the Whole budget sessions in early and mid-October, with department capital requests due October 9. She also noted that Resolution No. 2026-14 (County Budget Commission amounts and rates) must be adopted tonight to meet the October 1 deadline.

b. Safety-Service Director. Mr. Wiechers reported:
- Ralston Avenue resurfacing, Fifth Street to Elliott Road: base repairs are complete and surface paving is scheduled for Friday, September 11, weather permitting. Ralston will be closed to through traffic from 7 a.m. until the paving crew clears, likely early afternoon. Residents along the street received door hangers last week.

- The Washington Avenue waterline replacement is progressing; the contractor is working between Second and Third Streets.
- Engineering has completed review of the plat for Ordinance No. 2026-39 (alley vacation) and recommends approval.

d. Law Director. Mr. Cline had no report beyond the vacancy memorandum (see New Business) and stated he was available for questions on Ordinance No. 2026-35.

LEGISLATION

THIRD READING

Ordinance No. 2026-34. An ordinance amending Section 351.14 of the Codified Ordinances to prohibit parking on the east side of Agnes Street between Second Street and Third Street.

The Clerk read the ordinance by title. Councilwoman Sprow said residents of the block had asked for the restriction because emergency vehicles and the garbage truck could not pass when both sides were parked. Engineering had measured the street at 26 feet curb to curb.

SECOND READING

Ordinance No. 2026-35. An ordinance repealing and replacing Chapter 1355 of the Codified Ordinances, "Nuisance Abatement."

The Clerk read the ordinance by title. Councilman Pfahler asked whether lowering the grass height limit from 10 inches to 8 inches would mean more notices. Law Director Cline said the larger change is that one notice per growing season would cover the property, so the city would not have to re-notice each time the grass grew back, which should reduce staff time. Councilwoman Gaskins asked that the administrative fee be explained clearly in the notice letter. Mr. Cline said he would provide a draft notice letter before third reading. The ordinance will be placed on third reading September 22.

Ordinance No. 2026-37. An ordinance amending Section 1155.08 of the Planning and Zoning Code, "Fences and Walls."

The Clerk read the ordinance by title and noted the corrected Exhibit A. Councilman Pfahler said he had received two calls from residents who had been told their existing front-yard fences would become nonconforming. Law Director Cline said existing lawfully built fences may remain and be maintained under the nonconforming-use provisions; the new height limits apply only to new fences and replacements of more than 50 percent. Pfahler asked that this be stated in the city's permit handout. The ordinance will be placed on third reading September 22.

Ordinance No. 2026-38. An ordinance amending Sections 1042.03 and 1048.04 of the Codified Ordinances to adjust water and sewer rates and charges effective with bills issued on or after October 1, 2026, and declaring an emergency.

The Clerk read the ordinance by title.

Utilities Committee Chair Pfahler summarized the ordinance. Water and sewer rates would increase 6 percent on bills issued on or after October 1, 2026, the first increase since October 2023. For a typical inside-city residential customer using 9,000 gallons per quarter, the quarterly water and sewer bill would go from $100.00 to $106.00. He said the increase was needed to fund the Water Treatment Plant filter and clearwell project financed by the Ohio Water Development Authority loan, the Water Pollution Control Plant blower and sludge-handling work, and increased chemical and power costs, and to maintain debt service coverage of at least 1.15 times as required by the city's loan agreements.

Utilities Superintendent Shaffer answered questions. In response to Councilwoman Ruhl, he said chemical costs for the two plants were up roughly 30 percent since 2023. In response to Councilman Brinkman, Finance Director Marquardt said the Water and Sewer Funds are enterprise funds and that income tax revenue is not used to pay operating costs of the utilities; doing so would reduce General Fund money for fire, streets and parks. She said a senior discount would require a separate ordinance and a study of its effect on other customers, and that the Utilities Committee could take that up.

Councilman Bell said he supported the plant improvements and did not question that the money was needed, but would have preferred phasing the increase in over two years, 3 percent this year and 3 percent next, to ease the effect on residents on fixed incomes. He said he would vote no for that reason.

Councilwoman Okuley asked that the Division of Utilities include information on the new rates with the October bills and on the city website. Mr. Shaffer said a notice would be printed on the October 1 bill and posted online, and that a news release would go out this week.

Law Director Cline explained that the emergency clause is needed because the new rates must be in effect for the quarterly billing issued October 1. Without suspension of the rules, third reading would fall on September 22 and the ordinance would not take effect until 30 days after passage.

Motion by Pfahler, second by Ruhl, to suspend the rule requiring an ordinance to be read on three separate days.
Yeas: Pfahler, Okuley, Sprow, Ruhl, Brinkman, Gaskins. Nays: Bell. Motion carried, 6-1.

Motion by Pfahler, second by Gaskins, to adopt Ordinance No. 2026-38 as an emergency measure.
Yeas: Pfahler, Okuley, Sprow, Ruhl, Brinkman, Gaskins. Nays: Bell. Ordinance No. 2026-38 passed as an emergency measure, 6-1.

FIRST READING

Ordinance No. 2026-39. An ordinance vacating a portion of the 16-foot north-south alley lying between Jackson Avenue and Agnes Street, from Seventh Street to Eighth Street, upon petition of all abutting owners, and reserving a utility easement.

The Clerk read the ordinance by title. City Engineer Dunlap said the alley segment is about 292 feet long, unimproved gravel, and has not been maintained by the city for at least 20 years. An overhead electric line and a telephone line run along the alley, so the ordinance reserves a full-width utility easement. No city water or sewer lines are in the alley. The Planning Commission recommended approval at its July 28 meeting. The ordinance will be placed on second reading September 22.

NEW BUSINESS

a. Vacancy in the office of mayor. Law Director Cline summarized his memorandum of September 3. Under the Charter, Council fills a vacancy in the office of mayor by appointment for the unexpired term, and the person appointed serves until a successor is elected at the next regular municipal election at which the office can be placed on the ballot. Until the appointee takes the oath of office, the President of Council serves as acting mayor. Mr. Cline said the President of Council does not vote on the appointment.

President Lehman said that, after consulting members, he intended to call a special meeting of Council for Friday, September 11, at 4:00 p.m. in Council Chambers for the purpose of acting on the appointment. Notice would be posted and provided to the media at least 24 hours in advance as required by Council's rules and the Open Meetings Act.

Councilman Bell asked that the special meeting include time for public comment. President Lehman agreed.

Motion by Ruhl, second by Pfahler, to concur in the calling of a special meeting on Friday, September 11, 2026, at 4:00 p.m.
Yeas: Pfahler, Okuley, Sprow, Bell, Ruhl, Brinkman, Gaskins. Nays: none. Motion carried, 7-0.
