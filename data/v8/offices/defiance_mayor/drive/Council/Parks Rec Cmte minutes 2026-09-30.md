# Defiance City Council
## Parks and Recreation Committee: Minutes

**Wednesday, September 30, 2026, 5:00 p.m.**
**Conference Room B, City Building, 631 Perry Street**

**Members present:** Rita Okuley, Second Ward (Chair); Theresa Gaskins, At-Large; Nancy Ruhl, At-Large
**Staff:** Craig Fenstermaker, Parks and Recreation Director; Amy Gerken, Recreation Coordinator; Tom Wiechers, Safety-Service Director (item 3)

---

### 1. Call to order

Chair Okuley called the meeting to order at 5:01 p.m.

### 2. Minutes

The minutes of the July 22, 2026 meeting were approved by consensus.

### 3. Hammond Street Park restroom: construction status

Mr. Fenstermaker reported on the restroom replacement at Hammond Street Park (contractor: Kurtzman Site Development).

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Original contract | $146,300 |
| Change Order No. 1 (relocate water service tap; added cleanout) | $2,215 |
| Change Order No. 2 (unsuitable soil under footings; added stone and geotextile) | $8,940 |
| **Current contract** | **$157,455** |
| Project budget | $162,000 |
| Remaining contingency | $4,545 |

Change Order No. 2 was approved by the Board of Control in August. Mr. Wiechers said the soil problem was found when the footings were excavated and that the engineer's recommendation was to over-excavate about 18 inches and replace it with compacted stone. It added about two weeks to the schedule.

The building is under roof. Plumbing rough-in has passed inspection. The fixtures and partitions are on site. Substantial completion is now expected October 23, with a tentative reopening Monday, October 26 at 1:30 p.m. The portable toilets will stay until the building is open.

Councilwoman Gaskins asked whether the restroom would be open in the winter. Mr. Fenstermaker said it is designed as a three-season building, with the water shut off and the building winterized from mid-November to early April like the other park restrooms. A heated family restroom was considered in design but cost about $38,000 more.

### 4. Riverside Park shelter house roof

Mr. Fenstermaker reported that the August 24 storm damaged the shingle roof and fascia on the Riverside Park shelter house. The insurance adjuster has inspected and the claim is in process; the city's deductible is $5,000. Three quotes have been requested from local roofers and are due October 7. He expects to bring a recommendation to the Board of Control on October 12, with work done before November 15. The shelter remains usable but the two reservations remaining in October have been told about the tarps.

Councilwoman Ruhl asked whether the shelter roof was in the Capital Improvement Plan. Mr. Fenstermaker said it had been listed for 2028, so the storm moved it up.

### 5. Holiday lighting donation

Mr. Fenstermaker reported that the Downtown merchants' group has offered $2,500 toward downtown holiday lighting. The money would replace the oldest incandescent strands on the Clinton Street light poles (about 40 poles) with LED strands, which use less power and fail less often. Parks staff install the lighting the week before Thanksgiving.

Motion by Ruhl, second by Gaskins, to recommend that Council adopt a resolution accepting the donation. Vote: 3-0.

### 6. Fall programs and events

Ms. Gerken reviewed the fall calendar:

- **Fall cleanup volunteer day,** Kingsbury Park, Saturday, September 26: 96 volunteers, including students from Defiance High School and Defiance College and two scout troops. They mulched the playground, cleared the riverbank honeysuckle along the trail below the main shelter and painted 14 picnic tables.
- **Harvest on the Riverfront,** Kingsbury Park, Saturday, October 10, 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. (rain date October 11): 34 vendors confirmed so far, up from 29 last year, and Mr. Fenstermaker expects a few more. The Fire Prevention Bureau reviewed the booth layout on September 24. Parking at the high school lot with a shuttle. Street Department will deliver barricades and trash carts Friday.
- **Pumpkin Run 5K,** Pontiac Park, Saturday, October 10, 9 a.m. Registration is at 168 as of today.
- **Riverfront tree planting,** Saturday, October 17, 9 a.m. to noon, starting at the Riverside Park boat ramp lot. 40 trees (mostly swamp white oak, river birch and sycamore) along the trail.
- **Trunk-or-treat,** Fort Defiance Park, Saturday, October 31, in conjunction with trick-or-treat hours. Setup Saturday, October 17.
- **Babysitter class** with the Fire Prevention Bureau, Thursday, October 8.

Councilwoman Gaskins asked how many volunteers were needed for the tree planting. Ms. Gerken said 50 to 60, about three per tree plus a few for mulching, and that a student group from the college had already offered help after the Council meeting last week.

### 7. Shelter reservations and 2026 season wrap-up

Mr. Fenstermaker said 2026 shelter reservations close October 31. Through September, 214 shelter reservations were made (2025: 198), producing $15,240 in fees. Reservations for 2027 open online Monday, January 4, 2027.

The municipal pool closed for the season on Labor Day. Season attendance was about 9,400 swimmers. Concessions broke even for the first time in several years. The pool will need a new main drain grate and a repaint of the shallow end in spring 2027; both are in the department's operating request.

### 8. Kingsbury Park restroom replacement (CIP PK-27-01)

Mr. Fenstermaker said the department is still waiting on a decision on its state grant request for the Kingsbury Park restroom replacement ($185,000 total). He expects to hear in November. The project remains in the 2027 capital requests either way, with a smaller local-only scope if the grant is not awarded.

### 9. Other business

Chair Okuley relayed a request from the Second Ward neighborhood meeting for a second dog waste station in Hammond Street Park. Mr. Fenstermaker said he would add one when the restroom work is done.

A resident, Doris Kretschmar of 1120 Fifth Street, asked about adding a bench along the riverfront trail in memory of her husband. Mr. Fenstermaker explained the memorial bench program ($1,450 including the plaque and installation) and said he would mail her the form.

### 10. Adjournment

The meeting adjourned at 6:04 p.m. Next meeting: to be scheduled in January 2027 unless needed sooner.

*Recorded by S. Moll*
