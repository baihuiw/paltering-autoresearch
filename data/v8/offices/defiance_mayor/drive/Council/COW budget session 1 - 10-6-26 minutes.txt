DEFIANCE CITY COUNCIL
COMMITTEE OF THE WHOLE - 2027 BUDGET, SESSION 1
Tuesday, October 6, 2026, 6:00 p.m.
Council Chambers, City Building, 631 Perry Street

Present: President Gary Lehman (chair); Councilmembers Pfahler, Okuley, Sprow, Bell, Ruhl, Brinkman, Gaskins
Administration: Mayor Paul Haverkamp; Megan Ostrander, Chief of Staff; Angela Marquardt, Finance Director; Scott Wenninger, Police Chief; Tom Wiechers, Safety-Service Director; Dale Rummel, Fire Chief; Craig Fenstermaker, Parks Director; Kevin Shaffer, Utilities Superintendent; Rachel Dunlap, City Engineer
Recording: Sandra Moll, Clerk of Council
Public: 4

No formal action is taken by the Committee of the Whole. These are summary minutes.

1. OPENING

President Lehman opened the session at 6:00 p.m. He said the purpose of tonight's session was to review the revenue estimate and the departments' operating requests; capital requests are due Friday, October 9, and will be reviewed at the second session on October 20.

Mayor Haverkamp said that he had been in office three weeks and had inherited a budget process that was already well underway. He thanked the Finance Director. He said his priorities for 2027 were to keep the General Fund balanced without drawing down reserves, keep up with street and utility maintenance, and plan responsibly for fire apparatus. He asked departments to hold operating increases to wage and benefit costs plus contractual increases they cannot control.

2. 2026 PROJECTION AND 2027 GENERAL FUND REVENUE OUTLOOK

Ms. Marquardt said the 2026 General Fund is on track. Through August, revenue was 66.7 percent of the $14,680,000 estimate and spending 63.8 percent of appropriations, and income tax was 2.9 percent ahead of 2025. She expects to finish 2026 with a modest addition to the General Fund balance, but much of it comes from investment income and one-time items. September figures will be in the September financial report later this week.

She presented the preliminary 2027 estimate:

She said the income tax figure assumes 2.5 percent growth over the 2026 projection, below what the city is seeing this year, as a cushion. Investment income is budgeted about 20 percent below the 2026 projection because short-term rates are falling. The Local Government Fund is held flat. Licenses and permits reflect the 2027 fee schedule that takes effect January 1.

Councilman Brinkman asked about the state budget's Local Government Fund distributions. Ms. Marquardt said she had no reason to expect a cut but would not count on an increase.

3. 2027 OPERATING REQUESTS (GENERAL FUND)

Operating requests were due October 2. Department requests from the General Fund, excluding capital, total $15,335,400, which is $252,400 more than the preliminary revenue estimate. Ms. Marquardt summarized the main drivers:

- Wages: 2.75 percent for the AFSCME unit and 3.0 percent for police and fire under the current agreements; a 2.5 percent placeholder for non-bargaining employees, with the final figure to be recommended by the mayor and set by ordinance.
- Health insurance: the consortium's preliminary renewal is +7.8 percent; final rates are expected late in October.
- Wages and health insurance together add about $640,000 to General Fund costs before any other change.
- Pension contributions follow wages.
- Property and liability insurance: up, mostly because of property values and the cyber endorsement.

Ms. Marquardt said the administration would bring a balanced recommendation to the October 20 session. Options include holding two vacant positions open for part of the year, reducing the General Fund transfer to the Capital Improvement Fund, and deferring some equipment.

Councilwoman Okuley asked which positions were vacant. Ms. Marquardt said a maintenance worker in Parks and a clerk position in Building and Zoning. Mayor Haverkamp said he did not want to delay hiring in Building and Zoning because permit turnaround times were already a concern for contractors.

Councilman Pfahler asked whether the administrative assistant position in the Mayor's Office was new. Mayor Haverkamp said no; it is an existing position that has been vacant since the summer, the person hired starts October 14, and the cost is within the existing appropriation.

4. DEPARTMENT PRESENTATIONS (GENERAL GOVERNMENT, POLICE, FIRE/EMS)

General Government (Ms. Marquardt): The Mayor's Office, Council and Clerk, Law, Finance and Income Tax requests are essentially flat except for wages, insurance and software maintenance renewals. The Council Chambers audio-visual replacement is a capital request, not an operating item.

Police (Chief Wenninger): The Chief reviewed staffing and overtime. Overtime is running above 2026 levels because of vacancies, and the request carries overtime at the 2026 projected actual rather than the 2026 budget. Councilman Bell asked for the number of open positions; the Chief said two, which he expects to fill this fall from the current eligibility list.

Fire/EMS (Chief Rummel): The department's total operating request is $4,993,500, up 4.7 percent, of which $3,450,100 is General Fund and the rest the operating portion of the Fire/EMS Levy Fund. The largest General Fund change is personal services, including overtime to cover injury leave. He noted that the Ladder 1 repair ($18,400) is a 2026 cost and is not in the 2027 request, and that he would bring the apparatus schedule and the third-medic workload numbers to the October 20 session and to the levy work session on October 27. Time ran short, and President Lehman asked the Chief to hold the rest of his presentation for October 20.

Street, Parks, Engineering, Building and Zoning and Utilities will present on October 20.

5. PUBLIC COMMENT

Walter Brodbeck, 1022 Jefferson Avenue, asked whether Council would consider cutting its own pay. President Lehman said Council salaries are set by ordinance and cannot be changed during a member's current term under state law, but that he would note the comment.
