### ATTACHMENT A: Memorandum

**To:** Streets and Sidewalks Committee
**From:** Rachel Dunlap, P.E., City Engineer, and Code Enforcement
**Through:** Tom Wiechers, Safety-Service Director
**Date:** October 9, 2026
**Re:** Sidewalk snow and ice clearing on school walking routes

**Background.** At the September 23 Council meeting, Councilman Bell asked whether the city could enforce the sidewalk snow-clearing ordinance more actively along routes children walk to school.

**Current ordinance.** Codified Ordinance 521.06 requires the owner or occupant of property abutting a public sidewalk to remove snow and ice within 24 hours after a snowfall ends. If a walk is not cleared after notice, the city may clear it and bill the owner. Unpaid charges may be certified to the County Auditor for collection with real estate taxes.

**Current practice.** Enforcement is complaint-driven. In winter 2025-26 code enforcement received 37 complaints, issued 29 notices, cleared 4 walks with city forces and certified 1 unpaid charge ($135). Most notices are resolved within a day.

**Options.**

1. *No change.* Continue complaint-driven enforcement.
2. *Targeted patrols.* After snow events of 2 inches or more, code enforcement drives designated walking routes within 1/2 mile of the elementary and middle schools and issues notices. Estimated staff time: 6-8 hours per event. With one code enforcement officer in winter, this would require overtime or help from street department staff, who are plowing during the same hours.
3. *Courtesy reminders.* Mail a reminder before the first snowfall to property owners along designated walking routes (about 640 parcels). Estimated cost: $450 printing and postage.

**Staff recommendation.** Option 3 for this winter, combined with Option 2 on a trial basis after the first two significant snow events, then report back to committee in February. No ordinance change is needed for any option.

The Law Director has reviewed the ordinance and confirms that the existing notice and billing provisions apply to all options.

### ATTACHMENT B: Residential sidewalk repair cost-share program, 2027

**Program summary.** Under the Codified Ordinances, abutting property owners are responsible for maintaining sidewalks. The city's 50/50 cost-share program pays half the cost of replacing qualifying sidewalk through the city's annual sidewalk contractor. Owners may pay their half up front or by assessment over five years.

**2026 program results (through September 30):**

| Ward | Applications | Qualified | Linear feet replaced | City share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First | 9 | 7 | 412 | $2,890 |
| Second | 14 | 11 | 655 | $4,420 |
| Third | 6 | 4 | 230 | $1,640 |
| Fourth | 12 | 11 | 585 | $3,960 |
| **Total** | **41** | **33** | **1,882** | **$12,910** |

The 2026 appropriation was $15,000. The unspent balance ($2,090) returns to the Capital Improvement Fund.

**2027 notice list.** Engineering proposes sending repair notices in March 2027 to owners on the following blocks, chosen from the 2026 complaint log and staff inspections:

- Perry Street, First Street to Third Street (First Ward)
- Fifth Street, Jefferson Avenue to Holgate Avenue (Second Ward), coordinated with the water main replacement
- Ralston Avenue, Clinton Street to Harding Street (Second Ward)
- Hopkins Street, north of the 2026 resurfacing limits (Fourth Ward)
- Scattered locations citywide from the complaint log

Blocks inside the limits of a city sidewalk capital project are not included in notice lists, because that work will be done and paid for by the city.

**Program review questions for committee:**

1. Should the cost share increase from 50% to 60% for owner-occupied homes where the owner is 65 or older or disabled? Estimated additional cost: $1,500-$2,500 per year.
2. Should the five-year assessment option be extended to ten years for amounts over $2,000?
3. Should the city publish the complaint-log criteria used to build the notice list?

### ATTACHMENT C: 2026 curb ramp inventory summary

**Purpose.** Under Title II of the Americans with Disabilities Act, the city must keep a transition plan for bringing pedestrian facilities in the public right-of-way into compliance. Curb ramps are the largest item in that plan. In 2026, Engineering resumed the curb ramp inventory, which had not been updated since 2018.

**Method.** Each ramp was measured for running slope, cross slope, landing size and detectable warning surface, and rated for condition. Ward 3 locations were assessed by the city's design consultant as part of a sidewalk project. Other locations were assessed by the engineering summer intern in June.

**Results to date (84 locations assessed):**

| | Ward 1 | Ward 2 | Ward 3 | Ward 4 | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Locations assessed | 15 | 14 | 43 | 12 | 84 |
| Meet current criteria | see detail | see detail | 11 | see detail | |
| No ramp present | 0 | 0 | 5 | 0 | 5 |

Detailed results are in the inventory spreadsheet maintained by Engineering. Assessment of the remaining intersections citywide (approximately 600 corners) will continue in summer 2027 as staffing allows.

**How ramps get fixed.** The city rebuilds non-compliant curb ramps in three ways:

1. **With street resurfacing.** Federal guidance treats resurfacing as an alteration, so ramps on resurfaced streets are brought up to standard as part of the paving contract. Six ramps on Hopkins Street are being rebuilt this fall.
2. **Through sidewalk capital projects** listed in the Capital Improvement Plan, such as the Third Ward Sidewalk and Curb Ramp Improvements (CIP ST-27-04). Preliminary plans for that project are due from the consultant on October 16.
3. **On request.** Residents with a disability may ask for a ramp at a specific location. Requests are prioritized in the transition plan.

**Discussion requested.** Staff will ask the committee whether to recommend that Council update the ADA transition plan in 2027 using the new inventory data.
