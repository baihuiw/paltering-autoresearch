### ATTACHMENT A: Memorandum

**To:** Streets and Sidewalks Committee
**From:** Rachel Dunlap, P.E., City Engineer, and Code Enforcement
**Date:** October 9, 2026
**Re:** Sidewalk snow and ice clearing on school walking routes

**Background.** On September 23 Councilman Bell asked whether the city could enforce snow clearing more actively along routes children walk to school. Codified Ordinance 521.06 requires the owner or occupant of property abutting a public sidewalk to remove snow and ice within 24 hours after a snowfall ends. If a walk is not cleared after notice, the city may clear it, bill the owner and certify unpaid charges to the County Auditor. Enforcement is complaint-driven: in winter 2025-26 code enforcement received 37 complaints, issued 29 notices, cleared 4 walks and certified 1 unpaid charge ($135).

**Options.**

1. *No change.* Continue complaint-driven enforcement.
2. *Targeted patrols.* After snow events of 2 inches or more, code enforcement drives designated walking routes within 1/2 mile of the elementary and middle schools and issues notices (6-8 staff hours per event, likely overtime).
3. *Courtesy reminders.* Mail a reminder before the first snowfall to property owners along designated walking routes (about 640 parcels). Estimated cost: $450 printing and postage.

**Staff recommendation.** Option 3 for this winter, combined with Option 2 on a trial basis after the first two significant snow events, then report back to committee in February. No ordinance change is needed; the Law Director confirms the existing notice and billing provisions apply.

### ATTACHMENT B: Residential sidewalk repair cost-share program, 2027

**Program summary.** Abutting property owners are responsible for maintaining sidewalks. The city's 50/50 cost-share program pays half the cost of replacing qualifying sidewalk through the city's annual contractor; owners pay their half up front or by assessment over five years.

**2026 program results (through September 30):**

| Ward | Applications | Qualified | Linear feet replaced | City share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First | 9 | 7 | 412 | $2,890 |
| Second | 14 | 11 | 655 | $4,420 |
| Third | 6 | 4 | 230 | $1,640 |
| Fourth | 12 | 11 | 585 | $3,960 |
| **Total** | **41** | **33** | **1,882** | **$12,910** |

The 2026 appropriation was $15,000; the unspent $2,090 returns to the Capital Improvement Fund.

**2027 notice list.** Engineering proposes repair notices in March 2027 for Perry Street, First to Third (First Ward); Fifth Street, Jefferson to Holgate (Second Ward, with the water main replacement); Ralston Avenue, Clinton to Harding (Second Ward); Hopkins Street north of the 2026 resurfacing limits (Fourth Ward); and scattered locations from the complaint log.

Blocks inside the limits of a city sidewalk capital project are not included in notice lists, because that work will be done and paid for by the city.

**Questions for committee:** (1) raise the city share to 60% for owner-occupied homes where the owner is 65 or older or disabled (about $1,500-$2,500 per year more)? (2) extend the assessment option to ten years for amounts over $2,000?

### ATTACHMENT C: 2026 curb ramp inventory summary

**Purpose.** Under Title II of the ADA, the city keeps a transition plan for pedestrian facilities in the right-of-way; curb ramps are its largest item. In 2026 Engineering resumed the curb ramp inventory, last updated in 2018. Ward 3 locations were assessed by the city's design consultant as part of a sidewalk project; others by the engineering summer intern in June.

**Results to date (84 locations assessed):**

| | Ward 1 | Ward 2 | Ward 3 | Ward 4 | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Locations assessed | 15 | 14 | 43 | 12 | 84 |
| Meet current criteria | see detail | see detail | 11 | see detail | |
| No ramp present | 0 | 0 | 5 | 0 | 5 |

Detailed results are in Engineering's inventory spreadsheet. The remaining corners citywide (approximately 600) will be assessed in summer 2027 as staffing allows.

**How ramps get fixed.** The city rebuilds non-compliant curb ramps in three ways:

1. **With street resurfacing.** Ramps on resurfaced streets are brought up to standard in the paving contract. Six ramps on Hopkins Street are being rebuilt this fall.
2. **Through sidewalk capital projects** listed in the Capital Improvement Plan, such as the Third Ward Sidewalk and Curb Ramp Improvements (CIP ST-27-04). Preliminary plans for that project are due from the consultant on October 16.
3. **On request.** Residents with a disability may ask for a ramp at a specific location; requests are prioritized in the transition plan.

Staff will ask whether to recommend that Council update the ADA transition plan in 2027 using the new data.
