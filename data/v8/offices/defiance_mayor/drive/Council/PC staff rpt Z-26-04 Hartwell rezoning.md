The west side of North Clinton Street in this block has been changing from residential to small office use since the 1990s. The O-1 district on the applicant's existing lot was created by rezoning in 2007.

### 3. Comprehensive Plan

The 2019 Comprehensive Plan's future land use map shows the North Clinton Street frontage from Third Street to the bridge approach as **"Mixed Office/Residential Transition."** The plan text says this area should "allow small professional offices and similar low-traffic uses on lots fronting Clinton Street, with attention to buffering the residential streets behind them." The request is consistent with this designation.

### 4. Standards for a map amendment (Section 1113.04)

1. **Consistency with the Comprehensive Plan.** Consistent, see Section 3.
2. **Compatibility with surrounding uses.** A dental office open weekdays and two evenings is compatible with the adjacent office and residential uses if properly screened. The site plan provides a solid fence and landscaping along the residential lot lines.
3. **Adequacy of public facilities.** Water and sanitary sewer are available in North Clinton Street. Engineering finds the storm sewer in North Clinton has capacity for the detained release rate. North Clinton Street is a minor arterial; the expected increase in traffic (about 60 trips per day) is minor.
4. **Spot zoning.** The request extends an existing O-1 district along an arterial frontage and is consistent with the Comprehensive Plan. It is not spot zoning.
5. **Effect on property values.** Staff has no information indicating an adverse effect. The applicant's investment would replace two aging rental houses.

### 5. Permitted uses in the O-1 district (for reference)

Permitted: business and professional offices; medical and dental offices and clinics; banks and financial institutions without drive-through service; funeral homes; libraries and museums; existing single-family dwellings.

Conditional: day care centers; banks with drive-through service; religious places of worship.

Not permitted: retail sales, restaurants, motor vehicle sales or service, warehousing.

### 6. Public notice and comment

Notice was mailed September 18 to the owners of 23 parcels within 200 feet. As of this report, staff had received one phone call (from the owner of 1548 N. Clinton, asking about lighting and fencing; staff explained the site plan process) and no written comments.

### 7. Staff recommendation

Staff recommends that the Planning Commission **recommend approval** of Z-26-04 to City Council.

Buffering and lighting should be addressed through the conditions on Site Plan SP-26-11, which is on the same agenda. Staff suggests:

- six-foot solid privacy fence on the north and east lot lines;
- evergreen landscaping inside the fence per Chapter 1171;
- full-cutoff fixtures, maximum 16-foot pole height, maximum 0.5 foot-candle at residential lot lines, timer-controlled;
- replacement of sidewalk across the two driveways to be removed.

### 8. Next steps if recommended

1. Ordinance to Council for first reading (target October 13, 2026).
2. Council sets a public hearing; Clerk publishes notice at least 30 days before the hearing and mails notice to owners within 200 feet.
3. Council action after the hearing; ordinance effective 30 days after passage, subject to referendum.
4. Site plan approval becomes effective when the rezoning takes effect. Demolition permits may be issued before then at the applicant's risk.
