PROCLAMATION

Mayor Haverkamp read a proclamation declaring October 4 through 10, 2026 as Fire Prevention Week in the City of Defiance and urging residents to test their smoke alarms, practice a home escape plan with two ways out, and take part in the week's activities. He presented the proclamation to Fire Chief Dale Rummel and Fire Prevention Officer Lt. Erin Mayes.

Lt. Mayes thanked the mayor and Council. She said firefighters would visit every kindergarten through third-grade classroom in the city schools and both parochial schools during the week, would give safety talks at the senior center and two senior apartment buildings, and would hold the Station 1 open house on Saturday, October 10 from 10:00 a.m. to 2:00 p.m. She said the free home smoke alarm program had installed 265 alarms through August and that residents could call the Fire Prevention Bureau to sign up.

COMMUNICATIONS

a. The Clerk noted an invitation from the Defiance Area Chamber to members of Council to attend the Northtowne credit union ribbon cutting on Wednesday, October 14 at 10:00 a.m.

b. The Clerk noted a notice from the Defiance County Commissioners of a public hearing on the county's solid waste management plan update. The notice was for information and is on file with the Clerk.

PUBLIC COMMENT

Joyce Lantz, 812 Washington Avenue, asked about restoration of her front yard and driveway apron after the Washington Avenue waterline work. She said the trench had been backfilled but the apron was still gravel and her mail carrier had complained. City Engineer Dunlap said the contractor restores aprons and lawns in sections once the new main is tested and the services are transferred, and that the block between Second and Third Streets should be restored within about three weeks. She gave Ms. Lantz her direct number and said she would visit the site.

Ron Weidenhamer, 1415 Ralston Avenue, thanked the city for the Ralston Avenue paving, which he called "the best that street has looked in thirty years." He said the apron across the street at 1418 Ralston had been left lower than the new pavement and was collecting water. Safety-Service Director Wiechers said it was on the contractor's punch list and should be corrected by the end of the month.

Peggy Schroeder, 1104 Holgate Avenue, said she had attended the Second Ward neighborhood meeting the night before and wanted to repeat for Council what she and her neighbors said there: traffic on Holgate is too fast and there is no safe place for children to cross between Fifth Street and Pontiac Street. She asked for a crosswalk and a 25 mph sign near the park. Councilwoman Okuley said she had asked the administration for a traffic count on Holgate after the repaving. Mayor Haverkamp said he had heard the same concerns the night before and had asked the Safety-Service Director for a recommendation.

Aaron Mendez, Defiance College, 701 N. Clinton Street, spoke on behalf of a student service group. He asked whether the riverfront trail between the Pontiac Street bridge and Riverside Park could get additional lighting, since students use it to walk downtown after dark. He also offered student volunteers for the fall cleanup day and the riverfront tree planting. Parks Director Fenstermaker (by note to the Clerk; not present) had asked that volunteers sign up through the Parks office at ext. 240. Mayor Haverkamp thanked Mr. Mendez and said he would ask Parks to look at lighting costs for the 2027 capital requests.

Larry Gerdeman, 619 Seneca Street, said he was glad the water rates had been approved because "the plant has to be fixed" but asked Council to keep an eye on the cost of the treatment plant project. Finance Director Marquardt said the filter and clearwell project was within budget and that change orders to date were about one percent of the contract.

No one else wished to speak. Public comment closed at 7:27 p.m.

REPORT OF THE MAYOR

Mayor Haverkamp thanked Council for its confidence and said he was grateful to President Lehman for serving as acting mayor and for his help during the transition. He reported:

- Since taking the oath on September 15 he has met with each department head, toured the Water Treatment Plant and the Water Pollution Control Plant, ridden along with the Street Department, and chaired his first Board of Control meeting on September 21. He thanked the staff for "keeping the lights on and the water running" during a difficult month.
- He intends to meet individually with each member of Council over the next few weeks and has asked his scheduler, Brenda Kahle, to set those up.
- Chief of Staff Megan Ostrander will be the point of contact for Council members' requests to the Mayor's Office. Constituent questions and casework should continue to go to Luis Ortega, Constituent Services Coordinator, at ext. 207.
- The Mayor's Office will be announcing a senior services effort next week, built around existing programs such as the free home smoke alarms, with Mr. Ortega as a single point of contact for older residents and their families.
- Harvest on the Riverfront will be held Saturday, October 10, from 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. in Kingsbury Park, with a rain date of October 11. The site walk-through was held last Saturday.
- The fall cleanup volunteer day at Kingsbury Park is this Saturday, September 26. Volunteers should meet at the main shelter at 9:00 a.m.
- The homecoming parade is Saturday, October 3 at 10:00 a.m.

Councilwoman Gaskins asked whether the mayor would continue the previous administration's practice of a written monthly report to Council. Mayor Haverkamp said he would, beginning with the October 27 meeting, and would also send a short weekly update by email to members.

REPORTS OF THE ADMINISTRATION

a. Finance Director. Ms. Marquardt reported that the August financial report was reviewed by the Finance Committee on September 17. Through August, General Fund revenue is at 66.7 percent of the annual estimate and expenditures at 63.8 percent of appropriations. Budget workbooks and instructions go to departments tomorrow, September 24. Operating requests are due October 2 and capital requests October 9. The Committee of the Whole budget sessions are confirmed for Tuesday, October 6 and Tuesday, October 20 at 6:00 p.m. in Council Chambers. She explained Ordinance No. 2026-42 (see Legislation).

b. Safety-Service Director. Mr. Wiechers reported:
- Ralston Avenue was paved September 11. Pavement markings were completed September 16. The punch list, including the apron at 1418 Ralston, should be done by September 29.
- The 2026 resurfacing program begins Monday, October 5, with milling on Holgate Avenue from Fifth Street to Pontiac Street at 7 a.m. The schedule for Jefferson Avenue, Hopkins Street, Washington Avenue, Elliott Road and Summit Street will be posted on the city website and Facebook page. He asked residents to watch for no-parking signs, which will be posted at least 24 hours ahead.
- Fall hydrant flushing began September 14 and is about half complete. It should be finished by early October.
- The leaf collection schedule will be published by mid-October, with collection to begin late in the month. The brush and bagged leaf drop-off site on Island Park Avenue remains open Saturdays.
- In response to Mr. Mendez, he said he would report back on trail lighting through Parks.

c. City Engineer. Ms. Dunlap reported:
- Sixty percent plans for the Third Street storm outfall were received September 17. The construction estimate is $286,000. A U.S. Army Corps of Engineers Nationwide Permit will be needed for work at the outfall, and the application will be submitted in October. Bidding is planned for January 2027.
- The Harding Street culvert replacement (Hubbard ditch, Ralston Avenue to Spruce Street) is estimated at $84,000 for a 6-foot by 4-foot precast box culvert. The steel plate remains in place and is checked weekly. The Law Director has advised that the project should be competitively bid; Engineering is targeting advertisement in early November.
- The Washington Avenue waterline contractor has reached Third Street.
- The development review for a 9,200-square-foot retail building on a Northtowne outlot is underway and the site plan will go to the Planning Commission on September 29.

d. Fire Chief. Chief Rummel reported that the department had answered 301 calls in September through September 22, which is about on pace with last year, and that the new fire inspection software went live September 14 with about 1,140 occupancies imported. The two powered stretchers for Medic 2 and Medic 3 will be ordered once the appropriation is in place and the Board of Control approves the purchase, with half the cost paid by a Bureau of Workers' Compensation safety grant (see Ordinance No. 2026-42). He thanked Council for the Fire Prevention Week proclamation.

e. Law Director. Mr. Cline said the draft nuisance notice letter requested by Councilwoman Gaskins is in the packet with Ordinance No. 2026-35.

LEGISLATION

THIRD READING

Ordinance No. 2026-35. An ordinance repealing and replacing Chapter 1355 of the Codified Ordinances, "Nuisance Abatement," to update notice, abatement and cost-recovery procedures for weeds, rubbish and inoperable vehicles.

The Clerk read the ordinance by title. Councilwoman Gaskins thanked the Law Director for the draft notice letter and said it was clear. Councilman Pfahler asked whether the new $150 administrative fee would apply this season. Mr. Cline said it would apply only to abatements ordered after the ordinance takes effect, 30 days after the mayor approves it, which in practice means next season for weeds.

Motion by Gaskins, second by Pfahler, to adopt Ordinance No. 2026-35.
Yeas: Pfahler, Okuley, Sprow, Bell, Ruhl, Brinkman, Gaskins. Nays: none. Ordinance No. 2026-35 passed, 7-0.

Ordinance No. 2026-37. An ordinance amending Section 1155.08 of the Planning and Zoning Code, "Fences and Walls."

The Clerk read the ordinance by title. Councilman Pfahler said he still had concerns about limiting front-yard fences to 42 inches, since many existing decorative fences in the First Ward are 48 inches. He said he appreciated that existing fences could remain but would vote no. Councilman Brinkman, who serves as Council's representative on the Planning Commission, said the Commission had looked at 48 inches and chose 42 inches because of sight lines at driveways.

Motion by Brinkman, second by Ruhl, to adopt Ordinance No. 2026-37.
Yeas: Okuley, Sprow, Bell, Ruhl, Brinkman, Gaskins. Nays: Pfahler. Ordinance No. 2026-37 passed, 6-1.

SECOND READING

Ordinance No. 2026-39. An ordinance vacating a portion of the alley between Jackson Avenue and Agnes Street, from Seventh Street to Eighth Street, and reserving a utility easement.

The Clerk read the ordinance by title. There was no discussion. The ordinance will be placed on third reading October 13.

FIRST READING

Ordinance No. 2026-40. An ordinance authorizing the Mayor to enter into a lease of approximately 41 acres of city-owned land on Domersville Road for agricultural use for crop years 2027 through 2029.

The Clerk read the ordinance by title. Utilities Superintendent Shaffer said the field receives biosolids from the Water Pollution Control Plant under the plant's Ohio EPA permit, and that the city has leased it to a local farmer for crop production for many years. The current lease expires December 31, 2026. The city requested sealed proposals in August; two were received, and the high proposal was $142 per acre per year from the current tenant, Dale Wittenmyer, compared with $128 per acre under the current lease. Councilman Bell asked whether the tenant was required to follow the biosolids application schedule. Mr. Shaffer said yes; the lease requires the tenant to coordinate planting and harvest with the plant's application windows and prohibits use of the field for grazing. The ordinance will be placed on second reading October 13.

Ordinance No. 2026-42. An ordinance amending the 2026 annual appropriation ordinance to provide for additional appropriations and transfers within the General Fund, the EMS Capital Fund, the Street Construction, Maintenance and Repair Fund and the Water Fund, and declaring an emergency.

The Clerk read the ordinance by title. Finance Director Marquardt summarized the four sections:

1. General Fund: $4,000 from General Fund contingency to the Council legal advertising line, for the special meeting notice and ordinance summary publications, which have exceeded the original estimate.
2. EMS Capital Fund: receipt of a Bureau of Workers' Compensation safety grant of $34,206 and an additional appropriation of $68,412 for the purchase of two powered stretchers for Medic 2 and Medic 3. The city's half comes from the fund's unappropriated balance. The purchase will go to the Board of Control on September 28.
3. Street Construction, Maintenance and Repair Fund: a transfer of $12,500 from contractual services to materials and supplies for hot mix and cold patch used in pothole repair and utility cut restoration.
4. Water Fund: a transfer of $9,500 from Water Treatment Plant contractual services to chemicals, to cover chemical costs through the end of the current supply contracts on October 31.

She said the emergency clause is needed so the stretcher order can be placed against the grant deadline and so the transfers are available for September and October payables.

Motion by Ruhl, second by Brinkman, to suspend the rule requiring an ordinance to be read on three separate days.
Yeas: Pfahler, Okuley, Sprow, Bell, Ruhl, Brinkman, Gaskins. Nays: none. Motion carried, 7-0.

Motion by Ruhl, second by Okuley, to adopt Ordinance No. 2026-42 as an emergency measure.
Yeas: Pfahler, Okuley, Sprow, Bell, Ruhl, Brinkman, Gaskins. Nays: none. Ordinance No. 2026-42 passed as an emergency measure, 7-0.

Ordinance No. 2026-43. An ordinance authorizing the Board of Control to enter into a three-year contract for emergency medical services billing and collection services.

The Clerk read the ordinance by title. Fire Chief Rummel said the city's contract for EMS billing expires December 31, 2026. The department issued a request for proposals in July and received four proposals. A review team of the Fire Chief, the Finance Director and Lt. Mark Sanderson, EMS Coordinator, recommended Ameridale Revenue Services, LLC of Findlay at 6.75 percent of net collections, compared with 8.50 percent under the current contract. Based on 2025 net collections of $598,400, the lower rate would save about $10,500 per year. Councilwoman Okuley asked whether residents would see any change in how they are billed. Chief Rummel said city residents would continue to have their insurance billed and that the city's policy of not pursuing residents for balances after insurance would not change. The ordinance will be placed on second reading October 13, following the Safety Committee meeting on October 7.

RESOLUTIONS

Resolution No. 2026-15. A resolution certifying unpaid charges for the cutting of weeds and removal of rubbish on 38 parcels to the Defiance County Auditor for collection with real estate taxes, and declaring an emergency.

The Clerk read the resolution by title. Finance Director Marquardt said the 38 parcels had unpaid charges totaling $11,846.00 for abatements performed between May and August 2026. Each owner received an invoice and a second notice before certification. Councilwoman Sprow asked how many of the parcels were vacant. Ms. Marquardt said 21 were vacant structures or vacant lots and 6 were in foreclosure. The resolution must be certified to the Auditor by October 1 to be placed on the 2026 tax duplicate.

Motion by Ruhl, second by Gaskins, to adopt Resolution No. 2026-15 as an emergency measure.
Yeas: Pfahler, Okuley, Sprow, Bell, Ruhl, Brinkman, Gaskins. Nays: none. Resolution No. 2026-15 adopted, 7-0.
