Megan,

Attached is the first cut of the slides for the council work session on the fire and EMS levy renewal. It's 14 slides right now and I know that's too many for a 30-minute slot, so cut freely. What I think has to stay:

- Slide 3: run volume. We went from 3,912 runs in 2021 to a projected 4,600 this year, and about 72 percent of that is EMS. That's the whole story in one chart.
- Slide 5: what the levy pays for. It's roughly 31 percent of the department's operating budget, mostly the third medic unit's staffing and apparatus replacement.
- Slide 8: response times. Median is still under 5 minutes inside the city, but the 90th percentile has crept up to 8:40 because of simultaneous calls. That's the slide that explains why the third medic matters.
- Slide 11: the apparatus replacement schedule. Ladder 1 is 19 years old and this month is a good example of why.

What I'd cut or move to an appendix: the call-type breakdown (slide 4), the mutual aid table (slide 9), and the training hours slide (12). Council members have seen those before.

Angela checked the millage numbers on slides 5 and 6 this morning, so those should be right. She wants the slide 6 language to say "renewal, no increase in the rate" exactly that way, and not "no new taxes," because the county auditor's office has asked cities not to use that phrase on renewals.

On Ladder 1: the aerial is still out of service. The hydraulic leak turned out to be in the main lift cylinder, not a hose like we hoped. The apparatus shop in Toledo quoted $18,400 for the cylinder reseal plus the annual aerial certification while it's apart, which we'd have to do in November anyway. Tom, that's over my purchasing limit so it'll need to go to Board of Control. In the meantime we have the mutual aid agreement for an aerial on working fires, and Engine 1 carries the ground ladders. I'm not comfortable leaving it like that through the winter.
