Hi all,

Paul wants to hold his first citywide town hall in November, after the ward meetings wrap up (Second Ward was 9/22, Fourth Ward is 10/15). The idea is simple: an evening where anyone can ask him and the department heads questions about city services, with no agenda beyond a short update from him at the start.

Date: I'm thinking Thursday, November 19, 6:30-8:00 PM. Not a council week, it's before Thanksgiving, and the budget hearing will have been on the 10th so he can talk about the budget with real numbers.

Room options:
(a) Council Chambers. Seats about 120 with the extra chairs, has the sound system and the live stream. Downside: it feels formal, and people associate it with council meetings.
(b) Library community room. Feels friendlier, central, free parking. Seats about 90. No streaming unless Ben brings a kit.
(c) A school cafeteria. Lots of space, but we'd be at the mercy of the custodian schedule, and it's far from downtown.

I lean (a), but I'm open. Brenda, can you check Chambers and the library for the 19th?

Format questions for the group:
- Open mic or question cards? Open mic is more genuine but one person can take 15 minutes. Cards are fairer but people think we're screening.
- Which department heads come? I'd say Tom, Angela, Chief Rummel, Craig, Rachel and Kevin, and Luis at a table in the back for anyone who has a specific case.
- Hannah: how do we promote it? E-news, Facebook, a flyer at the library and senior center, the radio show on the 15th.

From the two ward meetings: seniors came to both, and the number one thing they said afterward was they couldn't hear. Chambers has the hearing loop and the sound system, which matters more than how formal it feels. I'd vote Chambers.

On format: what worked at the Second Ward meeting was open mic with a 3-minute timer and Rita Okuley politely enforcing it. People accepted that. Maybe offer both: open mic, and cards for people who don't want to stand up.

I'll bring my intake forms and a sign that says "Have a specific issue? Talk to Luis."
