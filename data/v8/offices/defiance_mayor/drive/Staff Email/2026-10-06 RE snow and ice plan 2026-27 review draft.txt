Tom,

Attached is the snow and ice plan with my redlines for this winter. Mostly the same as last year. Changes in summary so you don't have to read all 22 pages before Friday:

1. Routes. Still 8 plow routes plus the downtown hand crew. I moved the Hopkins St and Elliott Rd segments from Route 6 to Route 7 because Route 6 had 14.2 lane miles and Route 7 had 9.8. Now they're 12.1 and 11.9. Drivers have seen it and are fine with it.

2. Trucks. 11 trucks with plows and spreaders. Unit 22 (the 2009 single axle) is still in the lineup this winter since the new one won't be here for 10-14 months. It had a new hydraulic pump in August, so it should be OK, but it's my first one to go down. Unit 9 (the bucket truck) is not a plow truck so it's not in this count.

3. Salt. The dome on Carter Road is about 55% full. The ODOT contract is 1,400 tons at $71.84 a ton, down from $78.20 last year, with the Board's blanket PO letting us take up to 1,680 tons ($120,691.20) if we have a bad winter. I want to start pulling deliveries in early November so we're closer to full by Thanksgiving. Last year we were waiting on trucks in January and I don't want that again.

4. Brine. About 4,500 gallons carried over in the tanks at the dome. The calcium chloride purchase is on Monday's Board agenda (ODOT co-op, $1.06/gal, up to 26,000 gal). We pre-treat the bridges, the hills on Holgate and Ralston, and the hospital and school routes ahead of a storm.

5. Call-out. Same tiers. Tier 1 is the 3 a.m. crew of 4 for anything under 2 inches. Tier 2 is everybody. Tier 3 is 12-hour shifts. I added a line that the seasonal leaf laborers (Board approved up to four on 10/12, if it passes) are NOT plow drivers but can help with the downtown hand crew if they're still on payroll in December.

6. Mailbox policy. No change. We replace a mailbox only if the plow physically hit it, not if snow pushed it over. Standard post and box, $65 value. Luis gets these calls, so I copied the language at the bottom for him.

Rachel, page 14 is your section (bridges, the Pontiac St bridge deck especially).

Tom, Ron,

From my side, the top five snow calls last winter, roughly in order:

1. "When are you plowing my street?" (side streets after a big storm)
2. Plow left a windrow across my driveway after I shoveled
3. Mailbox knocked down
4. Neighbor's sidewalk not shoveled
5. Cars parked on the snow route

For the handout, if we could say plainly "main routes first, then side streets, usually within 24 hours after the snow stops," that would answer most of #1. People are OK with waiting if they know the order.

For #2, there's really no answer except "sorry," but a line that says plows can't avoid it would help.

For seniors specifically: a few of the senior building managers asked me last year whether we keep a list of residents who can't shovel. We don't, and I don't think we should promise one, but I'd like to put the church volunteer groups' numbers in the handout. Something to talk about Friday.
