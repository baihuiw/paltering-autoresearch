1. 2026 street resurfacing (fall paving list)

The contractor is mobilizing the week of 10/5. Milling first, then surface course about a week behind. Current order, weather permitting:

- Holgate Ave (Fifth St to Pontiac St): mill 10/5-10/6, pave 10/12-10/13
- Jefferson Ave (Third St to Sixth St): mill 10/7, pave 10/14

- Elliott Rd (Domersville Rd to corp limit): full-depth patching first, mill and pave the week of 10/19
- Summit St (Jefferson to Holgate): pave only, week of 10/19

Total contract is $1,184,600 against the $1,210,000 budgeted in the street levy fund, so we have about $25,000 of room for quantity overruns. Elliott Rd is where I'd expect them, because the base is worse than the cores showed. Hopkins St includes six curb ramps that we're rebuilding as part of the paving job, since ADA requires ramps to be brought up to standard when we resurface. Those six are already in the contract quantities.

Holgate gets the most calls because of the school traffic. We'll do door hangers on Holgate and Jefferson on 10/1 and the street department will post no-parking signs 48 hours ahead.

2. Harding St culvert

The 48-inch corrugated metal pipe under Harding St at the Hubbard ditch is rusted through along the invert, and the roadway settled about two inches over it in August. The steel plate is a stopgap. It's rated for the traffic, but the street department checks it twice a week and it's loud, so the neighbors aren't happy.

Replacement options:
a) Precast concrete box culvert, 6x4: estimate $84,000 installed, about 3 weeks with Harding closed between Ralston and Spruce
b) Reinforced concrete pipe, 54-inch: estimate $71,500, about 2 weeks closed, but it needs a headwall rebuild on the downstream side that I haven't priced yet

I'd recommend (a). It's over the bid threshold, so it either goes out for bid or we use the emergency procurement provision if the road keeps settling. Steve Cline should weigh in on whether this qualifies as an emergency. My view is it doesn't yet, as long as the plate holds. If we bid in November we could do it in April before the spring rains, and Angela says there's room in the stormwater fund.

3. Sidewalk snow clearing (Bell question)

Codified Ordinance 521.06 requires owners and occupants to clear sidewalks abutting their property within 24 hours after snowfall ends. Enforcement is complaint-driven. Code enforcement sends a notice, and if the walk still isn't cleared the city can clear it and bill the owner, with unpaid bills certified to the county auditor for collection with property taxes. In practice we did that four times last winter and certified one bill ($135).

Councilman Bell asked whether we could do proactive enforcement on school walking routes. That's a staffing question more than an ordinance question. Code enforcement is one person in winter. If council wants it, the ordinance doesn't need to change. It's a matter of who drives the routes. I'd suggest we bring a short memo to the Streets and Sidewalks Committee in October rather than answer it off the cuff.

- Ralston Ave / Clinton St signal: the controller is the original 1998 cabinet and it's failing intermittently. Replacement is about $38,000 and it's in the CIP for 2027. I'd like to move it up if anything frees up.
- Pontiac St bridge: ODOT inspection report came back, general appraisal 6, no action items beyond the usual deck sealing.
- Tree lawn trees: the Parks Dept. list of removals in the right-of-way is with me for sign-off. 41 trees, mostly ash. I'll coordinate with Craig so removals happen before the resurfacing on the affected streets.
- Utility permits: we issued 22 right-of-way permits in August, mostly fiber. One contractor has three open restoration items on Jefferson. I've sent a 10-day letter.
