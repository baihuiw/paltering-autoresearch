1. CALL TO ORDER

Acting Mayor Lehman called the meeting to order at 10:01 a.m. All four members were present and a quorum was declared. Mr. Lehman noted that he was chairing the Board as acting mayor under the Charter for what he expected to be the last time, since Mayor-designate Paul Haverkamp will take the oath of office tomorrow morning at 9:00 a.m. in Council Chambers. He thanked the members and department heads for keeping the regular schedule going since September 2.

2. APPROVAL OF MINUTES

The minutes of the regular meeting of August 31, 2026 were distributed in advance. There was no meeting on September 7 (Labor Day).

Motion by Director Cline, second by Director Wiechers, to approve the minutes of August 31, 2026 as distributed. Motion carried 4-0.

3. BILLS LIST AND THEN-AND-NOW CERTIFICATE

Director Marquardt presented the bills list for the period August 29 through September 11, 2026, covering two weeks because of the holiday, totaling $841,207.55 across all funds. The largest items were the July-August chemical and electric invoices at the Water Treatment Plant and the Water Pollution Control Facility, the semimonthly health insurance premium to the consortium, and the Ralston Avenue resurfacing progress payment.

One then-and-now certificate was presented under Ohio Revised Code 5705.41(D): after-hours call-out repair of the transfer switch on the Police Department's backup generator, $1,174.80, performed on a Saturday before a purchase order could be issued.

Motion by Director Wiechers, second by Director Marquardt, to approve the bills list and the then-and-now certificate. Motion carried 4-0.

4. POLICE DEPARTMENT - MARKED PATROL VEHICLES (2)

Chief Wenninger requested authorization to purchase two marked all-wheel-drive patrol utility vehicles through the State of Ohio cooperative purchasing program, replacing Cruisers 11 and 12. Cruiser 11 is a 2018 model with 128,400 miles and recurring electrical problems; Cruiser 12 is a 2018 model with 116,900 miles and was involved in a minor collision in June that was repaired at Mid-City Auto Body. These are this year's two units in the department's regular two-per-year replacement rotation. The cooperative contract price is $46,215.00 per vehicle. Equipment transfer and upfitting (lightbar, console, prisoner partition, push bumper, radio and computer mounts) are quoted at $10,215.00 per vehicle, for a total of $112,860.00 for both units.

Director Marquardt confirmed that the purchase is in the 2026 Capital Improvement Fund budget at $118,000.00. Chief Wenninger said dealer delivery is estimated at 14 to 18 weeks and the two units being replaced will be listed on the online surplus auction after the new vehicles are in service. Director Cline asked whether the department had looked at a hybrid model; Chief Wenninger said the hybrid version was not available on the state contract this cycle at a price within the budget but that he would look at it again for 2027.

Motion by Director Wiechers, second by Director Cline, to authorize the purchase of two patrol vehicles and upfitting through the State cooperative purchasing program in an amount not to exceed $112,860.00 from the Capital Improvement Fund. Motion carried 4-0.

5. ENGINEERING - MATERIALS TESTING, 2026 RESURFACING PROGRAM

City Engineer Dunlap presented a professional services agreement with Ross & Feltz Testing Lab for quality assurance testing on the 2026 resurfacing program, including asphalt density cores, mix temperature checks, and compaction testing on the full-depth patching on Elliott Road. Work begins with the Holgate Avenue paving in October. Three proposals were received:

- Ross & Feltz Testing Lab: $14,850.00 (unit-price estimate)
- Second firm (Findlay): $16,420.00
- Third firm (Toledo): $18,975.00

Ms. Dunlap noted that Ross & Feltz performed the testing on the Ralston Avenue project this summer and that their field technician is local, which helps on short-notice paving days. Funding is from the Street Levy Fund within the program budget.

Motion by Director Marquardt, second by Director Wiechers, to approve the agreement with Ross & Feltz Testing Lab in an amount not to exceed $14,850.00. Motion carried 4-0.

6. FIRE DEPARTMENT - STATION 2 APPARATUS BAY HEATERS

Chief Rummel requested approval to replace the two gas-fired unit heaters in the Station 2 apparatus bay. Both heaters are original to the 1994 building; one failed its combustion test during the August preventive maintenance visit and was locked out. Chief Rummel said the bay must stay above freezing to protect the pumps and the medic unit supplies.

Three quotes were received:

- Hensel Plumbing & Heating: $27,300.00
- Second contractor: $29,840.00
- Third contractor: $31,175.00

Director Wiechers said the low quote includes removal of the old units, new flues, and a programmable thermostat. Director Marquardt confirmed funds in the Capital Improvement Fund facilities line. Work is to be finished before October 31.

Motion by Director Cline, second by Director Marquardt, to award the Station 2 bay heater replacement to Hensel Plumbing & Heating in the amount of $27,300.00. Motion carried 4-0.

7. SAFETY-SERVICE - WORKERS' COMPENSATION GROUP RATING, POLICY YEAR 2027

Director Wiechers presented the renewal of the City's enrollment in a group rating program for Ohio Bureau of Workers' Compensation premiums for policy year 2027, administered by Ridgeway Comp Services. The enrollment fee is $4,850.00. The administrator's projected group discount is 38 percent, compared with 41 percent this year, and Director Wiechers said the City's own claims experience remains favorable. The agreement requires the City to keep its safety committee active and to report claims within the program's timelines. Director Cline reviewed the agreement and had no concerns.

Motion by Director Marquardt, second by Director Cline, to approve the 2027 group rating enrollment agreement and fee of $4,850.00. Motion carried 4-0.

8. WATER - ANTENNA SITE LEASE AMENDMENT, NORTH ELEVATED TANK

Superintendent Shaffer presented the First Amendment to the antenna site lease with Northwest Wireless Partners LLC for its equipment on the north elevated water tank. The amendment allows the tenant to add one equipment cabinet on the existing ground pad and to replace three antennas with newer models of similar size. In exchange, monthly rent increases from $1,850.00 to $2,100.00, with the existing 3 percent annual escalator continuing. The tenant will pay for a structural review of the tank by an engineer acceptable to the City before any antennas are changed, and all work on the tank must be scheduled through the Utilities Department at least 10 working days ahead.

Director Cline said the amendment does not extend the term of the lease, which runs through 2031, and is within the Board's authority. Rent is deposited to the Water Fund.

Motion by Director Wiechers, second by Director Marquardt, to approve the First Amendment to the antenna site lease. Motion carried 4-0.

9. SPECIAL EVENT PERMITS - STREET USE

Ms. Ostrander presented two special event applications requiring street closures, both reviewed by the Police, Fire and Street departments:

a. Defiance High School Homecoming Parade, Saturday, October 3, 2026, 10:00 a.m. Line-up on Jefferson Avenue; route along Clinton Street through downtown. Rolling closure with police escort. The school district provides its own insurance certificate and volunteers at intersections.

b. Pumpkin Run 5K, Saturday, October 10, 2026, 9:00 a.m., starting and finishing at Pontiac Park, sponsored by the Fort Defiance Running Club. The course uses park roads and residential streets near the park; no state route is crossed. The sponsor will place course marshals at each turn and has agreed to have the course clear by 10:30 a.m.

Chief Wenninger noted that October 10 is also the date of Harvest on the Riverfront and the Station 1 open house, and that he has scheduled extra officers that day. Chief Rummel said a medic unit would stage near the finish line for the 5K and then move to Kingsbury Park.

Motion by Director Cline, second by Director Wiechers, to approve both special event permits subject to the conditions in the department reviews. Motion carried 4-0.

Director Wiechers presented the following routine personnel actions:

b. Separation of 23 seasonal municipal pool employees at the close of the season, Monday, September 7, 2026.

Director Wiechers reported that the Fire Prevention Bureau's new inspection software went live this morning with about 1,140 occupancies imported, and that the Building and Zoning office is expected to join the system in January. He thanked Lt. Mayes and Ben Rohrs for the work over the past two months.

Director Marquardt said the August monthly financial report was distributed Friday and will be reviewed by Council's Finance Committee on Thursday, September 17, along with the draft 2027 budget calendar.

Mr. Lehman wished the incoming mayor well and said he would be returning his attention to Council business.

12. ADJOURNMENT

There being no further business, motion by Director Wiechers, second by Director Cline, to adjourn. Motion carried 4-0. The meeting adjourned at 10:52 a.m.
