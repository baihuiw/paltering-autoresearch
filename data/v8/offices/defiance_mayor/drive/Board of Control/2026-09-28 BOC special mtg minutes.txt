Notice of the special meeting was posted at the City Administration Building and on the City website and sent to the news media on Friday, September 25, 2026, in accordance with the City's open meetings rules.

Mayor Paul Haverkamp, Chair, called the meeting to order at 3:31 p.m.

Also present: Utilities Superintendent Kevin Shaffer; Parks Director Craig Fenstermaker; Street Superintendent Ron Diehl; EMS Coordinator Lt. Mark Sanderson; Megan Ostrander, Chief of Staff; Janelle Rohrs, Deputy Clerk (recording). Members of the public: two (sign-in sheet on file).

4. STREET DEPARTMENT - SINGLE-AXLE DUMP TRUCK WITH PLOW AND SPREADER

Street Superintendent Diehl requested authorization to purchase one (1) 2027 single-axle dump truck with stainless steel bed, 10-foot reversible plow and in-cab spreader controls through the Ohio Department of Transportation Cooperative Purchasing Program, replacing Unit 22 (2009, 141,000 miles), which failed its annual inspection with frame rust. The cooperative contract price is $187,465.00, including upfitting and delivery. Delivery is estimated at 10 to 14 months. Unit 22 will be kept in reserve this winter and sold on GovDeals after the new unit arrives.

Director Marquardt confirmed that the purchase was included in the 2026 Street Fund capital budget at $195,000.00. Director Cline asked whether a lease-purchase had been considered; Superintendent Diehl said it had and that the Finance Department recommended a cash purchase given the fund balance.

Motion by Director Wiechers, second by Director Marquardt, to authorize the purchase through the ODOT Cooperative Purchasing Program in an amount not to exceed $187,465.00. Motion carried 4-0.

5. PARKS - HAMMOND STREET PARK RESTROOM RENOVATION, CHANGE ORDER NO. 2

Parks Director Fenstermaker presented Change Order No. 2 to the contract with Kurtzman Site Development for the Hammond Street Park restroom renovation, in the amount of $8,940.00. During demolition the contractor found that the existing cast iron floor drain and the first 22 feet of the building sewer were broken and partly filled with sediment. The change order covers replacement of the floor drain, 22 feet of 6-inch PVC building sewer, a cleanout, and concrete floor patching. The City Engineer reviewed the unit prices and found them reasonable.

The original contract amount was $146,300.00. Change Order No. 1 (added ADA grab bars and a changing table, $2,215.00) was approved August 17, 2026. With Change Order No. 2 the revised contract amount is $157,455.00, which remains within the project budget of $162,000.00. Completion is now expected October 23, 2026, one week later than the original date. Director Fenstermaker said the park's portable units will stay in place until the restrooms reopen.

Motion by Director Cline, second by Director Wiechers, to approve Change Order No. 2 in the amount of $8,940.00. Motion carried 4-0.

6. WATER POLLUTION CONTROL FACILITY - CLARIFIER DRIVE GEARBOX

Utilities Superintendent Shaffer requested authorization to purchase a replacement drive gearbox for Secondary Clarifier No. 2 at the Water Pollution Control Facility. The existing gearbox (original to the 1997 plant upgrade) has shown rising vibration readings since July and was found during the September oil change to have bronze particles in the oil, indicating gear wear. Clarifier No. 2 remains in service at reduced torque; Clarifier No. 1 can carry average daily flow but not wet-weather peaks.

Three written quotes were received:

- Manufacturer's authorized representative, $41,780.00, 9-week lead time
- Industrial gear rebuilder (rebuild of existing unit), $33,950.00, 6-week lead time, 1-year warranty
- Second distributor, $44,210.00, 11-week lead time

Superintendent Shaffer recommended the new unit from the manufacturer's representative, citing the 3-year warranty and that the rebuild option would take the clarifier out of service for the full six weeks. Director Marquardt asked whether the rebuilt unit could be kept as a spare; Superintendent Shaffer said the existing housing is cracked at one mounting ear and he would not rely on it.

Motion by Director Wiechers, second by Director Marquardt, to authorize the purchase from the low responsive quote meeting specifications, in the amount of $41,780.00, from the Sanitary Sewer Operating Fund. Motion carried 4-0.

7. SANITARY SEWER - FOURTH WARD INTERCEPTOR, SEGMENT 4-7

Director Wiechers presented Resolution No. BOC 2026-41, declaring an emergency pursuant to Section 135.09 of the Codified Ordinances and awarding a contract for cured-in-place lining of Fourth Ward Interceptor Segment 4-7 to Vandersall Pipe Renewal LLC of Defiance, Ohio, in an amount not to exceed $480,000.00, without competitive bidding.

Director Wiechers reported that the fall closed-circuit television inspection of the Fourth Ward interceptor, completed in mid-September, found longitudinal and circumferential cracking, root intrusion and infiltration at joints in Segment 4-7, approximately 1,180 linear feet of 21-inch vitrified clay pipe installed in 1958 in the rear-lot easement between Pierce Street and Wayne Avenue. He stated that the segment carries most of the Fourth Ward's sanitary flow and that there is a risk of failure if rehabilitation is deferred through the winter and the spring high-flow season. He stated that preparing plans and specifications, advertising for two consecutive weeks and awarding under Section 135.03 would not allow the work to be done before cold weather.

Greg Vandersall, owner of Vandersall Pipe Renewal LLC, was present and stated that his crews could mobilize on September 30 and complete lining, service reinstatement and post-construction television inspection by October 16, 2026. The proposal includes bypass pumping, cleaning, lining, reinstatement of active service connections and manhole sealing.

Director Cline asked that the resolution set out the facts supporting the emergency determination, as Section 135.09 requires. Director Wiechers said the findings in the resolution were taken from the inspection results. Director Marquardt certified that funds are available in the Sanitary Sewer Capital Improvement Fund and stated that an amendment to the 2026 appropriation would be brought to Council for the balance of the year.

Motion by Director Cline, second by Director Marquardt, to adopt Resolution No. BOC 2026-41.

Roll call: Haverkamp, yes; Cline, yes; Marquardt, yes; Wiechers, yes. Motion carried 4-0.

8. WASTEWATER BIOSOLIDS FIELD - FARM LEASE RENEWAL

Superintendent Shaffer presented a renewal of the cash-rent farm lease for the 41-acre tillable portion of the City's biosolids application field on Domersville Road to Dale Wittenmyer, the current tenant, for crop years 2027 through 2029. Rent increases from $128.00 to $142.00 per acre per year ($5,822.00 annually), payable in two installments. The lease keeps the City's right to apply Class B biosolids after harvest under the WPCF's NPDES permit and requires the tenant to follow the City's nutrient management plan. Director Cline reported that the lease is in the City's standard form and that the term is within the three-year limit for leases approved by the Board without Council action.

Motion by Director Marquardt, second by Director Wiechers, to approve the lease renewal. Motion carried 4-0.

9. EMS - POWERED STRETCHER AND LOAD SYSTEMS

Lt. Sanderson, EMS Coordinator, requested authorization to purchase two powered stretcher and load systems for Medic 2 and Medic 3, completing the replacement program begun in 2025 with Medic 1. The purchase is through the manufacturer's national group purchasing agreement at a price of $68,412.00 for both units, including installation and a 7-year service plan. Lt. Sanderson said the powered systems have reduced lifting injuries in departments that use them and that the City's workers' compensation carrier offers a premium credit for them. The equipment is funded half from the EMS Capital Fund and half from a Bureau of Workers' Compensation safety grant of $34,206.00 awarded in July.

Motion by Director Wiechers, second by Director Cline, to authorize the purchase in an amount not to exceed $68,412.00. Motion carried 4-0.

10. GIS AND ASSET MANAGEMENT SOFTWARE - ANNUAL MAINTENANCE

Director Wiechers presented the annual maintenance and hosting renewal for the City's GIS and asset management software for the period October 1, 2026 through September 30, 2027, at $23,600.00, an increase of 3.1 percent over the prior year. The software holds the sanitary, storm and street asset inventories and work order history used by the Engineering, Street and Utilities departments. The cost is shared among the General, Street, Water, Sanitary Sewer and Stormwater funds according to the existing allocation.

Motion by Director Marquardt, second by Director Cline, to approve the renewal. Motion carried 4-0.

11. WINTER ROAD SALT - 2026-2027 SEASON

Street Superintendent Diehl reported that the City's participation in the ODOT winter salt contract for the 2026-2027 season has been confirmed at 1,400 tons, with a minimum purchase obligation of 80 percent and a maximum of 120 percent. The awarded price for Defiance County delivery is $71.84 per ton, down from $78.20 last season. The salt dome on Carter Road is currently at about 55 percent of capacity. Superintendent Diehl asked that the Board authorize blanket purchase orders up to 1,680 tons ($120,691.20) so that deliveries are not held up in January.

Motion by Director Wiechers, second by Director Marquardt, to authorize blanket purchase orders for road salt up to 1,680 tons at the ODOT contract price. Motion carried 4-0.

12. ACCEPTANCE OF DONATION - DOWNTOWN HOLIDAY LIGHTING

The Parks Department will order and install the lights. Director Cline noted that the Board may accept donations under $10,000.00 for a stated purpose.

Motion by Director Cline, second by Director Wiechers, to accept the donation with thanks, to be used for downtown holiday lighting. Motion carried 4-0.
