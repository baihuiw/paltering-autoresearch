3. STREET DEPARTMENT - LIQUID CALCIUM CHLORIDE, 2026-2027 WINTER SEASON

Street Superintendent Diehl presented the City's pricing under the Ohio Department of Transportation cooperative purchasing program for liquid calcium chloride (32 percent solution) for the 2026-2027 winter season. The calcium chloride is blended with salt brine for pre-wetting at the spreader and for anti-icing bridges and hills ahead of storms. The awarded vendor price delivered to the City salt dome is $1.06 per gallon, compared with $1.11 per gallon last season. The City's estimated use for the season is 26,000 gallons.

Diehl reported that the brine tanks at the salt dome currently hold approximately 4,500 gallons carried over from last season, and that the transfer pump and hoses were serviced in September. With the carryover and this season's order the City should be well positioned for an average winter.

Motion by Wiechers, second by Marquardt, to authorize the purchase of up to 26,000 gallons of liquid calcium chloride under the ODOT cooperative contract at $1.06 per gallon, not to exceed $27,560, from the Street Construction, Maintenance and Repair Fund. Ayes: Haverkamp, Cline, Marquardt, Wiechers. Nays: none. Motion carried.

4. STREET DEPARTMENT - FALL LEAF COLLECTION

Diehl reported that curbside leaf collection will begin Monday, October 26, and run through December 4, weather permitting, with crews working the city by ward in the published rotation. Leaf vacuum unit No. 2 needs a replacement impeller housing and fan bearings before the season. The dealer quote is $9,860 for parts and labor. Diehl noted the unit is 11 years old and recommended that replacement be included in the 2027 capital request.

Diehl also requested authority to hire up to four seasonal laborers for the leaf season at the established seasonal rate.

Motion by Wiechers, second by Cline, to approve repairs to leaf vacuum unit No. 2 at a cost not to exceed $9,860 and to authorize up to four seasonal laborers for the 2026 leaf season. Ayes: Haverkamp, Cline, Marquardt, Wiechers. Nays: none. Motion carried.

5. WATER TREATMENT PLANT - FILTER REHABILITATION AND CLEARWELL PROJECT, PAY APPLICATION NO. 5

City Engineer Dunlap presented Pay Application No. 5 from Hollister & Graf Mechanical, Inc. for work completed through September 30, 2026 on the Water Treatment Plant Filter Rehabilitation and Clearwell Improvements project. Work this period included completion of underdrain and media replacement in Filters 3 and 4, startup of the new air scour blower, and continued structural repairs in the north clearwell cell. Filters 1 and 2 remain in service. The project engineer has reviewed and recommends payment.

Gross amount of work completed this period: $214,600.00
Less 5 percent retainage: $10,730.00
Net amount due this application: $203,870.00

Dunlap reported that the project is approximately 58 percent complete by value and remains on schedule for substantial completion in May 2027. Change orders to date total $31,480, or 1.1 percent of the original contract amount of $2,846,000. The project is funded through the Ohio Water Development Authority loan approved in 2025 and the Water Capital Improvement Fund.

Motion by Marquardt, second by Wiechers, to approve Pay Application No. 5 to Hollister & Graf Mechanical, Inc. in the net amount of $203,870.00. Ayes: Haverkamp, Cline, Marquardt, Wiechers. Nays: none. Motion carried.

6. WATER TREATMENT PLANT - TREATMENT CHEMICALS, ANNUAL SUPPLY

Utilities Superintendent Shaffer reported on bids received September 30 for the annual supply of treatment chemicals for the period November 1, 2026 through October 31, 2027. Three bids were received for liquid sodium hypochlorite and two for ferric chloride.

Sodium hypochlorite (12.5 percent), estimated 46,000 gallons:
- Northwest Ohio Chemical Supply: $1.84 per gallon
- Lakeshore Water Products: $1.97 per gallon
- Midwest Process Chemicals: $2.11 per gallon

Ferric chloride, estimated 210 dry tons:
- Lakeshore Water Products: $612.00 per dry ton
- Midwest Process Chemicals: $648.50 per dry ton

Shaffer recommended award to the lowest bidder in each category. Both low bidders supplied the City in prior years without problems.

Motion by Wiechers, second by Marquardt, to award the annual sodium hypochlorite supply to Northwest Ohio Chemical Supply at $1.84 per gallon (estimated $84,640) and the annual ferric chloride supply to Lakeshore Water Products at $612.00 per dry ton (estimated $128,520), both from the Water Fund. Ayes: Haverkamp, Cline, Marquardt, Wiechers. Nays: none. Motion carried.

7. WASHINGTON AVENUE WATERLINE REPLACEMENT - CHANGE ORDER NO. 2

Dunlap presented Change Order No. 2 for the Washington Avenue Waterline Replacement project. During excavation near the intersection with Third Street, the contractor uncovered an abandoned 4-inch cast iron line not shown on City records, crossing the trench at the depth of the new main. Removing and capping the abandoned line and installing additional trench support required extra labor and materials.

Change Order No. 2: add $8,420.00. Revised contract amount: $612,935.00. No change to contract time.

Dunlap noted the abandoned line has been added to the City's GIS records.

Motion by Cline, second by Wiechers, to approve Change Order No. 2 in the amount of $8,420.00. Ayes: Haverkamp, Cline, Marquardt, Wiechers. Nays: none. Motion carried.

8. UTILITY BILLING - METER CLASS MULTIPLIER, PHASE 2 METER REPLACEMENT

Shaffer and Utility Billing Supervisor Pruett reported that the read multiplier for the billing system meter classes assigned to the phase 2 residential meters (HRE58 and HRE34) has been changed from 2 to 1, effective with reads taken on and after October 9, 2026. The phase 2 classes were originally set up from the phase 1 meter class. The October 1, 2026 quarterly billing for the approximately 1,140 phase 2 accounts was calculated using the prior multiplier.

Marquardt reported that the estimated total of adjustments for the phase 2 accounts, if processed for all affected accounts, is approximately $118,000 across the Water and Sewer Funds. She reviewed the current Water Fund cash position and the debt service coverage requirement on the Ohio Water Development Authority loan for the treatment plant project, and noted that third-quarter collections will be reviewed with Council's Finance Committee in November.

Discussion followed on processing options. Cline noted that the Board has authority under the utility billing rules to approve account adjustments.

Motion by Marquardt, second by Wiechers, that the corrected multiplier apply to all billings going forward and that adjustments for affected accounts be processed upon customer request through the Utility Billing Office. Ayes: Haverkamp, Cline, Marquardt, Wiechers. Nays: none. Motion carried.

9. UTILITY BILLING SYSTEM - ANNUAL SOFTWARE SUPPORT RENEWAL

Pruett presented the annual software support and maintenance renewal for the City's utility billing and customer information system for the period January 1 through December 31, 2027. The renewal includes software updates, hosting of the online payment portal, and telephone support. The quoted amount is $41,280, an increase of 3.5 percent over 2026.

Pruett noted that the vendor's handheld reading module will no longer be supported after 2027, which is not a concern once the meter replacement program is complete because all residential reads will be taken by radio.

Motion by Marquardt, second by Cline, to approve the 2027 software support renewal in the amount of $41,280, split equally between the Water Fund and Sewer Fund. Ayes: Haverkamp, Cline, Marquardt, Wiechers. Nays: none. Motion carried.

10. LEAD SERVICE LINE INVENTORY - ENGINEERING SERVICES AMENDMENT

Dunlap presented Amendment No. 1 to the professional services agreement for the City's lead service line inventory. The amendment adds field verification by test pit or camera at up to 220 additional service lines classified as "unknown" in the inventory, to support the next annual update to Ohio EPA. Amendment amount: $26,400. The work will be coordinated with the meter replacement program so that service line material observed at the meter setting during phase 3 is recorded and fewer test pits are needed.

Motion by Wiechers, second by Marquardt, to approve Amendment No. 1 in the amount of $26,400 from the Water Capital Improvement Fund. Ayes: Haverkamp, Cline, Marquardt, Wiechers. Nays: none. Motion carried.

11. PARKS - RIVERSIDE PARK SHELTER HOUSE ROOF

Parks Director Fenstermaker presented three quotes for replacement of the shingle roof on the Riverside Park shelter house, which was damaged in the August storm:

Motion by Cline, second by Wiechers, to award the Riverside Park shelter house roof replacement to Kuhlman Roofing in the amount of $23,800. Ayes: Haverkamp, Cline, Marquardt, Wiechers. Nays: none. Motion carried.
