This report is submitted under the City's investment policy (adopted by Council, last amended 2023) and Ohio Revised Code Chapter 135. All investments listed below are permitted under the policy. The City has no investments in derivatives, reverse repurchase agreements, or any security with a maturity over five years.

The balance rose about $1.23 million during the quarter, mostly from the second-half real estate tax settlement in September and normal income tax receipts. During the quarter we bought one Treasury bill ($1 million par, maturing December 10) to cover the December bond principal and interest payments, and one Farm Credit note ($1 million, 3.95%, three-year) to replace a maturing agency note. One FHLB note matured on August 14 ($1 million, 3.80%) and was rolled into the September purchase.

Short-term rates have come down slightly since spring. STAR Ohio was 4.32 percent at the end of September, compared with 4.51 percent at the end of June. I expect investment income for 2026 to finish about 15 percent above the budget estimate, and I am budgeting 2027 investment income about 20 percent lower than the 2026 projection.
