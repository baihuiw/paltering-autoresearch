**Date:** October 2, 2026

## 1. Overview

The department runs two stations with three shifts, staffing Engine 1, Engine 2, Ladder 1, Medic 1 and Medic 2 every day, and Medic 3 when staffing allows (cross-staffed at minimum). We have 37 sworn members below the Chief, plus one administrative assistant. The Fire Prevention Bureau has two inspectors (Lt. Mayes and FF/Inspector Heidt).

Our call volume keeps rising, and almost all of the increase is EMS.

| | 2021 | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 (projected) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total runs | 3,912 | 4,251 | 4,428 | ~4,600 |
| EMS share | 69% | 71% | 72% | 72% |
| 90th percentile response (all emergencies) | 7:48 | 8:02 | 8:05 | 8:40 (Sept) |

Through September 2026 we have 3,447 runs; September alone was 392 runs (284 EMS). The median response inside the city is still under five minutes. The 90th percentile has crept up because of simultaneous calls: when both front-line medics are out, the third call waits for Medic 3 or a cross-staffed engine. That is the main operational point for the levy renewal work session on October 27, and it's reflected in this request.

Using 2025's operating budget divided by 2025 runs, our average cost per run is about $1,040.

## 3. Explanations

**Overtime (+$28,000).** We have been over our overtime budget three years running and covered it by moving money at year end. This year we're at 412 hours for September alone (371 in September 2025), mostly backfilling two firefighters on injury leave and members in a paramedic class. Both injured members are expected back in October. The 2027 figure is our three-year average plus the wage increase. It is not a padded number.

**Fire prevention supplies (+$3,000).** The home smoke alarm program installed 312 alarms through September (265 through August and 47 in September). Sign-ups at the fall events are running well ahead of last year. The $12,000 state grant and the $4,500 supplies line will not cover the remaining requests this year, and the 2027 grant cycle doesn't open until January. I'm asking to raise the alarms portion of the supplies line from $4,500 to $7,500. That would cover about 100 more installs in a typical year.

**Operating supplies (+$4,500).** EMS supply prices are up about 6 percent on our main contract. The annual hose test in late September failed 9 lengths of 1-3/4 inch and 2 lengths of 5-inch supply hose. Replacement is about $4,860, and I'd like to do it in 2027 rather than as a mid-year transfer.

**Apparatus repair and maintenance (+$8,000).** Ladder 1 has been out of service since September 24 with a main lift cylinder leak. The $18,400 repair and annual aerial certification is on the Board of Control agenda for October 5, and the Toledo shop can take the unit the week of October 12. That cost falls in 2026. But the ladder is 19 years old, Engine 2 is due for replacement (CIP FR-27-01, $890,000, order in 2027 for 2028 delivery), and older apparatus costs more to keep running. The increase is a realistic estimate, not a one-time item.

**Training (+$1,000).** Required continuing education for paramedics and the state-required fire officer hours. One recruit academy is assumed (Firefighter I/II) for the eligibility list certified in September. The State Fire Marshal training grant has covered half of the tuition in each of the last two years, and we will apply again.

## 4. Fire Prevention Bureau notes

- The new inspection software went live September 14 with about 1,140 occupancies. Building and Zoning joins in January (its module is on the October 5 Board agenda). There is no new cost to the Fire budget in 2027 beyond our share of annual support.
- Inspections: 44 in September, 361 year to date, with two inspectors.
- The inspection fee schedule has not been updated since 2019. Lt. Mayes put a comparison with five similar Ohio cities in the September FPB report. We are not proposing a change in this budget. If the Mayor and Council want to take it up, it would be a 2027 ordinance.
- Senior Services Initiative: the FPB will continue free alarm installs, File of Life packets and talks at the senior buildings. Supplies come from the line above.
