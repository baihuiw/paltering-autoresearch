1. WHAT WE DO

Parks and Recreation maintains 14 parks and about 310 acres of parkland. That includes Kingsbury Park, Pontiac Park, Fort Defiance Park, Riverside Park and boat ramp, Hammond Street Park, and the riverfront trail. We also run the municipal pool, shelter house rentals, youth programs, and the fall and holiday events downtown and at Kingsbury. Since 2023 the City Arborist has been part of this department; the arborist schedules right-of-way tree work with the Street Department.

Staff: 9 full-time (Director, Recreation Coordinator, Arborist, Parks Foreman, 5 maintenance workers) plus seasonal staff. Seasonal staff peak at about 24 in July (pool and grounds) and drop to 2 to 4 in the fall.

3. LINES OVER 5 PERCENT OR $5,000

Personal services - full time (+$28,650). Contractual increases of 2.75% for AFSCME and 2.5% for non-bargaining staff, plus step increases for two maintenance workers hired in 2025.

Seasonal wages (+$8,400). This is the reason for v2. The seasonal rate was raised to $13.25 for 2026 to keep up with the minimum wage and with the jobs our lifeguards can get elsewhere. We still opened the pool on June 6 one guard short. For 2027 I'm asking for $13.75 for returning lifeguards and grounds staff and $13.25 for first-year staff. If we can't fill the lifeguard schedule, we either cut pool hours or run guards on overtime, and both cost more than this.

Utilities (+$5,200, +12.7%). Two things. The Hammond Street Park restroom reopens in late October (tentatively October 26) with year-round heat in the plumbing chase and two new water heaters. I estimate $2,900 a year in added gas, electric and water. The other $2,300 is the 4% electric increase on the rest of the system plus the pool pumps.

Repairs and maintenance (+$1,800). The playground at Pontiac Park is due for replacement in the capital plan (PK-27-02, 2028), and until then we're patching it. This year we replaced two swing hangers, a slide section and about 60 feet of border timber. I'd rather budget for that honestly than move money in the fall.


4. REDUCTIONS AND CHANGES

- Fuel is down slightly. We sold one older pickup in the spring and did not replace it.
- We stopped renting the second aerial lift for the downtown holiday lights. The merchants' group donated $2,500 for new LED strands this year (accepted by the Board September 28). The new strands are lighter and can be hung from our own bucket truck by borrowing Street Department time.
- Program guide printing moves to online-only, except for 300 copies for the Senior Center and the library. Savings about $1,100.

These are in the capital request due October 9, but they affect the operating budget:
