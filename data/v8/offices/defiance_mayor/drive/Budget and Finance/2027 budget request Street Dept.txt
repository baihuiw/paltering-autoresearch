WHAT WE DO

Street maintenance and repair, snow and ice control, fall leaf collection, street sweeping, signs and pavement markings, traffic signal maintenance (with contractor), alley maintenance, right-of-way mowing, brush drop-off at the Island Park Ave compost site on Saturdays, and help for every other department when they need a truck, a backhoe or barricades. This month alone that means the Homecoming parade on the 3rd, the Pumpkin Run and Harvest on the Riverfront on the 10th, and the trunk-or-treat setup at Fort Defiance Park on the 17th.

Resurfacing is not in this budget. It is paid from the Street Levy Fund and managed by Engineering. My crews do the prep work: adjusting castings ahead of the contractor, and patching.

Staff: 16 full-time (Superintendent, Foreman, mechanic, 13 operators/laborers). One Equipment Operator retires October 30 after 31 years and I'd like the position filled by January, before the snow. Up to four seasonal laborers for leaf season (I'm taking that to the Board on October 12) and two to four in summer.

The total is down only because the dump truck ($195,000 budgeted, $187,465 actual, approved September 28) was a one-time item in 2026. Without it, the operating request is up $86,100, or 3.6%.


1. SALT AND DEICING (-$8,000)

Good news for once. The ODOT contract price for this winter is $71.84/ton, down from $78.20. Our commitment is 1,400 tons (minimum purchase 80%, max 120%). 1,400 tons x $71.84 = $100,576. The Board authorized blanket POs up to 1,680 tons on September 28 in case it's a bad winter.

Liquid calcium chloride for the brine: ODOT co-op price is $1.06/gal (was $1.11). Estimate 26,000 gallons, about $27,560. That's going on the October 12 Board agenda. We have about 4,500 gallons carried over in the tanks at the salt dome.

So 2027 = $100,576 salt + $27,560 calcium chloride + about $3,900 bagged ice melt for the City Building, stations and park walks = about $132,000. These are 2026-27 season prices. If the 2027-28 price goes back up we'll need a transfer next fall.

The salt dome on Carter Road was about 55% full at the end of September. The fabric cover is near the end of its life (ST-27-12, $64,000, planned 2028). I'm not asking to move it up, but please keep it in 2028.


2. VEHICLE AND EQUIPMENT PARTS/REPAIR (+$6,000)

Our fleet is old. Unit 22 (2009 dump truck) failed its annual inspection for frame rust and will be kept in reserve this winter until the new truck arrives, 10 to 14 months out. Leaf vacuum No. 2 needs an impeller housing and fan bearings before the season ($9,860, also going to the Board on October 12). It is 11 years old and I'm putting a replacement in the capital request. Truck #9, the aerial bucket truck (2008), has worn boom hydraulics. It's in the CIP for 2027 (ST-27-13, $168,000) and I'm asking that it stay there. We use it for signals, banners, holiday lights and tree work.

The sweeper also needed parts this summer ($1,912.60 on a then-and-now in September). It's due for replacement in 2028 (ST-27-14).


3. OVERTIME (+$4,000)

Overtime is weather. Last winter was average and we used $86,400. I've budgeted for an average winter plus the wage increase. Leaf season OT has been about $14,000 a year. The leaf schedule is the same as last year: three passes starting October 26, each pass going First, Second, Third, then Fourth Ward.


4. STATE HIGHWAY FUND (202)

No change except salt on state routes inside the city, which is charged to 202 by lane miles (about 7%). Requested $175,000, same as 2026.


5. CONTRACT SERVICES (+$3,500)

Traffic signal maintenance contract (annual, 22 signalized intersections) plus long-line striping. The striping contractor's price is up 3%. The Ralston/Clinton signal controller (1998 cabinet) is in the CIP for 2027 at $38,000. We spent about $2,100 on it this year in after-hours service calls, and I'd like it replaced before it fails in a storm.


6. SIGNS (+$2,000)

The retroreflectivity replacement program (ST-27-09) is $10,000 a year from the levy fund. This line is for knockdowns, theft and new signs requested by Engineering. We replaced 61 stop and yield signs through September.


THINGS I'M NOT ASKING FOR BUT SHOULD MENTION

- A second mechanic. Our mechanic works on about 60 units, including Parks and some Water. I'll bring it up at the meeting with the Mayor.
- Stump grinding. Right now we grind stumps after the tree contractor takes trees out of the right-of-way. That's about 60 stumps this year with the ash removals. A contractor would be faster, but it would cost about $9,000 that isn't in anyone's budget.
