1. SUMMARY

Through September, nine months or 75.0 percent of the year:

- General Fund revenue is $11,320,240.48, or 77.1 percent of the annual estimate. Expenditures are $10,733,755.96, or 71.7 percent of appropriations. The cash balance is $5,870,601.94, up $586,484.52 from January 1. The unencumbered balance is $5,272,487.74.
- The County Auditor distributed the second-half real estate tax settlement on September 11. The General Fund received $387,513.28, the Street Levy Fund $652,840.00, and the Fire/EMS Levy Fund $714,912.40. Property tax revenue in all three funds is now close to the annual estimates. The small balance comes in with the December rollback and homestead reimbursements.
- Municipal income tax collections (all funds) through September are $10,760,696.38. That is 2.8 percent more than the same period of 2025 and 1.7 percent above the year-to-date budget estimate. September collections were $1,148,216.38, up 2.3 percent from September 2025. Third-quarter estimated payments were in line with last year.
- The Board of Control approved two large Street Fund purchases on September 28: the replacement single-axle dump truck ($187,465.00) and the road salt blanket orders for the winter (up to $120,691.20). Both are now encumbered, so the Street Fund's unencumbered balance fell from $1,134,781.31 in August to $813,449.55. The dump truck will not be delivered until 2027. Its encumbrance will carry over and be paid from 2026 money.
- Water Operating Fund cash is $944,717.66. It normally reaches its low point in September, before the October quarterly billing is collected. See section 7.
- Ordinance No. 2026-45 (Ladder 1 repair, leaf vacuum and seasonal laborers, Riverside Park shelter roof insurance proceeds) is on Council's October 13 agenda as the October appropriation amendment, with a request to suspend the rules so it takes effect that night. After that, I expect one final amendment in November for year-end adjustments to personal services lines.

7. WATER AND SEWER FUNDS

Water Operating cash at September 30 was $944,717.66, compared with $1,169,504.01 at August 31 and $1,082,330.18 at September 30, 2025. September is the low point of the quarterly billing cycle: July bills have been collected and the next quarterly billing had not yet gone out. The new rates under Ordinance No. 2026-38 (+6 percent) apply to bills issued on or after October 1, 2026, so this year's revenue includes only the fourth quarter at the new rates.

The OWDA loan for the Water Treatment Plant Filter Rehabilitation and Clearwell Improvements project is in the construction draw phase. The Water Capital Improvement Fund pays the contractor and is reimbursed by OWDA; the September draw was submitted September 18 and is in process. Pay Application No. 5 will be paid in October. Repayment of that loan does not begin until after the final draw (estimated mid-2028).

Debt service coverage for 2026 under the OWDA loan agreements has to be at least 1.15 times. I will present the year-end projection to the Finance Committee in November, after October collections are in.

Sanitary Sewer Operating cash was $1,947,036.60, which is healthy, but $412,606.96 of annual debt service on OWDA Loan No. 5841 is paid from this fund and the WPCF has capital needs coming (aeration blowers in the five-year plan).
