1. SUMMARY

Through September, nine months or 75.0 percent of the year:

- General Fund revenue is $11,320,240.48, or 77.1 percent of the annual estimate. Expenditures are $10,733,755.96, or 71.7 percent of appropriations. The cash balance is $5,870,601.94, up $586,484.52 from January 1.
- The County Auditor distributed the second-half real estate tax settlement on September 11: General Fund $387,513.28, Street Levy Fund $652,840.00, Fire/EMS Levy Fund $714,912.40.
- Municipal income tax collections (all funds) through September are $10,760,696.38, 2.8 percent more than the same period of 2025 and 1.7 percent above the year-to-date estimate. September collections were $1,148,216.38, up 2.3 percent from September 2025.
- The Board of Control approved two large Street Fund purchases on September 28: the replacement single-axle dump truck ($187,465.00) and the road salt blanket orders (up to $120,691.20). Both are encumbered, so the Street Fund's unencumbered balance fell from $1,134,781.31 in August to $813,449.55. The dump truck will not be delivered until 2027; its encumbrance carries over.
- Ordinance No. 2026-45 (Ladder 1 repair, leaf vacuum and seasonal laborers, Riverside Park shelter roof insurance proceeds) is on Council's October 13 agenda as the October appropriation amendment. I expect one final amendment in November for year-end personal services adjustments.
- Pooled cash and investments for all funds totaled $24,104,035.99 at September 30.

All departments are under the 75 percent mark. Projected General Fund spending for the year is $14.61 million, about 97.5 percent of appropriations. Fire/EMS is the one to watch: September overtime was 412 hours (371 in September 2025), mostly covering injury leave and a paramedic class. If both injured firefighters return in October as expected, the line should finish within its appropriation, but only just. The Mayor's Office line reflects about two weeks with no mayor's salary in September.


4. REVENUE PROJECTION

I project General Fund revenue for the year at $15.02 million, about $340,000 over the $14.68 million estimate, driven by income tax, investment income and permits. That is up from the $14.97 million shown at the October 6 Committee of the Whole, mainly because of September income tax. I am not recommending an amended certificate at this time. Fourth-quarter withholding can move, and the 2027 budget should not count on this year's investment yields continuing.

Income tax is distributed 80 percent to the General Fund and 20 percent to the Capital Improvement Fund. Debt service coverage under the OWDA loan agreements must be at least 1.15 times; I will bring the year-end projection to the Finance Committee in November.
