Nancy, Kyle,

Answers below. I'll have the same in the handout tomorrow. I'm copying Megan so the Mayor has it for his briefing binder. He and I went through income tax and the general budget status this morning.

1. Miscellaneous revenue ($140,225.34 against $112,000). The pieces over a normal year are the workers' comp group rating rebate in May ($31,880.00) and the insurance reimbursement for the City Building roof drain repair ($18,614.50). There are also two smaller items: a $6,200 refund from the electric aggregation reconciliation, and restitution paid through Municipal Court for damage to a park restroom last year ($2,350). Take those out and we're right at budget.

2. Street Levy. Suggested sentence: "The 2026 resurfacing contract was awarded in August at $1,184,600 and is fully set aside for that work; it starts the first week of October, so the money will be spent this fall, with the levy balance going to the 2027 program." The levy also paid the Ralston Avenue work this summer.

3. Water Fund. Not worried, but watching. Cash is lower mainly because of timing on the OWDA reimbursements for the treatment plant filter project. We pay the contractor first and OWDA reimburses us, usually within 30 to 45 days. Coverage for the year has to be 1.15 times debt service, and the projection meets it once fourth-quarter billing at the new rate is collected. I'll show the Committee the coverage calculation in November, when we have October and November collections in hand. That's when I'd tell you if there's a problem, and I don't expect one.

4. Police overtime: dollars. Hours are up a bit more than that (about 8%) because the officers working the overtime are on average a little lower on the pay scale than last year.

5. Health insurance: not final. 7.8% is the consortium's preliminary number. The final comes the last week of October. Last year the preliminary was 8.5% and the final was 6.9%, so I'm hopeful, but I'm budgeting the preliminary.

Kyle: yes, I'll attach the FIN-110 all-funds report to the handout. It's three pages. Most of the "other" funds are the utility capital funds, bond retirement, EMS capital, and a dozen small restricted funds (law enforcement trust, court computer, FEMA reimbursement, etc.) that carry a few thousand dollars each. And you're right about Safety-Service. I wrote that sentence before the August payroll posted and didn't fix it. I'll correct it in the file copy.

Calendar copies: will do, 10 copies. The draft has operating requests due October 2 and capital requests October 9. The draft has the two Committee of the Whole sessions on Tuesday, October 6 and Wednesday, October 21.

Rita - I'll check the 20th with the Mayor. And good idea on the paving question. I'll put a one-page "where the money comes from" summary in the budget packet. The short version: resurfacing is paid from the street levy, not income tax; the Capital Improvement Fund (20% of income tax) pays for things like park projects, vehicles and buildings; and the Street Fund (gas tax and license fees) pays for plowing, patching and the street crew. More income tax doesn't directly mean more paving, but it does help the capital plan.
