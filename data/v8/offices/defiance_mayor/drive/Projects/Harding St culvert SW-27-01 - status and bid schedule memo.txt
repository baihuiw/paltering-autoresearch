RE:      Harding Street Culvert Replacement (Hubbard Ditch), CIP SW-27-01 - current condition, temporary measures and bid schedule

----------------------------------------------------------------------
1. BACKGROUND
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Harding Street crosses the Hubbard ditch between Ralston Avenue and Spruce Street on a 60-inch corrugated metal pipe culvert installed, as best we can tell from the street records, in 1967. The pipe is about 46 feet long with concrete headwalls at each end. Average daily traffic on Harding at this location is about 1,900 vehicles (2024 count). It is a residential collector and a school bus route.

During the routine culvert inspection in August, the Street Department found that the invert of the pipe had corroded through for roughly 14 feet near the downstream end, and that the soil above the pipe had started to settle into the void. A depression about 3 feet across had formed in the eastbound lane over the pipe.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
2. TEMPORARY MEASURES IN PLACE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

- August 27: Street Department excavated the soft area, filled it with flowable fill (low-strength concrete) and placed a 1-inch steel plate (8 ft x 12 ft) pinned and ramped with cold patch over the eastbound lane.
- The westbound lane over the pipe shows no settlement. We are checking both lanes weekly. The Street Department checks the plate after every rain over one inch.
- Weight limit: none posted. The plate and fill are carrying normal traffic, including school buses and garbage trucks. If we see movement at the plate, the fallback is to close the eastbound lane and run one-lane alternating traffic with temporary signals, which the Street Department has on hand.
- Flow: the pipe is still carrying the full ditch flow. There is no sign of the bottom washing out further since the fill was placed. I walked it after the September 18 rain (1.3 inches at the WPCF gauge). No change.

Ron's crew has taken photographs at each check. They are in the project folder.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
3. REPLACEMENT DESIGN
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Engineering is designing the replacement in-house. Proposed structure:

- Precast concrete box culvert, 6 ft span x 4 ft rise, 48 ft long, with new precast headwalls and wingwalls
- Riprap at the outlet; bank grading 20 feet upstream and downstream
- Full-depth pavement replacement across the trench, curb and gutter replacement both sides, sidewalk panels where disturbed
- Relocation of a 2-inch gas service and a telephone cable crossing (utility owners notified Sept 10)
- Water main: the 8-inch main crosses above the culvert on the north side and will be supported in place, not relocated

Hydraulics: the 6 x 4 box has more capacity than the 60-inch pipe and passes the 25-year storm with 1.2 feet of freeboard. The ditch is a county-maintained petition ditch upstream of Ralston; I've sent the plans to the county engineer's office as a courtesy, and they have no objection.

Permits: the work is within the existing culvert footprint and does not need individual Corps of Engineers authorization. We will follow the general conditions for maintenance of an existing structure and the Ohio EPA construction stormwater general permit (disturbed area is under one acre, so a notice of intent is not required, but we will use the same erosion control details).

Engineer's estimate: $84,000, which matches the CIP number. Breakdown:

  Precast box, headwalls and wingwalls (furnish and set)  $41,500
  Excavation, removal of existing pipe and headwalls       $11,200
  Bedding and backfill                                     $ 7,400
  Pavement, curb and walk                                  $12,600
  Riprap and bank grading                                  $ 4,300
  Traffic control and detour                               $ 3,500
  Mobilization and erosion control                         $ 3,500
                                                           -------
                                                           $84,000

4. PROCUREMENT AND SCHEDULE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

At about $84,000 this is above the competitive bidding threshold, so it has to be advertised and bid. Steve reviewed the schedule with me on September 24 and will review the contract documents before we advertise. The temporary plate is holding, which gives us time to do the plans, advertisement and bid opening in the normal sequence.

Schedule:

  Plans and specifications complete .............. October 23
  Law Director review of contract documents ....... by October 30
  Advertise ....................................... Thursday, November 5
  Pre-bid meeting (optional, on site) ............. Tuesday, November 17, 10:00 a.m.
  Bid opening ..................................... Tuesday, November 24, 2:00 p.m.
  Board of Control award .......................... December 7 (target)
  Precast shop drawings / fabrication ............. December - March
  Construction (road closed approx. 3 weeks) ...... April 2027

Why April and not December: precast lead times are running 10 to 14 weeks, and we would rather not pour or backfill in freezing weather. Bidding in November lets the contractor order the box over the winter and gives us bid prices before the 2027 budget is final.

The road will be closed at the culvert for about three weeks in April. The detour will use Ralston Avenue and Spruce Street. We will coordinate with the school district transportation office in February.
