TRAFFIC SIGNAL REVIEW
Intersection: Ralston Avenue at Clinton Street
CIP reference: ST-27-02 (Ralston Ave / Clinton St signal controller replacement)
Prepared by: Rachel Dunlap, P.E., City Engineer, with traffic counts by Street Department
Status: DRAFT for internal review - October 2, 2026
Distribution: Tom Wiechers; Ron Diehl; Angela Marquardt (cost only)

======================================================================
1. WHY THIS STUDY
======================================================================

The signal cabinet and controller at Ralston and Clinton were installed in 1998. Since spring 2026 the controller has gone into flash six times (March 3, April 22, June 9, July 14, August 2 and September 11). Each time the Street Department reset it within 30 to 90 minutes. Parts for this model are no longer made, and the last spare board was used in July.

The CIP carries $38,000 in 2027 to replace the cabinet and controller. Before that money is spent, Tom asked whether the intersection still needs a signal, whether the timing is right, and what low-cost fixes could be made at the same time.

======================================================================
2. EXISTING CONDITIONS
======================================================================

Clinton Street (north-south): four lanes, 35 mph, no turn lanes at Ralston. Average daily traffic about 14,200 (2025 count).
Ralston Avenue (east-west): two lanes, 25 mph west of Clinton, 35 mph east. Average daily traffic about 6,400 east leg, 4,900 west leg. Ralston from Fifth Street to Elliott Road was resurfaced this summer (paved September 11). Pavement markings were replaced in kind: center line, edge lines and stop bars. There are no bike lanes on Ralston, and none were part of that project.

Signal equipment:
- 1998 cabinet and semi-actuated controller (Ralston on loops); the eastbound loop failed in 2022 and that phase has run on recall since
- Pedestrian signals without countdown; push buttons not accessible (no locator tone)
- LED vehicle heads (2014); mast arms in fair condition (2024 inspection)

Pedestrian activity is moderate: walking route to the pharmacy on Ralston and two bus stops. A twelve-hour count found 87 crossings, most of them across Clinton.

Crash history, 2021 through August 2026 (police reports): 23 crashes, 9 with injury, none fatal. 11 angle, 7 rear-end, 3 left-turn, 2 sideswipe. 4 of the 11 angle crashes happened while the signal was in flash or dark.

======================================================================
4. OPERATIONS
======================================================================

Current timing: 90-second cycle all day. Clinton green 52 seconds, Ralston green 26, yellow 4.0, all-red 1.0 on both streets.

Field observations, Sept 15-17:
- With the eastbound loop on recall, Ralston eastbound gets a full green every cycle, even at night.
- PM peak: westbound Ralston queues reached 11 vehicles and failed to clear in two cycles.
- Northbound Clinton left turns block the inside through lane (no left-turn lane).
- All-red of 1.0 second is short for a 64-foot crossing at 35 mph; guidance gives about 2.0 seconds.
- Pedestrian clearance across Clinton is 14 seconds; the minimum for 64 feet is 18.

Level of service (Synchro): existing B / B / C (AM / midday / PM); with new controller, video detection and revised timing, delay drops about 3 seconds in each period.

======================================================================
5. RECOMMENDATIONS
======================================================================

A. Replace the cabinet and controller (CIP ST-27-02). Estimate $38,000:
     NEMA TS2 cabinet and controller, furnished and installed ....... $24,500
     Uninterruptible power supply (battery backup) .................. $ 6,200
     Foundation and conduit modifications ........................... $ 3,800
     Signal timing, testing and contingency ......................... $ 3,500

B. Video detection on all four approaches instead of repairing loops, about $14,000 more (project total $52,000). Tom - your call whether to raise the CIP amount or do video in 2028.

C. Retime at installation: all-red 2.0 seconds on Clinton and 1.5 on Ralston; pedestrian clearance 18 seconds across Clinton; 70-second cycle off-peak and free operation at night.

D. Countdown pedestrian heads and accessible push buttons at all four corners, about $9,500, installed by the Street Department electrician during the cabinet change.

E. Not recommended now: a northbound left-turn lane on Clinton (needs widening and right-of-way at the southeast corner).
