# MEMORANDUM - DRAFT

**To:** Tom Wiechers, Safety-Service Director
**From:** Rachel Dunlap, P.E., City Engineer
**Cc:** Craig Fenstermaker (street trees, planters); Ron Diehl (maintenance)
**Date:** September 29, 2026
**Re:** Downtown Clinton Street streetscape - concept options for discussion (not a project yet)

---

Tom,

You asked after the downtown merchants' group meeting for a short write-up on what a streetscape project on Clinton Street downtown could look like, what it might cost, and how it would fit with things already in the capital plan. This is a concept memo only. Nothing here has been designed, and there's no money in the 2027 requests for it beyond the crosswalk item that's already in the CIP for 2029 (ST-27-08).

## 1. Study area

Clinton Street from the river bridge approach to Fifth Street, about 0.45 mile, plus the first half block of the side streets (First through Fifth). This is the downtown commercial corridor in the First Ward. On-street parking both sides, four travel lanes north of Third, two lanes plus parking south of Third.

## 2. What's there now

- **Sidewalks:** 12 to 14 feet wide, mix of brick paver bands (1990s) and plain concrete. Pavers are heaving at tree pits on about a third of the blocks. Ron's crew resets 20 to 40 pavers a year.
- **Street trees:** 46 trees in grates. 11 are dead or in poor condition (City Arborist list, August). Most are Callery pears from the 1990s project, which split in storms.
- **Lighting:** acorn-style pedestrian poles from the same project, 38 poles. Several fixtures have cracked globes; replacement globes are no longer stocked. The poles are on an old circuit that trips during heavy rain.
- **Crosswalks:** painted, worn by midsummer every year. Six marked crossings at intersections, none mid-block.
- **Furnishings:** benches and trash cans don't match block to block; several benches were donated over the years.
- **Holiday lighting:** outlets on about half the poles. The merchants' group donated $2,500 this year for holiday lights. They would like outlets on all poles.

## 3. Three concept levels

### Option A - Repair and refresh (about $180,000 - $240,000)

- Replace the 11 failing trees with a better species (honeylocust, hornbeam) and larger structural soil pits
- Reset heaved pavers, replace with concrete bands where pavers are beyond saving
- Retrofit the 38 pedestrian lights with LED fixtures that fit the existing poles; repair the circuit
- Add outlets to remaining poles for holiday lights
- New matching trash and recycling receptacles (18)
- High-visibility thermoplastic crosswalk markings at all six crossings

Could be phased over two or three years from operating and Capital Improvement Fund money. Little or no disruption to businesses.

### Option B - Targeted improvements (about $650,000 - $900,000)

Everything in A, plus:
- Bump-outs (curb extensions) at the Second and Third Street intersections to shorten crossing distance and add space for seating
- New crosswalks with decorative stamped asphalt or pavers set in concrete at four intersections
- Rapid flashing beacon at the mid-block crossing between Second and Third (people already cross there to the library parking)
- Planters and seating at the bump-outs

This is close to what is listed as ST-27-08 (crosswalk upgrades, $96,000, 2029), but bigger. A federal Transportation Alternatives (TAP) application could cover up to 80 percent of the eligible pedestrian items. The next application window is spring 2028.

### Option C - Full reconstruction (about $3.2 - $4.0 million)

Full-width reconstruction of sidewalks, new storm sewer and inlets, new lighting and conduit, possible lane reconfiguration north of Third (four lanes to three with a center turn lane), utility upgrades. This would need ODOT involvement because Clinton carries state route traffic, a multi-year design, and outside funding. Realistically a 2030 or later project. I'm including it so the range is clear; I don't recommend it as a near-term goal.

## 4. Things that would come up regardless

- **Water main.** The North Clinton Street water main (WA-27-02, 2028) is north of the study area, but the downtown main is also old (1930s in places). Any Option C would have to include the water main. Option A and B do not affect it.
- **Parking.** Bump-outs cost about one space each. Option B as sketched removes 6 of about 110 on-street spaces. The merchants were mixed on this when Craig raised it informally.
- **Maintenance.** Planters need watering in summer. Parks would need either a seasonal worker or a merchants' "adopt-a-planter" arrangement.
- **Snow.** Bump-outs change the plow pattern. Ron wants rolled curbs at the bump-outs or he'll be replacing them every winter.
- **Holiday lights and banners.** Any new pole should have banner arms and an outlet. Cheap to add at the start, expensive later.

## 5. Suggested next step

If the Mayor is interested, I'd suggest:

1. Fund a small piece of Option A in 2027 (trees and LED retrofit), maybe $60,000 - $80,000, from the Capital Improvement Fund. This fixes the most visible problems.
2. Hold a walk-through with the merchants' group in the spring to talk about Option B before we commit to a TAP application.
3. Ask the design consultant on our next on-call contract for a fee to prepare a concept plan and cost estimate for Option B, maybe $15,000 - $20,000, in 2027, so we'd be ready for the 2028 TAP window.

Happy to walk the corridor with you and the Mayor some morning. Early is better, before the parking fills.

Rachel
