# City of Defiance Parks and Recreation Master Plan

## Chapter 5 - Park-by-Park Recommendations (excerpt)

*Adopted by the Parks and Recreation Board; revised January 2025 to update cost ranges. Excerpt circulated by Craig Fenstermaker for 2027 capital planning, September 2026. Full plan on the S: drive under Parks\Master Plan.*

---

### 5.1 How to read this chapter

Each park section has four parts: what the park is today, what we heard from residents during the plan's public input, the recommended improvements, and a priority. Priorities are:

- **Priority 1** - needed for safety, accessibility or to keep a facility open; target within 3 years.
- **Priority 2** - meaningful improvement to use or condition; target within 3 to 6 years.
- **Priority 3** - desirable, depends on grants, donations or partnerships.

Cost ranges are planning-level, in 2025 dollars, and will change with design. They are not budgets. Projects move into the City's Capital Improvement Plan only when the Parks Director requests them and the Administration includes them.

### 5.2 Kingsbury Park

**Today.** Kingsbury is the largest of the riverfront parks and the City's main event space. It has the large lawn used for Harvest on the Riverfront and other gatherings, two shelters, a restroom building from the 1970s, a playground replaced in 2015, and the trailhead parking for the riverfront trail.

**What we heard.** The restroom was the single most common complaint in the survey (61 percent of Kingsbury users rated it "poor"). Residents also asked for more shade near the playground, better lighting along the walk to the parking lot, and electrical service at the event lawn so vendors do not have to run generators.

**Recommendations.**
1. Replace the restroom building with an accessible, year-round-capable restroom. Priority 1. $160,000 - $200,000. Pursue an ODNR NatureWorks grant.
2. Add two in-ground electrical pedestals at the event lawn. Priority 2. $25,000 - $40,000.
3. Shade sail or tree planting at the playground. Priority 2. $15,000 - $35,000.
4. Pedestrian-scale lighting from the parking lot to the shelters. Priority 2. $45,000 - $70,000.

### 5.3 Hammond Street Park

**Today.** A neighborhood park with two youth soccer fields, a small playground, one shelter and a restroom building. The fields are used by the youth league in spring and fall.

**What we heard.** Neighbors want the restroom fixed and kept open during league play. Parents asked for a walking loop around the fields and more benches. Several comments about drainage on the east field.

**Recommendations.**
1. Renovate the restroom interior: fixtures, partitions, accessibility, lighting. Priority 1. $120,000 - $160,000.
2. Walking loop (asphalt, about 0.4 mile). Priority 2. $85,000 - $110,000.
3. Regrade and add underdrains on the east field. Priority 3. $40,000 - $60,000.
4. Benches and trash receptacles along the loop. Priority 3. $8,000 - $12,000.

*Staff note (CF, 9/2026): item 1 under construction, completion October 2026.*

### 5.4 Riverside Park

**Today.** Boat ramp, courtesy dock, parking for trucks and trailers, one shelter house, fishing access and the downstream end of the riverfront trail.

**What we heard.** Anglers and boaters want the dock replaced and more trailer parking on summer weekends. Shelter users asked for a better roof and electrical outlets. Some comments about litter after weekends.

**Recommendations.**
1. Replace the courtesy dock. Priority 1. $80,000 - $110,000. ODNR Waterways grant (75 percent).
2. Shelter house roof replacement. Priority 2. $20,000 - $30,000.
3. Restripe the lot to add about 8 trailer stalls. Priority 2. $6,000 - $10,000.
4. Additional trash receptacles and a weekend pickup during the boating season. Priority 2. Operating cost.

*Staff note (CF, 9/2026): item 2 now being done this fall as a storm-damage repair through insurance.*

### 5.5 Pontiac Park

**Today.** Ballfields (three diamonds), playground, shelter, concession stand and restrooms. Used heavily by youth baseball and softball April through July and by adult softball in the summer. Start and finish for the Pumpkin Run and other road races.

**What we heard.** The playground is old and not accessible. Parents asked for shade at the bleachers. League organizers asked for a scoreboard on Field 2 and better infield mix.

**Recommendations.**
1. Replace the playground with an accessible design and poured-in-place surfacing. Priority 1. $200,000 - $260,000. Fundraising by the Parks Board with a City match.
2. Bleacher shade structures at Fields 1 and 2. Priority 2. $30,000 - $50,000.
3. Infield renovation, all three fields. Priority 2. $24,000 - $36,000.
4. Scoreboard on Field 2. Priority 3. Sponsorship.

### 5.6 Fort Defiance Park

**Today.** Historic site at the confluence, interpretive signs, lawn, walking paths. Low-intensity use, high visitor interest. Used for seasonal events such as trunk-or-treat.

**What we heard.** Visitors want better wayfinding from downtown and more interpretive content. Neighbors asked that events end at a reasonable hour.

**Recommendations.**
1. Wayfinding signs from Clinton Street and the riverfront trail. Priority 2. $12,000 - $20,000.
2. Refresh interpretive panels (several are faded). Priority 2. $18,000 - $28,000. Seek historical society partnership.
3. Path resurfacing. Priority 3. $35,000 - $50,000.

---

### 5.7 Riverfront Trail

**Today.** About 3.4 miles of paved multi-use trail along the river connecting Riverside, Kingsbury and Fort Defiance parks with downtown.

**What we heard.** The trail was the second most used facility in the survey after the municipal pool. Users reported cracking and root heaves on the older sections and asked for mile markers and more benches.

**Recommendations.**
1. Resurface the oldest 1.6 miles. Priority 2. $110,000 - $150,000. Clean Ohio Trails Fund.
2. Mile markers every quarter mile and emergency location signs (coordinated with Fire/EMS dispatch). Priority 1. $6,000 - $9,000.
3. Root barrier and patching at known heaves. Priority 1. Operating, about $8,000 per year.

---

### 5.8 Municipal Pool

**Today.** 50-meter pool with diving well and a zero-depth children's area; bathhouse and concession. Open early June through Labor Day.

**What we heard.** Strong support. Requests for more shade, longer hours in August, and a family changing room.

**Recommendations.**
1. Replace the pool liner and upgrade the pump room equipment. Priority 2. $350,000 - $450,000.
2. Family changing room in the bathhouse. Priority 2. $40,000 - $65,000.
3. Shade structures on the deck. Priority 3. $25,000 - $45,000.

---

### 5.9 System-wide recommendations

- **Shelter roofs.** Inventory shows three shelter roofs at or near end of life. Budget one per year. Priority 2.
- **Mowers and small equipment.** Replace the two oldest zero-turn mowers. Priority 2.
- **Accessibility.** Complete the accessible-route review at every park by the end of the plan's third year and fold findings into each park's list.
- **Volunteer program.** Formalize park cleanup days twice a year (spring and fall) with the service clubs and schools.
- **Tree canopy.** Continue replacement planting in parks where ash trees were removed; target at least 40 trees per year across the system.
