# Residential Water Meter Replacement Program
## Frequently Asked Questions

City of Defiance, Division of Utilities
Revised September 2, 2026 (phase 2 edition; replaces the March 2026 version)

This page is posted at cityofdefiance.com and printed copies are available at the Utility Billing Office counter. Counter and phone staff may use it to answer common questions.

---

### Why is the City replacing water meters?

Most residential water meters in Defiance were installed between 1988 and 2001. Mechanical meters wear over time and tend to under-record the amount of water passing through them, especially at low flows such as a dripping faucet or a running toilet. Many of the older meters also have to be read by hand, which means a meter reader has to get into the yard, the meter pit or, for inside meters, the basement.

The new meters are read by radio from a vehicle driving down the street. They are more accurate, they don't require anyone to enter your property for routine readings, and they record hourly usage that can help identify leaks.

### How many meters are being replaced, and when?

The program is replacing about 6,300 residential meters in three phases:

- Phase 1 (March - July 2026): about 3,410 meters, mainly in the First Ward and the western part of the Second Ward. Complete.
- Phase 2 (September 2026): about 1,190 meters in parts of the Second, Third and Fourth Wards (meter reading routes 14 through 21).
- Phase 3 (planned spring 2027): the remaining residential meters, about 1,700, in the rest of the Fourth Ward and the north side.

Commercial and industrial meters larger than 1 inch are handled separately by the City's own meter crew.

### Who is doing the work?

The installation contractor is Brandt Metering Services, LLC. Installers carry a photo ID badge and drive marked vehicles. If you are unsure about someone at your door, call the Utility Billing Office before letting them in.

### Do I need to be home?

If your meter is in an outdoor meter pit, no. The installer will knock, and if no one answers will proceed with the replacement and leave a door hanger.

If your meter is inside your home (usually in the basement), yes. You will receive a letter and a door hanger asking you to schedule an appointment. Appointments are available weekdays from 7:30 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. and some Saturday mornings.

### How long will my water be off?

Usually 15 to 30 minutes, a little longer if the water has to be shut off at the curb. Repairs to valves and plumbing on the customer's side of the meter are the property owner's responsibility.

### Is there a charge for the new meter?

No. The meters and installation are paid for by the Water Fund as part of the City's capital improvement plan. There is no separate charge on your bill.

### Will my bill change?

Your rates do not change because of the new meter. Because the new meter is more accurate than the old one, some customers, especially those whose old meters had been slowing down, may notice that recorded usage is somewhat higher than before. This reflects water that was being used but not fully recorded by the older meter.

Please note that water and sewer rates are increasing 6 percent with bills issued on or after October 1, 2026 under Ordinance 2026-38. That increase is separate from the meter program and applies to all customers.

Also keep in mind that third-quarter bills (July - September usage) are usually the highest of the year because of outdoor watering.

### What happens to the reading on my old meter?

The installer records the final reading on your old meter and photographs the register. That final reading is entered into the billing system so that your next bill includes all the water used on the old meter plus the water used on the new meter. You will not be billed twice for the same water.

### My bill seems higher than usual. What should I do?

Contact the Utility Billing Office. Staff can review your account's readings and usage history and help you check for common leaks. Running toilets are the most frequent cause of an unexpected jump in usage. A simple test: put a few drops of food coloring in the toilet tank, wait 15 minutes without flushing, and see whether color shows up in the bowl.

Once your new meter has been in place for a full billing period, staff will also be able to look at hourly usage to see whether water is running continuously, which usually points to a leak.

### Can I see my usage online?

Not yet. An online customer portal showing daily usage is planned once all three phases are complete. In the meantime, billing staff can provide usage information on request.

### What about lead service lines?

Meter replacement does not change your service line. During phase 3, installers will record the service line material at the meter setting to help complete the City's lead service line inventory.

### What if my property is damaged during installation?

Report it to the Utility Billing Office. Brandt is required by its contract to restore lawns and meter pit lids disturbed by its work.
