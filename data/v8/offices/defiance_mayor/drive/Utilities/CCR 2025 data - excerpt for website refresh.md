## A message from the Utilities Superintendent

We are pleased to present this year's Water Quality Report. It tells you where your water comes from, how it is treated, what we test for, and what we found. In 2025 the City's drinking water met all state and federal drinking water standards.

The City is in the middle of a major upgrade at the Water Treatment Plant. The filter rehabilitation and clearwell improvements project, funded through a low-interest Ohio Water Development Authority loan, is replacing the underdrains and media in all four filters and repairing the storage tank beneath the filter building. Construction started in 2026 and is scheduled to finish in 2027. The plant stays in service throughout.

If you have questions about this report or your water, please call the Water Treatment Plant at (419) 784-2101, ext. 260. You can also attend City Council meetings, held the second and fourth Tuesday of each month at 7:00 p.m. in Council Chambers, City Building, 631 Perry Street.

## Where does my water come from?

The City draws its water from the Maumee River through an intake at the Water Treatment Plant, with an upground reservoir for backup storage during low flow or poor river quality. The plant uses conventional treatment: coagulation with ferric chloride, lime softening, recarbonation, filtration through dual-media filters, and disinfection with chlorine. Powdered activated carbon is added in summer and fall for taste and odor and to remove algal toxins. Fluoride is added to help prevent tooth decay.

In 2025 the plant produced an average of 2.41 million gallons per day.

## Source water assessment

Ohio EPA completed a source water assessment for the City's intake. Because the source is a river, it is considered susceptible to contamination from agricultural runoff, municipal and industrial discharges upstream, accidental spills on roads and rail lines, and harmful algal blooms. The City's treatment is designed for these conditions and the plant monitors raw water continuously. A copy of the assessment is available at the Water Treatment Plant.

## Why are there contaminants in drinking water?

Drinking water, including bottled water, may reasonably be expected to contain at least small amounts of some contaminants. The presence of contaminants does not necessarily indicate that water poses a health risk. More information about contaminants and potential health effects can be obtained by calling the U.S. EPA's Safe Drinking Water Hotline (800-426-4791).

## Who needs to take special precautions?

Some people may be more vulnerable to contaminants in drinking water than the general population. Immuno-compromised persons such as persons with cancer undergoing chemotherapy, persons who have undergone organ transplants, people with HIV/AIDS or other immune system disorders, some elderly, and infants can be particularly at risk from infections. These people should seek advice about drinking water from their health care providers.

## Algal toxins (microcystins)

The City monitors raw and finished water for microcystins weekly from May through October. In 2025 microcystins were detected in raw (untreated) river water on 9 of 26 sampling dates, ranging from 0.31 to 1.86 ug/L. Microcystins were **not detected** in finished (treated) water on any date. The Ohio EPA drinking water threshold for microcystins is 0.3 ug/L for children under 6 and 1.6 ug/L for everyone else.

## Lead in drinking water

If present, elevated levels of lead can cause serious health problems, especially for pregnant women and young children. Lead in drinking water is primarily from materials and components associated with service lines and home plumbing. The City of Defiance is responsible for providing high quality drinking water and removing lead pipes, but cannot control the variety of materials used in plumbing components in your home. You share the responsibility for protecting yourself and your family from the lead in your home plumbing. You can take responsibility by identifying and removing lead materials within your home plumbing and taking steps to reduce your family's risk. Before drinking tap water, flush your pipes for several minutes by running your tap, taking a shower, doing laundry or a load of dishes. You can also use a filter certified by an American National Standards Institute accredited certifier to reduce lead in drinking water. If you are concerned about lead in your water and wish to have your water tested, contact the Water Treatment Plant at (419) 784-2101, ext. 260. Information on lead in drinking water, testing methods, and steps you can take to minimize exposure is available at http://www.epa.gov/safewater/lead.

**Service line inventory.** The City has prepared an inventory of service line materials. You can look up your address on the City website or call the Water Treatment Plant. If your service line is listed as lead, galvanized requiring replacement, or unknown, you have received or will receive a separate letter from the City.

## Definitions

- **Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL):** The highest level of a contaminant that is allowed in drinking water.
- **Maximum Contaminant Level Goal (MCLG):** The level of a contaminant in drinking water below which there is no known or expected risk to health.
- **Action Level (AL):** The concentration of a contaminant which, if exceeded, triggers treatment or other requirements.
- **Treatment Technique (TT):** A required process intended to reduce the level of a contaminant.
- **LRAA:** Locational running annual average.
- **ppm / ppb:** parts per million / parts per billion.
- **NTU:** Nephelometric Turbidity Units.
