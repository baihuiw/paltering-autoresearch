1. PROGRAM OVERVIEW
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Under the agreement between the City and Defiance City Schools (renewed July 2026 for two years), the City provides two school resource officers during the school year. The district reimburses 50 percent of the officers' salary and benefits for the school-year months. Officer Rodenberger is assigned full-time to the middle school/high school campus. Officer Steinke splits time among the elementary buildings and covers the campus when Officer Rodenberger is out.

SROs are police officers first. They do not handle school discipline, which is the school's job. Their role is safety and security, law enforcement when a crime occurs, and building relationships with students and staff.

Notes on the numbers:
- Fights: 6 incidents involving 14 students. Four of the six happened in the first three weeks of school, which is typical. Two resulted in juvenile court referrals for assault because of injuries.
- Threats: all 4 were investigated the same day with the building principal and the district's threat assessment team. None was found to be a credible threat. One involved a social media post made off campus on a Sunday night; parents were contacted that night and the student did not return until the threat assessment was complete.
- Vaping continues to be the most common issue at the middle school. The district handles nicotine vapes under its code of conduct. THC products are a criminal matter (1 case this quarter).

3. SAFETY AND SECURITY
----------------------------------------------------------------------

- Single point of entry with buzz-in is now in place at all buildings. Officer Steinke checked every building's entry in the first week; one door at an elementary building was found propped twice and was fitted with a door-ajar alarm by the district on Sept 2.
- Lockdown drills: each building completed the required drill. Average time to lockdown complete: 1 min 40 sec (goal under 2 min).
- Fire drills: coordinated with the Fire Prevention Bureau (Lt. Mayes attended two).
- Reunification plan: district and police reviewed the parent reunification site plan in September. The tabletop exercise is planned for January.
- Traffic: arrival and dismissal at the middle school continues to back up onto the street. SRO and the Street Department added cones and a "no left turn 7:30-8:15" sign on Sept 14. Morning backups are down from about 12 minutes to about 6.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
4. EDUCATION AND RELATIONSHIPS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

- Classroom presentations: internet safety (grades 5-6), dating violence awareness (health classes, grade 9), driver safety and OVI (driver's ed), "What police do" (grades 1-2).
- Fire Prevention Week (Oct 4-10): Officer Steinke joined the Fire Department's school visits at two elementary buildings.
- Homecoming week: SROs worked the Friday game and the Saturday parade (Oct 3).
- Lunch tables: Officer Rodenberger eats lunch in the cafeteria at least twice a week, which is where most informal contacts happen.
- Coaching: Officer Rodenberger is a volunteer assistant for middle school volleyball (off duty).

----------------------------------------------------------------------
5. ISSUES FOR THE NEXT QUARTER
----------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Social media threats: the district asked for a short presentation to parents on what to do when a child sees a threatening post. Planned for the November parent-teacher conference nights.
2. Coverage gaps: when either officer is in court or on leave, patrol covers with a road officer, which leaves patrol short. We'd like to discuss with the district a third, part-time officer for 2027-2028.
3. Elementary arrival traffic at the building on the west side: parents parking in the fire lane. Fire Department and SRO will do a joint courtesy notice in October.
4. Vape detectors: the district installed vape sensors in two middle school restrooms in September. SRO receives alerts but responds only if a THC product or a safety issue is involved.
