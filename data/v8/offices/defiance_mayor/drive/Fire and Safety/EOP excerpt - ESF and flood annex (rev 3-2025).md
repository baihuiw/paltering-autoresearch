# City of Defiance Emergency Operations Plan

## Excerpt: Section 4 (Direction and Control), Annex F (Flood and Severe Weather)

*Revision March 2025. Coordinated with the Defiance County Emergency Management Agency. Excerpt pulled by Chief Rummel for the new Mayor's orientation binder, September 2026. Contact lists in Appendix A are updated separately each quarter and are not included here.*

## Section 4. Direction and Control

### 4.1 Authority

The Mayor is the chief executive of the City and is responsible for the City's response to emergencies and disasters. In the Mayor's absence, the order of succession is:

1. President of Council (acting mayor under the City Charter)
2. Safety-Service Director
3. Fire Chief
4. Police Chief

The Mayor may declare a local state of emergency when conditions threaten life or property beyond the City's normal ability to respond. A declaration lets the City request county and state assistance and is a prerequisite for most state and federal disaster aid. The Law Director prepares the declaration. Template in Appendix C.

### 4.2 Emergency Operations Center (EOC)

- **Primary EOC:** Fire Station 1 training room. Backup power, radios, phone lines and the county EMA video link.
- **Alternate EOC:** Mayor's Conference Room, City Building, 631 Perry Street.
- **Activation levels:**
  - **Level 3 (monitoring):** Fire Chief and EMA monitor conditions. No EOC staffing.
  - **Level 2 (partial):** Fire Chief, Police Chief, Safety-Service Director, Street Superintendent and Utilities Superintendent report to the EOC. Public information officer on call.
  - **Level 1 (full):** All department heads, PIO, county EMA liaison, Red Cross liaison, school district liaison.

### 4.4 Public information

The PIO is the single source of City information during an emergency. All releases are cleared by the Mayor or, if unavailable, the Safety-Service Director. Channels in order: county emergency alert system (through EMA), City website and Facebook, local radio, e-news (city notices only), door-to-door in affected areas. Messages should say what is happening, where, what residents should do, and when the next update will come.

## Annex F. Flood and Severe Weather

### F.1 Hazard summary

Defiance sits at the confluence of two rivers. Flooding comes from three sources:

1. **River flooding** - slow-rising, usually late winter and spring, often with ice. Low-lying areas along the riverfront, parts of Riverside Avenue, and the parks (Kingsbury, Riverside, Fort Defiance) are most exposed. Forecasts from the National Weather Service give 24 to 72 hours of warning.
2. **Ice jams** - can cause rapid local rises with little warning, especially at bridges and bends.
3. **Street and basement flooding from heavy rain** - storm sewers overwhelmed in short, intense storms. Known trouble spots: Riverside Avenue at Wayne, the Hubbard ditch area, low points on Elliott Road.

Severe weather (thunderstorms, high winds, tornadoes, winter storms) is covered in the same annex because the response looks similar: debris, power outages, road closures.

### F.2 River stage action table (City gauge readings)

| Stage | Actions |
|---|---|
| Action stage | Fire Chief notifies Mayor and department heads. Parks closes riverfront trail low sections. Street Dept stages barricades. EOC Level 3. |
| Minor flood | Close Riverside Park boat ramp and lot. Barricades on Riverside Ave low section. Utilities checks lift stations LS-1 and LS-3 and riverbank outfall flap gates. PIO issues advisory. EOC Level 2. |
| Moderate flood | Close affected streets. Door-to-door notification in the riverfront low area (Fire and Police). Utilities prepares portable pumps. Evaluate WTP intake. Shelter on standby with Red Cross. EOC Level 1. |
| Major flood | Voluntary or mandatory evacuation of designated areas (Mayor's decision with Fire Chief). Shelter open. Request county/state resources. Consider emergency declaration. |

*Stage numbers are in the county flood warning plan and on the gauge sign at the boat ramp; they are not repeated here so there is only one place to update them.*

### F.3 Utilities considerations

- **Water Treatment Plant:** The intake and raw water pumps are above the record flood elevation. In major floods, high turbidity may require increased coagulant and more frequent backwashing. Operators may switch to the upground reservoir if river water quality becomes difficult to treat.
- **Water Pollution Control Facility:** High river levels slow the plant outfall. Plant staff monitor flow and use wet-weather operating mode. Lift stations near the river have generator backup and are checked every 4 hours during a flood.
- **Boil advisories:** issued by the Utilities Superintendent if pressure drops below 20 psi in any part of the system.

### F.4 Streets and debris

- Barricade inventory: about 140 Type III barricades and 60 road-closed signs at the street garage.
- Debris: storm debris (limbs) collected by ward after the event; the Island Park Avenue compost site accepts residents' limbs.
- Downed power lines: treat all as live. Fire secures the area and the power company is notified.

### F.5 Sheltering

Primary shelter sites (with Red Cross agreements): Defiance High School, Defiance College field house (by agreement), Senior Center (daytime warming/cooling only). Pets: county dog warden and the humane society coordinate pet sheltering.

### F.6 Vulnerable residents

The Fire Department keeps a voluntary list of residents who need help evacuating (oxygen users, mobility limits). The list is reviewed each spring. Senior apartment buildings and care facilities have their own emergency plans on file with the Fire Prevention Bureau. Luis Ortega in the Mayor's Office is the contact for residents who call the City asking to be added to the list (added Sept 2026 with the Senior Services Initiative).

### F.7 After the event

- Damage assessment teams (Engineering, Building and Zoning) start within 24 hours.
- Finance opens a separate cost code for every event so costs can be documented for reimbursement.
- After-action review within 30 days.

### F.8 Exercise schedule

The City participates in the county's annual tabletop exercise (usually in March) and runs its own flood tabletop every other year. The next City tabletop is due in spring 2027.
