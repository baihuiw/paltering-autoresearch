1. Background

The home smoke alarm program installs ten-year sealed-battery smoke alarms, and combination smoke/CO alarms where there is a fuel-burning appliance or attached garage, in owner-occupied and rented single-family homes and duplexes in the city at no cost to the resident. Apartment buildings with three or more units are not included, because the owner is responsible for alarms in rental units under the fire code; those are handled through the inspection program instead.

The program started in 2019 with donated alarms. Since 2023 it has been funded from the Fire Prevention Bureau's supplies line plus a state fire prevention grant. The 2026 grant award was $12,000, received in March. The bureau's supplies line carries another $4,500 for alarms in 2026.

2. Results through August 2026

  Homes visited ........................................ 108
  Alarms installed ..................................... 265
    Smoke only (10-year sealed) ........................ 219
    Combination smoke/CO ............................... 46
  Homes with no working alarm before the visit ......... 37 (34 percent)
  Homes with a resident 65 or older .................... 71 (66 percent)
  Average alarms per home .............................. 2.5
  Average crew time per home ........................... 48 minutes

For comparison, January-August 2025: 89 homes, 208 alarms.

Of the 37 homes with no working alarm, 22 had alarms on the ceiling with the battery removed or dead, 9 had alarms more than ten years old that no longer worked, and 6 had no alarm at all. The common reason given for a removed battery was nuisance alarms from cooking, which is why we install photoelectric units in or near kitchens.

Referrals came from: the sign-up sheet at public events (41 percent), phone calls to the bureau (27 percent), crews on EMS or lift-assist calls who noticed a missing or chirping alarm (19 percent), and county senior services and Meals on Wheels drivers (13 percent).

4. Fall plan

- Installs by on-duty engine crews on weekday afternoons, two homes per shift when call volume allows. The FPB will schedule and confirm with residents by phone the day before.
- File of Life packets left at every home with a resident 65 or older. We have 400 packets in stock.
- Battery-removed alarms we replace will be collected and recycled, not left in the home.
- Follow-up phone calls at 30 days for a sample of 10 percent of homes, to confirm alarms are still in place and working.

5. Apartment residents

We regularly get calls from apartment residents, especially in senior buildings, asking for free alarms. The answer under the code is that the owner must provide and maintain them, and we follow up with the owner if a resident reports a missing or non-working alarm. In 2026 the bureau has made 14 such owner contacts, and all but one were resolved within 30 days (the remaining one resulted in orders, now closed). Annual inspections of apartment buildings include a sample check of unit alarms.

For senior buildings, we also offer a community room talk on smoke alarms, cooking safety and what to do when the building alarm sounds. Two are scheduled in September (Ashford Place on 09/17 and Heritage House on 09/23). Building managers can request one at any time.
