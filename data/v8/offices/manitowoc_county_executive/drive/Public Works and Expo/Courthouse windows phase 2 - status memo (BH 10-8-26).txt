You asked at Monday's department heads meeting for a short summary on the Courthouse windows,
since supervisors asked about it at the Finance Committee review.

BACKGROUND
The Courthouse has 214 original-style wood windows, most of them replaced last in the 1970s with
single-pane aluminum inserts. They leak air and water, several sashes are painted shut, and the
heating plant works hard on the exposed sides. In 2024 the County Board approved a two-phase plan
based on the architect's window study: phase 1 (east and south elevations, 116 windows) in 2026 and
phase 2 (west and north elevations plus the rotunda clerestory, 98 windows) in 2027. The new units
are insulated aluminum-clad windows with a profile that matches the historic look, as the
architect recommended.

PHASE 1 (2026) - CLOSEOUT
- Contract: $587,400 (low bid, awarded by the Public Works Committee in February). One change
  order for $14,850 for rotted rough openings found behind the inserts on the south side
  (19 openings). Revised contract $602,250. Budget was $625,000, so we are under by about $22,750.
- Work ran May 4 to September 11. The contractor worked floor by floor, mostly 6:00 a.m.-2:30 p.m.,
  and on Saturdays near the courtrooms so court was not disrupted. Two hearings had to move rooms
  in June; the Clerk of Circuit Court's office handled it.
- Punch list walk was 9/16 (work order 26-1447). 23 items at the walk; 16 done. The 7 left:
    * 3 windows on the 2nd floor east with sticking operators (parts ordered, due 10/16)
    * interior trim caulk touch-up at 1 window in the Register of Deeds office
    * 1 cracked interior stool in Room 108
    * screen missing on 1 window, 3rd floor south
    * final cleaning of exterior sills on the south side
- We are holding retainage of $30,113 (5%) until the punch list is done and we have the
  warranty documents and as-built drawings. I expect to recommend final payment at the
  November 2 Public Works Committee meeting.
- Early results: the east offices held temperature without space heaters on the cold
  mornings in late September. Real heating numbers after the winter.

PHASE 2 (2027) - WHAT'S IN THE RECOMMENDED BUDGET
- 2027 capital request: $655,000, from the capital projects fund (borrowing already planned in the
  2027 issue; no new debt beyond what is in the budget). This includes:
    * window replacement, 98 units, estimate $548,000
    * lead paint abatement on interior and exterior sills and stools, estimate $46,000 (new - see
      below)
    * architect's construction administration, $21,000
    * contingency, $40,000 (about 7%)
- Phase 1 came in about 4% under estimate. Phase 2 has the clerestory and work at height, so
  I don't want to count on the same savings.

LEAD PAINT ON THE SILLS
In phase 1 the old paint on the wood sills and stools was left in place and covered by the new
trim. For phase 2 the architect's design removes and replaces the interior stools on the west side,
so we had the old paint sampled in September. Results came back 10/7: lead-based paint on the
interior stools and the exterior sills at all 12 locations sampled. This was expected for a
building of this age. It means:
- a licensed lead abatement contractor (separate bid, or a sub to the window contractor) has to
  remove the sills in containment before the window crew starts each room;
- each room gets clearance sampling before people move back in;
- offices on the west side will need to relocate for 2-3 days per room instead of one day.
The $46,000 is the architect's number. I asked for a second estimate from a firm we've used
on the Human Services building, expected the week of Oct 19.

SCHEDULE (if the budget is adopted Nov 9)
- December: architect finishes the phase 2 bid documents.
- January 2027: advertise for bids (two weeks), bid opening late January.
- February 2027: award at Public Works Committee, then County Board if over the threshold.
- April 2027: preconstruction meeting; relocation plan with each department.
- May - September 2027: construction, west side first so the north side clerestory work
  happens in the summer.
- Final completion and punch list: October 2027.

The offices most affected on the west side are Corporation Counsel, Veterans Service and the County
Clerk. Veterans Service will need a temporary room for walk-in hours;
I'm looking at the 1st floor conference room. The County Clerk's passport appointments may have to
pause for a few days; we'll schedule around the busy spring travel season if we can.
