Dear Mr. Krueger,

Thank you for your letter about the snowmobile trail that crosses the back of your farm. You asked when the trails will open this year, whether grooming will be the same as last season, and what the change near Maribel you heard about at the feed mill is all about. I checked with our Parks Manager, Dan Pautz, who runs the county's trail program, and here is what I learned.

The county's snowmobile trail system is about 270 miles long, and nearly all of it crosses private land like yours. It is groomed and signed by 14 local snowmobile clubs under annual agreements with the county. The state's snowmobile trail aid comes through the county to the clubs to help cover grooming, signs and brushing. The county's Planning and Park Commission approved the agreements for the 2026-27 season in August, and I signed them on October 1.

For this season, the routes and grooming standards are the same as last year, with one exception. About 1.2 miles of trail near Maribel has been rerouted because a landowner withdrew permission to cross their property. That is every landowner's right, and the clubs and Parks staff worked out a new route over the summer with neighbors who agreed to host it. Your segment is not affected. The club that grooms your stretch will be in touch as usual before it puts up signs and stakes.

On the opening date: the trails open only when Parks declares them open, once there is enough frost in the ground and enough snow to protect fields and crops. They never open before the end of the gun deer season, out of respect for landowners and hunters. Some years that means early December, and some years it is well into January. Please don't count on a set date. When the trails open, or close again in a thaw, Parks updates the snowmobile trail status line at (920) 683-4188 and the trails page on the county website. Kelly in my office also posts it on the county's Facebook page.

This year the county is also asking the clubs to send grooming logs every month instead of at the end of the season, which should help Parks spot any stretch that isn't getting enough attention. An updated county trail map will be on the website by November 15.

If you ever have trouble with riders leaving the trail, cutting fences or riding before the trails are open, please call Dan Pautz directly at (920) 683-4187, or the Sheriff's Office if it is happening at the moment. Dan and the club will follow up with you.

Landowners like you are the reason this trail system exists. Thank you for sharing your land, and for writing.

Sincerely,

Joel Stahlberg
Manitowoc County Executive
