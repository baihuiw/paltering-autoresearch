Dear Mr. Brey,

Thank you for your letter and the photos of your mailbox post. I'm sorry this has been hanging over you since February. You wrote that a county plow took out your mailbox and post during the storm in the second week of February, that you put up a temporary box on a fence post, and that you never heard back after you mentioned it to a driver at the end of the season. That's not the experience I want anyone to have with the county, and I appreciate your patience in following up.

Here is how the Highway Department's mailbox policy works. It was last revised in October 2025 and is unchanged for this coming winter.

- The county pays when a county plow or wing physically struck the mailbox or post, on a road the county plows. That includes county trunk highways like CTH V, state highways, and town roads in towns that contract with the county for plowing.
- Damage from snow thrown off the plow is not covered. Heavy, wet snow coming off a wing at speed can knock over a box that was never touched, and the county does not pay for those.
- Mailboxes have to be installed to the county standard (Manitowoc County Code 15.11) on height and setback. A box that sits too close to the road may be denied.
- When a claim is approved, Highway either installs a standard post and box for you or reimburses up to $50 toward your own replacement, with a receipt.
- Damage should be reported to the Highway Department at (920) 683-4345 within 30 days, and no later than April 30 for that winter.

Your claim is from last winter and was never formally reported, so it falls outside the usual window. The policy does allow past winters' claims to be reviewed case by case, and from your photos the post looks broken off low, which is what we usually see when the wing hits it. I have asked Highway to review your claim, check the route records for that storm, and let you know the result directly. Someone from Highway Operations will call you in the next couple of weeks. If you still have the receipt for the replacement post and box you bought in the spring, please keep it handy, since that is what the reimbursement is based on.

Looking ahead to this winter, the best thing you can do is set the new post at the standard height and setback and put a reflector or a tall marker on it so drivers can see it in blowing snow. If anything happens this season, call Highway right away rather than waiting until spring. It is much easier for them to confirm what happened when the storm is a day or two old.

Thank you for writing, and for giving us a chance to make this right.

Sincerely,
