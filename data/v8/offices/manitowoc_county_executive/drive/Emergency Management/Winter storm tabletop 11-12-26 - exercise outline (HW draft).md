## Purpose

Walk through the county's response to a major multi-day winter storm with the departments that would carry it, before the season starts. The last county winter exercise was in 2022, and several key people are new to their roles since then, including the County Executive.

## Objectives

1. Confirm when and how the EOC is activated for a winter storm, who decides, and how the County Executive and County Board Chair are briefed.
2. Review Highway's storm priorities (I-43 and state highways under the state contract, then county trunk highways, then contract town roads) and how Highway asks for help when it falls behind.
3. Practice the decision to open a county warming center: triggers, staffing, supplies, transportation and how the public is told.
4. Identify how Human Services, Public Health and the ADRC reach vulnerable residents (oxygen users, people living alone, clients with home care) during a power outage.
5. Confirm public information roles: who writes and approves messages, and which channels (county Facebook, website, County Update, radio, the state 511 system for highways).

## Storm narrative and injects

**Module 1 - Forecast (Monday, 3 days out).** The National Weather Service issues a winter storm watch: 12-18 inches of heavy wet snow Wednesday night through Friday, northeast winds 35-45 mph near the lakeshore, temperatures falling to single digits Saturday.
- Inject 1a: Highway reports 70% of salt capacity on hand. What's the order of call-outs and pre-treatment?
- Inject 1b: The Clerk's office asks whether Thursday's scheduled meetings should be canceled. Who decides on county building closures?

**Module 2 - Storm onset (Wednesday night - Thursday).**
- Inject 2a: Multiple semis jackknifed on I-43 southbound; State Patrol reports about 60 stranded motorists in vehicles for 3+ hours. Highway is asked for plows and a loader to clear around the scene.
- Inject 2b: Wet snow brings down lines; utilities report 14,000 customers out in the county, with restoration "possibly 48-72 hours" in rural areas.
- Inject 2c: Highway operators reach the 14-hour limit; two trucks are down. How are routes covered?

**Module 3 - Extended outage (Friday - Saturday).**
- Inject 3a: Temperatures dropping to 5 degrees. Human Services and Public Health recommend a warming center. The Heritage Center (1701 Michigan Ave.) has generator power for the main hall. Walk through opening it: staffing (Human Services, Red Cross volunteers), cots, food, pets, accessible transportation, security, hours.
- Inject 3b: The ADRC has a list of 37 home-delivered meal clients in outage areas; meal routes canceled Thursday. Who checks on them?
- Inject 3c: Public Health gets calls about carbon monoxide from generators used indoors. Message to the public?
- Inject 3d: A rural family calls the County Executive's office directly asking for help with a relative on home oxygen.

**Module 4 - Recovery (Sunday on).**
- Inject 4a: Power mostly restored; a few hundred customers remain out. When does the warming center close?
- Inject 4b: Highway's overtime and salt use far above budget. How are costs tracked for a possible state or federal declaration?
