Joel,

You asked at the department heads meeting how the heating season was starting. Here are the first two weeks of WHEAP (Oct 1-13), from Renee's intake sheet.

Applications taken: 412
- By phone appointment: 268
- Walk-in at Human Services (Mon-Thu 8:30-11:30 a.m.): 97
- Online through the state: 47
Same period last year: 371. So up about 11%, and phone is carrying most of it, which is what we want.

Phone appointment wait: 8 business days as of today. A resident who calls (920) 683-4237 today gets an appointment around Oct 26. Last year at this point it was 12 days. The difference is the two seasonal workers we brought on Sept 28 instead of mid-October, so Renee's team is six this year (four permanent Economic Support staff and two seasonal LTEs). Renee has trained both of them and they're taking their own appointments now.

Crisis cases (disconnect notice or out of fuel): 23 so far. All 23 were seen same day or next day. Most were propane customers who didn't fill in the summer and are now looking at a fall price. Nobody we know of is without heat tonight.

ADRC partnership: the ADRC staff helped 31 homebound seniors start applications by phone and mail in the first two weeks. ADRC staff gather the Social Security letter and the bills and send them over, and Renee's team calls the person to finish. That's working well and I'd like to keep it going. It saves a lot of people a trip they can't make.

What people need to send (this is the most common question): last 3 months' income (the Social Security award letter is enough for most seniors), a recent heating bill and electric bill, and Social Security numbers for everyone in the house. The benefit is one-time and paid straight to the utility or fuel company. We don't quote income limits over the phone without the worker checking the household, so if your office gets calls, send them to the energy assistance line rather than guessing.

I'll send numbers again at the end of October.

Sandra
