# Mailbox Damage by County Plows - Highway Department Policy

Manitowoc County Highway Department, 3500 STH 310, Manitowoc, WI 54220. Phone (920) 683-4345.
Revised October 2025 (Highway Committee, Oct 13, 2025). No changes for the 2026-27 winter.

## Purpose

Mailboxes sit inside the road right-of-way, where plows and wing plows have to work. Most winters the department receives 60 to 90 reports of damaged mailboxes. This policy explains when the county will repair or pay for a mailbox and when it will not, so that every resident is treated the same way.

## What is covered

The county pays only when a **county plow or wing plow physically struck the mailbox or post** while plowing a road the county maintains:

- county trunk highways (CTHs);
- state highways and I-43, which county crews plow under the county's contract with the state; and
- town roads in towns that contract with the county for winter maintenance.

## What is not covered

- Damage caused by snow, slush or ice **thrown from the plow** or the wing, even when it knocks a box over.
- Mailboxes or posts **not installed to the county standard** (below). Claims for these may be denied.
- Damage from vehicles other than county plows (private plow services, delivery trucks, cars sliding off the road).
- Decorative or custom mailboxes, brick or stone pillars, planters, lighting or landscaping in the right-of-way. When a county plow strikes one of these, the county's payment is limited to the remedy below.
- Damage on roads the county does not plow (see "Town plow damage").

## The county standard (Manitowoc County Code 15.11)

Mailboxes in the county highway right-of-way must follow Code 15.11 and U.S. Postal Service guidelines:

- **Post:** a single 4-by-4 inch wood post or a 2-inch diameter steel pipe, set no more than 24 inches into the ground. No concrete collars, railroad rails, milk cans, heavy steel or masonry. A rigid post is a hazard to drivers.
- **Height:** the bottom of the box 41 to 45 inches above the road surface.
- **Setback:** the front of the box 6 to 8 inches behind the edge of the shoulder (or the curb face where there is one).
- **Box:** a standard metal or plastic rural box. Large "locking" boxes are acceptable if mounted on a breakaway post.
- **Arm or support:** a swing-away or cantilever arm is encouraged; it lets the box move instead of breaking.

If you are not sure your installation meets the standard, call the Highway Department and a foreman can look at it before winter.

## What the county does when it is responsible

If Highway confirms the damage was caused by a county plow striking the box or post, the owner chooses one of two remedies:

1. **Highway installs a standard post and box** (4-by-4 post, standard rural box, new numbers on request). Crews install replacements when the ground allows; a temporary post may be set over the winter. **or**
2. **Reimbursement of up to $50** toward the owner's own replacement, paid by check after Highway receives a receipt for the materials.

The county does not pay for labor, decorative items or more than one replacement for the same event.

## How and when to report

- Call the Highway Department at **(920) 683-4345**, Monday-Friday, 7:00 a.m.-3:30 p.m., or leave a message after hours. Give your name, address, phone number, the road, and the date and approximate time the damage happened if known.
- Report **within 30 days** of the damage, and **no later than April 30** for damage during the winter season.
- A foreman will inspect the site, usually within two weeks. Please leave the damaged box and post in place, or take photos before removing them.
- The foreman checks plow route logs and the damage itself (scrape marks, the direction the post broke) to decide whether it was a plow strike or thrown snow.
- You will receive a call or letter with the decision. Reimbursement checks are mailed by the county Finance office, normally within 30 days of receiving the receipt.

Claims from **past winters** are reviewed case by case. Reports after April 30 are not routinely accepted.

## Town plow damage

Many towns plow their own roads. If your road is plowed by a town, a village or a city, contact that municipality; the county cannot pay for damage by another government's equipment. If you are not sure who plows your road, call Highway and we will tell you.

## Before winter: what residents can do

- Check that the post is solid and not rotted at the ground line. A post that is already loose is likely to fail from thrown snow.
- Attach a **reflective marker** or reflector to the post or box so plow operators can see it at night and in blowing snow.
- Mark the end of the driveway with a driveway marker or stake, set at least 3 feet off the shoulder.
- After a storm, clear the area in front of the box so your carrier can reach it.
