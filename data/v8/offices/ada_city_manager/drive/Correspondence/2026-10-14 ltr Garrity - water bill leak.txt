Dear Mr. and Mrs. Garrity,

Thank you for writing about your September utility bill. A bill of $412 when you usually pay about $85 is a shock, and I understand why you wanted an explanation before paying it.

I asked our Utility Office Supervisor, Gloria Nix, to review your account. Here is what she found:

- Your meter was read on September 24. It showed 41,800 gallons used in the billing period, compared with an average of about 4,500 gallons over the previous twelve months.
- Our meter technician went back to your house on October 12 and confirmed the meter is reading correctly. With every faucet off, the meter's leak indicator was still turning, which means water was flowing somewhere on your side of the meter.
- Your note mentions a plumber found and repaired a broken line under the house on October 10. That would explain the usage.

Because the leak was on the customer's side of the meter, the water itself is billed as used. However, the city's leak adjustment policy is meant for exactly this situation. Once in any 12-month period, a customer who has repaired a leak can have the **wastewater** portion of the bill adjusted down to their average usage, since leaked water under a house doesn't go into the sewer system. In your case that would reduce the bill by about $171. Ms. Nix has the form ready and will mail it to you; you can also pick it up at the Utility Office counter. Please return it with a copy of the plumber's invoice within 60 days of the bill date.

You also asked for more time to pay. The Utility Office can set up a payment arrangement that splits the remaining balance over your next three bills with no penalty, as long as the current bill is paid each month. Please call Ms. Nix at (580) 436-6300 this week so the arrangement is in place before the disconnect notice date; she will note your account in the meantime so it is not shut off while the adjustment is pending.

A suggestion for the future: you can see your daily and monthly usage on the online customer portal at adaok.com, and many customers check it after a hard freeze or when they're away. A sudden jump is often the first sign of a leak.

If you have any doubt about whether the repair fixed everything, here is a simple check our meter technicians recommend: turn off every faucet and appliance that uses water, then look at the small dial or triangle on the meter face (the meter box is usually near the street). If it is still moving after ten minutes, water is still flowing somewhere, often a running toilet. Our Public Works office can also send a technician to check it with you at no charge; call (580) 436-8100 to schedule.

Thank you for your patience while we looked into this. I'm sorry for the trouble.
