Dear Mrs. Fouts,

Thank you for writing, and I'm sorry you've been losing sleep. A dog that barks for hours every night is more than an annoyance, and you're right to expect the city to help.

You asked whether Ada has a noise ordinance that covers barking dogs. It does. The city's animal ordinance makes it unlawful for an owner to keep a dog that barks or howls habitually or for long periods so that it disturbs neighbors, and the general noise provisions of the city code apply as well. Enforcement is handled by our Animal Welfare officers, who are part of the Police Department.

Here is how the process works, so you know what to expect:

1. Call Animal Welfare through the police non-emergency line, (580) 332-4466, when the barking is happening, or use the contact form at adaok.com (choose "Complaint"). Animal Welfare officers work 7:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. on weekdays; after hours, patrol officers respond when they're available, but barking calls rank below emergencies.
2. An officer visits the dog's owner, explains the ordinance and leaves a written warning. Many cases end here: often the owner works nights or didn't realize how much the dog barks when no one is home.
3. If the barking continues after the warning, keep a simple log: the date, the times it started and stopped, and how it affected you. A phone video with the time showing is helpful.
4. With the log, the officer can issue a citation to the owner, which goes to Ada Municipal Court. For a citation to hold up, the person disturbed usually needs to sign the complaint and be willing to tell the judge what happened. Neighbors who are also bothered can sign too, and that carries weight.

I asked our Animal Welfare supervisor to look up your address. A warning was left at the house you mentioned on September 29 after your earlier call. Because you report that nothing has changed, an officer will make a second contact with the owner this week, and the supervisor will call you to explain what happens next. If you'd like to start a log, the officer can give you a form, but a notebook works just as well.

I know it can be uncomfortable to sign a complaint against a neighbor. You are not obligated to, and the officers will keep working with the owner either way. But it is the step that gives the court something to act on if warnings don't work.

One more thought: sometimes the most effective step is a friendly word with the owner, if you feel comfortable. Many people simply don't know their dog barks once they leave for work or go to bed. If that's not something you want to do, that's completely understandable, and the officer will handle it.

Thank you for taking the time to write and for being patient with a process that can take a few weeks. Please call my office if you don't hear from Animal Welfare by Friday.

Sincerely,
