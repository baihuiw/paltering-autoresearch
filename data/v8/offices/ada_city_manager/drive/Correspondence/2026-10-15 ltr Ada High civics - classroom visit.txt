Dear Civics Teachers,

Thank you for inviting me to visit your two civics classes on Wednesday, October 28, at 10:15 a.m. I'm glad to accept, and I'm already looking forward to it.

You asked for about 30 minutes in each class, with time for questions. Here is what I'm planning; please tell me if you'd like something different:

1. **What a city manager does (5 minutes).** Ada has a council-manager form of government. Residents elect the city council; the council sets policy, passes the budget and hires the city manager; the manager runs the day-to-day operations and carries out the council's decisions. It's a little like a school board and a superintendent, which your students may find familiar.

2. **Where the money comes from and where it goes (10 minutes).** Most of Ada's general budget comes from sales tax, not property tax, so every time your students buy a burger or a phone charger in town, part of that pays for police officers, firefighters, parks and streets. I'll bring a one-page chart of the city's budget, and we'll do a quick exercise: if you had $100 of city money, how would you split it between police, fire, streets, parks and the library? Then we'll compare their answers with what the city actually does.

3. **How a resident can change something (5 minutes).** I'll show them a real council agenda and explain "Citizens to Be Heard": anyone can come to a council meeting on the first or third Monday at 5:45 p.m., fill out a card and speak for three minutes. No appointment, no fee, no age limit. Several of the best ideas we hear come from people who had never been to a meeting before.

4. **Questions (10 minutes).** Anything goes. In my experience, high school students ask the hardest questions, and the most honest ones.

If time allows, I'd also like to ask your students a question of my own: what would make Ada a place they'd want to come back to after college or training? I ask every group I meet, and the answers from young people are usually the most useful I get.

I'll bring copies of the October 19 council agenda for each student. If your students would like to see a meeting in person, I'd be happy to have them as my guests at the November 2 meeting; Kristi Wofford in my office can reserve seats and arrange for the Mayor to recognize them.

Please let Kristi know whether you need me to check in at the front office, and where I should park. I'll plan to arrive about 10:00.

Thank you for teaching civics. It matters more than ever.
