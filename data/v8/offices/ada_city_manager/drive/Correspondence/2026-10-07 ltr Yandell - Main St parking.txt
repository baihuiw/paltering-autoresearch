Dear Mr. Yandell,

Thank you for your letter and for stopping by City Hall to drop it off. I'm sorry I missed you; Kristi tells me you waited a while.

You asked whether the city still enforces the two-hour parking limit on Main Street, because the same few cars are parked in front of your shop all day and your customers have to park a block away. The short answer is yes, the two-hour limit is still the rule, and enforcement started up again this week.

Here's what happened. Downtown parking is enforced by the Police Department's part-time parking enforcement officer, who works the two-hour zones on Main Street (Mississippi Avenue to Townsend Avenue) and the first block of the cross streets on each side of Main, Monday through Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. That position was vacant from June until last week. Patrol officers covered downtown when they could, but with three officer vacancies this summer, parking was not their first priority, and I think the people parking all day noticed.

The new parking enforcement officer started Monday, October 5. For the rest of October she is marking tires and recording plates, and she is leaving a printed warning on any vehicle parked longer than two hours, along with a flyer showing where all-day parking is available (the public lots north and south of Main, which have no time limit). Starting Monday, November 2, overtime parking will be ticketed. A citation is $20 the first time; repeat violations within 30 days cost more.

A suggestion that has worked in other downtowns: if the all-day cars belong to people who work downtown (employees or owners), the merchants could agree to keep the front spaces for customers. Kristi can share the public lot map if that would help you talk with your neighbors. I'll also mention it to the merchants' group.

You also asked about adding loading zones. There are two now, at Main and Broadway and Main and Rennie, each limited to 20 minutes for deliveries. If your block needs one, the merchants on the block can request it through Public Works, which reviews the request with the Police Department. Loading zones replace regular spaces, so we like to hear from all the businesses on the block before changing one.

If you still see the same vehicles parked all day after November 2, please call the police non-emergency line at (580) 332-4466 and ask for parking enforcement, or let my office know with the block and the times. That helps us send the officer where she's needed.

Thank you for writing, and for being part of downtown.

Sincerely,
