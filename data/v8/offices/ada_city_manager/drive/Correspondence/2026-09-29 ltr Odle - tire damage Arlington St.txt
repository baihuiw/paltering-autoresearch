Dear Mr. Odle,

Thank you for your message about the pothole on Arlington Street near 4th Street, and I'm sorry it cost you a tire. I'm new to Ada, but I've already driven that stretch, and I know what you mean.

First, the pothole itself. Our Street Division received your report on September 24 and patched that hole and two smaller ones beside it on September 25. Because Arlington carries a lot of truck traffic, I've asked the street superintendent to put that section on the list for a permanent hot-mix repair before the asphalt plant closes for the winter, rather than relying on cold patch, which tends to pull out on busy streets after a heavy rain.

For future reference, here's how pothole repairs work in Ada:
- Report a pothole to Public Works at (580) 436-8100 (weekdays, 7:00 a.m.-3:30 p.m.) or through the contact form at adaok.com under "Streets and traffic." Please include the nearest address or cross street and which lane.
- Potholes that can damage a vehicle on a main street are patched within one working day. Others are looked at within two working days and patched within five.
- The first patch is usually cold mix, which is temporary. If a patched hole opens up again, please report it again so it gets a new work order and moves up the list.

Second, the tire. You asked whether the city will pay for it. Anyone who believes the city is responsible for damage to their property can file a claim. The claim form is available from the City Clerk's office here at City Hall or on adaok.com under City Clerk > Claims. Please include a copy of the repair receipt and any photos. The City Attorney's office reviews each claim under state law, which looks at things like whether the city knew about the hazard and had a reasonable chance to fix it. I can't promise how your claim will come out, but I can promise it will be reviewed fairly and that you'll get an answer in writing. Our Street Division will provide the repair history for that location.

One more thing you may find useful: the city keeps a running list of streets that need more than patching, based on a pavement condition survey. This fall's overlay project (Oak Avenue and part of N. Rennie) goes to the council for award on October 5, and citywide crack sealing starts in October. Arlington Street is on the list for a future year. When the FY28 budget is prepared this winter, the council will decide how much to spend on streets, and the budget hearings are open to the public. If you'd like to see where Arlington ranks, our Public Works office can show you the list.

Thank you for taking the time to write. Reports like yours are how we find out where the streets need us most.

Sincerely,

Neil Hartsock
City Manager, City of Ada
