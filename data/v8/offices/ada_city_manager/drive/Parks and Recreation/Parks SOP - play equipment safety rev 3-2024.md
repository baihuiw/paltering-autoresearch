# Parks and Recreation Standard Operating Procedure 4: Play Equipment Safety

*City of Ada Parks and Recreation Department. Revision 3, March 2024. Approved by the Parks and Recreation Director.*

## 4.1 Purpose

To keep the city's playgrounds and play equipment safe for children and to document how the department inspects, maintains and repairs them.

## 4.2 Inspections

(a) **Annual comprehensive inspection.** Every city playground is inspected at least annually by a Certified Playground Safety Inspector (CPSI) on staff or under contract. Inspections are normally done in September, after the summer season.

(b) **Monthly visual checks.** Maintenance crews check each playground monthly for broken or missing parts, vandalism, sharp edges, surfacing depth and debris, and record the check on the work order system.

(c) **After events and storms.** Crews check playgrounds near event areas before and after large events and after storms with high winds.

## 4.3 Hazard priorities and response

| Priority | Meaning | Required response |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hazard that could cause permanent injury or death | Equipment with a Priority 1 hazard shall be closed to the public immediately until the hazard is corrected. Close with fencing and signs; notify the Director the same day. |
| 2 | Hazard that could cause serious injury | Correct within 30 days, or close the affected component. |
| 3 | Minor hazard | Correct at the next scheduled maintenance. |

Only the Director may reopen equipment closed for a Priority 1 hazard, after the inspector confirms the hazard is corrected.

## 4.4 Public information

When a playground or major play structure is closed for safety, the department posts signs at the site stating that the equipment is closed and, where practical, the expected reopening date. The Public Information Officer is notified so the closure can be shared on the city's website and Facebook page.

## 4.5 Surfacing

Engineered wood fiber is maintained at 12 inches under equipment and in use zones. Crews rake high-traffic areas weekly in season and add material as needed.

## 4.6 Replacement planning

Wooden play structures have an expected service life of 15-20 years. The Director keeps a replacement schedule and requests replacement through the capital budget. Structures rated at end of service life by the CPSI are prioritized.

## 4.7 Records

Inspection reports, work orders and closure notices are kept for at least five years in the department's shared folder.
