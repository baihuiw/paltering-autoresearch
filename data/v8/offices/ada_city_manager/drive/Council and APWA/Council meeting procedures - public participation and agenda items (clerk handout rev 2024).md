# Speaking at a City Council Meeting

*City Clerk's Office handout, kept at the back table of the council chamber and given to anyone who asks. Revised January 2024. Staff: use this when residents ask how to speak, how to get an item on the agenda or how meetings work.*

## When and where

- The Ada City Council meets the **first and third Mondays of each month at 5:45 p.m.** in the council chamber at City Hall, 231 S. Townsend. When a Monday is a city holiday, the meeting moves to Tuesday.
- The **Ada Public Works Authority** (water, wastewater and sanitation) meets immediately after the council meeting. The trustees are the same five council members.
- Agendas are posted by the Friday before each meeting at the City Hall public bulletin board (Townsend Street entrance) and at adaok.com under Agendas and Minutes. Council packets with staff memos are online by Friday afternoon.
- Meetings usually last 45 minutes to an hour and a half. The public is welcome to come and go.

## Three ways to speak

**1. "Citizens to Be Heard" (topics not on the agenda)**
- Near the end of each regular meeting the Mayor opens Citizens to Be Heard.
- Fill out a **Request to Speak card** from the table at the back of the chamber and hand it to the City Clerk (seated at the end of the dais) **before the meeting starts**. There is no sign-up in advance, no phone or email sign-up, and no fee.
- When the Mayor calls your name, come to the podium, state your name and address for the record, and speak for **up to three minutes**.
- The council **cannot discuss or act on** a Citizens to Be Heard topic at that meeting, because it wasn't on the posted agenda. That's state law, not rudeness. The Mayor will usually refer it to the City Manager or a department head, and staff will follow up with you. Leave a phone number or email on your card.
- Late arrivals: if you get there after the meeting has started, hand your card to the Clerk anyway. The Mayor generally takes late cards if time allows.

**2. Comments on an agenda item**
- If you want to speak on an item that is on the agenda (a rezoning, a contract, an ordinance), mark that item number on your card. The Mayor will call you when that item comes up, before the council votes.
- Public hearings (rezonings, the budget) have their own time for proponents and opponents. Three minutes per speaker; groups are asked to pick a spokesperson.

**3. Ask for an item to be placed on a future agenda**
- Residents can ask the City Manager's office to place an item on an agenda. Requests go to the Assistant City Manager, who prepares the agenda, by **noon on the Tuesday** before the meeting. Include what you want the council to consider and any materials.
- The City Manager and Mayor decide whether an item is ready for council or should go to staff first. Many requests can be handled by staff without a council vote (a stop sign study, a code enforcement case, a pothole), and those are usually faster.

## Tips from the Clerk

- Address the council as a body ("Mayor and council"), not individual members or the audience.
- Bring copies of anything you want the council to see: seven copies (five members, the City Manager and the Clerk). The Clerk will hand them out.
- Speak into the microphone; meetings are recorded and the recording is posted online.
- Personal attacks, profanity and campaigning for or against candidates are out of order.
- If your concern is about a specific property, such as a vacant or run-down house, a drainage problem or a code violation, give the address. Community Development can look up the case history the next morning.

## Recordings, packets and records

- Council meetings are video recorded and posted on the city's website within two business days. The Clerk keeps the official minutes.
- The full council packet, including staff memos, contracts and maps, is posted online with the agenda. Paper copies of the packet are available at the Clerk's office on request.
- Public records requests (copies of documents) go to the City Clerk's office in writing. Most simple requests are filled within a few business days.

## After the meeting

Minutes are approved at the next regular meeting and posted online. Draft minutes are available from the Clerk's office on request.
