STAFF NOTES (packet only)

Item 3. Large meters serve the hospital, ECU, the schools, the industrial
park and the larger apartment complexes. They are a small share of meters
but about 31% of billed water volume, so a meter reading 3% slow costs real
money. Last year's test found 7 of 46 under-registering by more than the
allowed tolerance; all were rebuilt. The contractor tests in place where
possible and schedules shutdowns with each customer at least 48 hours ahead.
Testing window: November 2 through December 18.

Quotes received:
- Quote 1: $14,720 (in-place testing, rebuild labor included, parts at cost
  plus 10%)
- Quote 2: $17,350 (shop testing only; meters removed and reinstalled)

Item 4. The Service Center fuel island fuels every city vehicle and piece of
equipment (about 11,500 gallons a month in summer). The north dispenser's
card reader has failed four times since June and the leak sensor alarm has
tripped falsely twice, which shuts the pumps down until a technician resets
it. Fleet has been fueling some vehicles at the backup tank with a manual
log. Replacement parts are in stock with the low quoter; installation two
days, scheduled on a weekend so the morning rollout isn't affected. The
APWA trustees approve APWA purchases of $10,000 and over (purchasing quick
reference, rev. 7-2025).

Quotes received:
- Quote 1: $11,400
- Quote 2: $12,980
- Quote 3: $14,215 (dispenser only; did not include sensor)

Background on the large-meter program: in FY26 the seven rebuilt meters were
re-tested after repair and the corrected readings added an estimated $38,000
a year in billed water. Most of the under-registration was in 4-inch and
6-inch compound meters at the oldest commercial accounts. Russell recommends
keeping the annual cycle for every large meter rather than testing the 3-inch
meters every other year, as some cities do.

Both items are within the FY27 APWA budget. No budget amendment needed.
