Brooke,

That's accurate. A few things people should know so they aren't frustrated:

1. Hours. Animal Welfare officers work 7 a.m.-6 p.m. weekdays (two officers, one on Saturdays 8-2 for impounds). Night barking calls go to patrol, and patrol answers them after higher-priority calls, so sometimes nobody comes that night. That's the number one complaint we get. The fix is that we follow up the next business day: if they call the non-emergency line at night, ask dispatch to "send it to Animal Welfare for follow-up" and give their name and number.

2. The process. First contact is a visit and a written warning to the owner (door hanger if nobody's home). Most cases stop there. If it keeps happening, the complainant keeps a log for about a week (date, start and stop times). With the log, we can cite. For the citation to stand up in municipal court, the complainant usually needs to sign the complaint and be willing to testify. Two households signing is better than one.

3. What we can't do: we can't take the dog for barking alone unless there's also a welfare issue (no food, water or shelter, injured, etc.). If someone thinks the dog itself is being neglected, tell them to say so; that's a different and faster track.

4. Anonymous is fine for the first visit, but we can't cite on an anonymous complaint.

Log form is on the PD page at adaok.com, or a notebook works.

Tina
