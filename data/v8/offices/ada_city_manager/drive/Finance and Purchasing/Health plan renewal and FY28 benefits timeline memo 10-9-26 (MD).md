**Re:** Employee health plan renewal (Jan 1, 2027) and what it means for the FY28 budget

You asked at Tuesday's budget calendar meeting why the health plan renews in January when our fiscal year starts in July, and how much uncertainty that puts in the FY28 numbers. Short version below, with the timeline.

## How the plan works

- The city provides health, dental and life insurance to 214 full-time employees through a pooled municipal health plan with other Oklahoma cities. The plan year is **calendar year** (January-December), set by the pool, not by us.
- The city pays **100% of the employee-only premium** for the base medical plan and 50% of the dependent premium. Employees who choose the buy-up plan pay the difference.
- Current enrollment: 214 employees; 118 employee-only, 41 employee + spouse, 23 employee + children, 32 family. 9 employees decline coverage (covered elsewhere).
- FY27 budgeted employer cost (all funds): **$3,412,000** (General Fund share about 71%; APWA funds the rest).

## Renewal for January 1, 2027

- The pool sent preliminary renewal rates on October 1: **+7.8%** for the base plan, **+4.1%** for dental, life unchanged.
- The pool's board adopts final rates November 12. Historically the final number has come in 0.5-2 points below the preliminary.
- Effect on FY27 (half year, January-June) at +7.8%: about **$133,000** more than budgeted across all funds, about $94,000 of it General Fund. We budgeted a 6% increase for the January renewal, so the real gap is about **$30,000** in the General Fund. Vacancy savings in the Police Department this year more than cover it; **no budget amendment needed**.
- Effect on FY28 (full year): a 7.8% increase plus a second renewal in January 2028 (we'll assume another 7%) puts FY28 employer cost at roughly **$3,790,000**, about $380,000 more than FY27 across all funds. That's the single largest increase in the FY28 base budget.

## Options to consider for FY28 (not for this year)

1. **Keep the current contribution policy.** Most predictable for employees; full cost falls on the city.
2. **Change the dependent contribution** from 50% to 45% for new hires only. Small savings at first; grows over time; no effect on current staff.
3. **Add a wellness incentive** (lower employee premium for completing an annual physical and screening). The pool says cities that did this saw claims grow more slowly, but the evidence is thin and it takes two to three years to show up.
4. **Market the plan.** We left the state pool in 2014 and came back in 2017 after two bad years with a commercial carrier. I don't recommend leaving again without a consultant's analysis.

I'd suggest we keep option 1 for FY28 and ask HR to study options 2 and 3 for FY29. Health insurance is one of the reasons we can hire police officers and operators against larger departments; changing it in the middle of a recruiting push would be hard to explain.

## Retirees

Eleven retirees continue on the plan at their own cost under state law (they pay the full premium; the city pays nothing). Their rates rise by the same 7.8%. HR will mail them renewal letters by October 30.

- **Pension:** police and fire contributions are set by the state plans; no change announced. The non-uniformed plan's actuarial report (due December) may recommend a small increase in the city's rate; I'll include whatever it says.
- **Workers' compensation:** renewal in July; claims experience is good this year (no lost-time injuries since March).
- **Uniform and boot allowances:** unchanged.
