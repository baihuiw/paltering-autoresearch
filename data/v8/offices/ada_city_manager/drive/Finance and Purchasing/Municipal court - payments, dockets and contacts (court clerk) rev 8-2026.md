# Ada Municipal Court: Payments, Dockets and Contacts

*Prepared by Tamara Easley, Court Clerk, for City Hall front counter staff, the utility office and the City Manager's office. Revised August 2026. The court clerk's office is part of the Finance Department for budgeting and cash handling; the judge is appointed by the council and the court is independent of city staff on case decisions.*

## Where the court is

- Ada Municipal Court is in **City Hall, 231 S. Townsend**. Use the north entrance; the Court Clerk's window is the first door on the left.
- Clerk's window hours: **Monday-Friday, 8:00 a.m.-5:00 p.m.** (closed city holidays). The window stops taking cash at 4:45 p.m.
- Court sessions are held in the council chamber.
- Phone: (580) 436-6300, ask for the court clerk (ext. 224). Email: court@adaok.com.

## Paying a citation before the court date

Most traffic and ordinance citations can be paid **before** the court date without coming to court. Paying is a plea of no contest (or guilty) and closes the case. The amount (fine plus court costs) is set by the court's bond and fine schedule; the clerk can tell you the amount by phone with the citation number.

Exceptions: the citation says **"Mandatory Appearance"** (for example, driving under suspension, no insurance with an accident, some juvenile matters). Those must appear in person.

**Ways to pay**

1. **Online** at adaok.com > Municipal Court > Pay a Citation. You need the citation number (top right of the ticket) and your last name. Credit or debit card. The online payment vendor charges a convenience fee of 3% (minimum $2); the court does not receive it. Online payments post the next business day. Print or save the confirmation.
2. **In person** at the clerk's window: cash, check, money order, credit or debit card.
3. **By phone** with a card during office hours.
4. **Drop box** (checks and money orders only) at the north entrance. Write the citation number on the check.

Online payment closes at 5:00 p.m. the business day **before** the court date. After that, pay in person or appear.

## Tag, license and equipment citations

For expired tag, expired license and some equipment citations (headlight, taillight), the judge's standing order allows the clerk to reduce the amount if the person shows proof the problem was fixed **before** the court date: a current tag receipt, a renewed license, or a repair receipt. That has to be done in person at the clerk's window (or by email with a clear photo, if the clerk confirms by phone). If you pay online first, the reduction can't be applied afterward.

## Payment plans and community service

- People who can't pay in full can ask the judge for a payment plan or community service at arraignment. The clerk can't set up a plan without the judge.
- If you miss a payment, the court can issue a failure-to-pay notice. Call the clerk before the due date if something comes up.

## Missed court dates

If you miss your court date and haven't paid, the court may issue a failure-to-appear warrant and notify the state, which can lead to a license suspension. Call the clerk as soon as possible; in many cases the clerk can reset the date once.

## What front counter and City Manager's office staff should and shouldn't do

- **Do** give location, hours, payment options and the clerk's phone number.
- **Do** tell people they can pay online before their court date unless the ticket says Mandatory Appearance.
- **Don't** give legal advice, estimate what the judge will do, or promise a reduction. Send those questions to the clerk.
- **Don't** take court payments at the utility window. Different cash drawer, different system; the money ends up in the wrong place and the citation isn't closed.
- City council members and the City Manager have no role in individual cases. If someone writes to the City Manager asking to "get a ticket dropped," reply with the clerk's contact information and the payment and docket options. Renee Calloway handles anything that looks like a complaint about a judge or prosecutor.

## Common questions the clerk gets

- **"Can I get my court date moved?"** Once, by calling the clerk at least two business days before the date. After that, only the judge can reset it.
- **"Will this go on my driving record?"** Moving violations are reported to the state when the case is closed. Parking tickets and most ordinance citations are not. The clerk can't advise whether to plead or contest.
- **"Can someone else pay for me?"** Yes. Anyone can pay a citation with the citation number.
- **"I already paid online; do I still come to court?"** No, unless the ticket says Mandatory Appearance. Keep the confirmation.
- **"Can I talk to the judge before court?"** No. Everything is heard in open court on the docket day.
