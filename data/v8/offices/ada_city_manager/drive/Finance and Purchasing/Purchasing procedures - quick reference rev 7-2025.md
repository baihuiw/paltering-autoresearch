# Purchasing Procedures: Quick Reference for Department Heads

*City of Ada and Ada Public Works Authority. Finance Department. Revised July 1, 2025. Replaces the March 2022 sheet. This is a summary; the purchasing ordinance and the APWA trust indenture control where they differ.*

## Who can approve what

| Amount (single purchase or contract) | City funds | APWA funds (water, wastewater, sanitation) |
|---|---|---|
| Under $5,000 | Department head; one quote is fine | Department head; one quote is fine |
| $5,000 to $9,999.99 | Three written quotes; City Manager approves | Three written quotes; City Manager approves |
| $10,000 to $24,999.99 | Three written quotes; City Manager approves | Three written quotes; APWA trustees approve |
| $25,000 and over | City Council approves | APWA trustees approve |
| Public construction above the state bidding threshold | Formal sealed bids, published notice, Council award | Formal sealed bids, published notice, trustee award |

Notes:
- Do not split a purchase into smaller orders to stay under a limit. If you buy the same thing several times a year and it adds up past $25,000, talk to Finance about an annual contract.
- The council meets the first and third Mondays. Agenda items are due to Renee Calloway by noon Tuesday the week before the meeting. Build that time into your schedule; a purchase that needs council approval in a hurry usually means a special meeting nobody wants.
- Emergency purchases (an immediate threat to public health, safety or property) can be made by the City Manager and reported to council at the next meeting. Call Finance the same day.

## Quotes

- "Written" means an email, a quote sheet or a printed web price with the date. A phone call written on a sticky note is not a quote.
- Attach all quotes to the requisition, including the ones you didn't pick.
- If you can't get three, say why on the requisition (only two vendors in the region, manufacturer-only parts, etc.).
- Sole-source purchases over $10,000 need a short memo explaining why there is only one source. Manufacturer parts for equipment we already own usually qualify.

## State contracts and cooperatives

Items on a state statewide contract or an approved cooperative purchasing contract count as competitively bid. You still need the approval level in the table above. Print the contract page and attach it. Vehicles and heavy equipment almost always go this way.

## Requisitions and purchase orders

1. Enter the requisition in the financial system with the account code. If you don't know the code, ask Finance before you guess; recoding after the fact takes longer than asking.
2. Finance checks the budget line and issues a PO number, usually within one working day.
3. No PO, no order. Vendors are told this, and the ones who work with us regularly will ask for the number.
4. When the goods arrive or the work is done, sign the packing slip or invoice ("received, OK to pay"), date it and send it to Accounts Payable.
5. Claims are paid after council approves the claims list (consent agenda, both meetings each month). Vendors who need faster payment can sign up for ACH.

## Credit cards

- Department heads and designated staff have city purchasing cards. Limit $2,500 per transaction unless Finance has raised it in writing.
- Receipts go to Finance by the 5th of the following month, with the account code and a one-line purpose.
- No personal purchases, no splitting, no gift cards, no alcohol. Fuel only at the Service Center fuel island unless you are out of town.

## Travel

Mileage at the federal rate for personal vehicles. Hotels at or near the state per diem. Registration fees go on a PO or purchasing card. Travel requests go through the City Manager's office for anything overnight.

## Surplus property

Nothing leaves city ownership without a council surplus declaration. List surplus items with Finance by April 1 each year for the spring online auction. Scrap metal goes to the Service Center bin; proceeds go to the General Fund.

## Common mistakes we see

- Ordering before the PO is issued, then asking Finance to "backdate" it. We can't.
- Using an old quote. Quotes older than 60 days need to be refreshed.
- Charging a capital item (over $5,000, useful life over one year) to a supplies account. It throws off the fixed asset list and the auditors catch it every year.
- Forgetting sales tax exemption. The city is exempt from Oklahoma sales tax; give the vendor our exemption letter (on the Finance shared drive).
