**Re:** E. 14th Street traffic count and the speed hump request

At the Oct 5 meeting council asked staff to count traffic on E. 14th Street before deciding on speed humps and to report back Nov 2. Here is how we'll do it and what council will see.

## The count

- **Dates:** Counters go down Monday, Oct 12, and come up Monday, Oct 19 (seven full days, including a weekend). No school holidays that week, so school-hour traffic will be normal.
- **Locations:** Three pneumatic tube counters:
  1. E. 14th between Hope Ave and Mississippi Ave (west end).
  2. E. 14th in the 1300 block (middle; where most of the calls come from).
  3. E. 14th near the east end, before the curve.
- **What they record:** volume by hour and direction, speed of every vehicle, and vehicle class (cars vs. trucks).
- The Police Department will also put out its speed display sign (no enforcement) the following week, Oct 19-23, so residents can see it's being looked at. The sign also records speeds; the chief will share the data.

## What council will see on Nov 2

1. Average daily traffic.
2. 85th percentile speed (the speed 85% of drivers are at or below). The posted limit is 25 mph.
3. Share of vehicles over 35 mph, and the hours they occur.
4. Cut-through estimate: the share of traffic that enters at one end and leaves at the other during the morning and afternoon peaks, compared with local trips.
5. Our recommendation, based on the city's traffic calming guidelines (adopted 2017). Under the guidelines a residential street is eligible for speed humps if:
   - it's a local street (not a collector or an emergency response route),
   - average daily traffic is between 300 and 3,000 vehicles,
   - the 85th percentile speed is at least 8 mph over the limit, and
   - a petition shows support from at least 60% of the households on the affected blocks.
6. A cost estimate. Asphalt speed humps run about $3,500-$4,500 each installed with signs and markings. E. 14th would likely need three or four.

## Fire and EMS

Chief Breedlove's position is that speed humps add about 5-10 seconds per hump to response times and can be hard on the engines. E. 14th is not a primary response route for Station 3, but he asked to review any proposal. If council wants to go ahead, "speed cushions" (humps with gaps sized for fire apparatus wheels) are an option at a slightly higher cost.

## Petition

Two residents have asked how to start a petition. Community Development has the form (the same one used for the 2019 W. 20th St request). It goes to each household on the 1100-1500 blocks. I suggest we hand it out now so that, if the numbers support humps, council isn't waiting another month for signatures.

## Other options if humps aren't warranted

If the count shows speeds or volumes below the thresholds, council may still want to do something, and residents will expect an answer either way. Lower-cost steps we can take on any residential street:
- **Targeted enforcement** during the hours the count shows the most speeding (the chief has agreed to two weeks of focused patrol after the count).
- **Speed display sign** rotated back every few months.
- **Pavement markings**: narrower painted lanes and "25" legends on the pavement, about $1,200 for the length of E. 14th.
- **Signs**: additional speed limit signs and "children at play" signs where residents request them (about $150 each installed).
- **Revisit in a year**: a follow-up count next fall to see whether traffic is growing, especially if the east side keeps adding homes.

None of these require council action; staff can do them under existing budgets.

## Calls so far

The City Manager's office and Public Works have logged nine calls and emails on E. 14th since Sept 8: seven in favor of speed humps, one opposed (concerned about noise from trucks hitting them), one asking for more enforcement instead. All callers have been told council will take this up Nov 2.
