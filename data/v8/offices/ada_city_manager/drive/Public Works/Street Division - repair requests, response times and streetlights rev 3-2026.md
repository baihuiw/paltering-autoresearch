# Street Division: Repair Requests, Response Times and Streetlights

*Public Works Department, Street Division. Service Center, Public Works line (580) 436-8100, Monday-Friday 7:00 a.m.-3:30 p.m. Prepared by Doyle Cantrell, Street Superintendent; approved by Russell Gaines, Public Works Director. Revised March 2026. For City Hall, the City Manager's office, dispatch and the utility office, so everyone tells residents the same thing.*

## How residents report a street problem

- **Phone:** Public Works, (580) 436-8100, during working hours. Leave a voicemail after hours; it's checked at 7:00 a.m.
- **Online:** the contact form at adaok.com, topic "Streets and traffic." The City Manager's office forwards these to Public Works the same day.
- **After hours, if it's dangerous** (a sinkhole, a missing manhole cover, a stop sign down, a tree across the road): Dispatch, (580) 332-4466. Dispatch calls the on-call street crew.

Every request gets a work order number. Residents can call back with the number or their address for a status. Ask for the nearest address or cross street, which lane and direction, and how big. "In front of the dentist going north" is fine; we'll find it.

## Potholes: what we promise

| Situation | Target |
|---|---|
| Hazard (deep enough to damage a tire or wheel, on a main street, or on a bus or school route) | Inspected and cold-patched within **1 working day** |
| All other potholes | Inspected within **2 working days**; cold-patched within **5 working days** |
| Permanent hot-mix repair (saw-cut and patch) | Scheduled by priority, **April through November** when the asphalt plant is running; 2-6 weeks depending on the list |

Notes:
- **Cold patch is temporary.** On busy streets it can pull out in days, especially after rain. If a patched hole reopens, it goes back on the list as a new work order and gets priority.
- **Hot-mix crew** runs Tuesday through Thursday. Monday is reserved for catching up on weekend calls; Friday for equipment upkeep and utility cut repairs.
- Potholes over a water or sewer line repair are fixed by the utility crew that made the cut, but the Street Division tracks them on the same list.
- Some streets (parts of Broadway, Mississippi and Main) have heavy truck traffic and older pavement under the overlay. Those break up fastest. They're on the long-term overlay list; until then we patch.

## Vehicle damage

If someone says a pothole damaged their vehicle, take their name and number and tell them they can file a claim with the city. The claim form is at the City Clerk's office in City Hall or on adaok.com (City Clerk > Claims). The City Attorney's office reviews claims; the Street Division provides the work order history for that location. **Don't tell anyone the city will or won't pay.** That's decided on the facts and state law.

## Streetlights

Who owns the light decides who fixes it.

- **Most residential and arterial streetlights** (on wood or steel utility poles) belong to the **power company**. The city pays a monthly charge per light. When one is out, the Street Division reports it through the power company's outage portal with the pole number. Their usual repair time is **5-10 business days**; we don't control it, but we follow up on anything over two weeks.
- **City-owned lights:** the decorative lights downtown (Main Street and Broadway), the lights in parks and parking lots, and lights in newer subdivisions where the developer installed underground wiring and the city accepted it (Countryside, Oak Hollow, Brookside and others listed on the streetlight map). These are fixed by the city's **signal and streetlight technician, Dwayne Pettigrew**, usually within **2-3 working days** for a bulb, photocell or fuse. Underground wiring faults take longer.
- If a resident can see a pole number (metal tag about eye level), ask for it. It saves a trip.
- Traffic signals out or flashing: call Dispatch any time; signals are always a priority.

## Other requests

- **Street signs** (missing, bent, faded): 5 working days; stop and yield signs same day.
- **Sidewalks:** adjacent property owners are responsible for sidewalk repair under the city code. Public Works can mark a dangerous section and refer it to Community Development.
- **Drainage, ditches and culverts:** inspected within 5 working days; work is scheduled by priority.
- **Speed humps and new stop signs:** these are traffic control decisions. Requests go to Public Works for a traffic count and then to council if warranted. E. 14th Street is in that process now.
- **Alleys:** graded twice a year (spring and fall) and on request.

## Winter

From about Thanksgiving to early March the asphalt plant is closed. During that time all pothole repairs are cold patch, and crews prioritize main streets and bus routes. Permanent repairs from the winter backlog are scheduled first when the plant reopens. When ice or snow is forecast, the Street Division shifts to sanding and plowing on the priority route map (hills, bridges, hospital and school routes first), and pothole work stops until roads are clear.

## Reports

The Street Division keeps a running log of repair requests (pothole, streetlight, sign, drainage) and reports totals weekly for the City Manager's Friday report.
