# Downtown trick-or-treat returns Saturday, Oct. 31; Main Street closed to traffic 3:30-6:30 p.m.

ADA, Okla. - Downtown Ada's trick-or-treat is back for its eleventh year on Saturday, October 31, from 4:00 to 6:00 p.m. on Main Street.

Downtown merchants will hand out candy from their storefronts, and children are encouraged to come in costume. The event is free and open to everyone.

**Street closure.** Main Street will be closed to vehicles from Mississippi Avenue to Townsend Avenue from 3:30 p.m. to 6:30 p.m. so families can walk in the street. Ada Police will set barricades at Mississippi, Broadway, Rennie and Townsend. Cross traffic on Broadway and the other north-south streets will be stopped at Main by officers as needed. Vehicles left parked on Main Street in the closed blocks after 3:15 p.m. may be towed at the owner's expense.

**Parking.** Free parking is available in the public lots one block north and south of Main, on the side streets, and in church and business lots where owners allow it. Please leave driveways and fire lanes clear. Accessible parking is reserved at the Broadway and Townsend ends of the closure.

**Safety.** Ada Fire Department will have a crew and an engine at Main and Broadway, and first aid will be available there. Parents are asked to:
- Walk with young children and set a meeting spot in case you get separated (the fire engine at Main and Broadway makes a good one).
- Check treats before children eat them.
- Use face paint instead of masks that block vision, and add reflective strips or a flashlight for the walk back to your car.
- Leave pets at home.

**Businesses.** Downtown businesses that want to hand out candy and haven't signed up can still join; there's no charge. Participating storefronts will have a pumpkin sign in the window. Businesses that are closed that evening are asked to leave a porch light or window light on so the sidewalks stay bright.

**Weather.** If it rains, check the City of Ada Facebook page by 2:00 p.m. Saturday for any changes.

"This is one of the best evenings of the year downtown," said City Manager Neil Hartsock. "Thank you to the merchants who make it happen, and to our police and fire crews who keep it safe."
