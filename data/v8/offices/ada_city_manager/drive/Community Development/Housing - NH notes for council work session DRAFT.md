# Housing: notes for the November council work session

*Neil Hartsock, draft 10-12-26. Not for distribution yet. Dean and Marcy to check the numbers.*

## What I heard

In my interviews every council member raised housing, and I've heard it every week since I started: at Rotary, from the hospital, from ECU, from two plant managers. People take jobs in Ada and live in Shawnee, Sulphur or Konawa because they can't find a house here, or they rent for two years waiting.

## What the numbers say (Dean to confirm)

- New single-family building permits, FY26: 31. FY27 so far (July-mid Oct): 22, including the first five Timber Bend lots.
- Platted subdivisions with open lots: Oak Hollow (Gentry Fork, about 9 lots left), Brookside (4 lots left), Timber Bend (28 lots, platted Sept 21).
- Our planning staff's rough estimate is that Ada could absorb 60-80 new homes a year if lots were available.
- Typical cost to bring a lot to "ready to build" in a new addition: $38,000-$45,000 including streets, water, sewer and drainage, before the house.

## Tools we have now

1. **Housing Incentive Policy and Res. 2025-11 Sec. 4.4.** Council can waive or reduce capacity fees for qualifying workforce housing by resolution. It has never been used. The policy's definition of "workforce housing" (price cap tied to area income) needs updating.
2. **Utility extensions.** APWA can share the cost of oversizing mains that serve future development.
3. **Permit turnaround.** Residential plan review is averaging 6 business days. Target: 3.
4. **Surplus city lots.** Finance has a list of 11 small lots the city owns; several could be sold for infill homes (Charter 4-3 limits apply over $50,000, which none of these approach).

## Ideas for the work session

- A clear, published capacity fee incentive for new subdivisions and infill homes under a price cap, adopted by council resolution so it applies the same way to every builder.
- Fast-track review for subdivisions with 10 or more lots.
- A "lot-ready" infrastructure cost-share pilot through APWA, capped at $250,000 a year.
- Work with ECU and the hospital on a homebuyer down-payment match for new employees (no city money; partners only).

## Message for residents

"Ada is growing, and we want people who work here to be able to live here. My priority is getting more homes built, and doing it in a way that is fair to every builder and every homebuyer."
