# Code Enforcement: Vacant, Open and Dilapidated Structures

*Community Development Department, City Hall, (580) 436-6300 ext. 236. Prepared by Dean Sizemore, Community Development Director, for staff answering calls and for council members' reference. Revised January 2026. This is a plain-language summary of how the city handles these cases; the city code and state law control.*

## Who to call

Residents who are worried about a vacant or run-down house near them should call Community Development (ext. 236) or use the contact form at adaok.com (topic "Permits and building" or "Complaint"). Give the address, or the house next to it, and what you're seeing: open doors or windows, people going in and out, roof caving in, animals, trash, tall grass. A name isn't required, but a phone number helps if the officer needs to ask a question.

Every complaint becomes a case with a number. Callers can ask for the status at any time.

If someone is in danger (a fire, a person inside who shouldn't be, a collapse), call 911 first.

## The three kinds of cases

**1. Tall grass, junk and inoperable vehicles (nuisance).** The most common cases by far. The code enforcement officer visits within 3 working days, photographs, and sends a notice to the owner of record (and posts the property). The owner gets 10 days to clean up. If it isn't done, the city's mowing contractor or crew does the work, and the cost plus an administrative charge becomes a lien on the property. Council confirms the lien amounts by resolution (most recently Resolution 2026-19, 11 properties, Sept 8).

**2. Open and unsecured structures.** A vacant building with open doors or broken windows is a hazard (fire, squatters, children). The officer posts a notice and mails it to the owner. The owner has 10 days to secure it. If they don't, the city boards it up and liens the cost. Most owners secure it themselves once they get the letter; in September two open structures on the west side were secured by owners without city boarding.

**3. Dilapidated buildings (demolition).** A building that has deteriorated to the point that it is dangerous or a blight and can't reasonably be repaired can be declared dilapidated under the procedure in state law. This is the slowest track, because the owner has rights at every step:
- The officer documents the condition (photos, a structural description).
- Notice goes to the owner, any mortgage holder and anyone else with a recorded interest, by certified mail and by posting. Owners who can't be found are notified by publication.
- A hearing is held (in Ada, before the council) at which the owner can respond.
- If the council declares the building dilapidated, the owner gets time to repair or demolish it. If they don't, the city may demolish it and lien the cost.
- Start to finish, a contested case takes **4 to 8 months**; one with a missing owner or a probate estate can take a year.

The city has budget for about 6-8 demolitions a year. Cases are ranked by danger (open to entry, near a school or park, structural collapse) and by how long they have been open.

## What we can't do

- We can't enter private property without the owner's permission or a warrant.
- We can't force an owner to sell, rent or occupy a house. A vacant house that's secured, mowed and not falling down isn't a code violation, even if neighbors dislike it.
- We can't share the owner's personal contact information beyond what's in the county's public property records, though we can tell a caller the case status.

## What helps

- Specifics: dates and times of activity, photos taken from the street or your own property.
- Calls to the police non-emergency line, (580) 332-4466, when people are going in and out. Police reports help show the building is a hazard.
- Neighbors who are willing to speak at a council hearing on a dilapidated building case.
- Residents may also speak about a property at a council meeting under Citizens to Be Heard (first and third Mondays, 5:45 p.m.). The council can't act that night, but staff follows up the next day.

## How long things usually take

| Step | Typical time |
|---|---|
| First site visit after a complaint | 1-3 working days |
| Notice to owner after the visit | 1-2 working days |
| Owner's time to comply (nuisance or open structure) | 10 days |
| City mowing or boarding after the deadline | 3-10 working days, depending on the contractor's list |
| Lien certified to the county after city work | Next quarterly resolution to council |
| Dilapidated building, notice to hearing | 6-10 weeks |
| Dilapidated building, hearing to demolition (if the owner does nothing) | 2-6 months |

## Staffing

One full-time code enforcement officer and the planning technician (part time on code cases). The FY28 request will ask for a second code enforcement officer; our case load per officer is about twice that of comparable cities.
