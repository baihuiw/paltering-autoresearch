# City of Cullman - Purchasing Procedures and Approval Levels

Revised October 1, 2026 (FY2027). Replaces the October 2024 sheet. Issued by the City Treasurer with the Mayor's approval. Questions: Accounting, ext. 215 or 216.

What changed this year: the Mayor's signature level is now $10,000 (was $7,500); purchasing card limits are unchanged; the quote form has a new box for "local vendor asked? yes/no."

## 1. Approval levels (per purchase, not per invoice)

| Amount | Quotes | Approvals |
|---|---|---|
| Under $1,000 | None required; use judgment | Department head |
| $1,000 - $4,999 | One written quote or a catalog/web price printout | Department head; purchase order required |
| $5,000 - $9,999 | Three written quotes on the City quote form | Department head and City Treasurer |
| $10,000 up to the state bid law limit | Three written quotes | Department head, City Treasurer and Mayor |
| Over the state bid law limit, or any public works contract over the public works limit | Formal sealed bids or a state/cooperative contract | City Council resolution; Mayor signs the contract |

The City Clerk keeps the current state bid and public works limits on file. When in doubt, call the Clerk before you call the vendor.

**Do not split purchases** to stay under a level. Two orders for the same thing from the same vendor in the same month count as one purchase.

## 2. Purchase orders

- Enter the requisition in the financial system before ordering. Accounting converts it to a PO, usually the same day.
- A PO is not needed for utilities, payroll items, debt service, postage, or reimbursements already approved by the Council.
- Emergency purchases (a water main break, a tree down across a street, a broken-down garbage truck) may be made by phone with the department head's approval. Enter a requisition within two business days and mark it "Emergency" with a one-line explanation.

## 3. Purchasing cards

- Card limits: $2,500 per transaction, $7,500 per month unless the Treasurer sets a different limit for a cardholder.
- Allowed: small supplies, registrations, travel, parts when the vendor won't take a PO.
- Not allowed: personal purchases (even if repaid), gift cards, alcohol, cash advances, splitting a purchase to stay under the transaction limit.
- Upload receipts to the card system by the 5th of the following month. Missing receipts after two reminders suspend the card.
- Alabama sales tax: the City is exempt. Show the exemption letter (copy on the shared drive) at the counter. If a vendor charges tax, ask for a refund.

## 4. Contracts and agreements

- Any agreement the City signs goes through the City Clerk for a number and filing, even if it costs nothing.
- Contracts over $10,000, leases, and anything with an indemnity clause go to the city attorney for review before the Mayor signs. Allow five business days.
- Annual renewals listed in the budget (grounds maintenance, janitorial, uniforms, pest control) still need a Council resolution if they are over the bid law limit.

## 5. Local vendors

The City may not prefer a vendor because it is local when the bid law applies. Below that level, departments are encouraged to ask at least one Cullman business for a quote. Record it on the quote form.

## 6. Year-end

- FY2026 invoices must reach Accounts Payable by October 23.
- Open POs for FY2026 operating items close November 13 unless the department asks Accounting to roll them.
- Capital project POs carry forward with the carryover resolution (November 9 Council meeting).

## 7. Surplus property

Vehicles and equipment are declared surplus by Council resolution and sold by online auction. Departments send the unit number, mileage or hours, and condition to the City Clerk. Recent example: Police units 14 and 22 (Res. 2026-95, October 12).

## 7a. Common mistakes (from the FY2026 audit and AP review)

1. Invoices dated before the PO. Get the PO first, every time, unless it's an emergency.
2. Missing receipts for purchasing card transactions, especially restaurant charges on travel. A credit card slip without the itemized receipt isn't enough.
3. Sales tax paid. About $2,100 in sales tax was paid to vendors in FY2026 and only part of it was recovered.
4. Splitting orders. Three orders of $4,800 from the same vendor in the same week for the same project count as one $14,400 purchase and needed three quotes and the Treasurer's approval.
5. Paying from the wrong year. Goods received by September 30 are FY2026 expenses even if the invoice comes in October.

## 8. Who to call

- Requisitions and POs: Accounts Payable, ext. 216
- Purchasing cards: City Treasurer, ext. 215
- Contracts and bid advertisements: City Clerk, ext. 210
