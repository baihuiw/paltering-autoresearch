# Open Burning Inside the City - Rules and Burn Permits

Cullman Fire Rescue, Fire Marshal's Office. Revised March 2025. Administration: (256) 734-2170, Monday-Friday 8:00 a.m. - 4:30 p.m. For an emergency, always call 911.

Leaf season brings more calls about burning than any other time of year. This sheet explains what is and isn't allowed inside the city limits.

## The short version

- **Burning leaves, limbs, yard waste and household trash inside the city limits is not allowed.** The City picks up leaves (November 2 - January 29), limbs and bulk items as part of garbage service.
- **Recreational fires** (fire pits, chimineas, outdoor fireplaces) are allowed with the rules below. No permit is needed.
- **Burn permits** are issued only for limited purposes: land clearing on larger tracts, agricultural burning, and fire department training.
- **Statewide fire alerts or drought burn bans** override everything on this sheet. When the state forestry agency issues a burn ban, no outdoor burning of any kind is allowed, including some recreational fires. Check the City's Facebook page or call Fire Rescue administration.

## Recreational fires (no permit needed)

1. Use clean, dry firewood or charcoal only. No leaves, trash, treated lumber, tires, plastics or construction debris.
2. The fire must be in a fire pit, ring or approved appliance, no larger than 3 feet across and 2 feet high.
3. At least **25 feet from any building**, fence, deck or overhanging branches (15 feet for an approved portable appliance with a spark screen).
4. An adult must be present until the fire is completely out. Keep a garden hose or extinguisher within reach.
5. Put the fire out if the wind picks up or if smoke is bothering neighbors. Fire Rescue may order any fire extinguished if it is a hazard or a nuisance.

Cooking fires (grills, smokers) follow the same common-sense rules: not on wooden decks under eaves, and never inside a garage.

## Burn permits (land clearing and agricultural)

- Available only for property of **at least 5 acres** in the city limits zoned agricultural or under active development, where vegetation can't reasonably be hauled away.
- Apply at Fire Rescue administration at least **2 business days** before burning. A fire officer will inspect the site.
- A state burn permit from the state forestry agency is also required for any burn over one-quarter acre or within certain distances of woodland. The City permit does not replace it.
- Burning allowed between 8:00 a.m. and 5:00 p.m. only; fire out by dark.
- Only natural vegetation from the site. No stumps dug up elsewhere, no building materials.
- Fee: $25 per permit (good for up to 7 consecutive days at the same site).

## Why the City doesn't allow leaf burning

Smoke from wet leaves is heavy, low and slow to clear, and it travels into neighbors' houses. In a city with houses this close together, a single pile can send smoke into a dozen homes. Leaf fires also get away from people faster than they expect in dry fall weather. The City added a second leaf vacuum truck this year so leaves can be picked up more quickly.

## What happens if someone burns illegally

Fire Rescue responds to smoke complaints. On a first call, the crew explains the rules and asks that the fire be put out. Repeat or dangerous burning can result in a citation to Municipal Court.

## Smoke detectors and space heaters

Since fall is also the start of heating season:
- Fire Rescue installs free smoke alarms for residents who can't afford them or can't install them. Call (256) 734-2170.
- Keep space heaters 3 feet from anything that can burn and plug them directly into the wall, not into an extension cord.
- Have chimneys inspected before the first fire of the season.

## Fire pit questions we get every fall

- *Can I burn in a metal barrel?* No. Burn barrels are open burning and aren't allowed inside the city limits.
- *Can I have a bonfire for a church or school event?* Larger fires for community events need prior approval from the Fire Marshal (call at least a week ahead). A Fire Rescue crew may be assigned to stand by.
- *My neighbor's fire pit smoke comes into my house every night.* Call Fire Rescue administration during business hours or police non-emergency after hours. A crew will check whether the fire meets the rules above and can order it put out if it is a nuisance.
