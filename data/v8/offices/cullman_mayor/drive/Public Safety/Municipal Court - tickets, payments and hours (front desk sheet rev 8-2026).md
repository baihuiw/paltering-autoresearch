# Cullman Municipal Court - Tickets, Payments and Hours

For the City Hall front desk, the Mayor's office and Police records. Revised August 2026 (new online payment vendor and fee). Please give callers this information as written and send anything legal to the court clerk.

**Court clerk's office:** (256) 734-2165. Court Clerk: Dana Faulk (dfaulk@cullmanal.gov).
**Window hours:** Monday-Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. (the window closes at 4:00 even though City Hall is open until 4:30). Closed on City holidays.

The Municipal Court handles traffic tickets and misdemeanors written by Cullman Police inside the city limits, and City ordinance violations. Tickets written by state troopers or the sheriff's office go to the county courts, not ours. The ticket says which court at the top.

## Can I pay online?

Yes, for most traffic tickets.
- Go to cullmanal.gov > Departments > Municipal Court > Pay a Ticket. You need the ticket (citation) number and your date of birth.
- Tickets usually appear in the online system **5 business days** after they are written. If it isn't there yet, wait a few days or call the clerk.
- The online vendor charges a convenience fee of 3.5% (minimum $2.00). The City does not receive the fee.
- Visa, Mastercard, Discover and debit cards. Payment posts immediately; print or save the receipt.
- Pay by phone through the same vendor: number on the court web page and on the back of the ticket.

**Paying the ticket is a guilty plea.** If you pay before your court date, you do not need to come to court, and the conviction is reported to the state (points may apply to your license).

## Tickets that cannot be paid online

These require a court appearance:
- speeding 26 mph or more over the limit, or any speed in a school zone when children are present;
- DUI, reckless driving, leaving the scene, driving while suspended or revoked;
- no insurance (second offense) or any ticket involving a wreck with injury;
- any misdemeanor arrest or summons (theft, assault, etc.);
- any case where the ticket says "court appearance required."

## Court dates and sessions

- Court is held in the Municipal Courtroom. Traffic arraignments: Tuesdays at 9:00 a.m. and Thursdays at 1:30 p.m. Trials are set by the court.
- Your first court date is written on the ticket. Arrive 15 minutes early; bring the ticket and your driver's license.
- If you want to plead not guilty, come to the arraignment or call the clerk before the date to request a trial setting.
- To ask for a continuance (a new date), call or come to the window **at least 2 business days before** your court date. One continuance is usually granted; after that it is up to the judge.
- Missing your court date without paying can lead to a failure-to-appear charge and a suspended license.

## Paying in person or by mail

- In person at the court clerk's window: cash, check, money order, or card (same 3.5% card fee).
- By mail: check or money order payable to "City of Cullman Municipal Court" with the ticket number on it. Must be received, not postmarked, by the court date.
- Payment plans: the clerk can set one up for fines and costs over $200. Ask at the window.

## Driving school and deferrals

The judge may allow a defensive driving course for some speeding tickets (generally less than 15 mph over, no tickets in the past 3 years). You must request it in court or by calling the clerk before your court date. It is not available online.

## What the Mayor's office should not do

The Mayor, Council and City staff outside the court cannot reduce, dismiss, or "take care of" a ticket, and should not discuss the facts of a case. If a caller asks, explain that the court is independent and give them the clerk's number. If an officer's conduct is the concern, refer them to the Police Department's administration, (256) 734-2160.

## Callers who say they never got a ticket in the mail

Cullman Police tickets are handed to the driver at the stop; the court doesn't mail tickets. If someone has a court notice for a ticket they don't remember, they should call the clerk with their name and date of birth. If the license was suspended for failure to appear, the clerk can tell them what is owed and how to get the case back on the docket.

## Fine amounts

Fines are set by the court's schedule and state law, and court costs are added. The clerk can tell a caller the total for a specific ticket. Typical speeding (1-10 mph over) totals with court costs run about $150-$200.
