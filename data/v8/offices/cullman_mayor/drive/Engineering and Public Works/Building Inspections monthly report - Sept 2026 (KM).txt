2. INSPECTIONS

Inspections performed: 486 (Sept 2025: 452). Average wait from request to inspection: 1.1 business days. Re-inspections (work not ready or corrections): 61, or 12.6%. The most common corrections: missing hurricane ties / roof uplift connectors on accessory buildings, deck ledger attachment, and missing GFCI protection.

5. ITEMS FOR THE MAYOR

a. Website. The permit page still has the 2019 fee table. The current fee sheet (rev. July 2025) with a worked garage example is ready for Bryce. Most phone calls ask "how much is a permit for a garage" and "do I need drawings." The answer to the second is a simple site plan and floor plan for most residential accessory buildings; no engineer's seal under 1,000 square feet for wood frame.
b. Contractors' business licenses. About one in five out-of-town contractors arrives without a City business license. We send them to the Clerk's counter before issuing the permit. With renewals coming Nov 2, I'd like the Clerk's notice to remind contractors that a current City license is required to pull permits in January.
c. Staffing. One inspector retires March 31, 2027. HR is posting the position in December so there is overlap for training. Certifications take time; a new hire needs at least the residential building certification within a year.
d. Short-term rentals. Ord. 2026-32 (first reading Oct 26) adds a business license classification; Building Inspections will do a one-time life-safety walk-through (smoke alarms, egress windows) when a short-term rental first licenses. About 40 are operating in the city now, by our count from the listing sites.
e. Floodplain. The FEMA flood map comment period ends Nov 12. Engineering is handling comments; no permit changes for us until the new maps are effective.

6. CARPORTS AND METAL BUILDINGS

A reminder for front-desk staff since several callers have asked: metal carports and prefab buildings bought from a lot or online still need a permit if over 200 square feet, and they must be anchored to the manufacturer's specifications. Many arrive with stamped anchor drawings; ask the customer to bring them.
