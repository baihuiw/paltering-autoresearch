# Building Inspections - Permit Fee Schedule and What to Bring

City of Cullman Building Inspections. City Hall, 204 2nd Avenue NE (enter by the side door on the parking lot; first office on the left). Monday-Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. Phone (256) 734-2150, ext. 240.

Chief Building Official: Ken Matthis (kmatthis@cullmanal.gov). Permit technician at the front counter takes applications and payments.

Revised July 1, 2025. The fee schedule is adopted by Council ordinance; this sheet is a summary. If this sheet and the ordinance differ, the ordinance controls.

## When you need a permit

A building permit is required inside the city limits to build, add on, move, or structurally change any building, including:
- new houses, additions, garages (attached or detached), carports, accessory buildings over 200 square feet, decks more than 30 inches off the ground;
- commercial buildings, tenant finish-outs, change of use;
- roofing on commercial buildings (residential re-roofing with no decking replacement does not need a permit);
- swimming pools and in-ground spas; retaining walls over 4 feet;
- demolition of any building.

Electrical, plumbing, gas and mechanical work need their own trade permits, pulled by a licensed contractor in that trade (homeowners may pull trade permits only for the home they live in).

Who can apply: the property owner, or a contractor with a current City of Cullman business license and the state license the work requires. Contractors pull the permit when they are doing the work.

## Building permit fee schedule (based on total construction valuation)

| Valuation | Fee |
|---|---|
| $0 - $2,000 | $35 |
| $2,001 - $50,000 | $35 plus $6.00 per $1,000 (or fraction) over $2,000 |
| $50,001 - $100,000 | $323 plus $5.00 per $1,000 (or fraction) over $50,000 |
| $100,001 and up | $573 plus $4.00 per $1,000 (or fraction) over $100,000 |

Valuation is the full cost of the work (labor and materials), whether you hire it out or do it yourself. For new residential construction the office uses the published building valuation table; for garages and accessory buildings, $45 per square foot (wood frame) or $35 per square foot (metal building on slab), unless your contract price is higher.

**Example:** a 24 x 24 detached wood-frame garage is 576 sq ft x $45 = $25,920. That is 24 thousands over $2,000: $35 + (24 x $6) = **$179**. Electrical permit is separate.

Other charges:
- Plan review (commercial only): 50% of the building permit fee, paid at submittal.
- Re-inspection (work not ready or not corrected): $50.
- Work begun without a permit: double the permit fee.
- Demolition permit: $50 residential, $100 commercial.
- Trade permits: $40 base plus the fee schedule on the trade application.
- Occupancy permit (CO), commercial: included.

## What to bring

**Residential accessory building or detached garage**
1. Completed application (counter or cullmanal.gov, Building Inspections page).
2. A site plan: a simple drawing of the lot showing the property lines, the house, the new building, and the distances from the new building to each property line and to the house. A hand drawing to scale is fine; a survey is better.
3. Construction drawings: floor plan with dimensions, door and window locations, and a wall section (foundation, wall framing, roof framing). Drawings do not have to be sealed by an engineer or architect for a wood-frame residential accessory building up to 1,000 sq ft. Metal buildings: the manufacturer's stamped drawings and anchor details.
4. Contractor name and license number, if any.
5. Zoning check. The counter checks zoning when you apply. Accessory buildings in residential zones must be behind the front line of the house and at least 5 feet from side and rear property lines (more in some zones). The building may not be used as a separate dwelling unless zoning allows it.

**New house or addition**: full plan set, site plan, energy code compliance form, and sewer/water tap receipts if new service.

**Commercial**: sealed plans (architect or engineer), fire department review (Fire Rescue fire marshal), and a business license for the occupant before the CO is issued.

## How long it takes

- Residential accessory buildings, decks, small additions: usually 3 to 5 business days if the drawings are complete.
- New homes: 5 to 10 business days.
- Commercial: 2 to 4 weeks depending on fire review.

Most delays are missing setback distances on site plans. Please measure before you come in.

## Inspections

Call ext. 240 by 3:00 p.m. for an inspection the next business day. Typical inspections for a garage: footing (before concrete), slab or foundation, framing, electrical rough-in (separate), and final. Keep the permit card posted where it can be seen from the street.

## Rental and investment property

Owners of rental property may apply for permits on their property in their own name. If the owner does the work personally, the owner is responsible for inspections and must be present or have someone present. Work by a hired contractor must be permitted by that contractor.
