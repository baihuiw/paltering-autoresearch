Dear Mr. and Mrs. Holcomb,

Thank you for your letter of September 23 about traffic on your block of 9th Street NW, and for including the photographs of the cars parked along the curb in the mornings. I read every word, and I'm sorry this has gone on as long as it has.

You asked two things: whether the City will put in speed bumps, and whether anyone looked at the speed numbers from the trailer that was on your street in August.

Here is where things stand. Our Police Traffic Unit placed a speed trailer on your block from August 11 to 18. It counted about 2,300 vehicles that week. The average speed was 26 miles per hour in a 25 zone, and 85 percent of drivers were going 31 or slower. The fastest car it caught was going 44, which is too fast for any neighborhood street, and I understand why that one stays with you. The Traffic Unit mailed you that summary on August 20.

Under the City's traffic calming procedure, speed humps or cushions are considered when the speed that 85 percent of drivers stay under is at least 8 miles per hour over the limit; on your street that would be 33. Your block came in just under that line. I know that can feel like a technicality when you're the one standing at the mailbox. The reason for the rule is that speed humps slow every vehicle on the street, including fire trucks and ambulances, and they tend to push traffic onto the next street over, so the City saves them for the streets where the numbers are highest.

What I can do, and have done:
- I've asked Chief Wakefield to schedule directed enforcement on 9th Street NW during the morning hours you described. Officers will be there at different times over the next several weeks.
- I've asked the Traffic Unit to put the trailer back on your block in the spring, after the school year's routines settle, so we can see whether anything has changed.
- If the numbers rise, Engineering will do a full speed study and walk you through the neighborhood petition that is part of the process.

On the parked cars: parking on both sides of the street is allowed on your block, and it actually tends to slow traffic down. If a vehicle is blocking your driveway or is parked within 15 feet of a fire hydrant, please call Police non-emergency at (256) 734-2160 and an officer will respond.

You mentioned the school bus stop at the corner. Our school resource officers and the school system's transportation office work together on bus stop locations; if you think the stop should move, I'm glad to pass that along to the transportation office for you.

I've enclosed a copy of the traffic calming procedure so you can see each step. If you have questions about it, Matt Coggins in Engineering is at (256) 734-2150, ext. 230.

Thank you for caring about your street and for writing to me.

Sincerely,

Denise Kreuzer
Mayor, City of Cullman
