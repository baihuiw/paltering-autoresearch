Dear Ms. Garrison,

Thank you for your letter, and for the petition with 212 signatures asking the City to build a public dog park. That's a lot of dog owners, and I'm glad you sent it to me in my first week.

I asked our Parks and Recreation Director where things stand. Here is what I learned.

A dog park is not in the City's budget for this fiscal year, which started October 1. The FY2027 Parks capital budget went to the lighting at fields 3 and 4 (partly paid for by a state grant) and to replacing one neighborhood playground that dates from 1998. Those were decided before I took office, and I think they were the right priorities.

That said, a dog park is not an expensive project compared with most parks work. The Parks Director estimates $60,000 to $90,000 for a fenced area of about an acre with separate sections for large and small dogs, double gates, a water fountain for dogs, benches, shade and waste stations. The bigger questions are where to put it and who keeps it up. A dog park needs level ground, parking, and enough distance from houses that barking isn't a nuisance.

Here is what I've asked Parks to do:
1. Look at two or three City-owned sites and report back by the spring, so a dog park can be considered for the FY2028 budget.
2. Find out whether a local group would help with fundraising or upkeep. In other cities, a "friends of the dog park" group often raises money for benches and shade and helps keep the rules posted. If you'd be willing to help start one, please call the Parks office at (256) 734-2195.
3. Check whether any state or private grants are available. Some pet food companies and national foundations give small grants for dog parks.

You also asked whether the City would let dogs off leash in a part of an existing park until a dog park is built. I asked about that too. The City's leash ordinance applies in every park, and an informal off-leash area without fencing tends to cause problems with children's play areas and ballfields nearby. I'd rather do it right once.

I can't promise a date, but I can promise it will be on the list when the FY2028 budget is put together next summer, and I've put your petition in the file so the Council sees it.

In the meantime, dogs on leashes are welcome on the greenway and in all City parks, except on athletic fields and playgrounds.

Thank you for writing.

Sincerely,

Denise Kreuzer
Mayor, City of Cullman
