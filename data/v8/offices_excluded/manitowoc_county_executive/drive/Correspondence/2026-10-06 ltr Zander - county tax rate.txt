Dear Mr. Zander,

Thank you for your letter about property taxes. You wrote that your bill has gone up every year you've owned your home and that you read in the paper the county budget is going up again. You deserve a straight answer, so let me explain what the county's 2027 recommended budget does and does not do to your bill.

There are two numbers people often mix up: the levy and the rate. The levy is the total dollar amount the county collects in property tax from everyone in the county. The rate is what each taxpayer pays per $1,000 of equalized (market) value.

Under the budget I sent to the County Board on October 1, the county levy would go from $41.62 million this year to $42.31 million in 2027, an increase of 1.7%. That increase is within the state levy limit, which only allows growth for net new construction and debt service. Because property values in the county grew faster than the levy, the estimated county tax rate would actually go down, from $4.18 to $4.09 per $1,000. On a home with an equalized value of $200,000, the county portion of the bill would be about $818, roughly $18 less than this year. Your own number will depend on your home's value and on your city's assessment.

It is also worth knowing that the county is only one of several governments on your bill. The county portion is about a fifth of the total. The rest goes to your school district, the City of Manitowoc, and Lakeshore Technical College. Each of those sets its own levy and holds its own budget hearing, and the county has no say over them. If your total bill goes up in December, it may be because of one of those, so I encourage you to look at the line-by-line breakdown on the back of the bill.

The County Board will hold its public hearing on the 2027 budget on Monday, November 9, at 6:00 p.m. at the Heritage Center, 1701 Michigan Ave., Manitowoc. Anyone may speak. Fill out a speaker card at the door before 6:00 p.m. with your name and municipality; each speaker has 3 minutes. The Board listens during the hearing rather than answering questions, and then holds its annual meeting, where it may amend and adopt the budget the same night.

If you can't come, you can send written comments to the County Clerk by Friday, November 6, at 4:30 p.m., by mail to the Courthouse or by email to countyclerk@manitowoccountywi.gov. Written comments are copied to all 25 supervisors. You are also welcome to call or write your own County Board supervisor. The full recommended budget is on the county website and at the County Clerk's office and the Manitowoc, Two Rivers and Kiel libraries.

I spent 31 years as a plant controller, and I take the job of watching every dollar seriously. Thank you for holding us to it.

Sincerely,
