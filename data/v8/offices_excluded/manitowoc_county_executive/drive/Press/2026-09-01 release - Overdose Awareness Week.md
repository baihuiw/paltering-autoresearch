## Manitowoc County marks Overdose Awareness Week, August 31 to September 6

Manitowoc County Public Health and Human Services mark International Overdose Awareness Day and the week that follows with free naloxone kits, a remembrance table and information on treatment and recovery help.

"Every overdose is a neighbor, a son, a daughter," said Health Officer Nicole Fessenden. "Naloxone is a safe, simple way to save a life, and anyone can carry it."

**What is available**
- Free naloxone kits and a five-minute how-to at the Public Health office, 1028 S. 9th St., Monday through Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.
- A remembrance table with names and photographs families have shared, at the Courthouse first-floor rotunda, Wednesday, September 2 through Friday, September 4.
- Information on how to reach a county peer recovery coach, (920) 683-4230.

**Peer recovery coaches.** Since March 2025 the county's peer recovery coaches, paid from the county's opioid settlement fund, have worked with people after an overdose or a crisis. "A coach who has been there can reach someone in the hospital in a way the rest of us can't," said Human Services Director Sandra Lohrey. Coaches made contact with 87 percent of people referred within 72 hours in 2026.

The county receives money from the national opioid settlements. The County Board set that money aside in a separate fund in November 2025 so that it is used only for opioid response.

To talk with someone now, call or text 988, the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline.
