# Speaking at the County Budget Hearing

**Manitowoc County Clerk** | Handout for the annual budget hearing | Rev. October 2024

*Kelly: this is the Clerk's standing handout. Clerk's office confirmed 10-9-26 it is unchanged for this year; dates below filled in for 2026. Use it for answering resident mail and for the newsletter. -TG*

Every fall the County Board of Supervisors holds a public hearing on the proposed county budget before it adopts the budget and sets the property tax levy. The hearing is required by Section 65.90 of the Wisconsin Statutes, and anyone may attend.

## This year's hearing

- **When:** Monday, November 9, 2026, 6:00 p.m.
- **Where:** Heritage Center, County Board Meeting Room, 1701 Michigan Ave., Manitowoc. The building is accessible; parking is in the lot off Michigan Ave.
- **What follows:** The County Board's annual meeting begins right after the hearing closes. At the annual meeting the Board may amend the budget and may adopt it and set the levy that same night.

## Before the hearing

The proposed budget is on the county website (Finance page), at the County Clerk's office in the Courthouse (1010 S. 8th St.), and at the Manitowoc, Two Rivers (Lester) and Kiel public libraries. The budget summary and hearing notice are published in the county's official newspaper as a Class 1 notice; in 2026 the publication date is Friday, October 23.

## How to speak

1. **Fill out a speaker card.** Cards are on the table at the meeting room door. Write your name and the city, village or town where you live. Turn the card in to the Clerk's staff **before 6:00 p.m.** You do not need to sign up in advance or give your address.
2. **Wait to be called.** The Chair calls speakers in the order cards were turned in.
3. **Come to the microphone** and state your name and municipality for the record.
4. **You have 3 minutes.** A timer is on the podium. The Chair may extend the time for a speaker, or for everyone, if the number of speakers allows.
5. **One turn per person.** You may speak about any part of the budget. Groups are asked to choose one or two speakers rather than repeat the same comments.

## What to expect

- **The Board listens; it does not answer questions during the hearing.** Supervisors and staff will not respond to speakers or debate during the hearing. If you have a question you want answered, contact your supervisor or the department before the hearing, or ask after the meeting.
- The Clerk keeps a list of speakers and a short summary of each comment in the minutes.
- Signs, banners and props are not permitted in the meeting room. Please silence phones.
- The meeting is recorded and posted on the county website within two business days.

## Written comments

You do not have to attend to be heard. Written comments may be sent to:

- **County Clerk**, 1010 S. 8th St., Manitowoc, WI 54220, or
- **countyclerk@manitowoccountywi.gov**

Written comments must be **received by Friday, November 6, 2026, at 4:30 p.m.** The Clerk's office copies every written comment to all 25 supervisors before the hearing. Comments received after the deadline are passed along but may not reach supervisors before the vote.

## Contacting your supervisor

Manitowoc County has 25 supervisory districts. To find your district and your supervisor's phone and email:

- use the "Find my supervisor" map on the County Board page of manitowoccountywi.gov, or
- call the County Clerk's office, (920) 683-4004, Monday-Friday, 8:00 a.m.-4:30 p.m.

Supervisors are glad to hear from residents at any time, not only at the hearing.

## Questions about the budget itself

- Numbers and fund questions: Finance Department, (920) 683-2740
- A specific department's budget: that department (see the county directory)
- The County Executive's recommended budget: Office of the County Executive, (920) 683-4000, countyexecutive@manitowoccountywi.gov

## Who decides what

The County Executive proposes the budget. The County Board's Finance Committee reviews it with the departments. The full County Board amends and adopts it. The County Executive may veto parts of the adopted budget, and the Board may override a veto by a two-thirds vote. The County Board, not the hearing, makes the final decisions; the hearing is how residents are heard before they do.
