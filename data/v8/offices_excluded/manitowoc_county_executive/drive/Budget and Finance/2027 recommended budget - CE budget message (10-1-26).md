# 2027 Recommended Budget - County Executive's Budget Message

**To:** Chair Ron Zastrow and Members of the Manitowoc County Board of Supervisors
**From:** Joel Stahlberg, County Executive
**Date:** October 1, 2026

I present the 2027 recommended budget for Manitowoc County. I came into this office two weeks ago with a budget already well underway, and I thank Finance Director Mark Esslinger, the department heads and their staff for the work they did before and after the change in leadership. The budget reflects their requests, the Board's priorities and a few choices of my own.

## The numbers

| | 2026 Adopted | 2027 Recommended | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total expenditures | $128.4 million | $131.9 million | +2.7% |
| Property tax levy | $41.62 million | $42.31 million | +1.7% |
| Estimated county tax rate (per $1,000 equalized value) | $4.18 | $4.09 | -$0.09 |

The levy increase is within the state levy limit, which allows growth based on net new construction plus debt service. Because equalized values grew faster than the levy, the estimated county tax rate goes down nine cents. On a home with an equalized value of $200,000, the county portion of the tax bill would be about $818, roughly $18 less than this year.

There are no new positions funded by the levy. Wage increases follow the county pay plan adopted last year (3% general increase). Health insurance premiums rise 6.5%, less than the 9% we were warned about in the spring.

## What this budget does

**Public safety and 911.** The budget includes $412,000 to upgrade the Joint Dispatch Center's computer-aided dispatch (CAD) system and prepare for Next Generation 911, with go-live in January 2027. Most of the cost is paid from the county 911 fund, with $96,000 from the levy. The upgrade gives dispatchers faster mapping and better caller location, and it positions the county for text, photo and video 911 in the years ahead.

**Roads.** The highway budget includes $4.1 million for resurfacing about 14 miles of county trunk highways, a deck overlay on the CTH Q bridge, and two new tandem plow trucks. County crews will continue to maintain I-43 and the state highways under our contract with the state.

**Human services.** Human Services' budget grows 3.1%, mostly for children's long-term support and crisis services, offset in part by state and federal revenue. The ADRC keeps all congregate meal sites open.

**Parks.** The budget funds the first year of improvements at Fischer Creek Park: an accessible kayak and canoe launch and the first phase of trail resurfacing, about $300,000 from the parks fund and donations, with no levy. The proposed campground will go to the County Board separately after the Planning and Park Commission's recommendation.

**Recycling.** All six rural drop-off sites stay open on their current schedule. The Recycling Center budget includes a replacement skid steer and roof repairs over the tip floor, paid from recycling revenue.

**Expo and Ice Center.** Parking lot sealcoating and a new dasher board system at the Ice Center, paid from Expo revenues and the youth hockey association's $60,000 pledge.

**Buildings.** Courthouse window replacement (phase 2) and a new roof on the Human Services building.

## What this budget does not do

It does not use one-time money for ongoing costs. It does not draw down the general fund balance, which stays at about 22% of expenditures, inside the Board's policy range. It does not add debt beyond the borrowing already planned for the highway program.
