# Rural address signs and new addresses - how it works

Manitowoc County Planning & Land Information / Emergency Services. Reference sheet for front-desk staff and the County Executive's office. Rev. March 2026.

## Who assigns addresses

In the towns, Manitowoc County assigns rural addresses (sometimes called fire numbers) under the county's rural addressing ordinance. Cities and villages assign their own addresses.

A property owner needs an address before a building permit is issued for a new home or other occupied building. The town or the owner applies to Planning & Land Information; the GIS Coordinator assigns the number based on the driveway location and the county grid.

New streets in a subdivision get their names approved by the town and checked by the county against the county's street name list, so two roads in the county don't end up with the same or similar names. Numbers are assigned once the final plat is recorded.

## What happens after an address is assigned

1. The address goes into the county GIS address points (usually within a few days).
2. The county notifies the town, the post office, the school district and the utilities.
3. The Joint Dispatch Center (JDC) gets the address for its dispatch system.
4. The owner picks up a rural address sign at Planning & Land Information (first sign $45 including the post; replacement $35). Highway or town crews install signs on request.

## Sign rules

- Blue sign with white reflective numbers, on a post at the end of the driveway, on the same side of the road as the driveway.
- Visible from both directions. Keep brush and snow away from it.
- If a sign is damaged by a plow or a mower, call Planning & Land Information. Damage by a county plow is replaced at no charge.

## If someone asks about their address and 911

- When you call 911, give your full address and the town you live in. On a cell phone, the dispatcher may also see an approximate location, but it can be off by hundreds of feet in rural areas.
- If you are new to the area, tell the dispatcher a cross road or landmark.
- Questions about a specific address: GIS Coordinator, Planning & Land Information, (920) 683-4185.
- Questions about 911 service: Emergency Services, (920) 683-4207.
