# Tax-deeded property: how Manitowoc County sells it

Public Works Department. Reference for staff, the County Executive's office and anyone who calls. Rev. April 2024.

## How the county ends up owning property

When real estate taxes go unpaid, the County Treasurer starts a foreclosure after the required waiting period. If the owner does not redeem by paying the taxes, interest and costs, the court grants the county title (a "tax deed" or in rem judgment). The county does not want to own this property. Our goal is to put it back in private hands and back on the tax roll.

Public Works manages the property once the county owns it: securing buildings, mowing, insurance, appraisal and sale.

## How sales normally work

1. **Appraisal.** Each parcel is appraised before it is offered. The appraisal is the minimum bid unless the Public Works Committee sets a different minimum.
2. **Sealed-bid list.** Parcels are offered twice a year (spring and fall) on a sealed-bid list approved by the Public Works Committee.
3. **Advertising.** The list is published three times in the official newspaper and posted on the county website. Signs are placed on vacant lots.
4. **Notice to interested parties.** Adjoining owners and anyone on the interested buyer list for a parcel are mailed a copy of the listing.
5. **Bid opening.** Bids are opened in public at the stated time. The Public Works Committee accepts the high bid or rejects all bids.
6. **Closing.** Buyer pays in full within 30 days. The county conveys by quit claim deed, without title insurance or warranties.

## Exceptions

- **Low-value parcels** (appraised under $5,000), such as road remnants and landlocked strips, may be offered to adjoining owners first.
- **Parcels with environmental problems** are held until a cleanup plan or closure letter is in place.
- **Direct sales.** The Public Works Committee may approve a negotiated sale in unusual cases (for example, a parcel that drew no bids on two lists). The reason should be stated in the minutes.

## Where the money goes

Sale proceeds first repay the delinquent taxes, interest and costs on the parcel. Any remainder goes to the county general fund. Prior owners may have rights to part of the surplus under state law; refer those questions to Corporation Counsel.

## Results

In 2025 the county sold 14 tax-deeded parcels on its two sealed-bid lists, returning about $420,000 in assessed value to the tax roll. On average, sealed-bid sales brought 118% of appraisal.

## Interested buyer list

Anyone may ask to be notified when a specific parcel, or any parcel in a town or city, is listed. Call Public Works at (920) 683-4054 or email the department. Names stay on the list for two years.
