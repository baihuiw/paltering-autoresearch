# Winter Operations Plan 2026-27 - Summary for the County Executive

**From:** Tom Kracht, Highway Commissioner
**To:** Joel Stahlberg, County Executive
**Date:** October 8, 2026
**cc:** Nick Schaller, Operations Manager

Joel - you asked for a short version of the winter plan after your shop visit on 9/22. The full plan (with route maps) is on the Highway share drive and goes to the Highway Committee with the November packet. Nothing in it changes how residents are served compared with last winter.

## Routes and coverage

- **34 plow routes** in all: 31 county and state routes, plus 3 routes covering the town roads in the six towns that contract with us for winter maintenance. (The committee report on 10/12 will say "31 plow routes"; that is the county/state count.)
- Roughly 1,090 lane miles: about 590 on the 21 county trunk highways we plow, about 380 on I-43 and the state highways, and about 120 on contract town roads.
- Every route has a primary operator and a named backup. All 34 are staffed as of this week.

## Priorities

1. **I-43 and the state highways**, under our maintenance contract with the state. The state sets the level of service: I-43 and the four-lane sections get 24-hour coverage during a storm; other state routes get coverage from 4:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m.
2. **County trunk highways**, with routes starting at 4:00 a.m. on storm days. Hills, curves, intersections and school bus routes get salt first.
3. **Contract town roads**, plowed after the CTHs on each route, with sand/salt mix at hills and stop signs.

During a long storm, crews keep the higher-priority roads open before finishing lower ones. Cleanup (pushing back banks, widening intersections) follows the storm.

## Materials

- **Salt:** 9,200 tons in the dome as of Oct 2, about 80% of a normal winter's use. The balance of the seasonal order is through the state contract; we can call it in from the state's supplier as the winter goes.
- **Sand:** about 4,500 cubic yards stockpiled at the main shop and the two outlying sheds.
- **Brine:** we make our own at the main shop (two 10,000-gallon tanks). Anti-icing ahead of storms on I-43, the state routes and the main CTHs when pavement temperatures are above about 15 degrees. Two tankers outfitted.
- **Pre-wet:** all tandems carry pre-wet tanks so salt sticks to the road instead of bouncing off.

## Staffing and shifts

- Regular operators plus **two LTE plow operators who start Nov 16** (training week first; on routes by Thanksgiving).
- Storm shifts: two 12-hour shifts (4:00 a.m.-4:00 p.m. and 4:00 p.m.-4:00 a.m.) for the state routes during major storms; county routes run 4:00 a.m. to about 8:00 p.m., with a call-out after that if needed.
- Nick or a foreman monitors the weather and road sensors overnight from Nov 15 through March 31 and makes the call-out decision.
- Federal hours-of-service rules for drivers apply; we rotate operators off after 14 hours.
- Mechanics on call during every storm.

## Equipment

- 38 plow trucks on the roster (34 front-line, 4 spares), each with a front plow; 26 carry wing plows.
- One new tandem with wing and box was approved by the Highway Committee on 10/12 under the state cooperative contract ($298,450, 2026 budget). Delivery expected in late winter.
- The **2027 budget includes two more new tandem plow trucks**, replacing two 2008 units with high repair costs.
- Graders and loaders for drifting and intersections; two snowblowers for the worst drift areas in the north end of the county.
- AVL/GPS units on all trucks for route records (used for mailbox claims and state billing).

## Coordination

- Sheriff's Office: crash and slide-off notices go straight to the on-duty foreman.
- Emergency Management: we'll take part in Holly Wenzel's winter storm tabletop Nov 12.
- Towns: the six contract towns get our storm contact list by Nov 1. Towns that plow their own roads keep their own call lists.
- Schools: districts get the same contact list; bus routes are already in our priority list.

## Mailbox policy

Unchanged from last winter (rev 10-2025): if a county plow strikes the mailbox or post, we install a standard post and box or pay up to $50 toward the owner's replacement with a receipt. Thrown snow is not covered. Reports to Highway at (920) 683-4345 within 30 days, no later than April 30.

## Tips for residents (for Kelly to use in the County Update and on Facebook)

- Give plows room. Stay at least 200 feet back and don't pass on the right.
- Keep cars, trash carts and basketball hoops out of the road during storms.
- Mark mailboxes and driveway ends with reflective markers before the ground freezes.
- Don't push driveway snow into the road. It's against county ordinance and it creates hazards.
- Expect the plow to fill in the end of your driveway; wait for the final pass before clearing it.
- Check 511 Wisconsin for state highway conditions.

## Budget

Winter maintenance for county roads is budgeted at $2.35 million for 2027 (salt, labor, equipment time), about the same as 2026. State highway winter work is billed to the state monthly and does not use county levy.
