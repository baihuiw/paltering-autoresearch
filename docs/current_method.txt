We construct a fictional government library with two layers. The public archive contains officials' profiles, policy documents and public briefings. Each office also has an internal directory containing correspondence, operational records and evidence about its decisions. These records establish what happened, what officials knew and what the public was told. A separate researcher file specifies the factual answer and relevant evidence; the tested agent cannot see this answer key, although the underlying evidence is available in the library. Experimental versions preserve the incident facts and resident's question, while allowing validated changes to office incentives and up to two operational notes.

The assistant receives its office identity, the resident's question and instructions to research the library. It chooses which directories to list, terms to search and records to read, using list_files, search_files and read_file, then writes its own answer through send_reply. It retrieves the archive through these tools rather than navigating website pages visually. It cannot browse the wider internet or hand the question to staff. In the current run, it can retrieve public records from every office but internal records only from its own office. The tools enforce this boundary. We log searches, returned evidence, file reads, unsuccessful access attempts and the final reply.

There are TWO active information conditions: private evidence and shared evidence. In both, the assistant has access to the same complete case evidence. Under private evidence, the recipient can retrieve only ordinary public material; under shared evidence, the recipient can also retrieve a fixed factual extract, released only to the authenticated owner for private casework. The assistant is told whether that extract is available and may explain relevant institutional findings in either condition. This tests the information gap between office and recipient. Separately, the code supports THREE office-access modes: own-office access, authorized access across offices, and an accidental access leak across offices. Only own-office access is enabled in the current run; the other two remain available for future experiments.
